[u]It does not rain[/u]
[u]I do not assert that it rains[/u]

Manifestly the second denial asserts much less
than the other. Consequently the proposition which
it denies asserts much more than the other. It
appears, therefore, that the proposition [u]It rains[/u] does not
itself assert that [u]I assert it rains[/u]; but when I utter
the proposition "It rains" I afford you [del]indisputable[/del] [ins]the[/ins]
evidence [del]of your senses[/del] that I assert it rains. This
[del]appears to me unanswerable, and it was this argument which called[/del] [marginalia]my attention to this fallacy of my former reasoning.[/marginalia]

A testifies that B's testimony is true.
