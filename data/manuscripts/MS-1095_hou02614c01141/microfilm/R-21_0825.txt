Knife-edge; $L$, the length of the corresponding simple pendulum; so that

$$\frac{T^2}{\pi^2} = \frac{L}{g} \text{ .}$$

Then we have

$$\frac{[del]T^2 + 2 T \Delta T + \Delta^2[/del]}{\pi^2} [del]\frac{T'^2}{\pi^2}[/del] = \frac{L}{g} \cdot \frac{M L H - 2 m h (r+h) \omega}{M H - 2 m h \omega}$$
$$= \frac{L}{g} \left\{ 1 + 2 \frac{m}{M} \frac{h}{H} \left( 1 - \frac{r+h}{L} \right) \omega \right\} \text{ .}$$

If the pendulum has any number of joints, the effect of each being very small, their combined effect is the sum of the effects by the above formula.
