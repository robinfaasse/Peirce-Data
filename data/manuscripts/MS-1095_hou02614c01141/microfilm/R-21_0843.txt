[u]Length of Metre in Inches.[/u]

1 A. R. Clarke (Philosophical Transactions for 186[del]6[/del][ins]7[/ins], p. 180) found
the Toise to be equal to 76.734402 inches. But he
finds Bessel's toise to be shorter by 1.90 millionths of a
yard. Now the metre was intended to be made equal
to 443.296 lines, of which 864 make a toise. But
Benoît finds that the metre is really shorter than
that proportion of Bessel's toise by its 74000 th
part. Required to calculate from these data
the length of the metre in inches.

2 Mr. Rogers found the metre equal to 39.37027 inches;
but little reliance is to be placed upon this.

3 General Comstock found the metre equal to 39.36985
inches; and this is, without doubt, the best determi-
nation yet made.

4 The Spanish four-metre bar was found by Clarke [ins]etc[/ins]
(Phil. Trans. for 1873 [del]p. 469[/del]) to be at [del]61.25 Fahr.[/del] [ins]16°.25 Centigrade[/ins]
equal to 157.497652 inches. It has been found that
this bar is 309.84 microns too short at 0° Centigrade
and that its average coefficient of expansion
from 0° C up to t° C is 45.701 + 0.0326 t microns
per degree centigrade.

5 An iron bar long used in Washington under the name
of the committee metre was found by Clarke to have
a length of 39.3[del]6[/del][ins]7[/ins]978 inches. But Messrs Barnard
and Tresca declare this to be too long by 3.4 microns.

We may conclude metre = 39.3699 inches
