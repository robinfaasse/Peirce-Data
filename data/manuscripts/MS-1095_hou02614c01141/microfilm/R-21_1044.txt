[fig]Right triangle with sides labeled ds, de, R, R and angles d\theta, \theta[/fig]

By this triangle $d\theta = \sin\theta \frac{de}{R}$
or $D_{\theta}e = R \csc\theta$

[marginalia]This agrees with definition of $\theta$, not with last formula[/marginalia]

Integrating $e = R \log\tan\frac{\theta}{2}$

When $e=0$ $\log\tan\frac{\theta}{2} = 0$ $\tan\frac{\theta}{2} = 1$ $\theta = 90^{\circ}$ showing the [del]angle for[/del] position at which the zero of $e$ begins.

In the equation
$D_e s = \sin\theta$

Substitute for $e$ its value
$D_{R\log\tan\frac{\theta}{2}} s = \sin\theta$

$\frac{ds}{R d\log\tan\frac{\theta}{2}} = \sin\theta$

$\frac{ds}{\frac{d\tan\frac{\theta}{2}}{\tan\frac{\theta}{2}}} = R\sin\theta$

$\frac{ds}{d\tan\frac{\theta}{2}} = R \frac{\sin\theta}{\tan\frac{\theta}{2}}$

$\frac{ds}{\frac{1}{2}\sec^2\frac{\theta}{2} \cdot d\theta} = R \frac{\sin\theta}{\tan\frac{\theta}{2}}$

$\frac{ds}{d\theta} = R \frac{\sin\theta}{2\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}} = R$

$s = R\theta + C$

or $s = R(\theta - 90^{\circ})$

$e = -R \log(1 - \tan^2\frac{\theta}{2})$[/marginalia]
