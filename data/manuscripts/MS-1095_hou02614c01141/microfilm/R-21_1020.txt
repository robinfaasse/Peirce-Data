Knife-edge; L, the length of the corresponding
simple pendulum; so that

$$\frac{T^2}{\pi^2} = \frac{L}{g} .$$

Then we have

$$\frac{(T+\Delta T)^2}{\pi^2} = \frac{1}{g} \cdot \frac{MLH - 2mh(r+h)\omega + 2mg^2\omega}{MH - 2mh\omega} ,$$

$$\frac{\Delta T}{T} = \frac{m}{M} \frac{h}{H} (1 - \frac{r+h}{L}) \omega .$$

If the pendulum has any number of joints, the
values of $\omega$ being [del]now[/del] all very small, their com-
bined effect is the sum of the [ins]single[/ins] [del]se[/del] effects by the
above formula.

If the pendulum is reversible and if $r_1$, $r_2$,
etc. and $h_1$, $h_2$, etc. are the values of $r$ and
$h$ for the different pieces referred to one
knife-edge and $r'_1$, $r'_2$, etc. and $h'_1$, $h'_2$, etc.
the values for the same joints referred to
the other knife-edge, we have
$$r'_i = L - r_{i+1}$$
$$h'_i = r_{i+1} - r_i - h_i .$$
