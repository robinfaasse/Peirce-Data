Suppose that instead of a moving plane
we have a parabolic cylinder defined by
the equation
$$z = C x^2$$
which oscillates in the direction of $y$. We will
assume $u=0$, $w=0$, [del]and[/del] but $v$ is a function
of $x$ and $z$. At the [del]plane[/del] cylinder $v=V$
and $V = c \sin nt$
The differential equation is
$$D_t v = \mu' (D_x^2 v + D_z^2 v)$$
Write
$$v = P \sin nt + Q \cos nt$$
where $P$ and $Q$ are supposed to be indepen-
dent of the time. Then
$$D_t v = n P \cos nt - n Q \sin nt$$
$$= \mu' (D_x^2 P + D_z^2 P) \sin nt + \mu' (D_x^2 Q + D_z^2 Q) \cos nt$$
so that
$$P = \frac{\mu'}{n} (D_x^2 Q + D_z^2 Q)$$
$$Q = - \frac{\mu'}{n} (D_x^2 P + D_z^2 P) = - \frac{\mu'^2}{n^2} (D_x^4 Q + 2 D_x^2 D_z^2 Q + D_z^4 Q)$$
We only want to know what $P$ and $Q$ become
in the immediate neighborhood of the cylinder.
[del]For[/del] [ins]Let[/ins] a certain point $x_1, z_1$ lie on the cylinder
so that
$$z_1 = C x_1^2.$$
