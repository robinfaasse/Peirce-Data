On an Error in absolute determinations
of gravity by the Reversible pendulum.
By C. S. Peirce.

In the reversible pendulums of Repsold's
large model (of the small one I cannot
speak), as well as in those I have construc-
ted, the tube composing the stem of the pen-
dulum is cut away about the knife-edges
so much as to be greatly weakened at those
points, so that the question arises whether
the flexure at the heavy end does not
invalidate the determinations.

The mathematical problem offers no
special difficulty, but the solution would
present too complicated an expression
if we were to introduce the idea of a
portion of the pendulum rotating about
[del]the weak point[/del]. The effect of [del]it[/del] [ins]its[/ins] bending at
the weak points can only affect, 1[sup]st[/sup] the
vis viva and, 2[sup]nd[/sup] the force of restoration.
