To determine these four functions we
have the conditions that the [ins]^[/ins] velocity [del]s[/del] and
pressure must be real, that the velocity
[del]and[/del] must vanish at all points at infinity,
and that the air adjacent to the pendulum
must follow its motions [del]is[/del] as it is observed
to do. Namely, the air probably adheres to every
part of the pendulum and does not slip over
its surface; or, if there is any slipping, it may
at first be neglected. The whole difficulty
of the problem consists in fulfilling these
conditions.

Let us put
$$x = r \cos \theta \qquad y = r \sin \theta$$

Then,
$$x + iy = r \odot^{i\theta} \qquad y + ix = i r \odot^{-i\theta},$$
and [del]we have[/del] [ins]we may write[/ins]
$$\chi = F_1(r \odot^{i\theta}) + F_2(i r \odot^{-i\theta}) + i r \odot^{-i\theta} F_3(r \odot^{i\theta}) + r \odot^{i\theta} F_4(i r \odot^{-i\theta}).$$

Then, we have
$$u = D_y \chi = i F_1'(r \odot^{i\theta}) + F_2'(i r \odot^{-i\theta}) + F_3(r \odot^{i\theta}) - r \odot^{-i\theta} F_3'(r \odot^{i\theta}) + i F_4(i r \odot^{-i\theta}) + r \odot^{i\theta} F_4'(i r \odot^{-i\theta})$$
$$v = -D_x \chi = -F_1'(r \odot^{i\theta}) - i F_2'(i r \odot^{-i\theta}) - i F_3(r \odot^{i\theta}) - i r \odot^{-i\theta} F_3'(r \odot^{i\theta}) - F_4(i r \odot^{-i\theta}) - i r \odot^{i\theta} F_4'(i r \odot^{-i\theta})$$
$$D_x p = 2 i \mu [-F_3''(r \odot^{i\theta}) + F_4''(i r \odot^{-i\theta})]$$
$$D_y p = 2 i \mu [-F_3''(r \odot^{i\theta}) - F_4''(i r \odot^{-i\theta})]$$
