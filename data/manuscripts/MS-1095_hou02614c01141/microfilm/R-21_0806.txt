For our pendulums, $h_d = 3 h_u$ consequently a change of 3 microns in the position of the knife-edge would only change $T_d^2$ by $\frac{1}{500,000}$ and $T_u^2$ by $\frac{3}{500,000}$. [del]If the change occurred in the edge most remote[/del] The rule adopted in the computations is to give $T_d^2$ a greater weight [del]in the combin[/del] than $T_u^2$ [del]in combination[/del] combining them as periods of two invariable pendulums in the ratio of $h_d$ to $h_u$. It follows that whether the change occurred at one end or the other it would only affect the value of gravity by $\frac{3}{2,000,000}$ of itself. If the pendulum escaped other accidents so that the mean of the results at the initial station were taken, the error would be halved, and would only amount to $\frac{3}{4,000,000}$. Even the larger error is perfectly insignificant compared with the changes frequently occurring with the Kater pendulums, and yet Colonel Herschel does not refuse to call them invariable.
