[u]A Problem relating to the Construction
of a reversible pendulum[/u]

[u]Datum[/u]. A pendulum consists of two
rigid parts, one above the knife-edge, the
other below. These two parts are joined at
the knife-edge and there is a finite resistance
to bending at the joint.

[u]Quaesitum[/u]. How stiff must this joint be
in order that the period of oscillation shall
not be affected by the bending by more
than one two-millionth part?

[u]Solution[/u]. Adopt this notation
$\left. \begin{matrix} \varphi_1 \\ \varphi_2 \end{matrix} \right\}$ angle of displacement of the $\left\{ \begin{matrix} \text{lower} \\ \text{upper} \end{matrix} \right\}$ part from the vertical
$\left. \begin{matrix} I_1 \\ I_2 \end{matrix} \right\}$ moments of inertia
$\gamma_1$ $\gamma_2$ Moments of gravity
[del]$\gamma_1$ $\gamma_2$[/del] $\varepsilon$ Elasticity of the joint

Then the equations are
$I_1 D_t^2 \varphi_1 = -\gamma_1 \varphi_1 - \varepsilon (\varphi_1 - \varphi_2)$
$I_2 D_t^2 \varphi_2 = +\gamma_2 \varphi_2 + \varepsilon (\varphi_1 - \varphi_2)$
