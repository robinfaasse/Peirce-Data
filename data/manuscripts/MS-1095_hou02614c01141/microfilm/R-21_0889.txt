The differential coefficient of $U$ relatively
to $\theta_k$ is

$$D_{\theta_k} U = dl \cdot \varepsilon_k D_l^2 \theta_k + g \cdot dl \int_{m_k}^M dm \cdot \sin \theta_k$$

The Lagrangian equation is therefore

$$\left\{ \int_0^k d\lambda \int_{m_k}^M dm + \int_k^I d\lambda \int_{m_\lambda}^M [del]dm[/del] \left[ \cos(\theta_k - \theta_\lambda) \cdot D_t^2 \theta_\lambda \right] + \left[ \sin(\theta_k - \theta_\lambda) (D_t \theta_\lambda)^2 \right] \right\}$$

$$+ D_l m_k \cdot \gamma^2 \cdot D_t^2 \theta_k + \varepsilon_k D_l^2 \theta_k + g \left( \int_{m_k}^M dm \right) (\sin \theta_k) = 0$$

[del][fig]crossed out integral expression[/fig][/del]

Supposing that the pendulum is pretty stiff
and that the oscillations are small, this reduces
to

$$\left\{ \int_0^k d\lambda \int_{m_k}^M dm + \int_k^I d\lambda \int_{m_\lambda}^M dm \right\} D_t^2 \theta_\lambda + D_l m_k \cdot \gamma^2 \cdot D_t^2 \theta_k + \varepsilon_k D_l^2 \theta_k$$

$$+ g \left( \int_{m_k}^M dm \right) \theta_k = 0.$$

To get rid, at once, of the arbitrary functions,
we will assume

$$\theta_k = A_k \cos \left\{ \sqrt{ \frac{g \int dm \cdot l}{\int dm \cdot l^2} } + i \cdot t \right\}$$

where $A_k$ is a [del]vessel[/del] [ins]constant[/ins] varying with $k$ and
$i$ is a very small constant.
