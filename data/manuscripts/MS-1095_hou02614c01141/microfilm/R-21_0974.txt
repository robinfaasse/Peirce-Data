[u]Two pendulums vibrating parallel act upon each other[/u]

Let $s_1$ denote position of one
$\ \ \ \ \ s_2$ that of the other
$\ \ \ \ \ t$ the time
$\ \ \ \ \ g$ gravity
$\left. \begin{array}{l} \lambda_1 \\ \lambda_2 \end{array} \right\}$ their lengths
$\left. \begin{array}{l} m_1 \\ m_2 \end{array} \right\}$ their masses
$\ \ \ \ \ a$ the tension of their connection

The equations are
$$D_t^2 s_1 = - \frac{g}{\lambda_1} s_1 + a \frac{m_2 \lambda_2}{m_1 \lambda_1} (s_2 - s_1)$$
$$D_t^2 s_2 = - \frac{g}{\lambda_2} s_2 + a \frac{m_1 \lambda_1}{m_2 \lambda_2} (s_1 - s_2)$$

Put these equations in the form
$$D_t^2 s_1 = - ( \frac{g + a \frac{m_2}{m_1} \lambda_2}{\lambda_1} ) s_1 + a \frac{m_2 \lambda_2}{m_1 \lambda_1} s_2$$
$$D_t^2 s_2 = - ( \frac{g + a \frac{m_1}{m_2} \lambda_1}{\lambda_2} ) s_2 + a \frac{m_1 \lambda_1}{m_2 \lambda_2} s_1$$

Here we see that one effect is to increase the apparent force of gravity, while another effect is to introduce a force dependent on the position of the other pendulum. To abbreviate these equations write
$$G_1 = \frac{g + a \frac{m_2}{m_1} \lambda_2}{\lambda_1} \ \ \ \ \ G_2 = \frac{g + a \frac{m_1}{m_2} \lambda_1}{\lambda_2}$$
