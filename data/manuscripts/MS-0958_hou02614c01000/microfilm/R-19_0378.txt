question at issue.

To prove its existence, Dr. Carus adduces
a geometrical proposition, "that two congruent
regular tetrahedrons, when put together, will form
a hexahedron." This is says is absolutely certain,
and thus proves that absolute certainty exists. But
the proposition happens to be erroneous, as stated.
What is true is that if two tetrahedra are so put
together that one face, and one face only, of each
is superposed upon (or in other unique relation
to) a face of the other, and if the faces superposed
(or otherwise related) are not counted, then, since
the two tetrahedra have 8 faces, and 2 are not
counted, there remain 6 faces counted. Dr.
Carus pronounces this "a very wonderful thing."
