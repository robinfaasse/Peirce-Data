[u]Section 6. Mental Causation[/u] (p. 612.) This
section simply makes the justice of my remarks
on Dr. Carus's theory of mental causation quite
evident. For if the action of mind on matter
is similar to that of a magnet in the snout
of a toy fish, it is plain the law of the conserva-
tion of energy is violated. For if such a fish
could repel the hook when he pleased and
attract it when he pleased, he could generate
[ins]and destroy[/ins] mechanical energy. He could leave a position
and come back to it with greater velocity, or less.
Of course, it may be said that it only generalizes
the law of the conservation of energy [del]by making[/del]
correlating mental energy with mechanical.
But this agrees with my comment.

I may here note that Prof. Oliver has pointed
out to me that his theory does not necessarily
violate the law of action and reaction. But it
does make the will a rigid thing. It seems to me
less acceptable, even, than the theory of Dr. Carus.
