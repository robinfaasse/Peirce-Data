Dr. Carus next discusses the principle of
identity; and states, very erroneously, that the
[del]principle of identity[/del] logical formula A = A does
not mean that everything is absolutely identical
with itself. That is just what it does mean. "That,"
says Dr Carus, makes it [ins]a[/ins] "meaningless and useless."
Very well; if he thinks so, the proper and scientific
course is to abandon the formula and adopt
another, not to confuse the subject by adhering
to the formula while changing the meaning.
That is the way to muddle things.

After demonstrating his weakness in
English philosophical nomenclature, Dr.
Carus proceeds to criticise a [del]number of[/del]
my definitions in the Century Dictionary.
Same is there defined as "one in substance," which
is correct. Weakened meanings are "of one
nature or general character, of one kind,
degree, or amount." Perhaps Dr. Carus fancies
"substance" is here used in its chemical sense; if
so, he gathers a totally wrong idea; the word is
used in its philosophical and proper sense.
