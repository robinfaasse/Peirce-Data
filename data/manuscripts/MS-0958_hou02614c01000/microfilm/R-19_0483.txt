In Section III., Dr. Carus calls attention to views
expressed by me in 1877 in an article on the
Fixation of Belief. Of these, Dr. Carus expresses
an approval which is very gratifying to me, except
as to what I there said of the [u]a priori[/u] method. I
acknowledge that that passage requires modifi-
cation.

In Section IV, the principles of the essay just
alluded to are turned against my tychism, but only
in this way, that I there [del]defined the fated opinion,
which I hold to be the truth,[/del] defined reality as
the matter of the fated opinion [ins]in[/ins] which we are
destined ultimately to rest. In this fate, says Dr.
Carus, is implied necessity. Certainly; I fully
assent to that, [del]I am far from denying necessity[/del]
and a very important remark it is. I am far
from denying necessity: I only deny its absoluteness.
