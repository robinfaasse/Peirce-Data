The next paragraph is based on some [del]mis[/del] misunderstanding of the first quotation from my former papers. I can see no argument in it; but the conclusion that "supposing there were four- or five-dimensional space somewhere, we could state with absolute precision all the formal laws by which bodies of so many dimensions were governed," is not tenable. We cannot even tell in our [del]s[/del] space whether the sum of the three angles of a triangle is greater or less than two right angles, even if I grant we may be sure it is not [ins]exactly[/ins] equal thereto.

The section ends with the assertion that the rigidity of formal laws is universal. This is mere assertion. It is not shown that there are any "forms" in the Kantian sense, nor that, if there are, they have any laws, properly speaking, nor that if they have, these laws are exactly [del]conforms[/del] obeyed.
