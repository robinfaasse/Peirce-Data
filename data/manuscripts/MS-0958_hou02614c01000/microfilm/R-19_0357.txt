§ 11. By "sameness," Dr. Carus means nothing
but agreement, or likeness. By the "idea of sameness," (§ 77)
he means what he means by the "law of identity," which is,
(§ 96) that things which have a common character
are alike. This he says (§ 77) "has a solid basis
in the facts of experience." I think it is mere
tautology, as useless as the genuine principle of
identity. That by "sameness" he means merely likeness
is shown by his definition (§ 102): "That feature in
two things or states of things, which brings it to pass
that the one may be replaced by the other without
altering for a certain purpose the state of things."
"Sameness," he says, (§ 106), "depends upon a special
purpose." He probably means it depends upon there
being a purpose, and things alike for one purpose,
are widely different for another.
