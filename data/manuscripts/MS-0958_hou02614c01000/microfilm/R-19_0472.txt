But is not this [ins]t[/ins] somewhat factitious? Creations
and miracles afford no difficulty to primitive
man; it is only after he has been impressed with
the regularity of the universe that this difficulty
begins. Is it not rather gratuitous from him, then
to turn round upon his formula of regularity
to which alone any difficulty he may have with
admitting miracles is due, and complain of
it as being miraculous? [del]But[/del] Unless regularity
is [u]ipso facto[/u] miraculous, I should think that if
miraculousness were found to infest the formula
adopted, that formula had better be rejected and
another one substituted. This, I suppose, is what
Dr. Carus intends. He gives up the old notion
of causality and substitutes the dynamical
law. That is, I grant, in the realm of physics,
a great improvement; but it has no bearing
on the Hume-Kant problem of causation.
