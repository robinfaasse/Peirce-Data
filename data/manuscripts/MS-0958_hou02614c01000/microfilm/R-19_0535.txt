But furthermore, if we are to look at the axioms
of geometry in this light, we ought for consistency to
extend the same views to the philosophical axioms,
and in particular that we ought not to suppose
the principle of cause and effect to be rigidly
accurate.

Laws that are not rigidly exact will hardly
be believed to be primordial; they will rather be
thought to be results of some process of evolution.
The laws of [del]matter[/del] mechanics have no principle
of growth within themselves, But it is different
with the great law of mind, the tendency to take
habits. It is [del]not[/del] essentially not rigid, but it
[del]likewise[/del] grows by its own virtue, being itself
a habit, and consequently all the time strengthening
itself. It may consequently have arisen from the [del]a[/del]
first mere fortuitous germ.
