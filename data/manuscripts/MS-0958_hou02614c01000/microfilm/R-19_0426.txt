Dr. Carus next quotes some passages which
I wrote 16 years ago. All that is there said
I still adhere to; but I did not then see the
truth of tychism. It was reflecting upon the
things there said, which brought me to it shortly
after; and I took twelve years to think it all
out and consider the pros and cons, before
I ventured to propound my views publicly.

The first of these passages is an argument
against what is called inverse probabilities.
The others are directed against the idea of a
world entirely devoid of any regularity. These
passages have not the slightest bearing, [del]that[/del]
[ins]I can see, upon[/ins] Dr. Carus's proposition that
"the calculus of probabilities is not applicable
to the order of the world as to whether it may or
may not be universal;" or if they have any bearing
it can only be directly against that proposition.
