In accepting the social theory of reality, Dr.
Carus has admitted a thought which is destined to
destroy his opinion that, concerning things other than
the Absolute, absolute synthetical propositions can
be true. For that theory will admit nothing as true
which is not determined by external reality, in
contradistinction to inward tendencies, or laws, of
thought. In other words, it admits no synthetical
proposition further than induction and hypothetic
inference will bear it out. Now these processes will
not legitimate absolute universality, exactitude, or
necessity.

In section V., Dr. Carus very properly calls to
mind that I am bound to explain, or at least
to make a beginning of explaining, in some detail
on my principles, the laws of nature, including
the properties of space and time. That obligation
I fully recognize and [del]seem[/del] [ins]am[/ins] prepared to fulfil,
when I get time to do so.
