Dr. Carus admits that "ideas cannot be
explained by mechanical laws," and further that
it is false that "the laws of mechanics determine
everything that happens." [del]But[/del] It is certain
that the laws of mechanics suffice to completely
determine all physical motions; so that, if
motion is determined by anything except
mechanical conditions, unless indeed by
the initial conditions which have been
fully determinative for all time, then the
laws of mechanics are violated. No atom
can swerve from its path [ins]without[/ins] such a
violation. Now will Dr. Carus say that mind
has no real influence on matter, or will he
say the laws of mechanics are violated?
There is no other alternative. If the former,
the ordinary phenomena of the action of
volition suffice to show that the laws of
mechanics do determine everything that
happens. If the latter, it would be better
logic to abandon necessitarianism.
