When the logic of relatives has been studied, this
rather inconsequent drama will never get acted
any more, simply because the mares' nest of
nominalism will be avoided. Causation
affords a difficulty only to the nominalist; and
for him there are but two solutions; he must
become either a realist or an idealist. If the
unsatisfactory idea of causation had never been
applied to physical phenomena, but modern
dynamics had [del]existed[/del] [ins]been implanted[/ins] as an
instinct in primitive man, the nominalist would
have met with the same difficulty, namely, that it
is a law. Not to speak of sides of this difficulty,
to which Kant draws no attention, it would be
said, This law is universal and necessary, and
consequently, no induction can prove it to be true.
That is the only difficulty which troubles Kant
with causation. It would be, besides, an incom-
prehensible thing how mere individual things
could carry a real law.
