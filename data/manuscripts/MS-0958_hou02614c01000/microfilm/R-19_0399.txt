To answer this question, no real experience is ne-
cessary: it is only necessary to attend to our
own diagrams. We can prove that 2 x 2 = 4 by
demonstrative reasoning, thus:
[u]2[/u] is, by definition, 1+1. Hence 2 x 2 = 2 x (1+1).
But multiplication is, by definition, distributive.
Hence, 2 x (1+1) = (2 x 1) + (2 x 1). But by the defini-
tion of multiplication, the product of any number by
1 is that number. Hence (2 x 1) + (2 x 1) = 2 + 2.
By the definition of 2 we have 2 + 2 = 2 + (1+1).
But addition is, by definition, associative. Hence,
2 + (1+1) = (2+1) + 1. By definition, 3 is 2 + 1.
Hence, (2+1) + 1 = 3 + 1. By definition, 4 is 3 + 1.
Hence, 2 x 2 = 4. The only uncertainty here arises
from the possibility of slips of logic. That is, we
have to observe the formulae, and in all observation
there is liability to error. Accordingly, it is not
absolutely certain that 2 x 2 = 4. But, neglecting
