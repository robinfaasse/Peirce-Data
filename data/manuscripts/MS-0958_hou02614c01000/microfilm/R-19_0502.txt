I prefer not to express now, without argument,
an opinion which standing by itself might
seem ungracious.

A new argument against the evolution of law
is here adduced. It is that natural laws cease to be
such when conceived as products of evolution. There
is a charming facility about this argument. "Eter-
nity is the characteristic feature of natural law, --
its conditio sine qua non." I believe that this
argument is really [ins]the[/ins] one which influences
Dr Carus and other necessitarians. I need not
qualify its logic.

to explain natural laws, as if they were [del]simple[/del] facts as
if they were [u]concrete[/u] facts. [del]This is the[/del] Here Dr. Carus
falls into the nominalistic Slough of Despond. What
is a [u]simple[/u] fact?

Dr. Carus says I regard the everlasting as if it
were the transient; but this is inaccurate; I do
not regard the everlasting as if it were transient;
but what Dr. Carus fancies to be everlasting I
think to be transient.
