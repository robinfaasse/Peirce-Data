But I endeavor to penetrate the haze which envelops my critic's meaning, at another point. He remarks that two congruent tetrahedra put together make a hexahedron. This, he says, might to a confused mind seem wonderful; since two congruent rectangular parallelopipedons (I put this example into his mouth) put together make nothing but a new rectangular parallelopipedon. Nevertheless, the law of form makes us [u]absolutely[/u] sure that two congruent regular tetrahedra will invariably and unconditionally make a hexahedron.

I wish Dr. Carus had plainly stated what he conceived to be the bearing of this remark upon that unstated question which he is discussing.
