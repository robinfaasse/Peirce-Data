Dr. Carus now proposes to ascertain the
meaning of the word same; and first inquires
into its derivation. But the derivation has
so little to do with the matter, that it would far
better be passed by. He utterly overlooks the only
proper mode of settling the question, namely,
by an examination of good usage. He accepts
the [del]idea[/del] definition given in the Century Dic-
tionary [del]are secondary, as[/del] [ins]expressing[/ins] the proper meaning.

Why there should be no need of discussing
the "truism", as he calls it, the "manifest falsity",
as I call it, that there is no absolute sameness,
I cannot see. According to the nominalistic
theory, there is now only no absolute sameness,
but there is not even any real agreement or
likeness between individuals. Such likeness
only consists in the individuals being called by
one name, or, in some systems, exciting one
idea. But on the realistic theory, identity,
though a relation of reason, is entirely real.
To deny that, would on that theory be equivalent
to saying that a thing is not identical with
itself. Thus, on either theory, absolute sameness
has the same degree of reality as resemblance has.
