As to the importance of transformation as
an element of mathematical certitude, I
grant this, that mathematical reasoning consists
in making a [ins]mental[/ins] diagram according to a certain
rule, and in mentally manipulating that
diagram in various ways, especially by
adding to it. In that way, we are led to remark
relations between the parts of the diagram
over and above those which sufficed [del]for[/del] to
determine it in the construction. While a
positive [ins]act of[/ins] observation is requisite, yet the univer-
sality is insured by definitions of the construction
[del]what[/del] and of the results, which are compared by
another subsidiary diagram in the same
manner. In one sense, this is transformation,
since the theorem deals with the same diagram
as the original hypotheses; but in a more
determinate sense, I do not think that every
mathematical proposition is a case of
transformation.
