Dr. Carus quotes four passages from a paper
I wrote 16 years ago to show that I then
thought the doctrine of chances was inapplica-
ble to the question whether the order of the world
may or may not be universal. [del]In the first[/del]
These passages do not exhibit the slightest
discrepancy between my position then and
my position now. It is true that there is
a difference between the position I then held
and my present position. Namely, my
present ideas were then new to me, and I
did not venture to express them; I left that
question untouched. But I do not now say
that the doctrine of chances is applicable
to the question stated, if I understand the
meaning of that question; for I do not apply
the doctrine of chances to decide what
"may or may not be," logically, but to
decide what probably is or is not the case.
