§ 16. I will now pass from the consideration of Dr. Carus's system to that of his strictures upon mine. In doing so, I will repeat my opinion that his philosophy is one enlightened by modern ideas, which it synthetizes to an unusual extent. The one thing it wants to perfect it, is the lopping the heads off of all the absolute propositions whose subject is not the Absolute.[ins]*[/ins] The great fault of the system is the incongruity of different parts of it. In this respect, it is open to severe criticism, as I intend to show more fully, in an examination of Fundamental Problems. But if the absoluteness which ill accords with its general character were taken out of it, this fault would disappear. With that modification, bringing it into far better harmony with itself, Dr. Carus's philosophy would present no general or violent contradiction to my opinions.
