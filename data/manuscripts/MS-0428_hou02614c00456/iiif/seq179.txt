91.

The popular notion is that Reason is far superior to any instinctive way of reaching the truth; and from your desire to study logic, I am perhaps warranted in presuming that such is your opinion. If so, in what respect do you hold reasoning to be superior to instinct? Birds and bees decide rightly hundreds of times for every time [del]that[/del] they err. That would suffice to explain their imperfect self-consciousness; for if error be not pressed upon the attention of a being, there remains little to mark the distinction between the outer and the inner worlds. A bee or an ant cannot, [del]– could not, though he were able to indulge in the pastime of introspection, –[/del] ever guess that he acted from instinct. Accused of it, he would say, "Not at all! I am guided entirely by reason." So he is, in fact; [ins]in the sense that whatever he does is determined by reasoning.[/ins] He uses reason to adapt means to ends[ins], [u]– that is to his inclinations, –[/u][/ins] just as we do; [del]The[/del] the point at which instinct intervenes is precisely in giving him inclinations which to us seem so singular.
