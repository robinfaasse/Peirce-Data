There are certain parts of your logica utens which nobody
really doubts. Hegel and his have loyally endeavored
to cast a doubt upon it. The effort has been praiseworthy;
but it has not succeeded. The truth of it is too evident.
Mathematical reasoning holds. Why should it not? It relates
only to the creations of the mind, concerning which there
is no obstacle to our learning whatever is true of them.
The method of this book, therefore, is to accept the reason-
