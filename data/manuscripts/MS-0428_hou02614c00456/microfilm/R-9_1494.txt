[ins]Should[/ins] a confused perception that a man who [del]was to think[/del] [ins]and reason[/ins] in certain ways would act at cross-purposes to himself, this confused perception in its lower grades partaking somewhat of the nature of an instinct. I am inclined to think that any confused perception which is indispensable in life will in its lower grades partake of the nature of an instinct. At any rate, it seems to be true of this one. My reason for thinking that it is not an independent instinct is that, if it were so, then a man who had arrived by analysis and criticism at the conviction that certain modes of reasoning were good and certain others bad would still retain his instinctive sense of logicality, even if it were weakened in the course of [del]that[/del] education, and [del]would[/del] thus become conscious of two independent ways of judging of reasonings, the one critical, the other instinctive; and it would be very strange if these two were not, in some cases, more or less in discord.
