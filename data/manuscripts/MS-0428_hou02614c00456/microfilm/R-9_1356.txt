[del]15[/del] [ins]16[/ins][/pn] compels you to admit that there is knowledge, so that that branch of this second question needs no further [del]and[/del] attention. But it will be well to notice roughly in what sense this argument compels you to admit the existence of knowledge.

The knowledge which you are compelled to admit is that knowledge which is directly forced upon you, and which there is no criticizing, because it is directly forced upon you. For example, here I sit at my table with my inkstand and paper before me, my pen in my hand, my lamp at my side. It may be that all this is a dream. But if so, that dream there is, is knowledge. But hold: what I have written down is only an imperfect description of the percept [ins]that is[/ins] forced upon me. I have endeavored to state it in words. In this there has been endeavor, purpose, — something not forced upon me but rather the product of reflection. I was not forced to this reflection. I could not hope to describe what I see, feel, and hear, just as I see, feel, and hear [del]that[/del] it.
