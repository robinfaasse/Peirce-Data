in many cases, trust to the veracity of the natural in-

nality consists in that judgment. I have already
completely refuted that argument; but there is one
point to be made against it which I have reserved
for this place, because it so well illustrates the modes
of thinking of the German Logicians. Suppose this case.
In the course of a long country ramble, I meet a boy whom
I never saw before, and inquire my way of him. I
take the turn which he directs me to take, [del]but[/del] [ins]and[/ins] have not
gone many steps further before I am overtaken by a
man who informs me that that boy's mother has been
arrested upon a charge of perjury and that I am
wanted to testify to her veracity. "But," I say, "I never
so much as heard of the woman. I did not know she existed."
