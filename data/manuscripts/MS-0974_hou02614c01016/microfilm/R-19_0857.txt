[u]Laches[/u]. What is courage?
To show that knowledge alone is valuable, by showing that even courage is knowledge.
Rather assumed than shown, Plato having little appreciation of the subconscious. He is a thorough-paced rationalist.

The first part of the dialogue shows the advantages of knowledge; the second part, our lack of it in regard to the most important and interesting questions.

[u]Euthydemus[/u] The first of a series of dialogues [ins]This, Protag. Gorgias[/ins] against the sophists and rhetoricians and their conception of cognition.
Euthydemus and his brother Dionysodorus are teachers of rhetoric, or eristic, and of virtue. They are practitioners of the most abject sophisms, and maintain there is no such thing as ignorance or error.

[u]Protagoras[/u]. A highly dramatic dialogue, much beyond any that precede it. Protagoras is the great sophist. He gives a myth of Prometheus & Epimetheus, Socrates makes a rather long speech about a poem of Simonides.
To show that virtue consists in knowledge. But more to show the relative emptiness of rhetorical teaching.

[u]Republic[/u], Book I. Considers several points connected with justice. 1st, that it is a satisfaction to an old man to look back on a just life. 2nd, that justice cannot produce injustice, so that it is not just to return evil for evil. 3rd, the just is the skilled, the unjust the unskilled; 4th, the just are happier than the unjust. 5th, Might is not right.
