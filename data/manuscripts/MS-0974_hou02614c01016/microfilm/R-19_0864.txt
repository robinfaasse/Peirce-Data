[u]Protagoras[/u], continued. The argumentative part of this dialogue
is hardly the principal part. Such as it is, it is as follows:

[u]1st part[/u] _ Before the speech and interruption _
Socrates sets out to show that justice, temperance [ins]σωφροσύνη[/ins], and holiness
are not [del]heterogenous[/del] parts of virtue. For if so courage and
wisdom are also parts of virtue. Now folly is the contrary
of wisdom and also [del]but in another sense[/del] of σωφροσύνη. Hence
since nothing has two contraries. Wisdom and σωφροσύνη are
the same. Besides, an unjust man cannot have σωφροσύνη.
Protagoras says he can. Socrates says σωφροσύνη is good sense.
Now the good is that which is expedient for man. And injustice is not
good. Hence the unjust has not good sense and cannot have
σωφροσύνη. Protagoras replies that the good maybe inexpedient.

[u]2nd part[/u] _ Protagoras admits that righteousness, holiness,
σωφροσύνη and wisdom are somewhat similar; but holds
courage to be very different from them. Socrates urges the same argument
