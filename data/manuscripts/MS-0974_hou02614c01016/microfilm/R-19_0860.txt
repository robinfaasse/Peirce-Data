[u]Symposium[/u]. A most wonderful work of art; but it
is difficult to fathom all its meaning.

[del]Discor[/del] [ins]Speeches[/ins] on Love. 1[ins]st[/ins] By Phaedrus. Son eldest of the gods. Incentive
to great virtue. Instances Alcestis and Achilles.

2[ins]nd[/ins] By Pausanias. Two Loves, the heavenly and the common. Discusses
paederasty rather favorably.

3[ins]rd[/ins] By Eryximachus, an Asclepiad. Reconciliation of opposites.

4[ins]th[/ins] By Aristophanes, the Comic poet. Love is desire for reunion with
the other part of oneself.

5[ins]th[/ins] By Agathon, the Tragic poet. Love is flexible, fair, just, temperate,
courageous, wise, a poet and maker of poets, an artist and creator
of order, a peacemaker, a saviour, etc.

6[ins]th[/ins] By Socrates. Love is continually receiving and continually
losing. He is the daemon who mediates between men and God. Beauty per se.
Power of procreation in beauty.

Alcibiades comes in and tells of the self restraint of Socrates.
His almost superhuman power over his body. It is plain that
Plato looks upon it as far beyond ordinary powers.
