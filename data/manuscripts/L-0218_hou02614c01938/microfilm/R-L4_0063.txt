My dear Professor Peirce:-

I am astonished on reading and closely examining your paper to find that there are 8,500 words in it. I thought I could divide it into two parts at the end of your remarks about Taine at the bottome of the sixteenth sheet. I find that [del]that[/del] [ins]I should[/ins] [del]would[/del] still [del]give[/del] [ins]have[/ins] [del]me[/del] two papers longer than I can possibly publish. I think you will remember that when you first spoke to me I suggested that the paper should be not over 3,000 words in length. Really I do not know what to do with three times that length. I cannot think of publishing it in three parts, [del]and[/del] [ins]In[/ins] the present condition of our columns two is all I can possibly undertake. It is [ins]very[/ins] excellent [ins]and[/ins] and is so condensed now that I should be at a loss to know where to strike out anything, or even a word. It is plainly impossible, however, for me to undertake to publish over 6,000 words in all, and I shall have to throw myself on your considerate regard to say what is to be done. [ins]I have mailed you the two latest copies of the paper[/ins]
