[u]On Real Curves.[/u]
By C. S. Peirce

The object of this paper is to investigate the real parts of curves, without any reference to their imaginary parts; for everything that is usually stated about higher curves is, for every purpose of hydrodynamics and other branches, not only useless but false.

I shall at the out set limit myself to really unicursal curves, as containing all the general forms. I shall also begin by limiting myself to curves without real cusps and inflections; for if a curve be conceived as generated by the motion of a line and a point, the line always turning round the point in one direction and the point always advancing along the line in one direction, at a cusp the line will make an entire half-rotation, and at an inflection the point must make the [del]in[/del] [ins]path[/ins] through infinity to enable the line to turn the right direction.
