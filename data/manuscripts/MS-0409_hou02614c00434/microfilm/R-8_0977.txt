The prepositions [u]kua[/u], [u]on[/u], [u]in[/u] (this last a locative), and [u]inter[/u] come
from the same root.

[fig]hieroglyph of a bearded head[/fig] (Hieroglyph: a bearded head, which is the ideograph of
primacy) [del]over, upon[/del] [ins]meaning[/ins] [del]onto[/del] upon, has the idea of mastery.
This preposition is equivalent to [u]ἐπί[/u], having the same root
as [u]optimus[/u], [del]and[/del] [u]opulent[/u], [u]opus[/u], the idea of which must
also be mastery.

[u]Hr[/u] (Hieroglyph: a face) meaning above, upon, is the
same word as the noun meaning face.

[u]Xr[/u], with the same spelling as [u]Xr[/u], fight, means
under and with. Our [del]with[/del] [ins]English word[/ins] means etymologically against.

[u]Gr[/u], meaning with, is the same as the word meaning
taking.

[u]Ha[/u], behind, is the same as the [del]word meaning[/del] [ins]name of[/ins] denoting the back of
the head.

[u]Nesw[/u], meaning "meaning", or "belonging to", or "concerning", is
the same as the word for tongue. [ins]The hieroglyph shows they were regarded as identical.[/ins]

[u]Yr[/u], meaning "since", is the same as the word for prison,
end, stop.

[fig]hieroglyph of an arm[/fig] [u]Armen[/u], meaning until, is the same as the [del]word[/del] [ins]name[/ins] for a
man's arm. Note the curious resemblance to our word.

[u]Xeft[/u], meaning opposite, is the same as the name for 'face'.
