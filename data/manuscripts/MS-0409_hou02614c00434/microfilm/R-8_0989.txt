§68. Meantime, the student should be warned that mere definitions of words must not be understood to inculcate any doctrine. To conclude from the above definitions that there is nothing analogous to possibility and necessity in the real world, but that these modes appertain only to the particular limited information which we possess would be even less defensible than to draw precisely the opposite conclusion from the same premises. It is a style of reasoning most absurd. Unfortunately, it is so common, that the moment a writer sets down these definitions nine out of ten critics will set him down as a [del]the[/del] nominalist. The question of realism and nominalism, which means the question how far [del]the[/del] real facts are analogous to logical relations, and why, is a very serious one, which has to be carefully and deliberately studied, and not decided off hand, and not decided on the ground that one or another answer to it is "inconceivable." Nothing is "inconceivable" to a [del]man[/del] [ins]the[/ins] who sets seriously about conceiving of it.
