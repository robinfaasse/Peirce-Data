Of course, it is not rational for a man to assume that
he is utterly irrational. A man cannot be speaking
the truth in saying that everything he says is false. For this
very thing is one of the things he says; and if this be [del]true[/del] [ins]false[/ins],
then in what it says of itself it is true, and therefore false.
But this remark does not clear up the matter; and we
shall leave the problem for the present, to return to it later.

As to the second question, it is important to remark
that the [u]nota notae[/u] does not declare that there is any
infallible mark of anything, or any rule without excep-
tions. If, as we have seen, the [u]nota notae[/u] itself is not
absolutely certain, nothing else ought to be so regarded.
We cannot go so far as to declare that absolutely no
rule is without exceptions; for this declaration is itself
a rule. Nor can we say that no rule but this is without
exceptions. For this rule either has exceptions or it
has not. [del]As[/del] If it has exceptions and every other rule
has exceptions, it has no exceptions. But if it has
no exceptions, then in accordance with its declaration
it has exceptions. We are, thus, obliged to admit that
there are rules without exceptions, or at least that the
denial of it has no sense.* But we ought not to suppose
that we can identify any [ins]special[/ins] proposition as being certain.
