[u]The Axioms of Geometry.[/u]

Attempt at enumerating them, by C. S. Peirce.

[u]Axiom 1.[/u] All points of space have the same properties. Thus, there [del]is[/del] [ins]are[/ins] no central point, no point on [del]the edge[/del] [ins]a limit[/ins] of space etc.

[u]Axiom 2.[/u] All pairs of points in space have the same properties. Thus, two points lying in one direction have the same properties as two points lying in another direction. So [del]two[/del] [ins]a pair of[/ins] points at once distance have the [del]same[/del] [ins]general[/ins] properties as a pair of points at another distance. So that space is infinitely divisible. It also follows that the sum of the angles of a triangle are equal to two right angles, and that space is infinite and not hyper-infinite.

[u]Axiom 3.[/u] Space has three dimensions. That is to say, three numbers are requisite to define the position of a point.

[u]Axiom 4.[/u] Space permits [del]a[/del] rigid body [del]to[/del] motion of translation. That is to say all the points A', B', C', D', E', etc of any body retaining all their relations to each other A' can be brought to coincide with any other point A whereupon B' will become B, C', C etc. If we take B' for A the points A' B' B will be equidistant in a straight line. [ins]and that by a continuous change such that all the motions [ins]of all the points[/ins] will be equal.[/ins]
