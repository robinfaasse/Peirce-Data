Specimen of a Study of
German Prepositions

[u]ab.[/u] Etymologically, our 'of'. √APO
Sanskrit & Zend apa
Gr. [greek]ἀπό[/greek] Lat ab
Gothic af- E of, off, after, eft
Original meaning [u]off[/u].
German obsolete. In [u]Strom ab[/u]
down stream, it is an adverb
Strom being accusative. But it
is here an incipient preposition.
As separable prefix common, gene-
rally meaning 'down' or 'off'.

[u]abseits.[/u] Prep. with Genetive.
Ein Viertelmeile abseits der
Heerstrasse, = a quarter of a
German mile [u]off the[/u] main road

[u]abseits von.[/u] Prep with Dative
= abseits

[u]abwärts.[/u] Prep. with Genetive; but
rarely. Usually adverb.
Means: [u]off from[/u], [u]downward from[/u].
"abwärts des Einganges" (Mussäus)
= 'off from the entrance'
"abwärts der Brust" (Streckfuss)
= 'downwards from the breast'
[u]aftar.[/u] Our after. Old Persian apatara
Gr. [greek]ἀπωτέρω[/greek]. Dat & Acc. Obsolete.

[u]an.[/u] Etymologically our 'on' √ANA
Sanskrit a weak form of ana Zend ana
Greek [greek]ἀνά[/greek], [ins]very often translatable by
German an[/ins]
Gothic: ana English, on
Original meaning [u]on[/u]
Its general signification is
German is [u]attachment[/u]
It takes Dative & Accusative
The latter when expressing [del]tendency[/del] [ins]towards[/ins] [u]onto[/u]
Greek [greek]ἀνά[/greek] usually takes Accusative,
(hence it is mistakenly said to mean [u]up[/u])
often Dative, Not often Genetive.

With reference to Place it
often expresses proximity [ins]of
things that have (or are regarded as having)
no other connexion of nature.[/ins]
