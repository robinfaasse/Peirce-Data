[u]Abduction[/u]. This word is omitted. On some accounts, it would seem to stand particularly in need of explanation. There seems to be no room for its insertion. Yet it will be well to indicate what I would say in such cases, for the reason that such statements may be valuable in reference to the question of whether it will be best to affix Addenda to the work.

[u]Abduction[/u]. The word commonly employed to translate Aristotle's [greek]ἀπαγωγή[/greek] in [u]II. Anal. pr.[/u] cap. xxv. Unfortunately, the meaning of this chapter, and with it the signification of that ought to be attached to the word [u]abduction[/u], is questionable. It is one of those curiously numerous passages where the reader of Aristotle seems to himself to be anticipating a profound or brilliant conception which the Stagyrite seems to have begun to express, until, all at once, the thought takes a turn and what is expressed seems too obvious and trivial. Why should such disappointments be so much more frequent in Aristotle's writings
