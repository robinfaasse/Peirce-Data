[u]All[/u] positions of [u]Jupiter[/u] are on an [u]ellipse[/u]
These positions are positions of Jupiter
These positions are on an ellipse

And we may also make the following inference à posteriori:
Whatever moves over this ellipse passes through these points
[u]Jupiter moves over this ellipse[/u]
Jupiter passes through these points.

These inferences seem nearly the same; but there is an essential difference between them, in that the inductive inference [u]restricts[/u] Jupiter to the ellipse without opposing a further restriction, while the inference à posteriori [u]extends[/u] his motion over the ellipse without opposing a further extension.

No inference can be drawn from the same premises in two different ways, unless its [del]division[/del] into two terms is the same in both cases. For otherwise its terms could not be combined with middle terms so as to give [del]the[/del] equivalent propositions in the two cases unless one of those propositions were like this "[del]all[/del] [ins]rational mortals are rational[/ins] [del]swift horses are swift[/del]." Now such propositions as this are in fact no propositions at all.
