The reason was that the voluminous Galen whom every medical man studied was an original logician of a good deal of power with strong Stoical affiliations. Now the Stoics were gross materialists of the most philistine type. In fact the popularity of Stoicism was due to its philistinism, — its commonplace ideas of conventional decency unbacked by any soul at all. Out of this school came Raymond Lully, for example. These medical logicians naturally had a liking for modern science; but the more they became scientific the less their nominalism was accentuated. They could not lead natural science, if for no other reason, because medicine was far too concrete a science to be ripe for any scientific method, a revolution of the last few years of our time. Still Gilbert was a doctor, [ins]and so was Harvey.[/ins]
