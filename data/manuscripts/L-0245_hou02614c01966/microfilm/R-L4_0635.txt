[pn]I.[/pn]

Exact philosophy is a branch of science which has hitherto not been successfully prosecuted. For, however true many philosophemes may be, they are not universally acknowledged to be so by all competent minds; and that is as much as to say that they have not been satisfactorily established. I hold the reason of this to be, that metaphysics must rest upon logic in a sense in which no other science does, Now logic is not yet completely developed; and before me there was but a fragment of it that [del]had[/del] been thoroughly studied. I have not completed the reformation of it; but I have done enough to throw a great light upon the matter and thus upon metaphysics.

Exact logic is logic so treated as to leave no room for dispute upon any of its enunciations. As I have alluded to myself as having greatly furthered it, it is proper that I should say, at the beginning, who are the author's of it. Whether or not there was any exact logic before Aristotle we do not know. There is not the slightest reason to suspect the smallest step had been taken by any non-Greek. After him, there are vestiges of work done by some ancient logicians, but it is mostly lost. I see no evidence of anything exact [del]contai[/del] having been contributed to logic by any Arabian writer. As for Prantl's "Byzantine logic," I do not believe there was any such school; for I hold the work of Psellus to be a mere translation [marginalia]from?[/marginalia] [u]Summulae[/u] of Petrus Hispanus. Logic was very deeply studied in the western world, especially at Paris, during the middle ages.
