This work is divided in the sharpest manner, — as sharply as chemistry
before and after Lavoisier, — into two periods, of which the one coincides
with the period of round arched cathedral architecture and the,
other with that of Gothic architecture. In the former period fell
the great controversy between the Realists and Nominalists. This
was before the organization of the University. In its earlier portion
hardly any of the works of Aristotle were known, but in its last portion
all the logical treatises of Aristotle were at hand. During this furious
contest the power of logical thought was wonderfully stimulated;
and at last the truth about universals came out pretty clearly,
and the differences became little more than differences of phrase-
ology. Then, certain other works of Aristotle, [del]in fact[/del] [ins]indeed[/ins] all the non-
logical [del]parts[/del] works, were brought to light both in their Greek original
and in [del]two [or more][/del] Arabic versions. [del][Those who look minutely into the
matter enumerate [del]many[/del] [ins]several[/ins] Arabic versions of some of the works; but I think
there were only two, each having various revisions.][/del] Above all the Meta-
physics of Aristotle, — a philosopher already almost deified, — plainly con-
cerned men's souls. Now, a man's soul was a serious concern A.D.
1200. The University of Paris was organized mainly with a view to
the study of theology in the light of this newly discovered work. The
controversy ceased because students were too much occupied in reading
the new books. During these years of quiet between the two acts of the
