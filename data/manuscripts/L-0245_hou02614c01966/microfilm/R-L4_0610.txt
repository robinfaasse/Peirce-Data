Milford Pa [date]1898 July 24[/date]

My dear Mr. Lathrop:

I have at length found your copy of the rules for existential graphs, which I enclose to you. On careful examination in the light of studies I have made since last summer, I think the Basic Rules are open to some criticism for reasons which I have written on the MS in pencil. Rule III attempts to justify the transformation of [ins]sins[/ins][del]sings[/del] into [del]sins[/del][ins]sins[/ins] as being a transformation by erasure which could be performed if the part erased stood alone. But the truth is that it makes a great difference whether the part is connected or disconnected. On the whole, I think a separate rule must permit the cutting of a bond. Moreover, another rule (not often wanted) seems called for to express the principle that more than one individual exists, without which [ins]basic[/ins] logic would have no raison d'être. I rearrange the rules and make some minor modifications, as follows:

Rule I. The whole graph may be erased.

Rule II. Graphs separately scriptible are scriptible together.

[del]Rule III.[/del] Any graph forming a separate unenclosed part of another is transformable as if it stood alone.

Rule IV. Any heavy line may be broken.

Rule V. Any index of an existing individual may be written unenclosed with loose ends.

Rule VI. Indices joined by an uncrossed heavy line are mutually transformable.

Rule VII. Mutually transformable indices may be joined by a heavy line.

Rule VIII. Every heavy line might be joined to an index did we know what index to choose.

Rule IX. If graph A is transformable into B, then B is transformable into A.

Rule X. If A is transformable into B, then B is transformable into A.

Rule XI. We can write [fig]graph with circle and line[/fig].
