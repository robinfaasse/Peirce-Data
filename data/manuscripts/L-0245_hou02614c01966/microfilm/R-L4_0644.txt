London [date]22nd October 1901[/date]-

My Dear Professor Peirce,

I have received your letter of the 8th inst. together with the first chapter of the Logic, which last I have only had time to look over hastily, but am promising myself much pleasure from a more careful reading. I am sure that it cannot fail to deeply interest anyone of intelligence who reads it, — not only by its presentation of the subject in hand but also by the light thrown in other directions — There are portions, — especially in this "prospectus" part, that I find it difficult to follow, but I am not sure that I should agree with you as to striking that part out — It seems to me that it might be used as a preface, — leaving the rest to serve as the introductory chapter.

I can see that in this you have had in mind your first plan of making the work appeal to the laity; — and as that I advised you not holding to in the succeeding chapters — For the first one it is perhaps just as well to have done this, since I am showing it to some friends here.

I have strong hopes of getting money enough promised to carry the work forward during the next few months, on the basis of the suggestions made in your letter of Aug. 15th in which you spoke of giving an unlimited copyright

[marginalia]
I think that I can send you later other volumes in the German Edition. — F.L.
[/marginalia]

[marginalia]
P.S. I am sending you by post Zeller's History of Aristotle and the Early Peripatetics and Calderwood's Handbook of Moral Philosophy, and Wundt's Lectures on "Human and Animal Psychology" — F.L.
