Let me here say a word about the pendulum clock. Galileo made no such instrument; but it seems to be pretty well established that his son, with some aid no doubt from his father, did so. But that son labored under the severe prejudice which attaches to a great man's son: Worse than that, he was also a poor man's son: Worse still, he was nothing but the legitimated bastard of a poor gentleman.

[del]At the same time[/del] [ins]On the other hand[/ins], Christiaan Huygens [ins]van Zulichem[/ins], one of the most brilliant geniuses that the history of science can show, the son of [del]a wealthy[/del] [ins]an aristocratic[/ins] of Leiden, the pet and pride of the scientific world, and an ornament of courts, wrote a great work on the theory of the pendulum, which established great principles of dynamics. He, too, quite independently, but a little later, invented the pendulum clock, and from his invention all [del]modern[/del] [ins]our pendulum[/ins] clocks are derived.
