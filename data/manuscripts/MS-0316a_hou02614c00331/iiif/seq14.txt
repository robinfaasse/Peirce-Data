[del]To such as have any true sensibility of heart it
is impossible to read the pathetic description
which the historians of the day give of the sorrows of
this unfortunate people without the deepest emotions of
sympathy. Many of them were far advanced in age
and could look back upon long years of usefulness and
happiness passed in the [ins]homes of their[/ins] childhood which were then for-
ever lost. Some were separated from those whom they
had held most dear, and were driven away into a
solitude which to their feeling hearts was worse than
death. No objects met their view but those which tended
to deepen the gloom that was gathering around them
all was dark around them - and all was cheerless before
them. No hope of a more auspicious hour arose in their
breasts, for they knew that when once they left their
native land, they were to look upon it no more forever.
They could only look forward to a long [del]uninterrupted[/del]
course of sorrow which was to end only with their
lives.[/del]
