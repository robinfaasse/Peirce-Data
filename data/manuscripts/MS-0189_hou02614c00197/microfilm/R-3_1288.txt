[u]Average[/u]

"Benjie, my boy," said Lydia, "I am going to explain to you what is meant by an average, or arithmetical mean. A common year has 365 days. But besides common years, there are leap years which have 366 days. Commonly, there come three common years and then a leap year then three more common years and then a leap year. Now, I must tell you that there are two ways of counting time, called [u]Old Style[/u] and [u]New Style[/u]. [u]Old style[/u], called also the Julian calendar, is used in Russia and in Greece, and used, [ins]200 years ago,[/ins] to be used here. [u]New style[/u], called also the Gregorian calendar, is used here and in [del]most of[/del] England, France, Germany, Spain, Italy, and [del]many[/del] other places. This has nothing to do with the average; but I am going to talk about Old Style and New Style in order to explain [ins]what an[/ins] [del]the[/del] average is.

In [del]all[/del] the Julian calendar every fourth year is always a leap year. Then if we take 365 and multiply it by 4, it will be one day less than [ins]any[/ins] four successive years of the Julian calendar.
