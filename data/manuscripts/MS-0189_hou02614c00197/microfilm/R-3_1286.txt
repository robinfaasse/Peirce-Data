[fig]Long division of 365 by 7 showing repeating decimal 52.142857[/fig]

You see these 6 figures 142857 keep coming
round and round. So 365 divided by 7 gives
[align-center]
$52 \frac{1}{7}$
or $52 \cdot 1 \frac{3}{7}$, that is, $52 \cdot 1$ and $\frac{3}{10}$,
or $52 \cdot 14 \frac{2}{7}$, that is, $52 \cdot 14$ [ins]and[/ins] $\frac{2}{100}$,
or $52 \cdot 142 \frac{6}{7}$, that is, $52 \cdot 142$ [ins]and[/ins] $\frac{6}{1000}$,
or $52 \cdot 1428 \frac{4}{7}$, that is, $52 \cdot 1428$ [ins]and[/ins] $\frac{4}{10000}$,
and so forth.
[/align-center]
We also write this
$52 \cdot \dot{1}4285\dot{7}$
where we put dots over the 1 and 7, to show that
those figures and all between them, come round
over and over again. That is called a circu-
lating decimal.
