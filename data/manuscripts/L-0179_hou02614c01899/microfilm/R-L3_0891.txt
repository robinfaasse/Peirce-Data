[u]CLARK UNIVERSITY.[/u]
[u]WORCESTER, MASS.[/u]

Feb. 20, 1900.

My dear Professor Peirce,

I need not say that your letter interests me exceedingly, and reminds me again in a very delightful manner how stimulating every contact with your mind has always been to me. How I wish we were not so poverty stricken and could tempt you on here to state to us your views. We shall be either better or worse off, however, very soon.

I am especially interested in what you say about Herbart, but I am accustomed to base my pedagogy on psychology, not to be sure the psychology of the schools, but a kind of philosophical biology, vague, general, but aiming only to be directive in a large way.

What you say about demolishing Herbart strikes a very sympathetic vein; what you hint by abstract rhetoric, I cannot understand. I wish there were an opportunity of a friendly talk along the lines of your letter. Do you never come this way?
