[u]An Apology for the Method of Infinitesimals.[/u]
By C. S. Peirce

I have been repeatedly called to account for a remark
(see [u]Century Dictionary[/u] s.v. [u]limit[/u]) that the method of infi-
nitesimals is more in harmony with advances in mathe-
matics which, writing in 1883, I called "recent", than is the
method of limits. I proceed to justify that remark.

I.

I must produce a definition of an infinitesimal;
and, in order to render it clear that that definition involves
no contradiction, I must define the whole system of
quantities of which infinitesimals form a part.

For that purpose I begin with some things that are
already familiar; but I prefer to restate them in my
own form. [del]I begin by defining the system of integers.[/del]
