What has been said constitutes the first clause of the
definition of '[u]any[/u]' and '[u]some[/u]'. It is called the dictum de
omni. The second clause is the converse of the first. Name-
ly, to predicate a character of '[u]any m[/u]' means no more
than that such predication cannot be refuted in the
manner described; while to predic- nobody has a right
to predicate a character of '[u]some m[/u]' unless he can sup-
port the predication in the manner described in the first
clause.
