I call a subject of predication _definite_, if and only if,
the principle of contradiction applies to it, in the sense that
the affirmation and negation of the same character of this
subject cannot both be true, [del]but[/del] [ins]but[/ins] one or other must be
false. I call a subject of predication _individual_, if and only if,
the principle of excluded middle applies to it, in the sense
that [del]the[/del] [ins]of[/ins] affirmation and negation of the same character of
this subject [del]must[/del] [ins]one or other[/ins] must be true and [del]both[/del]
cannot be false. And here I must be understood to mean
that 'Some [u]m[/u]' is not definite because 'Some [u]m[/u] is blue'
and 'Some [u]m[/u] is not blue' may both be true; while
'Any [u]m[/u]' is not individual because 'Any [u]m[/u] is blue'
and 'Any [u]m[/u] is not-blue' may both be false. A
subject [del]whose[/del] [ins]which is[/ins] definite and individual is singular.
