[u]Topical Singularities.[/u]

A [u]topical singularity[/u] of a place [u]A[/u] is a place [u]B[/u] in [u]A[/u] [del]or[/del] from which the [del]mobile[/del] [ins]image[/ins] that fills it can move [del]with in it[/del] [ins]in[/ins] a greater or less [del]number[/del] [ins]variety[/ins] of ways than from ordinary places of like dimensionality in [u]A[/u]

A point [del]figure[/del] place has no singularities

A line place (that is, a place having nowhere a higher dimensionality than a line, or a place occupied by a point place in the course of time) has only point singularities. These are

1[sup]ly[/sup], [u]Topical acnodes[/u] or isolated points
2[sup]ly[/sup], [u]Extremities[/u] [del]or[/del]
3[sup]ly[/sup], [u]Topical nodes[/u], or points of branching, between several branches.

The nodicity of a three way node [fig]three-way node[/fig] may be taken as unity. A four-way node is formed by the [del]union[/del] [ins]coalescence [ins]by shrinkage[/ins][/ins] of two 3-way nodes [fig]four-way node[/fig] and thus has nodicity 2. An N-way node is formed by the coalescence [ins]by shrinkage[/ins] of N-2 3-way nodes and thus has nodicity N-2. The nodicity of an ordinary point is 0. The coalescence [ins]by shrinkage[/ins] of a 3-way node and an extremity makes an ordinary point; and therefore the nodicity of an extremity is -1. The coalescence [ins]by shrinkage[/ins] of 2 extremities makes an acnode; and therefore the nodicity of [del]an acnode is -2. Two acnodes [del]cannot coalesce, because there is only[/del][/del] [ins]or an extremity and an acnode[/ins] [ins]by mere shrinkage.[/ins]
