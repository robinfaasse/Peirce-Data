[del]System of the Ancients is crazy stuff. There is no reason[/del]
[del]to think the ancients had an mathematical theory of[/del]
[del]gravitation; but there are very strong reasons to think[/del]
[del]they had none. There is no reason to suppose the Great[/del]
[del]Pyramid had anything to do with any such theory.[/del] But
even granting for the sake of argument that the [ins]Great[/ins]
Pyramid was built solely to illustrate the law of gravita-
tion as applied to a body falling from an infinite
height, and granting also that the word pyramid (which,
if it be not [u]pir-em-us[/u], "the out-going of the saw, is
not Egyptian, at all) means death by fire, there is
still a chasm wide and deep between these premises
and the conclusion that that edifice bears witness
to the Fall of Lucifer. Now, when you make the
most cautious inferences, likely to be erroneous only
one in a hundred, and pile them one upon another
a hundred deep, the chances are about 3 to 1 that the
conclusion will be worthless. How is it then when every
step is a conjecture of the sort which throws suspicion
upon the author's complete sanity?
