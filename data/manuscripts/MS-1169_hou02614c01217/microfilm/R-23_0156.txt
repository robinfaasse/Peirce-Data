Direct science, a science conversant about objects, as contradistinguished from one conversant about the modes of knowing objects. Historical science, one whose business is to record facts, or events that have actually occurred. Liberal science, one cultivated from curiosity, and not for a living. Lucrative science, a science cultivated as a means of living, as law, medicine, theology, etc. Professional science, the same as a lucrative science. Occult science, originally experimental physics and chemistry, so called because the properties of bodies so found out are occult, that is, cannot be deduced from immediate appearances, without experiment. But such studies being forbidden to the regular clergy, who formed the great body of learned men in the middle ages, occult science came to be looked upon as containing wicked, or at least dangerous to the soul, and then the superstitious modes of reasoning of the chemists, who were mostly inclined to mysticism, naturally allied such science to magic, and finally by an occult science was understood a science mixed with magic. Reflex science, a science conversant about the manner of knowing objects. Simple science, the same as direct science.

SCIENTIFIC experience, (Tr. of Gr. Empeiria methodike) relatively complete experience about any class of objects, obtained by systematical research. Scientific knowledge, knowledge of the causes, conditions, and general characters of classes of things. Scientific logic, logic properly speaking, the knowledge of the theory of reasoning and of thinking in general, as opposed to natural skill and subtlety. Scientific method, a regulated procedure for investigating truth.

STRICT sense, a meaning of a word or phrase narrower than another, so that it is saying more to predicate a term in its strict sense than in a broader sense; and since it requires careful attention to be distinctly aware that we are using a word in a narrow sense and not a broader one, the strict sense most frequently coincides with the more precise and exact sense. But the strict sense is not always the most proper sense.
