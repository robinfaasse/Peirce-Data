[cols]
[col]
Cesare.
No M is P.
All S is P.
Ergo, no S is M.

Short Reduction.
No P is M.
All S is P.
Ergo, no S is M.
[/col]

Long reduction, one in which the major premise of the original syllogism becomes the minor premise, and vice versa, and in which one of the premises and the conclusion are converted. The following is an example:

[cols]
[col]
Camestres
All M is P.
No S is P.
Ergo, no S is M.
[/col]
[col]
Long Reduction.
No P is S.
All M is P.
Ergo, no M is S.
[/col]
[/cols]

Apagogical reduction, or reduction per impossibile, one in which the contradictory of the conclusion becomes one of the premises, and the contradictory of one of the premises the conclusion. Example:

[cols]
[col]
Baroko.
All M is P.
Some S is not P.
Ergo, some S is not M.
[/col]
[col]
Reductio per impossibile.
All M is P.
All S is M.
Ergo, all S is P.
[/col]
[/cols]

Apagogocel reduction is an application of the reductio ad absurdum or ad impossibile, which bears nearly the same name.

REDUCTIVE, a. Reductive conversion, in logic, is a conversion of a proposition in which there is some modification of the subject or predicate; as, no man is a mother, therefore no mother is some man.
