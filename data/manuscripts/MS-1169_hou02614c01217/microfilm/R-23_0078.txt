MATHEMATICAL WORDS IN Q; CONTINUED. [u]Ms. 1169[/u]

[del]Scalar quantity, the ratio between two quantities of the same kind, a real number; this is the definition of Hamilton, but subsequent writers sometimes include imaginaries among scalars.[/del]
[del]Semi-infinite quantity, a system of quantity which is limited at one end and extends to infinity in the other.[/del]
[del]Similar quantities, quantities of the same kind, whose ratios are numbers.[/del]
[del]Superinfinite quantity, a system of quantity which extends through infinity into a new region.[/del]
[del]Hyperbolic quantity is a special kind of superinfinite quantity, in which there are only two regio regions.[/del]
[del]Syncategoreumatic quantity, quantity as expressed by a syncategoreumatic word or generally by any word not a noun.[/del]
[del]Terminal quantity, in logic, the quantity of a term, as opposed to the quantity of a proposition.[/del]
[del]Transcendental quantity, intensive quantity, as opposed to predicamental quantity; so called because different from the quantity treated by Aristotle under the category of quantity.[/del]

There is also another Quantity improperly so called, which consists not in the extension of parts, but in the perfection and vertue of everything. Hence uses it to be call'd, the Quantity of Perfection, and Quantity of Vertue. For the essential perfections of things and Vertues are compos'd of divers degrees, as the Quantity of a Heap or Mole [is composed] of several parts. This, because diffused almost through all the Categories, uses to be call'd a Transcendental Quantity. Burgersdicius, tr. by a Gentleman. [Translation slightly corrected.]

[del]Unidimensional quantity, a system of quantities all of the same kind, otherwise called simple quantity.[/del]
[del]Unlike quantities, quantities which have not a numerical ratio between them.[/del]
[del]Unlimited quantity, a system of quantities such that any two, A and B, being given, a third [ins]C[/ins] exists such that B lies between A and C.; a system of quantity which has no absolute maximum nor minimum in any direction. Unlimited quantity must not be confounded with infinite quantity.[/del]
[del]Variable quantity, a quantity whose progressive changes are under consideration.[/del]
[del]Vector quantity, the quantity which belongs to a right line considered as having direction as well as length, but which is equal for all parallel lines of equal length; any quantity capable of representation by a directed right line, without considering its position in space; a quantity whose square is a negative scalar.[/del]
[del]Virtual quantity, the same as the quantity of virtue or intensive quantity.[/del]

The following phrases were omitted out of the alphabetical order, and should be inserted:

[del]Dual quantity, a system of quantity having only two values in any one direction; as in the Boolian Boolean algebra.[/del]
[del]Infinitesimal quantity, a quantity infinite[del]ly[/del] less than every measurable quantity.[/del]
[del]Infinite quantity, a quantity infinitely greater than every measurable quantity.[/del]
[del]Measurable quantity, a system of quantity every one of which can be stated to any desired degree of approximation by the sums of numerical multiples and submultiples of a finite number of units; a system of quantities, embracing only finite quantities together with certain isolated infinities.[/del]
[del]Limited quantity, a system of quantities all finite and having an absolute maximum and minimum in every direction.[/del]
[del]Finite quantity, a quantity such that an inference of transposed quantity will hold good with reference to its parts.[/del]
[del]Transposed quantity, logical quantity transposed from one subject in the premise to another in the conclusion.[/del]
[del]Definite quantity, in logic, the quantification of a proposition in a more definite way than by the distinction of "some" and "all." There is a great variety of systems of definite quantity.[/del]
