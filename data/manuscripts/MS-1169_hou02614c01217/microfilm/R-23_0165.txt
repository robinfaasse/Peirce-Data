SERPHA. [Ar. al-sarpha, the changer of the weather] The star beta Leonis, or denebola, which see.

SEXTANS or URANIES SEXTANS. A constellations inserted by Hevelius [ins]in 1690[/ins] between the fore-legs of the lion and hydra. It represents the instrument used by Tycho Brahe in Uranienburg, but it is placed between Leo and Hydra, two animals of a fiery nature according to the astrologers, to commemorate the burning of his own instruments and papers in 1679. The brightest star of the cinstellation is of magnitude 4.5.

SHELIAC [Gr. chelys] A yellowish variable star, having a period of 12.9 days, its magnitude at its maximum being 3.4 and at its minimum being 4.5. It is the northern one of the preceding pair in th the strap of the lyre, and is known to the astronomers as beta lrae.

SIRIUS. [Gr. seirios, seiros, seir, the sun or the dog-star] A very white star, the brightest in t the heavens, and more than half a magnitude brighter than Canopus, the next brightest. Its magnitude is -1.4. It is situated in the mouth of the dog, and has been called the dog-star from the earliest times.

High o'er his head the beams of Sirius glow,

SVALOCIN. [Unintelligible] A yellowish variable star of about the 4th magnitude, the northern one on the preceding side of the quadrilateral in the dolphin, alpha Delphini. So called by Piazzi.
