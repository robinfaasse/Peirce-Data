RIEMANN'S SURFACE. [Named for its inventor, the celebrated mathematician Georg Friedrich Bernhard
Riemann, b. 1826-d. [blank] ] An imaginary surface to represent an n-valued function by n infinite planes
planes crossing into one another along certain lines, each of these planes representing the whole
spread of imaginary quantity, and one value of the function belonging to each point of each plane.

RECIPROCAL, n. Reciprocal determinant, the determinant formed with the minors corresponding to each
element of the original determinant.

RECIPROCITY. Hermite's law of reciprocity [named for the French mathematician, Charles Hermite,
b. 1822, still living] the proposition that the number of invariants of the nth order in the coef-
ficients possessed by a binary quantic of the pth degree is equal to the number of invariants of
the order p in the coefficients possessed by a quantic of the nth degree.

RESIDUATION. Theory of residuation, the theory of trans-tangentials to a plane cubic curve.

Residual, n. Add: If a curve intersects a plane cubic curve in a set of points, any part of that
set is said to be the residual of the other part.

RESTITUTION. Add: Coefficient of restitution, the ratio of the relative velocity of two balls the
instant after their impact to their relative velocity the instant before.

REVERSION. Add: Principle of reversion, the principle that if any material system in which the for
forces acting depend only on the positions of the particles be in motion, if at any instant the
velocities of the particles be reversed, the previous motion will be repeated in a reverse order.
Method of reversion, a method of studying the properties of curves, especially conics, by means of
points the reverse of one another.

REVERSE. Two points are said to be the reverse of one another with reference to two fixed origins
and two fixed axes when the line through the first origin and [del]its[/del] [ins]the[/ins] first point
meets the first axis at the point where the line through the second origin and the second point
meets the same axis, while the line through the first origin and the second point meets the second
axis at the same point where the line through the second origin and the first point meets the same
axis.

REVERSOR, n. A linkwork for reversing a figure.

RULE-DRILLER, n. A teacher who drills his pupils upon rules, instead of educating their understand-
ings.

I speak to the teacher, not the rule-driller. De Morgan, Math. Books, Introduction, p. xxii.
