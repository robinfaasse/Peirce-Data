[u]REPRESENTATIONISM[/u], n. The doctrine, held by Descartes and others of his time, that in the perception of the external world the immediate object of consciousness is vicarious or representative of another and principal object beyond the sphere of consciousness.

REPRESENTATIONIST, n. One who holds the doctrine of representationism.

REPRESENTATIVE, a. For 5, substitute:
5. In psychology and logic, mediately known, known by means of a representation, or object which signifies another object. Representative being, being as an immediate object of consciousness Representative faculty, the faculty of representing images which the reproductive faculty has evoked; the imagination. Representative knowledge, knowledge of a thing by means of a mental image, but not as actually existing.

REPRODUCTION, n. Add:
4. In psychology, the act of repeating in consciousness a group of sensations which has already been presented in se perception. Synthesis of reproduction, the name given by Kant to that association of idias by which one calls up another in the mind; empirical synthesis of reproduction, an association by the principle of contiguity, depending on the associated ideas having been presented together or successively; pure transcendental synthesis of reproduction, an association of ideas such th t one will suggest the other, independent of experience, and due to innate laws of the mind, and one of the necessary conditions of knowledge.

REPRODUCTIVE, a. Reproductive imagination, the elementary faculty by virtue of which one idea calls up another, of which memory and imagination, as popularly understood, are special developments.

Philosophers have divided imagination into two, - what they call the reproductive and the productive. By the former they mean imagination considered simply as reexibitin[ins]g[/ins]m, representing the objects presented by perception, that is, exhibiting them without addition or retrenchment, or any change in the relations which they reciprocally held, when first made known to us through s sense. Hamilton, Lect. on Metaph. XXXIII.

Reproductive faculty, in the psychology of Sir W. Hamilton, the faculty of association of ideas, by virtue of which one suggests a definite other, but not including the faculty of apprehending an idea a second time.

Contradictory opposition, the relation of two propositions one of which must be true and the other false, the relation of two characters such that every individual, in a certain universe or general category of things alone under consideration, must possess the one and want the other. Loosely, disagreement, inconsistency, contradiction. The principle of repugnance, the same as the principle of contradiction.
