3. Mediate, by the intervention of a third term. Remote cause, the cause of a cause ; a
cause which contributes to the production of the effect by the concurrence of another cause of

A cause efficient is said to be two ways next ; to wit, either generally os specially. In
general, that cause is said to be next which neither by its existence, [they commonly say suppo-
situm] or virtue, is joined to the effect. For such a virtue being an instrument of the principal
cause, it is deemed with the principal cause to be one only. And therefore when the principal caus
cause by its virtue is joined to the effect, it is itself esteemed to the effect. Neither is it
any matter whether that virtue be an instrument conjoined or separate. For example: the fire is
said to be the next cause of ustion ; parents of their children ; notwithstanding the fire acts
by the mediation of heat, and the parents of seed : for although heat and seed are instruments, ye
yet do they not effect by their intervening ; but that the principal cause may be said to be
next to the effect. A cause therefore is said to be remote in this sense, viz. which neither by its
existence nor virtue is joined to the effect: as when the grandfather is said to be the cause of
his son's son, or grandson. Burgersdicius, tr. by a Gentleman. Bk. I. Chap. xvii. Ax. 35, §1.

A remote mark, a mark of a mark. Remote matter, matter unprepared for the reception of any par-

[u]REMOTIVE, a.[/u] Removing, in the sense of declaring impossible. Remotive proposition, in logic, one
which declares a relation to be impossible ; thus, to say that a man is blind is only privative,
to say that a statue is incapable of seeing is remotive.
