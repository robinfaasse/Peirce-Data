TUBUS ASTRONOMICUS. A constellation, the same as TELESCOPIUM, which see.

TEREBELLUM. [Corruption of Gr. tetrapleuron, quadrangle, applied to this group by Ptolemy].
A group of four stars in the form of a quadrilateral at the root of the tail of Sagittarius. The
north preceding is of magnitude 5.0; the north following 5.0; the south preceding 4.7; and the
south following 4.7.

A pair of stars of magnitudes 3.8 and 4.4 following the trapezium in Eridanus, upsilon 1st and 2nd
or 50 and 52 Eridani. The name became applied to these stars by an inadvertency of Bayer, who
wrote the name against these instead of against two others following these after the bend of the
river, , upsilon 3rd and 4th, or 43 and 41, of magnitudes 4.0 and 3.3. The name was originated
by Grotius, who thought the name Beemin, formerly applied to upsilon 3rd, had been applied to the
pair collectively, and that it must be a corruption of theemin, or teomin, Hebrew for the twins;
although beemin is more likely to be the Hebrew behamaim, in the water.

TIGRIS. An obsolete constellation where Vulpecula now is first found in the Planisphere of Bartsch
1624, and usual for more than a century following.

TRIANGULUM. An ancient northern constellation, representing as some said the delta of the Nile.
It has one star, beta, of the third magnitude.

TRIANGULUM AUSTRALE. A southern constellation, the southern triangle, added by Petrus Theodori in
the XVIth century, south of Ara. It contains one second and two third magnitude stars.

TRIANGULUM MINUS. A constellation, the lesser triangle, introduced by Hevelius in 1690 immediate-
ly south of triangulum. It is no longer in use.

TUCANA. A southern constellation, the toucan, made by Petrus Theodori in the XVIthe century. It is
situated south of the Phoenix, and has one star of the third and two of the fourth magnitudes.

TURDUS SOLITARIUS. A constellation introduced by Le Monnier in 1776, on the tail of Hydra and en-
croaching on the southern scale of Libra. The constellation is no longer used.

THUBAN. [Ar. al-thubān, the dragon] A very white star of magnitude 3.6 in the tail of the dragon,
alpha draconis.

TUREIS. The star otherwise called scutulum, which see.

URSA MAJOR and MINOR. The constellations of the great and little bear. See BEAR.

UNUKALHAY. [Ar. 'unk-al-hayyah, the serpent's neck] The star otherwise called RAS ALAGUE, which see.
