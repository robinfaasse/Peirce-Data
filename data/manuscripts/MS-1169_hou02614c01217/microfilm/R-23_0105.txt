[u]THEODOLITE[/u]. In regard to the derivation of this word, note first, that it first appears in En-
glish, being recent in the continental languages; second, the usual seventeenth century spelling
was theodelite; third in Bourne's Treasure for travellers, London 1578, the word athelida is
used with the same meaning; fourth, there can hardly be a doubt that this athelida is a corrup-
tion of alhidada. See Philosophical Magazine for April 1846.. The statement in the Imperial
that the word first occurs in 1704 is ridiculous. It is found in Digges's Geometrical Practise, 1
1594. [ins][Give a Figure of a modern theodolite.][/ins] N. Tartaglia in 1538 gives a description of a theolite
[ins]or worse minds the telescope. He calls it bossolo. Theodolite is sometimes written theodelet.[/ins]

[u]TANGENT[/u] Under 1, add this citation.
When there is a line at through a point a of a curve, having the property that, any angle
being proposed, however small, it is always possible to find a point b so near to a on one side
that every chord inside ab makes withh at an angle less than the proposed angle; then this
line at is called the tangent to the curve up to the point a on that side. Clifford, Dynamic,
p. 47.

[u]TWIST-VELOCITY, n.[/u] The state of a body at any instant when it has a rotational velocity round
a certain axis, compounded with a linear velocity along that axis.
