RELATE, n. 1. Anything as being in a relation to another thing
[ins]Any object[/ins]
2. Anything as being the first term of a relation to another thing
This word is little used, most writers pre ferring either, Relatum, Relative, Related, or Rela-
tion, as shown in the following quotations.

The relatum and its correlate seem to be simul natura. Grote, Aristotle, Chap. iii.

Before a relative can suggest its correlate, the two must have been previously known to be
mutually related. J. Clark Murray, Handbook of Psychology, Bk. I. Part II. Chap. i. § 2.

The application of the word Relative to things is not only an offence against philos ophy,
but against propriety of language. The correct designation for things that are called by relative
names, is not Relative , but Related. J, S. Mill, Foot-note to his Ed. of James Mills Analysis of
the Phenomena of the Human Mind, Chap. xiv. Sect. 3.

Relateds are reciprocated. That is, every related is referred to a reciprocal correlate.
Burgersdicius, tr. by a Gentleman. Bk. I. Chap. vii. Ax. 10.

Relative has, indeed, within my memory, by a ridiculous affectation of false and unfounded
accuracy, crept into use to the exclusion of relation. Horne Tooke, Diversions of Purley, Part II.
Chap. viii.

Relations are defined as terms whose being is "of" others. Edwin Wallace, Philosophy of Aristo
tle, s 10.

If the relation which agrees to heteronyms has a name, one of the two relateds is called the
relate, to wit, that from which the relation has its name ; the other the correlate. * * * *
In every relation are required subject and term. That is called the subject to which the rela-
tion is attributed ; or that which is referred to some other thing. That, the term to which the
subject is referred. The subject is said to be the relate, and the term the correlate, when af-
fected with a relation and considered together with it. Burgersdicius. Loc. cit.

A relative is a term whose definition describes what sort of a system of objects that is whos
whose first member [which is termed the relate] is denoted by the term ; and names for the other
members of the system [which are termed the correlates] are usually appended to limit the denota-
tion still further. C. S. Peirce, On the Algebra of Logic, Chap. iii, § 2.
