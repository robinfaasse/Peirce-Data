OF FIXED STARS AND CONSTELLATIONS IN Q, R, S.

[del]QUADRANS MURALIS. A constellation inserted by Lalande between the right foot of Hercules, the left hand of Bootes, and Draco, in memory of the instrument with which he and his nephew Le Francais had observed many thousand stars. The constellation is now obsolete.[/del]

RASALAS. [Ar. rās al-ased al-shemālī, the northern head of the lion] An orange star of magnitude 2.2, the middle and following of three in the neck of the lion, gamma Leonis.

RAS-AL-HAGUE [ras-al-ha-gyoo] [Moorish pronunciation of Ar. rās el-hhawwā, the serpent-charmer's head] A very white star of magnitude 2.2 in the head of Ophiuchus, alpha Ophiuchi.

From Altair let a ray be cast where we Arcturus view,
One third that distance will reveal the star ras-al-hague. Smyth.

RASALGETI [ras-al-ja'-tī] [Ar. rās el-jāthī, the kneeler's head] An orange star varying from magnitude 3.1 to magnitude 3.9 in an irregular period, and situated in the head of Hercules, alpha Herculis.

REFLECTOR HERSCHELII. A constellation which the Abbé Hell, in 1789, proposed to insert under the southern eye of the bull; but it does not seeem to have been adopted by anybody.

REGULUS [LL. used by Copernicus, tr. of Gr. Basiliskos] name found in Ptolemy] A very white star on star on the heart of the of magnitude 1.4 on the heart of the lion, alpha Leonis.

RETICULUM or RETICULUS RHOMBOIDALIS. A southern constellation, introduced by La Caille, in remembrance of his arrangement for observing the southern stars.

RHOMBUS. An obsolete constellation, introduced in the sixteenth century by Isaac Habrecht, near the south pole.

RIGEL [rī'jel] [Ar. rijl el-jauzā el-yusrā, the left foot of the giant] A bluish star of magnitude 0.2, the sixth brightest in the heavens, in the tip of the left foot of Orion, where it dips into the river, beta Orionis.

ROBUR CAROLINUM. A constellation introduced by Halley in 1677, between Argo and Centaurus, to represent the royal oak in which Charles II was hidden after the battle of Worcester. The constellation is no longer in use.

ROTANEV. [Unintelligible] A very yellow star of magnitude 3.7 the southern of the preceding side of the rhomboid in the dolphin, beta delphini. The name first appears in Piazzi's Catalogue.

RUKHBA. [Ar. knee] A very white star of magnitude 2.8 on the knee of Cassiopeia. epsilon Cassiopeae.
