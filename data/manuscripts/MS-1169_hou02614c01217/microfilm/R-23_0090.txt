SIDEREAL, a. Suppress No. 2 altogether. Put:
Sidereal time, time as measured by the diurnal motion of the stars; only for convenience,
the sidereal day, the fundamental period of sidereal time, is taken to begin and end with the pass
sage over the meridian of the vernal equinox, the first point of Aries, or the origin of right
ascension (three names for the same thing) There is just one more sidereal than mean solar day in a
sidereal year. Sidereal year, the time in which the su earth makes one complete revolution round
the sun. It is a tropical year multiplied by 1+ yearly precession/360° ; it is therefore 20[ins]m[/ins] 23. 17[ins]s[/ins]
longer than a tropical year; that is, it was 365 days 6 hours 9 minutes 9. 13 seconds in 1850 and
it is shortening at the rate of about half a second a century at the present time. The sidereal day
is 3[ins]m[/ins] 55. 91[ins]s[/ins] shorter than a mean solar day[ins].[/ins]The sidereal time of mean noon is 0 hours on March 22nd,
6 hours on June 21st, 12 hours on September 20th, and 18 hours on December 21st. Sidereal clock
or chronometer, one that keeps sidereal time.

SIDEROSTAT, n. For the definition put: A heliostat regulated to sidereal time.
