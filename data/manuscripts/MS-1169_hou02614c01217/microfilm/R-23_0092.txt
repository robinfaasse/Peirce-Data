SINGULAR, a. Add:
9. In math., exceptional; belonging to a continuum and yet discrete or not in continuous relation with objects like it. Singular point, a point of a curve, surface, etc., which presents any non-metrical peculiarity 5 such, for instance, are nodes, or points of crossing, conjugate or outlying points, not adjacent to any other real point, stationary points or cusps, points of stopping in certain transcendental curves, points of contrary flexure, etc. In the same sense, there are singular tangents and tangent planes. Singular root of an equation with one unknown quantity, an equal root, a root resulting from the coincidence of two roots, so that if the absolute term were altered by an infinitesimal amount, there would be either two real roots or two imaginary roots in place of that root. Singular root of an indeterminate equation, one which corresponds to a double point one the curve, surface, etc. which the equation represents. Singular solution of a differential equation, a solution not included in the complete primitive. [del]If the primitive be plotted as a curve, the envelope of this curve represents the singula[/del] This solution is the envelope of the family of curves represented by the primitive with its arbitrary constant, in the case of a differential equation of the first order. Singular integral of a partial differential equation, a solution not included under the complete integral nor under the general integral. It represents the general envelope of the surfaces represented by the complete integral.

SINGULARITY, n. Add:
5. In geom., a singular point tangent, tangent plane, etc., in a curve, surface, etc. Or a singularity may be constituted by any incidence or coincidence of parts or essential adjuncts of the locus. The ordinary singularities of plane curves are double points or binodes, double tangents or bitangents, stationary points or cusps, and stationary tangents or inflexions. The higher singularities are formed by the coincidence of two or more ordinary singularities.

SKEW, a. Add:
2. In math., involving a relation when certain quantities are unequal different from that which subsists when they are equal. The word is hardly intelligible apart from the phrases in which it is used. Skew determinant, one in which the conjugate constituents are equal in magnitude but opposite in sign. Skew symmetrical determinant, [del]SSS[/del] a skew determinant whose principal constituents vanish. Skew helicoid, the screw-surface. Skew curve, a curve in three dimensions. So skew cubic, skew cartesian, etc. Skew invariant, one which changes its sign when $x$ and $y$ are interchanged. Skew surface, a ruled surface in which two successive generators do not in general intersect. So, skew quadric, etc. Skew reciprocal, a locus in line coordinates proportional to the point-coordinates of another locus, or vice versa. Skew antipoints, four points the vertices of an imaginary tetrahedron all the edges of which are of zero length except two which are perpendicular to each other and to the line joining their middle points.

SLIDING, Instantaneous sliding axis, the axis of the helix along which a particle is moving at any instant.
