Mr. Shadworth Hodgson, in his Philosophy of Reflection, 1878, uses the term to denote one
of three fundamental modes of consciousness, namely that in which the objective and subjective
aspects of what is present are discriminated without being separated as person and thing.

The particular reflection that states of consciousness are things, or that the subject is
it objects, constitutes the reflective mode of consciousness. Perception is the rudimentary func-
tion in reflection as well as in primary consciousness; and reflective conception is a deriva-
tive from it. Hodgson, Philosophy of Reflection, i 128.
