TIME, n. Arrange the definition as follows:
1. The system of those relations which any event has to any other, this relationship being
realistically conceived as a sort of self-subsistent entity, or object of contemplation. It is
conceived as a stream flowing through the field of the present. The notion is most confused, and
serves mainly to give names to conceptions which are otherwise apprehended. Leibniz saw that time
is the confused apprehension of a system of relations, but looking at the matter too much from the
mathematical point of view, he failed to notice that it is not, however, a general idea, but is
contracted to the individual system of relations of the events that actually do happen. Kant in
calling time the form of an intuition corrected this oversight, while at the same time losing his
hold of the truth contained in Leibniz's view. Time is personified as an old man bald-headed,
with a forelock, carrying a sythe and an hour-glass.

Ewen so-ch is time that takes in trust
Our youth our joys our all we have
And pays us but with age and dust. Raleigh.

He was not for an age! but for all time. Jonson.

Ye gods, annihilate but time and space,
And make two lovers happy! Pope.

2. Age, long duration. Also, personified. Time immemorial, time to long to be remembered.

the joiner squirrel, or old grub,
Time out of mind the fairies' coachmakers. Shak. Romeo and Juliet 1 4.

The soul's dark cottage, battered and decayed,
Lets in new light through chinks that time has made. Waller.

3. Duration as a quantum, something susceptible of being measured and apportioned. To be maer
ter of one's time, to have leisure, to be not too busied with necessary matters as not to be able
to do pretty much as one pleases. To waste time, to act to no purpose, though a cosideraple space
of time. To spend time, to apply one's energy in any way for the space of time considered. So,
to have time to spare, or time on one's hands. [ins]So, opposed to eternity.[/ins]
[ins][u]Make use of time as thou valuest eternity.[/u] Fuller.[/ins]
Do not squander time for that is the stuff life is made of. Franklin.

4. An epoch, an individual interval or instant, a distinct place in history. Also, the state
of society and the manners and customs and so forth characterizing such an epoch. Now, generally
in the plural. Old time or old times, a date [del]long[/del] [ins]time gone by[/ins] passed. Good time, a pleasant interval, usually
a period of years, but especially in the U. S. a period of a few hours, or even less. Hence, an op-
portunity; as, choose your own time. Nick of time, just the right moment.
