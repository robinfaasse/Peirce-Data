A system of measuring [ins]duration, and of fixing epochs.[/ins] [del]time.[/del] Apparent time, the measure of the day by the apparent positions of the sun; it has had different varieties, but as now spoken of by astronomers, it is determined by apparent noon, or the instant of passage of the centre of the sun over the meridian. Mean time, see mean. Sidereal time, the measure of time by the hour-angle of th the first point of Aries, or the origin of right ascension. Astronomica time is mean time reckoned from noon to noon, the hours after midnight having numbers above 12. Nautical time is the same as astronomical time, except that the date of the day agrees with the civil or ordinary time for the morning hours, while with astronomical time it is the afternoon hours during which the date is the same as in civil time. Local time, a system of measuring time dependent on the instant of the pas-sage of some heavenly body or point over the meridian of the place for which the time is to be use used. Standard time, a system of determining time in use in the United States, in which the minute minutes and seconds agree with those of Greenwich time, while the hours are taken so as to accord as nearly as convenience will allow with those of local time. This system was invented by Professr Benjamin Peirce. Universal time, a system of measuring time which shall be the same for all places on the earth. Equinoctial time, the mean longitude of the sun according to Delambre's tables, con-verted into time at the rate of 360° to the tropical year. This system was invented by Sir J. F. W. Herschel.
