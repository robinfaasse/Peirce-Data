In place of the absurdly ignorant attempt at explaining the Method of Least Squares, put the fol-
lowing: Least Squares, the method used by astronomers geodesists and others, of deducing the most
probable or best result of their observations, in cases in which the arithmetical mean of a num-
ber of observations of the same quantity is the most probable or best value of that quantity. The
[del]xxxxx[/del] adoption of the mean value of a number of observations may be considered as the simplest
application of the method of least squares. When the observed values depend upon several unknown
quantities, the rule which results from the principle of the arithmetical mean is to adopt such
values for the unknown quantities as to make the sum of the squares of the concluded errors of the
observations the least possible. When there are certain conditions that must be fulfilled, as for
example in geodesy that the sum of the angles of each triangle must equal two right angles plus th
the spherical excess, the rules become still more complicated. There are also rules for calculatin
probable errors, etc.

STATIONARY, a. The example given about the sun suffices to show that stationary does not mean fixed
1. Without velocity, whether this condition is only instantaneous or whether the body spoken
of remains motionless for an interval of time. A planet is said to be stationary at a turning poin
point of its motion, when its longitude is neither increasing nor diminishing. The sun is said
to be stationary when it reaches one of the tropicsand it begins to turn toward the equinoctial.
Under 2, strike out the first "not."
Phrases. Stationary contact, a contact between two surfaces at two consecutive points, so tha
that the curve of their intersection has a stationary point or cusp where there is stationary con-
tact. Stationary point, on a curve, a point where the point generating the curveis stationary
and turns back, a cusp, a binode whose two tangents coincide. Stationary tangent of a curve, a
tangent where the [ins]moving[/ins] tangent generating the curve is stationary and turns back, an inflexion, a link
or bitangent whose two points of contact coincide. Stationary tangent plane of a surface, a tan-
gent plane which has stationary contact with the surface, a doubly tangent plane whose points of
contact coincide, the tangent at a parabolic point. Stationary motion, such a motion of a sys-
tem that no particle continually departs further and further from its original position, nor has
its velocity continually increasing nor diminishing.

STEADY, a. Steady motion, a motion of a fluid such that the velocity at each point remains con-
stant in magnitude and direction.
