STRAIGHT, a. For 1, put:
1. Lying as a line evenly between its points. The principal property or characteristic of a straight line is that it is completely determined by any two points taken upon it. The idea of measurement does not enter into the idea of a straight line, and it is unnecessary to introduce that idea into the definition; therefore a straight line should not be defined as the shortest distance between two points. [del]The definition[/del] The definition adopted is that of Euclid. It has been objected that it does not serve to prove anything; but this is far from being true though Euclid does not make use of it. Thus, it evidently follows that two straight lines cannot enclose space. Also, if there be any shortest or longest distance between two points, this must be measured along the straight line through them. Also, a body not acted on by any force must move along a straight line; for a force is an acceleration, so that if there is no force there is no change in the velocity; and the velocity determined by the positions at two consecutive instants, so that the locus of the body at all times is determined by its positions at two instants that is by two positions alone; but the only locus determined by two points only is the straight line. The theory of nets also follows from this definition.

STRICTION, n. [L. strictio] A drawing or pressing together. Line of striction of a ruled surface, the locus of points on the generators of a ruled surface where each is nearest to the next consecutive generator.

STURM'S THEOREM. [Named after the Swiss mathematician, Jacques Charles Francois Sturm, b. 1803-d. 1855, who discovered the theorem in 1835.] A proposition in the theory of equations for determining the number of real roots of an equation between given limits.

STURMIAN, a. Pertaining to the Swiss mathematician Sturm. Sturmian function, one of the series of remainders obtained in the process of finding the greatest measure of an [del]integral[/del] integral function and its derivative, provided the sign of each is changed as we proceed.

SUBNORMAL, n. For the definition, put:
That part of the axis of abscissas of a curve which is intercepted between the normal and the ordinate. Polar subnormal, the line drawn from the origin of polar coordinates perpendicular to the radius vector to meet the normal.
