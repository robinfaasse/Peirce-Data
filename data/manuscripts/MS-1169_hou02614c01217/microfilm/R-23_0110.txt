MATHEMATICAL WORDS IN U.

[del]U, in quaternions, is an operational sign which prefixed to the symbol of a quaternion denotes the versor of that quaternion. In the theory of heat, it is used to denote the energy, or the sum of the increment of heat and the heat consumed. u, in the calculus, is used to denote a function. u, v, w, in hydrodynamics, are used to denote the rectangular components of the velocity.[/del]

ULTRAELLIPTIC, a. Hyperelliptic.

UMBILIC, n. Strike out the t. Add:
In geom., a point of a surface where the radii of curvature are all equal. The number of umbilics, real and imaginary, on a surface of the n th order is n(10n²-25n+16).

UNIBILICAR, a. Pertaining to an umbilic. Umbilicar focal conic of a quadric surface, one which passes passes through the umbilics.

UMBRA, n. Add: In algebra, a symbol which when paired with another makes the symbol of a quantity. See UMBRAL notation.

UMBRAL, a. Pertaining to an umbra. Umbral notation, a notation for determinants invented by [del]Charl[/del] [ins]Charles[/ins] Auguste Vandermonde, b. 1735-d. 1796., in 1772. Each constituent of the determinant is represented as the product as the product letters one for the row, the other for the column, which letters do not of course denote quantities, but only the numerical position of the row or columns so that the product of one of one set by one of the other is equal to a quantity. If the umbral multiplication is commutative the determinant is symmetrical, if polar skew symmetrical. The name was given by Sylvester.

UNDULATION. Add: In geom., a point of undulation is a point where a curve has a [del]4-pointic[/del] contact with its tangent.

[ins]in ancient astron.,[/ins]
[del]U[/del]NFORMED stars, stars not included in any constellation-figure, but considered as belonging to on one of the constellations. Generally used with reference to Ptolemy's catalogue, as the shapes of the constellation-figures are not so determinate as to distinguish whether stars not given by Ptolemy are in all cases within or without the figure.

UNICURSAL, a. On one path of a moving element. Unicursal curve, one which can be expressed as the locus of a point defined by rational functions of a single parameter. [It is not necessary that the functions should be integral.] Not every [del]u[/del]nipartite curve is unicursal, because though such a curve may be expressed in terms of a single parameter, it may be only by means of an irrational function having but one real value; but such curves are only of odd orders. A unicursal curve may have several branches, owing to its passing through infinity.

UNIPARTITE, a. Not separated into two unconnected parts. Unipartite curve, one whose real part forms one continuous whole, with the understanding that a passage through infinity does not constitute a severing of the curve.

UNIMODULAR, a. Having only one modulus. Unimodular transformation, in alg., one whose modulus is equal to unity.

UNIPLANAR, a. Lying in one plane. Uniplanar node, a degenerate form of a node or conical point on a surface, where the cone degenerates into two coincident planes. Same as Unode, which see.
