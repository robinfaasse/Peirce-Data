[u]Pure reason[/u], reason used in strictly a priori deduction. See PURE.

Reason is pure if in reasoning we admit only definitions and propositions known a priori. Tr. [ins]from[/ins] [del]of[/del] Baumeister, Philosophia Definitiva, 2nd Ed. 1738. § 823.

[u]Ratiocinant reason[/u], or reasoning reason, the human understanding, the discursive reason.
[u]Ratiocinate reason[/u], the [ins]intelligible[/ins] essence or quiddity of a thing.

The distinctions of reason reasoning and reasoned may be discerned by this sign. Those things which are distinguished by reason reasoning are predicated the one of the other in an identical predication. Those by reason reasoned are not predicated, or if they are, their predication is not identical. Burgersdicius, tr. by a Gentleman. Bk. I. Chap. xxi. Ax. 4. §2. [This distinction supposes there are no synthetical propositions a priori.]
