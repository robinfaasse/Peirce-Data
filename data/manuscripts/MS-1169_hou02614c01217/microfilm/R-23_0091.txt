SIMILAR, a. In place of all the geometrical definitions, put:

3. In geom. Of the same shape; that is, said of two figures which have all their correspond-
ing lengths proportional, whence it will follow, for ordinary Euclidean space, that all their
corresponding angles will be equal, that their corresponding areas will be in the duplicate ratio
of their lengths, and that their corresponding volumes will be in the triplicate ratio of their
lengths. In the non-Euclidean systems of geometry, these consequences are all falsified, so that
there are no similar figures. The following are the definitions of Euclid's elements. Similar seg-
ments of circles are those embracing equal angles or in which the angles are equal. Bk. III. Def. 11
Similar rectilinear figures are such as have their angles equal each to each, and the sides in-
cluding the equal angles proportional. Bk. VI. Def. 1. Similar solid figures are those contained
under similar planes equal in number. Bk. XI. Def. 9. [Tr. of Gr. hsmotos]

SIMILARLY. Add: Similar and similarly placed, [Tr. of Gr. homoios kai homoios keimenoi] Said of
similar figures placed so that all their corresponding lines are parallel.

SIMILITUDE, n. Add:

4. In geom., the relation of similar figures to one another. Centre of similitude, for two
similar figures similarly placed, a point which considered as being common to the two figures does
not destroy their similitude; a point such that radii vectores from it to the corresponding point
points of the two figures are proportional and either directed the same way, or (as is commonly
understood) t opposite ways. External and internal centres of similitude for two circles, the
intersections of their common tangents on the line joining their centres; the external centre
is the one from which a point can move on either tangent to infinity without meeting either circle
the internal centre is the one from which this is impossible. Circle of similitudi, a circle view-
ed from [ins]any point on[/ins] the circumference of which two given circles look equally large. Axes of similitude, four
lines on which lie three by three the six centres of similitude of three circles.

SIMULTANEOUS, a. Simultaneous equations, equations satisfied at the same time, that is, with the
same system of values of the unknown quantity; or in the case of differential equations, with the
same system of primitives.

SIMPLE quantity, in math., one expressible by means of a single number.

SINE, n. For the definition put:

A function of an angle, the ratio of one leg of a right triangle to the hypotheneuse, the
angle opposite the leg being the angle to which the sine belongs. This is the modern definition,
but in the early trigonometry the sine is considered as a line the half-chord, and belongs to an
arc rather than to an angle. Natural sine, the sine as above defined; the expression arose when
sine was still understood as a half-chord and meant the sine for radius unity (or some multiple of
ten.) Logarithmic or artificial sine, the logarithm of the sine. Lines of sines, a [del]sines[/del] scale
having divisions marked with values of an angle in arithmetical progression, the distances of the
divisions [del]from the centre being proportional to the sines of these angles.[/del]
