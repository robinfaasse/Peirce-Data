[u]Quadrivium[/u], n. (L.L. corrupted from
[u]quadruvium[/u], used by Boetius; probably a
translation of a Greek word [u]τετραόδιον[/u], which
might perhaps be found in Nichomachus.)
The collective name for the four branches
of mathematics according to [ins]the[/ins] Pythagoreans [del]s[/del],
arithmetic treating of number in itself, music
treating of applied number, geometry treating
of stationary number, and astronomy treating
of number in motion. This Pythagorean
quadrivium, preceded by the trivium of
grammar, logic, and rhetoric, made up
the seven liberal arts taught in the
schools of the Roman empire.
