TRANSFORMATION, n. Add to what I have given above the following:
It is also used, nearly as a synonym of correspondence, to signify the passage from one geo-
metrical locus to another whose points correspond to those of the first locus, the point-coordin-
ates being connected by equations. Rational transformation one in which for every point of [ins]eithe[/ins] one
locus there corresponds just one point of the other. For example, the trilinear coordinates x, y,
z, of the variable point in one plane might be connected with the coordinates x', y', z', of the
variable point in another plane by the equations x': y': z':: -yz: zx: xy so that for every point of
the first plane would correspond one point of the second, and the equations could also be put int
into the form x: y: z:: y'z': z'x': x'y', showing that for every point of the second plane there would
be just one point of the first, and these two facts taken together constitute the transformation
as a rational one. Homographic transformation1 one which leaves the anharmonic ratios unchanged.
Cremona transformation, see CREMONA.
