2. A character of a plurality of things; a fact concerning two or more things regarded as
predicate of one of them connecting it with the others, and considered abstractly. Thus, suppose
a locomotive blows off steam; this fact constitutes a relation between the locomotive and the
steam, so far as the "blowing" is conceived to be a character of the locomotive, and another re-
lation, so far as the "being blown" is conceived as a character of the steam, and both these re-
lations together are embraced in the same relationship, or plural fact. The two subjects or things
to which the plural fact relates are termed the relates or correlates; the one which is conceived
as subject is termed the subject of the relation, or the relate; the others are termed the cor-
relates. Dual relations, relation between a pair of things. or between a relate and a single cor-
relate. Plural relation, a relation between a relate and several correlates, as when A aims a shot
at B, [del]or[/del] [ins]or more[/ins] C. Relation of reason, one which depends upon a fact which can be stated as an aggregate
of two facts, one concerning the relate, the other concerning the correlate, such that the an-
nihilation of the relate or the correlate would only destroy one of the these facts, but leave the
other intact; thus, the fact that Franklin and Rumford were both scientific Americans consti-
tutes a relationship between them, with two correlative relations; but these are relations of
reason, because the two facts are that Franklin was a scientific American and that Rumford was a
scientific American, the first of which facts would remain true even if Rumford had never existed,
and the second even if Franklin had never existed. Real relation, one the statement of which
cannot be so separated into two facts one relating to the relate and the other to the correlate,
such as the relation of Cain to Abel, as his killer. For the fact that Cain killed somebody, and
that Abel was killed, do not together make up the fact that Cain killed Abel. Relation of equi-
parance, one which confers the same relative name upon relate and correlate; thus the being a
cousin of somebody is such a relation, for if A is cousin to B, B is cousin to A. Relation of dis-
quiparance, one which coonfers unlike names upon relate and correlate. . Self-relation, one of
such a sort that a thing can be in that relation to itself, as being the killer of. Alio-rela-
tion, one of such a nature that a thing cannot be in that relation to itself, as being previous
to. Accidental relation, an indirect relation of A to C, constituted by A being in some rela-
tion to B, and B being in an independent relation to C. Thus, if a man throws away a date-stone,
and that [del]da[/del]te-stone strikes an invisible genie, the relation of the man to the genie is an acci-
dental one.
