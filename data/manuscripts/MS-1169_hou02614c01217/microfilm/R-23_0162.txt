If the prospect of interjacent objects be that which suggests the idea of further distance,
and this idea of further distance be as that brings into the mind the idea of greater mag-
nitude, it should hence follow that if one looked at the horizontal moon from behind a wall, it
would appear no bigger than ordinary. ... Nor will it suffice to say, the memory doth even then
suggest all that extent of land, etc., which lies within the horizon; which suggestion occasions
a sudden judgment of sense, that the moon is further off and larger than usual. Berkeley, Theory
of Vision, 377.
