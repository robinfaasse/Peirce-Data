To be frightened out of one's seven senses, to be so frightened as to lose ones understanding for
the time being. This phrase seems to refer to some primitive psychology. A poem attributed to the
ancient Welsh bard Taliesin speaks of his seven faculties, "as seven senses of my reason" and con-
nects them with the seven planets. These is an analogous passage in Macrobius; and certain mys-
tical theologians of the middle ages, as Hugo of St. Victor and Bonaventura say that there are
six natural grades and one supernatural grade of understanding.
