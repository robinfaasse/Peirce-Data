VIRGO. An ancient constellation and sign of the zodiac representing a female winged and standing
figure with an ear of wheat in the left hand. It contains one star of the first magnitude, 6 of th
the 3rd, and 9 of the 4th.

VULPECULA CUM ANSERE. A constellation, the fox with the goose, first appearing in the Prodromus
Astronomiae of Hevelius, 1690. It lies between the eagle and the swan, and is generally called
Vulpecula. It has one star of the fourth magnitude.

VEGA. See WEGA.

WEGA. [Ar. el-nesr el-wâki, the falling eagle] A bluish star of magnitude 0.4 the 7th brightest
in the heavens ; called also Vega, Lyra, alpha lyrae, and the falling grype. It is a star seen
all summer in the northern hemisphere, and easily recognized by the two small ones making an equi-
lateral triangle with it.

WEZEN. A yellowish star of magnitude 1.8 at the top of the left thigh of the dog, delta Canis ma-
joris. [Ar. al-wezn, the weight, on account of its not rising high above the horizon]

XIPHIAS. A constellation made by Petrus Theodori in the XVth century in the south pole of the
ecliptic, and now called Dorado.
