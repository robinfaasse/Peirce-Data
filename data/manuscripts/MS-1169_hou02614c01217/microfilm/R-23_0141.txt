RELATIVE, a. Add: Relative beauty, beauty consisting in the adaptation of the object to its end.
Relative enunciation, one whose clauses are connected by a relation; as, "where the carcase is,
there will the eagles be." Relative ground of proof, a premise which itself requires proof.
Relative opposites, the two terms of any dual relation. Relative place, place as def[del]i[/del][ins]i[/ins]ned by
the situations of other opjects. Relative pleasure or pain, a state of feeling which is pleasure
able or bainful by force of contrast with the state which preceded it. Relative syllogism, one
whose major premise is a relative enunciation; as,
Where Christ is, there will also the faithful be;
But Christ is in heaven;
Therefore there also will the faithful be.

are of thes nature.

REMEMBRANCE, n. For the quotation from Locke, put:

The perception which actually accompanies, and is annexed to any impression on the body,
made by an external object, being distinct from all other modifications of thinking, furnishes the
mind with a distinct idea, which we call sensation; which is as it were the actual entrance of
any idea into the understanding by the senses. The same idea, when it again recurs without the
operation of he like object on the external sensory, is remembrance. Locke, Essay concerning
Humane Understanding, Bk. II. Chap. xix, § 1.

When the word perception is used properly and without any figure, it is never applied to
things past. And thus it is distinguished from remembrance. Reid, Intellectual Powers, Essay I
Chap. i.
