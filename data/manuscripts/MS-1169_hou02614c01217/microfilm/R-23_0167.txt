TAURUS PONIATOVII. The bull of Ponitowski, a constellation made by the Abbé Poczobut in 1777, in honor of the last king of Poland. It was situated over the shield of Sobieski between the east shoulder of Ophiuchus and the eagle containing most of the hyades. The constellation is no longer in use.

PLEIAD. I think, on the whole, it is best to refer from the names of the single pleiades to the word PLEIAD, where a figure of the whole group should be given and the necessary explanations made. Therefore under ALCYONE ASTEROPE; ATLAS; CELENO; ELECTRA; ELECTRA MAIA; MEROPE; PLEIONE; TAYGETA put A star, one of the group of the pleiades, see PLEIAD. And for the whole group, put the following:
PLEIAD [Gr. pleias, in Homer] One of a group of stars which is very conspicuous in the northern hemisphere on winter evenings. It is about 23 degrees north of the equator, and comes to the meridian at midnight in the middle of November. For some unknown reason, there were anciently said to be seven pleiads, although only six were conspicuous, then as now. Hence, the suggestion of a lost pleiad. In mythology, the pleiads were said to be the daughters of Atlas and Peione, and were named Alcyone, Merope, Celaeno, Electra, Sterope or Asterope, Taygete, and Maia. These names, together with those of the parents, have been applied by modern astronomers since Riccioli, A.D. 1665, to the brightest stars of the group, as follows: Four of the brightest stars are at the corners of a trapezoid, with one in the base near the star at the northern angle; and one outside the trapezoid, like a handle to a dipper. Alcyone, the brightest of the group, is a greenish star of magnitude 3.0 at the east end of the base of the trapezoid; it is eta Tauri. Electra is a very White star of magnitude 3.8 at the westernmost corner of the trapezoid on the short side opposite the base. Taygeta is a yellowish star of magnitude 4.4 at the northern corner on the base. Merope is a yellowish star of magnitude 4.2 at the southernmost corner, not on the base. Maia is a yellowish star of magnitude 4.0 on the base of the trapezoid, close to the northern angle but not in it. Asterope is a star of magnitude 5.7, not very conspicuous forming an equilateral triangle with Taygeta and Maia and lying outside of the trapezoid. Celaeno is a star of magnitude 5.2 halfway between Electra and Taygeta just a little outside the western slanting side of the trapezoid. Atlas is a yellowish star of magnitude 3.8, the second or third brightest in the group, which lies out of the trapezoid, considerably to the east, as in the handle of the dipper. Pleione is a star of magnitude 5.1 a little north of Atlas. [Insert the citations given by me in my previous article.]

TEGMINE. [Blunder by which the ablative is put for the nominative tegmen, in copying Bayer's remark that Avienus so called the star.] A very yellow star of magnitude 4.7 below the after claws of the crab, zeta Cancris.

TELESCOPIUM. A southern constellation, introduced by La Caille in 1752. It contains one star of the fourth magnitude.

TELESCOPIUM HERSCHELII. A constellation inserted by the Abbé Hell in 1789 between Lynx, Auriga, and Gemini. It is now disused.
