SAGITTARIUS. An ancient constellation and sign of the zodiac, the shooter, representing a satyr
or centaur drawing a bow.

SCEPTUM BRANDENBURGICUM. A constellation, the sceptre of Brandenburg, created by Gottfried Kirsch
the first astronomer of the Royal Prussian Society of Sciences in 1688. It consisted of four stars
lying in a straight line, in the first bend of Eridanus west of the hare. The constellation was
used by Bode early in this century, but is now obsolete.

SCEPTUM ET MANUS JUSTICIAE. A constellation made in 1679 by Royer in honor of Louis XIV. The con-
stellation has been displaced by Lacerta.

SCHEAT [sheet] 1. [Ar. sâ'id, arm] A very yellow star of the 2.6 magnitude on the right shoulder
and the root of the leg of Pegasus, beta Pegasi. 2. [Ar. sâk, shin] A yellowish star of magnitude
3.4, the southern of two in the right knee of Aquarius, delta Aquarii. This star is south preced
ing the other Sheat.

SCHEDIR. [Ar. sadr, breast] A very yellow star, varying between magnitudes 2.2 and 2.8 in an ir-
regular period, and situated on the chest of Cassiepea, alpha Cassiepeae.

SHOMLEK. [Ar. al-shaula, the tail] A star of magnitude 1.9 the following of two in the sting of
the Scorpion, Lambda scorpii.

SCUTUM SOBIESCIANUM. A constellation made by Hevelius late in the XVIIth century and representing
the shield of the king of Poland Johann Sobieski, with a cross upon it to signify that he had
fought for the Christian religion at the seige of Vienna. It lies in the brightest part of the
milky way in the over the bow of Sagittarius. Its brightest star is of the fourth magnitude.

SCUTULUM [L. a little shield, tr. of Gr. aspidiske, a name applied by Ptolemy to another star, con
fused with this.] A very green star of magnitude 2.9 the following of two in the highest point
of the poop [akrostolion] of the ship, iota Argus.

SEPTENTRIONES [L. from septem triones or teriones, the seven dhaught oxen or threshing oxen]
The seven stars of the great bear; also the seven stars of the little bear. Septentrio came in La
tin to mean the north pole of the heavens, and septentrional means northern.

SERPENS. An ancient northern constellation, the serpent, represented as an enormous boa, held in
the hands of Ophiuchus, or the serpent-bearer. Its brightest star, cor serpentis, is of magnitude
2.7.
