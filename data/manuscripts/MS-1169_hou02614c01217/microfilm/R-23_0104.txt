TRIANGULATION, n. For the definition put:
The operation and result of [del]observing[/del] [ins]measuring[/ins] the angles of a network of triangles laid ou
out on the earth's surface by [del]occupying[/del] [ins]marking[/ins] their vertices. The triangulation usually proceeds from
a baseline the geographical positions of whose extremities are [del]previously[/del] ascertained, and by
trigonometrical calculations, called the reduction of the triangulation (commonly involving
a process of distributing the errors by least squares, called the adjustment of the triangu-
lation) the geographical positions of all the other vertices are calculated, assuming the figure
of the earth to be known. By the combination of the triangulations of different countries, the
figure of the earth is ascertained.

TRIGONOMETRICAL survey. This article is too long Substitute.
Trigonometrical survey, a survey by triangulation, the measurement of baselines, and as-
tronomical observations of latitude, longitude, and azimuth. A trigonometrical survey should be
followed by a planetable survey; but the latter operation is sometimes included in the term tri-
gonometrical survey.

TRIGONOMETRY. Too long. Substitute:
The mathematical doctrine of the relations of the magnitudes of the sides and angles of
triangles. Plane trigonometry treats of rectilinear triangles; spherical trigonometry of sphe-
rical triangles. [del]Modern[/del] Trigonometry also includes the general theory of singly periodic
functions, hence called trigonometrical functions, namely the sine cosine, tangent, cotangent,
secant, and cosecant.
