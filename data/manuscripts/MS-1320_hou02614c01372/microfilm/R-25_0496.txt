[u]Napoléon intime. Par Arthur Lévy. Paris: 1893.[/u]

Of all the men that ever have lived, Napoleon is certainly the one concerning whom there is the most published information. The general history of Europe during fifteen years is but a chapter of his biography. That history he has himself in great part set forth in the Bulletins of the Great Army, and has reviewed in the [u]Mémorial de Ste Hélène[/u]. His early ways of thinking are displayed in his "Dialogue [del]sur[/del] [ins]on[/ins] [del]le[/del] Love," his "Letter to Bottafuoco," his "Discours sur les vérités et les sentiments qu'il importe le plus d'inculquer aux hommes pour leur bonheur," and his "Souper de Beaucaire." The thoughts of his whole life-time are recorded in the 32 quarto volumes of his correspondence, which are said to contain about one third of all his [del]all[/del] extant letters.
