to be divided, quite irrationally and most unequally, into i, Crystallography;

But crystallography is rather an offshoot from chemistry,
which it furnishes few facts, but hardly a principle. It is highly
mathematical and depends also on elaterics. Biology might be
regarded (although as a matter of fact, no such view is taken)
as the chemistry of the albuminoids and of the forms they assume.
It is probable that all the differences of races, individuals, and tissues,
are chemical, at bottom. At any rate, the possible varieties of
albuminoids are [ins]amply[/ins] sufficient to account for all the diversity
of organic forms.

[del]Pure chemistry[/del] [ins]Chemistry[/ins] seems, at present, to consist of 1, Physical
Chemistry, consisting of the old chemical physics and the modern
chemical dynamics; 2, Organic Chemistry, aliphatic and
Aromatic; 3, Inorganic Chemistry, consisting of the doctrine of
the elements, their atomic weights, periodicity, etc., and the
doctrine of compounds.
