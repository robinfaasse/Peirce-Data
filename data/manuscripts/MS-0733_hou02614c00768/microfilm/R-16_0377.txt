[u]Logic Chapter 2[/u]

Intuition, or the immediately [del]present[/del] [ins]perceived[/ins], is that which is [del]to be[/del] held for true without any reason. There is no intuition except the nervous excitation which [del]constitutes[/del] [ins]is[/ins] the bare sensation. Sensation is a manifold. [del]To understand it,[/del] [ins]To be thought,[/ins] it must be reduced to the unity of consistency. All the hypothetical elements that are introduced, all the aspects under which it is regarded in order to reduce it to unity, are said to be mediately perceived, or conceptions. Thus, every conception is a hypothesis or supposition.

Abstraction, or precision is separation in conception. This is to be distinguished, therefore, first from separation in fact, as that of a ship from the shore; [ins]and[/ins] second from separation in the imagination, as when we call up an image of a man without eyes. It consists in attending to a part of what is imagined (which part is said to be prescinded) and in neglecting a part (which is said to be [del]neglected[/del] [ins]ignored[/ins]). It does not follow that because we can prescind A from B, that we can also prescind B from A. Thus, we can prescind space from colour, but we cannot prescind colour from space. The reason is that colour is immediately perceived, but space is a conception which it is necessary to introduce in order to think colour, that is, in order to unify the [del]sensations[/del] [ins]nervous excitation[/ins] of the eye.
