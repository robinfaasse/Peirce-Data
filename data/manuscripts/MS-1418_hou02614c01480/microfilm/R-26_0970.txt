[u]The Boy's Book of Inventions: Stories of the Wonders of Modern Science.[/u] By Ray Stannard Baker. New York: Doubleday and McClure Co. 1899 8[sup]o[/sup]. pp. 354.

[ins]vol 69 p 1790 / 317[/ins]

There is a fairly good book for boys. It describes nine of the inventions which have recently attracted attention, namely, Lake's submarine boat, liquid air, wireless telegraphy, automobiles, the Roentgen rays, modern kites, the phonograph, high buildings, and Langley's aerodrome. These are inventions of the most diverse characters, ranging from important scientific discoveries & methods of construction based on no new knowledge but only on new economic conditions, ranging from things whose principles every boy can comprehend as well as the outline of practical problems they involve to things whose "modus operandi" working must remain a mystery to the boy and whose practical value is not yet ascertained.
