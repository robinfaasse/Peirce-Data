What is the logical nature of such inferential triumphs
of new ideas. Take the overthrow of the early feeling that the
"truth", — or rather the proposition [del]one[/del] [ins]a man[/ins] ought to support was the
[del]the[/del] proposition he had previously wedded. Experience showed
that he couldn't do it. When all the world said it was a ridiculous
idea, his courage failed him and he yielded. He found a man
could not stand out against the world. So far, the process was
deduction. His original idea was

The proposition for a man to defend is the one he will stick to through thick & thin

But he found

A man can't stick through thick and thin to a personal whimsy
$\therefore$ No personal whimsy is a proposition for a man to defend.

This is an [del]argument[/del] [ins]syllogism[/ins] in [u]Celantes[/u], and a good example of that unusual mood.

But when he came to conclude that the proposition for a man to
defend was the one which was consonant to the authority of the Church,
this was hypothetic inference. The inference was

[del]Any proposition fit to be defended is one I can stick to through thick & thin[/del]
Any proposition backed by the authority of the Church I can so stick to
$\therefore$ Hence Any proposition so backed is a proposition fit to be defended.
