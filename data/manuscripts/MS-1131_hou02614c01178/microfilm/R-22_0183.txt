The first volume of the Census of 1870, which has just
been issued, contains a number of interesting and artistically ex-
ecuted maps some of which show the geographical distribu-
tion of the whole population, — of the blacks, of the Ger-
mans, of the Irish, of the English and Welsh (we regret that
there should have been put together), of the Scandinavians, of the
British Americans, and of the Chinese; while others of the
maps exhibit the distribution of wealth and of illiteracy. Such
an aid to comprehending the census is invaluable. Many points
are thus made manifest which would otherwise escape de-
tection. The map which shows the illiteracy in different parts
of the country affords a good example of this. In this there
are five different shadings showing the per centage of those
who cannot read and write. As might have been ex-
pected, the south looks pretty dark. But other things are
