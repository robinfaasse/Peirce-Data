December is a very stormy month. For a large part of the country, the year might, perhaps, be better divided into six seasons than into four. On the coast of Maine, where the winter is earlier and the spring later, the distinction between the stormy season of November and December and the sharp frosts of mid-winter are even more marked, but there is no more rain in the dog days than in June. Passing southward, on the other hand, to Carolina, Georgia, and Florida, the dog-days gradually assume the character of a tropical rainy season.
