The general philosophy of storms may be stated
as follows. All atmospheric disturbances are ulti-
mately due to [del]changes[/del] differences of temperature. Sup-
pose the ground at a certain place is heated. It heats the
air above it, and this air expands. In consequence of
this expansion the atmosphere reaches up higher above
that place. In consequence of this elevation of its level
it flows off from that space at the top. In consequence
of this flowing off, there is less air above that place,
and the pressure of the atmosphere is less. In consequence
of this diminished [ins]near[/ins] pressure [del]at[/del] the [del]bott[/del] surface of the
earth, the air flows in from other places where the [del]baro-[/del]
[del]me[/del] pressure is greater, and thus a wind [ins]toward the heated place[/ins] is produced.
So if a certain place is cooled, the air above it is cooled
and contracts and the top of the atmosphere there be-
comes lower. The air flows in, therefore, at the top so that
there is a greater weight of atmosphere over that place,
and a wind away from the cooled place springs up.
