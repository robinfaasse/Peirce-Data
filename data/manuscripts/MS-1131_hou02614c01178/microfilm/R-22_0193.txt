The cause of these differences between different seasons
must be elucidated as soon as a study is made of
the maps which are now being published by General
Myer, the Chief Signal Officer of the army. These maps
are issued three times a day and each contains a record
at a cordon of stations now amounting to seventy, of the
state of the sky, the height of the barometer, the tempera-
ture, the moisture of the atmosphere, and the force and
direction of the wind. The number and complexity of the
facts upon each map are somewhat confusing, but the
mind is assisted by lines which are drawn upon the
charts so as to indicate the shapes and intensities of the
atmospheric disturbances which are continually following
one another over the country. As a still further assistance
a general summary of the condition of the weather is
printed in the corner, with the probabilities for the next
