Let us [del]ded[/del] first deduce from the theorem the definition of the "aggregate" of census-numbers. If two aggregates have a common part, $M$, the separate parts being $L$ and $N$, the aggregate is also an aggregate of $L$, $M$, and $N$, and $C(L+M+N) = CL + CM + CN$. Therefore by the aggregate of $C(L+M)$ and $C(M+N)$ must be meant their sum less $CM$. Therefore, if a common part occurs $m$ times, $m-1$ times its census must be subtracted from the sum. Thus, to get the census of the surface of Fig. 3 we must add the census-numbers of the three surfaces, subtract the census numbers of the three common lines and add the census number of the point common to all three parts.
