With topics, however, the case is different. In this field of thought we still suppose objects to move about in Space. But we suppose that, at will, any of these objects can be made to expand, to contract, to bend, to twist, and in sort to move free from any law [del]or condition[/del], excepting only that it is nowhere to be broken or welded; [del]and th[/del] [ins]— or, to state the condition precisely, that no two parts of[/ins] [del]or limits of it,[/del] shall at one instant to occupy one and the same place and at another instant separate places. In the usual phrase of the mathematicians, we suppose the connection of parts to be undisturbed. Now this connection of parts, which is the sole law of topical movables, is a property of the very Space itself. Besides, the topicist holds himself at liberty to suppose even this law of connection to be broken, provided the violation [del]takes place[/del] [ins]be explicitly supposed to take place[/ins] on a definite occasion and by a defined motion. Now mathematics dealing as it does with purely hypothetical objects, can know no experiences or peculiar feelings. [del]But[/del] Space, therefore, for this science, can only be a general concept; [del]and the only general concept of Space we have, or can have, is as it is a law of certain changes of objects, namely, their motions.[/del] [ins]and the only general concept of Space we have, or can have, is as it is a law of [del]certain[/del] [ins]supposed[/ins] changes of objects, namely, their motions.[/ins] Topics is thus the only mathematics of pure Space that is possible.
