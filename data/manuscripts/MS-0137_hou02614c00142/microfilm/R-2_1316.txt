A [u]punctual[/u] [ins]marked[/ins] figure is a point or collection of points. A [u]linear figure[/u] is a line or lines with or without outlying points. A [u]superficial figure[/u] is a surface or surfaces with or without distinct lines and points.

A [del]punctual[/del] [ins]punctual[/ins] figure can have no singularity. The singularities of linear figures are points of furcation (from each of which a particle can move away [ins]in more than two ways[/ins] while remaining in the linear figure) extremities, and outlying points. The grade of singularity of an outlying point is -2, that of an extremity is -1, that of a furcation is two less than the number of branches that unite at that point. The total grade of singularity of a line figure is minus [del]twice[/del] [ins]the excess of[/ins] the number of its [del]entirely[/del] separate parts [ins]over the number of independent rings[/ins].
