Thus it is demonstrated that a step by step progress [ins]goes[/ins]
[del]entirely[/del] [ins]clear[/ins] through the series of ordinals, and as one may say
dehausts them ([u]not[/u] exhausts, for, as that word has been used
repeatedly in this paper, it would imply that the step by step
progress would be capable of going further had the series of
ordinals extended further.) It follows, at once, that the following
form of reasoning is valid for ordinals.

[del]The particular [ins]ordinal[/ins] [u]m[/u] is [del]of[/del] has the character Q;[/del]
The particular ordinal, [u]m[/u], has the character Q;
Whatever ordinal [u]m[/u] may be, if [u]m[/u] is Q, then the N of [u]m[/u] is Q;
[u]Ergo[/u], Whatever ordinal is of [u]M[/u] is Q.

A particular case of this valid form is the following:
0 is Q
Whatever ordinal [u]m[/u] may be, if [u]m[/u] is Q, then the N of [u]m[/u] is Q;
[u]Ergo[/u], Every ordinal is Q.
(Some steps in the reasoning, here skipped, are readily made out.)
