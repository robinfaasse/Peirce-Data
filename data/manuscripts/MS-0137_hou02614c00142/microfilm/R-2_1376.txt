A significant feature of the second definition is, that,
by Clause II, although, [del]p[/del] [ins]p[/ins] and q being any ordinals, p is not g
to q nor is q of g to p unless the one or the other relation be
logically necessitated, yet, no matter what ordinals p and q
may be, it will always happen either that p is g to q or that
q is g to p. We naturally ask, How, then, is the necessitation
brought about? We find, on close scrutiny, that the sole founda-
tions of it are, [del]that[/del] first,
that Every ordinal has one other that is N to it,
and, secondly,
that every ordinal, m, is g to m,
that the N to m is g to m,
that the N to the N to m is g to m,
that the N to the N to the N to m is g to m,
and, in short,
that the N to a g to m is g to m.
