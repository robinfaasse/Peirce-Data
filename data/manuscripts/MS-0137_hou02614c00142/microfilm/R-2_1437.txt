[u]Summits for four faces and six edges[/u]

Let us do a little sum in algebra. How many regions on a perspective plane can all touch one another. Let $x$ be the number required. Then each of the $x$ regions [del]will have[/del] [ins]between them[/ins] there will be $\frac{1}{2}x(x-1)$ edges and $\frac{1}{3}x(x-1)$ summits. Hence

[del]$x - \frac{1}{3}x(x-1) + \frac{1}{2}x(x-1) = \frac{1}{6}x^2 + \frac{5}{6}x = +1$[/del]
$x - \frac{1}{2}x(x-1) + \frac{1}{3}x(x-1) = -\frac{1}{6}x^2 + \frac{7}{6}x = +1$

of which the only useful solution is $x=6$. Fig. 7 shows the arrangement. [del]No[/del]

How many regions on an anchor-ring can all touch one another. The equation is

$-\frac{1}{6}x^2 + \frac{7}{6}x = 0$
