[pn]r' to A.[/pn] Nothing therefore negatives the possibility that every
'B is r' to an A, except the indefinite suspicion that the
Bs not r' to any As may be too multitudinous. Now if there
were a contradiction [ins]in supposing the[/ins] [del]to take place in any but[/del]
[del]the[/del] process of making Bs r' to As [del]had to take place in[/del]
[ins]one[/ins] particular way, say by successive installments, it might
be that the multitude was too great to be compassed in that way.
It would be one actuality exhausting another. But here there is
no such contradiction requiring the process to take place otherwise
than by the directly filling the Bs with As not otherwise needed.
Because by the express hypothesis, there always will be
a superfluity of As not otherwise needed, even after all
the Bs have been filled (that is, after we have [del]supposed[/del] [ins]supposed[/ins] that
every B is r' to some A). It is simply the question whether a
collection of individuals can be too multitudinous for a
general conception to embrace: if they be, how did we
get the conception of such a multitude?
