A punctual figure is a point or collection of points. A
linear figure is a colligation of lines and
points; and so on.

A punctual figure can have no singularity. The
singularities of a linear figure are furcations (from
each of which a particle can move on its line in more
than two ways) extremities, and outlying points. The
grade of singularity of an outlying point is $-2$, that of an
extremity is $-1$, that of a furcation is $2$ less than the
[del]total singularity of a line figure is minus twice the excess of the number of separate pieces over that of the independent[/del] [ins]number of ways by which a particle might leave it, thus[/ins]
