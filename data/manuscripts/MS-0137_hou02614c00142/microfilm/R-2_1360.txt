The [ins]formal[/ins] doctrine of Time which topics presupposes has nothing to do, [del]in the first place,[/del] with the feelings excited in our minds upon contemplating the flow of time, its frightful swiftness, its long-weariness, its preciousness, [ins]essential[/ins] [del]total[/del] though some of these are thought of by us as characteristics of time, they do not belong to purely objective Time; and if they did, they would be foreign to the Time of a pure mathematical hypothesis. [ins]in the second place[/ins] Nor has the pure formal doctrine of Time anything to do with those characters [ins]of Time[/ins] which are relative to human cognition, purposes, etc. Even common sense does not suppose that there is any difference between past time and future time except relatively to ourselves. Common sense no doubt does think that the relation of the subsequent to the previous is different [ins]in time itself[/ins] from the converse relation of the previous to the subsequent; but this is an illusion.
