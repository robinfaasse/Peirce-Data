Nothing could well be more contrary to
the facts of history than to make Descartes
[del]the author of the idea that God is infinite.[/del] It was
not only the doctrine of Plotinus, but also of
Damascius, who was an immense authority
for the schoolmen. Accordingly, the same doc-
trine will be found in Scotus who discusses
at great length not whether God is infinite, for
that he takes as axiomatical almost but whether
the infinite can be actual. See [u]Opus Oxon[/u] I. ii. q. 2.
"Perfectissimum ens est infinitum" See Ed. of 1560
Vol I p. 143 b. He returns to the matter IV. xlix. 1. Same Ed
Vol IV p. 392 b.

Afterward in modern times it became
one of the main points of Cardinal Cusa's
philosophy that God is infinite, although he
held that it is as Deus implicitus that he is
infinite and as Deus explicitus is finite.

It will be observed that all the old creeds
begin: I believe in God the Father Almighty.
Almighty is infinitely mighty.
