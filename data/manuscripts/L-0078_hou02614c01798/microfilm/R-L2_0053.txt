COLUMBIA UNIVERSITY
DIVISION OF
PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND ANTHROPOLOGY

Garrison-on-Hudson, N. Y., December [del]14[/del] [ins]12[/ins], 1903.

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

Professor Woodbridge will conduct [u]The Journal of[/u]
[u]Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods[/u], concerning
which I wrote you earlier in the week. I had always wanted
to make 'The Psychological Review' a 'Centralblatt' of this
character - the research work from the universities being
combined in archives - and my separation from Baldwin seemed
to offer an opportunity to carry out my plan. But I already
have enough to do in the way of editing; and as I find that
Professor Woodbridge is willing to undertake the work and
know that he will do it admirably, I am relinquishing it to
him. I am somewhat influenced by the fact that Professor
Baldwin objects to my editing such a journal. It was agreed
that I should be entirely free to start a new journal, and
President Hall, Professor Titchener and Professor Creighton
cordially welcome the plan of such a 'Centralblatt'; but much
to my surprise Professor Baldwin thinks it would interfere
with 'The Psychological Review'.

I thank you for your very kind reply to my letter.
I know that you will give Professor Woodbridge the same as-
sistance that you would have given me. He will doubtless
write to you.

Very truly yours,

[pn]J. McK. Cattell[/pn]

Mr. C. S. Peirce,
6 Prescott Hall,
Cambridge, Mass.
