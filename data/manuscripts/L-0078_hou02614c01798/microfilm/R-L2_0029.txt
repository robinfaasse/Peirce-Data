THE POPULAR SCIENCE MONTHLY
EDITORIAL DEPARTMENT.

Garrison-on-Hudson, N.Y. [date]May 13. 1900[/date]

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

Certainly I should be very glad indeed to have the ser-
ies of articles from you, If they are adapted as you say
they would be to people in a somewhat 'infantile stage of
intellectual development'. In regard to the great phil-
osophical and scientific value of the articles there is no
question.

The finances of the MONTHLY are, however, by no means
in a flourishing condition. It was conducted at a loss
of some $8,000 to $10,000 a year and was doubtless one of,
the causes of the failure of the Appleton's. We must
now do the best we can to make ends meet, otherwise the Jour-
nal must be given up which I really beleive is of great
importance to the cause of science. Under existing con-
ditions the publishers cannot afford to pay more than $25 for
an article. Though your articles might be arranged so
as to be somewhat shorter than 5000 to 6000 words.

If, however, your articles were once written they
might be published in book form and you would of course have
the usual royalty. If there were a good sale the receipts
