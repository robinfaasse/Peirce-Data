COLUMBIA UNIVERSITY
DIVISION OF
PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND ANTHROPOLOGY

Garrison-on-Hudson, N. Y. April 4, 1906


Dear Mr. Peirce:-

It would be in many ways pleasant if Royce would pre-
sent a paper on his logical researches at the approaching meet-
ing of the Academy. I am not, however, sure that he would
like to do this, if he knew that he was a candidate for election
at the time. I do not know whether he knows this or not,
but he may guess it from the fact that I got from him some time
since a list of his published papers. I see no reason why the
decision should not be left with him, if he fully understands
the circumstances.

I was very glad to have your letter in regard to the
membership of the academy and the classification of the sciences
which will interest the other members of the academy when they
see it. I hope that I may have the pleasure of talking over
these matters with you at the approaching meeting of the academy.

Very truly yours,

J McKeen Cattell


Mr. C. S. Peirce,
Arisbe,
Milford, Pa.


Dictated. Signed in Mr. Cattell's absence.
