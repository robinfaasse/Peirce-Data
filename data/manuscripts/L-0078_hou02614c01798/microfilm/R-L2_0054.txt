[date]December 15, 1903[/date]

Dear Professor Peirce:--

Your note of the 11th reaches me as I am on the point of taking a train for Washington, where I am called to give evidence in regard to a pending copy-right bill which I have had introduced. I am also receiving today a letter from Professor Cattell, in which he asks to be relieved of the editorial responsibility from the Science Series.

I am, therefore, at the moment without any scientific advisers. I do not myself possess the technical knowledge to pass upon such a scheme as that submitted by you for the volume now proposed, nor have I available on my staff any one whose counsel would carry weight for a subject so special as that here submitted. Being now deprived of the opportunity of taking counsel with Professor Cattell, I will ask to defer my report concerning your suggestion until I have had time (after my return from Washington) to arrange for the service of some other scientific adviser.

Regretting the unavoidable delay in passing upon the suggestion, I am

Yours very truly,
G. H. Putnam

Professor C. S. Peirce,
6 Prescott Hall,
Cambridge, Mass.
