COLUMBIA UNIVERSITY
DIVISION OF
PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND ANTHROPOLOGY

Garrison-on-Hudson, N. Y. [date]October 12, 1905[/date]

Dear Peirce:-

I am much obliged to you for your letter, and am much interested in what you say in regard to Royce. I am not competent to form an opinion on his work in mathematics, but I have assumed that this is of value. He has done work in psychology that appears to me to be good, including at least two experimental researches - one of these, a study of invention, was published in [u]The Psychological Review[/u] about six years ago and will, I think, be the starting point of further work. In advocating the election of Professor Royce to the Academy, I am, however, doubtless influenced by his philosophical brilliancy. His writings appear to me diffuse and I am very far from agreeing with them, but on the whole I think they may fairly [ins]be[/ins] taken into account in an election to the academy. I have a nomination blank signed by James, Newcomb and myself, and shall be very glad if you can sign it at the New Haven meeting. I shall look forward to seeing you then, and also to the publication of your work on the [u]logic of vague conceptions.[/u]

Very truly yours,

J. McK. Cattell

Mr. C. S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.
