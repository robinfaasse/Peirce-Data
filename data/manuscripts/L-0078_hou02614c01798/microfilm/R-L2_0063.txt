THE POPULAR SCIENCE MONTHLY [fig]checkmark[/fig]
EDITORIAL DEPARTMENT

Garrison-on-Hudson, N.Y. [date]October 8, 1904[/date]

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

It is certainly one of the chief drawbacks to the advancement of science that scientific work, whether research or articles intended to advance the interests of science, can not be directly paid for, which is the case in nearly all other professions. A scientific man must be a professor or be in the government service or something [ins]of[/ins] the sort to earn his living, and is in a way not a professional man of science at all, but only an amateur. I trust that there will be a change in these conditions in the future, but this does not help us in the present. As I said in my letter, [del]w[/del]hich was sent to our leading men of science, THE POPULAR SCIENCE MONTHLY was published at a very considerable loss by the Appletons, and it was only possible to continue it by paying inadequately or not at all for contributions. I quite understand that when you earn your living by writing, it is impossible for you to contribute under these circumstances, and this I deeply regret.

Very truly yours,

J. McK. Cattell

Mr. C. S Peirce,
'Arisbe',
Milford, Pa.
