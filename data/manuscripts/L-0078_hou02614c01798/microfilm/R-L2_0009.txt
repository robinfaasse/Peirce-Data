THE SCIENCE SERIES
G. P. PUTNAM'S SONS
NEW YORK & LONDON
——
OFFICE OF THE EDITOR

Garrison-on-Hudson, N.Y. [date]February 26. 1898[/date]

Dear Mr. Pierce :-

I have not replied to your letter regarding the volume on the 'History of Science' in part because I was waiting for the publishers to do this. I presume that they have done so, though I have not definitely heard from them to this effect and they have not returned your letter to me.

The books are expected to be about 100[del]0[/del],000 words in length. It seems to me not impossible to write a book on the History of Science that would be of very great value to those not familiar with the subject, within these limits, though in this case it would be necessary to ignore much. It would also be possible to divide the subject into two parts, either chronologically or by subject-matter, and unless the publishers actually lost on the first volume they would be glad to proceed with the publication of farther volumes [del]if necessary[/del].

Greatly appreciating the value of this volume to the SERIES.

Very truly yours,

J. McK. Cattell

Mr. C. S. Pierce
Milford, Pa.
