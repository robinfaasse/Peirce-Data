Garrison-on-Hudson, N.Y. [date]January 7, 1911[/date]

Dear Peirce:-

I am sending you an editorial copy of the Biographical Directory, in case it or the articles printed as an appendix may be of any interest to you. Perhaps you will be able to write a note on it for the Nationm but this is not a matter of consequence.

Your letters have given me much apprehension, and I wish that it were possible for me to do any thing that would help you in your work. I have always regarded you as one of the ablest men that this country has produced, and I deeply regret that circumstances have prevented you from publishing the work you have planned and in part prepared. I trust that nothing will interfere with the completion of the system of logic, which should be a most important contribution to philosophy and science.

I trust that Mrs. Peirce is now somewhat better. Please give her my kind regards. Do not hesitate to tell me if at any time I can be of service to you.

Very truly yours,

J. McKeen Cattell

Dr. C. S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.
