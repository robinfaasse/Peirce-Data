[u]Principle III. Of the Enclosures.[/u] A scroll is a line, distinguished from a line of connexion by being drawn lightly or in some other way, which [del]makes[/del] returns into itself after making two complete circuits, [del]with[/del] once cutting itself. There are thus two parts of the scroll each of which describes a circuit from the node to the node, and is called an [u]enclosure.[/u]

The scroll never cuts a spot [ins]nor another scroll,[/ins] and may, for the present, be considered as [del]separating[/del] [ins]dividing[/ins] the entire graph into three partial graphs; one, [ins](say [u]a[/u]),[/ins] outside both enclosures; one, say [u]b[/u], inside the outer enclosure but outside the inner one; and one, say [u]c[/u], inside both enclosures.
