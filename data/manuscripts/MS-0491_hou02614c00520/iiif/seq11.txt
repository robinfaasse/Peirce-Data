    The essential difference between term, proposition,
and argument, is that they are signs once, twice, and
three times over respectively. Thus, the definitions
given suggest the question whether or not it is pos-
sible for a symbol specially to show its intended in-
terpretant without indicating its object. If we try to pro-
duce such a symbol, we shall get something like this:
“If the multitude of atoms is such that any finite
whole number being given, a collection of atoms of
that number exists, then the entire collection of 
atoms is too great for any finite number whatever
to count.” But this is evidently nothing but a con-
ditional proposition of which the consequent describes
a state of things while the antecedent separately
indicates what state of things it is that is so described.
