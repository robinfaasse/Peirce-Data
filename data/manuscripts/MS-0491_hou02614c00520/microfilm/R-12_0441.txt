The scroll has no effect upon the outermost graph, [u]a[/u], which is independently asserted. But it is to be understood as cutting off its contents from the sheet, the outer enclosure cutting off [u]b[/u] absolutely, but the inner enclosure so far restoring [u]c[/u], that it is asserted conditionally upon [del]the truth of[/del] [ins][u]b[/u]; so that the scroll[/ins] and its contents may be read "If [u]b[/u] is true, [u]c[/u] is true," this conditional proposition being understood [del]de inesse[/del], that is, in the sense that either [u]b[/u] is false or [u]c[/u] is true.

If in place of [u]c[/u], a pseudograph be substituted, such as "What is false is true," the whole may be read: "If [u]b[/u] is true the false is true." This reduces [u]b[/u] to absurdity, and is equivalent to a denial of [u]b[/u]. In such case, to simplify the writing, the inner enclosure maybe made indefinitely small, or be suppressed; so that Fig. 2 denies [u]b[/u]; and generally, a single [fig]circle containing b[/fig] enclosure has the effect of denying the whole graph which it contains.
