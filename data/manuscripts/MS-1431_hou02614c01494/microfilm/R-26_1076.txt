The kinetical theory of gases is without doubt the great
triumph of that corpuscular philosophy which endeavors
to explain the universe by turning the splendid tapestry
wrong side out and showing us that it is all nothing but
bits of matter moving under the three laws of motion
together with their attractions and repulsions. The
artist who designs a tapestry [ins]perhaps[/ins] conceives that there is
something more to it than the weaving of it. But, apart
from that, the doctrine, as an ultimate explanation
even of that which it contemplates explaining was doomed
from the moment when the seed of evolutionism was
dropped into the intellectual soil, since it provides
no possible way in which the state of things which
it supposes, the molecules and their attractions, could
have come about, — not to speak of the laws of motion
themselves, with Space and Time, the semi-rationality
of which calls loudly for further elucidation. This
defect of the doctrine, however, does not prevent it from
