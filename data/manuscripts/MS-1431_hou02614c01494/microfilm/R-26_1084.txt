The kinetical theory of gases is unquestionably the greatest
triumph of that corpuscular philosophy which endeavors to
explain the universe by supposing discrete particles to have
fixed attractions and repulsions for one another at different
distances and to move under the three laws of motion; and
although that philosophy, as an ultimate one, was [ins]perhaps[/ins] doomed
from the moment that the seed of evolutionism was dropped
into the intellectual soil, yet it does not follow that it was
wrong or illogical, as a philosophy. For, as a philosophy, it
only endeavors to explain so much as [del]it[/del] may turn out expli-
cable by the elements of its hypothesis. Now it is a sound rule,
scientific procedure that we ought to begin with the most
thoroughgoing and uncomplicated hypothesis of manageable
elements so as to learn from its shortcomings what modification
to make in it. The number of phenomena of gases which
