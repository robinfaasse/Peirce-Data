Then, there are the applied sciences, astronomy
first, then geology, and others, and finally history,
which is the latest conquest of science, lying on
a contested borderland. Then, there are the sci-
entific arts, branches of engineering, which are
almost, if not altogether sciences. All these
branches are penetrated with the spirit of science;
and those who prosecute them feel that they are
neighbors and kinsmen.

On the other hand, aloof from science, and hostile
to it, stand law and politics, theology, art and litera-
ture, and the money-making crafts. When I say these
branches are hostile to science, I simply record the
observed phenomenon. They are, however, less unfriendly
to science, than science is to them. Thus, the lawyer
with no particular purpose to serve is not very disrespect-
ful of science; but the scientific man jeers at the
lawyer's method of establishing a fact. The clergy,
again, are great readers of the [del]more popular[/del] [ins]less[/ins]
technical of the scientific books; but you will not
find many scientific men with any great penchant
for [del]the[/del] books of theology. Artists and literary men,
on the other hand, frequently hold science in detes-
tation; although scientific men have most commonly
some love of art.
