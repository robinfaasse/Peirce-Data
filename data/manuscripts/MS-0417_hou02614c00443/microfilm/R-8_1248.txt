The symbol [fig]plus-like symbol[/fig] which resembles a plus sign, but
is plainly not that, will be taken as the sign of logical
aggregation. Namely,
[fig]a plus-like symbol b[/fig]
will be that proposition which is true if either of the propo-
sitions [u]a[/u] or [u]b[/u] is true, and is false only if both are false.
It may generally be read [u]vel[/u], or "or". Some persons,
wrongly as I think, maintain the English "or", like the
Latin [u]aut[/u], makes one alternative exclude the other.
But surely, nobody can maintain that this is
the case with [u]vel[/u]. "Give me my goods [u]or[/u] the
money," Is that a refusal to take both?

At any rate, whatever the usage of ordinary
speech may be, the sign [fig]plus-like symbol[/fig], conveniently read [u]vel[/u],
means one, or the other, or both.

The complex proposition [u]a[/u] [fig]plus-like symbol[/fig] [u]b[/u] will be termed the
aggregate, and its parts [u]a[/u] and [u]b[/u], the aggregants.
