$$\underline{x}\cdot\underline{y} + \underline{x}\cdot\underline{z},$$
$$\therefore \underline{x} + \underline{x}\cdot\underline{z},$$
$$\therefore \underline{x} + \underline{x},$$
$$\therefore \underline{x}.$$

And at the same time
$$\underline{x}\cdot\underline{y} + \underline{x}\cdot\underline{z},$$
$$\therefore \underline{y} + \underline{x}\cdot\underline{z},$$
$$\therefore \underline{y} + \underline{z}.$$

But since
$$\underline{x},$$
$$\underline{y} + \underline{z},$$
we have
$$\underline{x}\cdot(\underline{y} + \underline{z}).$$

We have thus proved, on the one hand the
validity of the inference
$$\underline{x}\cdot(\underline{y} + \underline{z})$$
$$\therefore \underline{x}\cdot\underline{y} + \underline{x}\cdot\underline{z},$$
and, on the other hand, the validity of the
converse inference
$$\underline{x}\cdot\underline{y} + \underline{x}\cdot\underline{z}$$
$$\therefore \underline{x}\cdot(\underline{y} + \underline{z})$$
and these two together constitute our meaning
when we write
$$\underline{x}\cdot(\underline{y} + \underline{z}) = \underline{x}\cdot\underline{y} + \underline{x}\cdot\underline{z}\text{ *}.$$
