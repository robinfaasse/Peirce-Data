$$\overline{\text{t}}\,\Psi\,\overline{\overline{\text{n}}\,\Psi\,\text{f}}\text{;}$$
for the sign of aggregation is to be regarded as
the negative of the sign of composition. This is, in
fact, the fourth rule.

Let me repeat for the hundredth time that the
"if" is here taken in a peculiar "ut nunc" sense,
in which it will frequently clash with all rational
use of ordinary speech. Thus,
$$\overline{\overline{\text{t}}\,\Psi\,\text{n}}\,\Psi\,\text{f}\text{,}$$
or if from Tom's testimony being true Nick's being
a knave follows then Fanny is a foolish flibbertigibbet.
