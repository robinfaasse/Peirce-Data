She further [del]assumes[/del] [ins]adopts[/ins] a functional symbol
$\log \underline{x}$. She [del]writes[/del] [ins]writes[/ins]
[del]By means of these two symbols, writing[/del] as usual,
$\log^2 \underline{x}$ for $\log \log \underline{x}$,
$\log^3 \underline{x}$ for $\log \log^2 \underline{x}$,
etc.,
and $\log^{-1} \underline{x}$, so that in general
$\log \log^n \underline{x} = \log^{n+1} \underline{x}$
where the $+$ in the exponent is the $+$ of integral
arithmetic. She then defines an infinite series of
operational symbols $+_1, +_2, +_3$, etc., as well as
$+_{-1}, +_{-2}$, etc. by the equation

$\log (\underline{a} \underset{n+1}{+} \underline{b}) = \log \underline{a} \underset{n}{+} \log \underline{b}$,
or
$\log^n (\underline{a} \underset{n}{+} \underline{b}) = \log^n \underline{a} \underset{0}{+} \log^n \underline{b}$.

Of course, a special relation must subsist between
the symbol "$\log$" and the symbol $\underset{0}{+}$ in order
that $\underset{1}{+}$ should be distributive with reference to $\underset{0}{+}$
as multiplication is distributive with reference to
addition. Supposing that to be the case, however, then
every operation $\underset{n}{+}$ is distributive with reference to $\underset{n-1}{+}$.
