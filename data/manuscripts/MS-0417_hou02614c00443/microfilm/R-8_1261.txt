It is required to eliminate yellow, and to express
the conclusion in terms of green (by W. B. Grove.)

Let $w$ mean The piece [ins]spoken of[/ins] is striped with white,
$g$ " " " " [ins]green[/ins] [del]with yellow[/del],
$b$ " " " " with black,
$y$ " " " " with yellow,
$r$ " " " " with red and orange,
$c$ [del]b[/del] [del]y[/del] " " " " with blue.

The first observation consists really of two; first, that
no matter what piece be taken
$\bar{w} + \bar{g} + b \cdot y$
Second, that no matter what piece be taken
$\bar{y} + \bar{b} + w \cdot g$
The second observation has likewise two parts; first,
that
$\bar{r} + c \cdot y$
and second that
$\bar{c} + \bar{y} + r$

The first premise gives us by rule 3
$(\bar{w} + \bar{g} + b \cdot y) (\bar{w} + \bar{g} + b \cdot y)$
and by rule 2 $(\bar{w} + \bar{g} + b) (\bar{w} + \bar{g} + y)$
