[u]Chapter XI.[/u]

[u]The Boolean Calculus.[/u]

§160. [del]The logician calls in the mathematician, just as the physicist, or the statist does. Give a mathematician a hypothetical state of things, and he will work out its necessary consequences; that is his business. Those who restrict the mathematician to problems of quantity should ask whether problems of quantity do or do not involve all other problems as simpler cases. This is not a question to be answered off hand; but those who restrict mathematics to quantity virtually do so answer it, and unluckily answer it the wrong way.[/del]

The logician calls in the mathematician and the mathematician when he is called in, will go to work in his own way. He will make use of [ins]the idea of[/ins] number or not, as he pleases. [del]The[/del] [ins]One[/ins] advantage of doing so is that he has the calculus of numbers ready made to his hand.
