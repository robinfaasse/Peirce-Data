To return to the consideration of just what pragmatism is, every
experiment obviously has two parts, in the first of which the experi-
menter, having some general hypothesis in his mind whose consequence
he wishes to verify or refute in a particular instance, directly acts upon
something so as to really to modify it, while in the second part, the
world (positively or negatively) reacts upon him in an experience. It
is not everything that is susceptible of being directly acted upon, but
only what has actual and protracted existence. Such a thing may be
called an [u]existent[/u]. The reaction of nature in the second part of the
experiment consists in producing some positive [ins]sensational[/ins] effect.
