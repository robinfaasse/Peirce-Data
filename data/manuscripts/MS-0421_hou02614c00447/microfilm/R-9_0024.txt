[ [del]Hoppe[/del] reverses the law of Kant, and maintains that the wider the concept the greater its content. His idea, translated into Aristoteleand phraseology, is this. He admits the second antepredicamental rule, that the differences of different genera are different. (This, of itself, removes him widely from logicians for whom the distinction of comprehension and extension is the turning-point of logic.) [del]Negro[/del] is not a conception formed by the union of the two concepts [u]man[/u] and [u]black[/u], but the peculiar [del]of[/del] differences of [u]negro[/u] belong to negroes alone of all beings. This naturally carries him a step further; and he says the difference is of itself sufficient to constitute the pure concept, so that the genus is not an essential predicate. Thirdly, he finds that the characters of the narrower difference are less important ([u]wirkungsreich[/u]) than those of the higher, and to have less important consequences is to have a smaller measure of predicates. — [date]1893[/date]]
