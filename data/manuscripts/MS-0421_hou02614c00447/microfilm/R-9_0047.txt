[del]531.[/del] The Incognizable, or Unknowable, may be taken or
pretended to be taken, in several different senses. In
the first place, one may talk of qualities absolutely
inconceivable. It has often been supposed that a
man blind from birth would be unable to form the
least idea of redness. It is quite possible that some
animals may have had feelings as different from
any of those we know as color is from grief. Now,
though it cannot be contended that [ins]there are[/ins] [del]any[/del] feeling-quali-
ties [del]that cannot be felt[/del], it may be fancied that
there is something in some vague way similar to a
quality which no enlargement of our faculties could,
from the nature of that quasi-quality, enable us to
apprehend. In the second place, one may talk of things
absolutely unknowable. [del]Some of[/del] The English school of
