In the [u]Critic of the Pure Reason[/u], at least, there is no padding.
Nothing is over-stated, under-stated, or mis-stated. Nothing is out
of its proper place. Since most sections of the book have to be
[del]often[/del] many times re-read and deeply pondered, its reader comes
to be very grateful for its style. Still, nobody would say that the
style was artistic or exhibited delicacy of feeling. Kant is
said to have been an agreeable conversationalist. But in order
to show that he was below the average on his [del]generous[/del] [ins]primitive[/ins] side,
it is sufficient to mention that although physical geography
was one of his chief studies, he never saw a mountain, and
probably not the neighboring sea.
