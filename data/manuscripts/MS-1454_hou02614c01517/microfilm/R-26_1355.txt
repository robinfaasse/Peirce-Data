The world-shattering Kant had absolutely no element of greatness but his logical subtlety, — of all powers the one which seems to have smallness as a part of its essence. Nor was his subtlety sufficient to enable him to solve a single problem quite rightly; few will maintain that it was. Yet what mark he has engraved upon the human mind, like a deep glacier-cutting upon a rock!

Professor Paulsen opines that Kant might have become one of the fine writers of Germany. We can only say that the reasons he gives are most persuasive, most persuasive of the contrary.
