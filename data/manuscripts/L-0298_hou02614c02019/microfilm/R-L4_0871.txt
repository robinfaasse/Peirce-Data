I believe that [u]carbonic acid has never before been used[/u]
[u]to sour bleach[/u]; and if so a patent could probably be based upon
this; although the fact that CO₂ does under certain circumstances,
not accurately known, decompose hypochlorites is a familiar one
to chemists.

This souring of the bleach by carbonic acid is one of
the points of the process requiring further study. It is a some-
what slow operation; and is probably influential when the coloring
matter is left over night in the vat.

The bleach, or hypochlorite of soda which is formed by
the action of the chlorine upon the alkali, is itself the subject
of electrolysis, by which it is decomposed into sodium at one pole
and ClO at the other pole.

The [u]electrolytic decomposition or souring of bleach[/u] is a
reaction which should, I think, be made the basis of a patent.

The sodium, as before, decomposes water to form caustic
soda, while the action of the ClO must be a matter of serious in-
vestigation. One atom of it would certainly be as effective as two
atoms of chlorine in bleaching, and possibly still more so.
