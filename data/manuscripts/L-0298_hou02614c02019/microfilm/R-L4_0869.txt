Now, the conductivity of a weak solution of chloride of sodium is
about 1 1/2 that of a solution of the same percentage of neutral
sodium carbonate, but is only about 1/3 that of a solution of
equal strength of hydrate of soda (caustic soda). Consequently,
when a small quantity of carbonate of soda is added to a much
larger amount of common salt, and the mixed solution is subjected
to electrolysis, the action is mainly upon the common salt, and
very little of the carbonate is electrolyzed. But when caustic
soda is employed, the proportion of common salt electrolyzed is
considerably reduced.

Another well-established law of electrolysis is that when
a simple salt or hydrate is decomposed in solution, it is decom-
posed into the same constituents as when it is decomposed in the
melted state. That is, the common salt is decomposed into sodium
at the negative pole, or cathode, and chlorine at the positive
pole, or anode. The liberated sodium immediately decomposes the
water and forms hydrate of soda. The chlorine will at ordinary
