Buckingham Hotel, New York,

1892, June 16.

Thomas J. Montgomery, Esq.,

Dear Sir:-

The most essential feature of the Woolf process of bleaching, which must belong to any process which could rival that one, does not seem to have been explicitly claimed in any patents and is evidently not used in the Hermite process. This feature is that the coloring matter is decomposed by direct electrolysis, and not merely by the chemical action upon it of some product of electrolysis.

In order to assure oneself that this is the fact in regard to the Woolf process, it is only necessary to consider what takes place in all electrolysis, and to remark certain very obvious phenomena of the process under investigation. Every electrolyte, or body capable of being decomposed by direct action of the electric current, is composed of an electronegative and an electropositive constituent, present in equivalent proportion.
