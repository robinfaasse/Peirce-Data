Dear Sir:

In Woolf's ordinary process, as far as I am informed, (for I have as yet merely gone on information), a solution of three per cent chloride of sodium is mixed with a small quantity of caustic soda, or better of neutral carbonate of soda. I propose now to show essentially what takes place in the electrolysis of this solution.

The constitution of salt-solutions is a vexed question upon which dozens of the ablest chemists in the world have been at work for many years without reaching any certain conclusion. But according to the generally accepted theory of Arrhenius a salt in solution, especially in dilute solution, is to a considerable extent dissociated into its ions. The solution of chloride of sodium will, according to this, contain free atoms of chlorine and of sodium, the solution of carbonate of soda will contain free radicals of CO₃.
