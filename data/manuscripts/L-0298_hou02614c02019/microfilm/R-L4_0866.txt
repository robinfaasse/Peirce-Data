directly electrical bleaching, we electrolyze the solution so as to
make chlorine or bleach of any kind, which afterwards acts chemically.

In this chemical action, heat is disengaged in considerable quanti-
ties; for this is the case with all chemical actions excepting a few
which are very peculiar. This heat represents a large amount of me-
chanical energy which has been expended in producing the chlorine or
the bleach in excess of what was required to produce the final result
direct. All of this goes to waste.

Thus, it is perfectly clear that economy of bleaching re-
quires us to work as far as possible by direct electrolysis. This
shows us, at once, the directions in which we are to look for econo-
my and the directions in which it is not to be expected. Namely,
