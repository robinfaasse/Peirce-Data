This hypochlorite of soda is the bleach of Javelle water.

We see at once one important object subserved by the stirring, or "agitation between the electrodes", which Mr. Woolf has found so advantageous. Namely, it brings the chlorine into immediate contact with the soda which is formed at the opposite pole, so as to make the bleach.

We also see that in the formation of the bleach half of the work of the current goes to waste, through the reformation of chloride of sodium.

If caustic soda has been added to the solution, this is also decomposed by the current to three times its proportionate amount, producing sodium and the constituents of peroxide of hydrogen. The sodium immediately decomposes water; and at the other pole it remains to investigate what occurs, although it does not seem likely that anything happens except the formation of water with the evolution of oxygen. If this is so, the electrolysis of the caustic soda is a mere waste of energy, and consequently the amount used should be reduced as far as possible.
