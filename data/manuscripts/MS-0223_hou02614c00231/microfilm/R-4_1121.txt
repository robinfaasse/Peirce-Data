§471 To find the signification of $d\rho/ds$
For since a const. added to $\rho$ can make
no difference, assume $\rho_0 = 0$
and since the axes maybe turned any
way assume $d\rho = dx \cdot i$ Then $dx = ds$;
and $d\rho/ds = i$, i.e. means what Gibbs
says.

Next, what does $d^2\rho/ds^2$ mean?
Add such a constant vector to $\rho$ [del]that[/del] [ins]so[/ins] [del]it[/del]
[del]turn the axes so[/del] that the fixed origin is the
centre of curvature, the radius of curvature
being $r$ and turn the axes so that $\rho_0 = r i$
and $d\rho/ds = j$. Then if $\theta$ be such an
infinitesimal angle that $ds = r \theta$ we shall
have
$\rho_{-1} = r \cos \theta\ i - r \sin \theta\ j$
$\rho_0 = r \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ i$
$\rho_{+1} = r \cos \theta\ i + r \sin \theta\ j$

[del]If[/del]
$d^2\rho = 2r(\cos \theta - 1)i = -r \theta^2 i = -ds^2/r\ i$.
$d^2\rho/ds^2 = -\frac{1}{r} i$ as Gibbs says.

Next, What does $\frac{d\rho}{ds} \times \frac{d^2\rho}{ds^2}$ mean?
Under the last arrangement of axes
$\frac{d\rho}{ds} = i$  $\frac{d^2\rho}{ds^2} = -\frac{1}{r} i$  $\frac{d\rho}{ds} \times \frac{d^2\rho}{ds^2} = \frac{1}{r} k$
as Gibbs says.
