[u]SOME DIVISIONS OF SIGNS.[/u]

I shall first consider how signs may differ as to the modes of being of the [del]signs[/del] [ins]objects[/ins] themselves. An [del]object[/del] [ins]thing[/ins] may be entirely unreal, and yet may be regarded as a sign. I question, however whether [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] figment can really be itself a sign. I incline to think that in such a case there is a real idea of an unreal object, and that it is only this idea, and not the unreal object itself, that can really be a sign. The distinction that I propose to notice is between signs that are real but whose mode of being is different.

Consider, for instance, the different kinds of objects that are called "words". An editor will say to one of his staff of writers, "I wish you to bring me [del]a[/del] [ins]an[/ins] MS on such and such a subject of so many thousand words." He here means by a word an actually existent [del]series[/del] [ins]session[/ins] of significant series of letters separated from others by blank spaces before and after it but not itself separated into parts by any blank space.
