[del]In that case where the light is already polarized
the intensity of the light is proportional to the square
of the electric force in it and the components
along the principal directions [del]of the[/del] [ins]in the[/ins] medium are
accordingly
[fig]figure[/fig]
E cos θ  and  E sin θ
The intensities along these directions are
therefore E² cos² θ and E² sin² θ . If these
travel with different velocities there will be
a phase difference δ between them, and the 
combined intensity will be E² cos² θ + E² sin² θ + 2 E² sin θ cos θ cos δ
= E² ( 1 + sin 2θ cos δ )
[fig]figure[/fig]
This will give the maximum and minimum
intensities for different values of δ.
Suppose however the light is not polarized.
Then it may be regarded as made up of two
vibrations at right angles and independent in
phase.
Let their intensities be A² and B²
Then the combined intensity will be A² + B²
and this will not be changed by any phase
difference which may be introduced between them.
[fig]figure[/fig]
Consequently we cannot detect any phase difference
between two [del]beams[/del] [ins]rays[/ins] of light which are not
polarized, and we cannot detect any birefringence
in a medium unless the light is polarized.[/del]
