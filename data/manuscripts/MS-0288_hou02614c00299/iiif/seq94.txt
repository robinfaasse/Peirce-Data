[del]When a row[/del] A true doubt is an uneasy state of mind in
which one wavers between two opinions. It
cannot exist unless there is a reason or what
is mistaken for a reason for each of the
two opinions. [fig]figure[/fig] I do not doubt whether the inhabi-
tants of Saturn have red hair; for I do not think there
is the slightest indication one way or the other. I do doubt,
when I come to consider it, whether [ins]the story of[/ins] the Wandering Jew is not
true. For though the reasons to the contrary are as the ocean
to a drop of water, still there has been such a belief. Now there
has been one case, at least, of a popular belief generally ridiculed
by educated people turning out to be true, and this affords an
argument, an excessively weak one, yet still a positive
argument for the existence of the Wandering Jew, in con-
sequence of which I can, with an effort, entertain, not a
reasonable doubt but still I think a real doubt
(it is so small that I am quite sure of its reality) about the matter.
