[ins]ceremony[/ins] The scene is the [del]conferring[/del] of the degree of M. D. at Montpellier. 
[u]Primus Doctor.[/u] Si mihi licentiam dat dominus praeses, 
Et tanti docti doctores, 
Et assistantes illustres, 
Très savanti bacheliero, 
Quem estimo et honore, 
Domandabo causam et rationem quare 
Opium facit dormire. 

[u]Bachelierus.[/u] Mihi a docto doctore 
Domandatur causam et rationem quare 
Opium facit dormire. 
A quoi respondeo, 
Quia est in eo 
Vertus dormitiva 
Cujus est natura 
Sensus assoupire. 

[u]Chorus[/u] Bene, bene, bene, bene respondere, 
Dignus, dignus est intrare 
In nostro docto corpore 
Bene, bene respondere. 

According to John Locke, who was present at such 
a ceremony three years later with its violins and its 
diatribes against the circulation of the blood, there was 
more truth than caricature in Molière's intermède.
