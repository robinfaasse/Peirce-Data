Lagermark and Eltekoff say the substance produced has no character of an

2 C₂H₂ + H₂O = C₄H₆O

That is, acetic aldehyde is first formed which under the influence of dilute
sulphuric acid produces crotonic aldehyde.

1877 May 5. Berthelot (Bulletin de la soc. chim. [2] XXVII. 540) replies
to Lagermark and Eltekoff by a memoir on the hydrates of acetylene.
He says the hydrate of acetylene is formed in absorbing acetylene in concen-
trated sulphuric acid, diluting & distilling. These are nearly the conditions for
the synthesis of alcohol by ethylene. The dilution & distillation ought to be
effected as promptly as possible. Otherwise a great part is transformed into
polymeric compounds almost free of similar species. The same absorption takes
place with a more dilute acid, but more slowly. The weight of the hydrate of
acetylene corresponds sensibly to that of the acetylene absorbed. The odor of
hydrate of acetylene is much stronger than that of aldehyde.
It is not crotonic aldehyde. For hydrate of acetylene is not [del]reduced[/del] [ins]affected[/ins] by
CaCl₂ while Kekulé (Ann. der Chemie und Pharmacie CLXII 97) makes
this one of the most important properties of crotonic aldehyde.
