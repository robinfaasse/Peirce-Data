[date]1866 Jan 8[/date] (Comptes rendus LXII p 94) Berthelot sends a paper on the
formation of acetylene in incomplete combustions, showing that it is
usually formed. Again he speaks of its extreme stability. Acetylene is
formed in the incomplete combustion even of those hydrocarbons which
contain less carbon. It is formed from ethylene, chlorhydric ether,
propylene, methylic ether, marsh gas, ether, amylene, hydride of
amylene, benzene, acetone, methylformic ether, etc.

[date]Feb. 17[/date] P de Wilde writes to the French Academy claiming priority (C.R. [ins]LXI[/ins] 460)
To this Berthelot replies that it was naturally to be expected that acetylene
would be formed from the incomplete combustion of ethylene. But it is a very
different thing to show that it is a general phenomenon of incomplete combus-
tions, even of such bodies as benzene, naphthalene, etc.
