([u]Comptes Rendus[/u] I ([date]1860[/date]) p. 805) His seven notes are reprinted in the [u]Annales de Chimie et de Physique[/u]. 3[ins]d[/ins] Series. LXVII. p. 52. We does not at first notice Davy's work, but he inserts a mention of it in the reprint.

He names the gas acetylene or quadricarbide of hydrogen. That is he doubles the formula HC₂ given by Davy and writes H₂C₄, the French [del]chemists[/del] at that late date sticking to the "equivalents" instead of the atomic weights.

Besides the matters noticed by Davy, Berthelot adds in his First Note, the following:

It has a characteristic disagreeable odor.

Its flame is smoky.

He briefly mentions C₄H₄Br₂ and [del]copper and argentic compounds[/del] [ins](though he subsequently explains that it does not seem to form a bibromide except...)[/ins]

He mentions acetylsulphuric acid, which Davy had also made, & says this substance is really crotonaldehyde.

He also makes the important discovery that ammonia acting on zinc in the presence of acetylide of copper gives olefiant gas.
