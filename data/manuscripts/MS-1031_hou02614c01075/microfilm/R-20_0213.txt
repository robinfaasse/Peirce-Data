In 1862 March 3 (Comptes rendus [del]LIV. 515[/del]) Berthelot produced a
note, announcing the fundamentally important fact that acetylene is
produced when the electric spark is passed through marsh gas, and even
(though with difficulty) by heating marsh gas. In a third note (also March
24 Comptes rendus LIV p. 640) He showed that acetylene can be produced by
direct synthesis from H and C, by using carefully purified carbon (purified
from [del]animal black[/del] [ins]from carbon charcoal by chlorine[/ins]) as electrodes of an arc light in an
atmosphere of hydrogen. In this note Berthelot calls acetylene "the most
stable of all the hydrocarbons." In a fourth note (1862 May 12 Comptes
rendus LIV, p 1042) Berthelot showed that wood charcoal or graphite [del]can[/del]
be used. At the same sitting of the Academy in a fifth note, he remarks
four facts.
1ˢᵗ Acetylene is decomposed by the spark of a Ruhmkorff coil.
2ᵈ Chlorine can act on acetylene in two ways. 1ˢᵗ it can explode.
C₂H₂ + Cl₂ = 2C + 2HCl
and second it can form acetylene dichloride, an oleagenous liquor
C₂H₂ + 2Cl = C₂H₂Cl₂
