[u]R. P. de Wilde[/u] (Bulletin de la soc. chimique [2] XII 103) showed that acetylene combines directly with H in the cold [ins]only[/ins] under the influence of Pt black producing ethane $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6$. But if H is not present in small proportion ethylene is apparently produced.

[u]H. Bassett[/u] (Chemical News XIX 28) showed that a stream of illuminating gas passed through a solution of hydrargic iodide in iodide of potassium and decomposed with potash lye gives a bright yellow $\text{C}_2\text{H}\text{Hg}_2\text{I} + \text{HgO}$ which on explosion slight heating and with $\text{HCl}$ yields acetylene.

It having been known that Br acts sometimes easily and sometimes with difficulty on acetylene [u]Berthelot[/u] (Bull soc. chim [2] XI 372) found that light had much to do with it.

[u]Berthelot and Jungfleisch[/u] (C.R. LXIX 542) studied the effect of the action of acetylene on $\text{SbCl}_5$. It gives at once $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{SbCl}_5$ in fine very voluminous crystalline plates. Water decomposes it at once. By heating it splits into $\text{SbCl}_3$ and $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{Cl}_2$ a colorless fluid with strong smell of chloroform. Boiling pt $55^\circ$. Its vapor tastes sweet and causes headache. Moist air alters it & at $180^\circ$ water in closed vessel decomposes it gradually into $\text{HCl}$ and products of condensation.

If $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{SbCl}_5$ is heated with $\text{SbCl}_5$ it gives $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{Cl}_4$. This is a colorless fluid liquid of chloroform taste & smell. Boils $147^\circ$. Heated with water to $180^\circ$ it gradually alters. In an atmosphere of $\text{Cl}$ it becomes $\text{C}_2\text{Cl}_6$. Alcoholic solution of $\text{KHO}$ deprives it of $\text{HCl}$ and gives the chloride of monochloracetylene, $\text{C}_2\text{HCl}.\text{Cl}_2$ a colorless liquid boiling at $88^\circ$, decomposed by moisture and alcoholic potash lye without giving [del]di[/del] acetylene compound.
