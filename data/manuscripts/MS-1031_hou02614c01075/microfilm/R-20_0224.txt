[u]In 1867[/u]
(Comptes rendus LXIV 35)
Berthelot took up [del]the[/del] [ins]the[/ins] study of the oxidizing effects of permanganic acid.
Permanganate of potassa acting on C₂H₂ in the cold gives oxalate of potassa.
This breaks up into formate and carbonate especially in acid solution.

Berthelot also examined action of HI (Comptes rendus LXIV, 760, 788, 829)
He found it gave iodhydrate of acetylene C₂H₂.2HI. This supports his ammonia
theory, the acetylene being as it were, bivalent.

R. Rieth (Zeitschrift für Chemie) found a Bunsen burner when in partial
combustion gave large amounts of acetylene. This was therefore a good way
of preparing it.

[u]In 1868[/u]. Berthelot (Comptes rendus LXVII, 1141) found that acetylene
unites directly with nitrogen under Ruhmkorff spark to form prussic acid.
(This is the kind of thing that ought to be tried with argon.)

Berthelot (Annales de chemie et de physique [4] XIII 143) also describes
his apparatus for the direct synthesis of acetylene from its elements.

A. Bistrow and O. Liebreich (Berichte der deutschen chemischen
Gesellschaft 1868 p 220) showed that acetylene combines with the
red blood corpuscles just as CO does; only the combination is more
easily decomposed with acetylene.

[u]In 1869[/u]. Berthelot examined the effect of the spark upon mixtures
of acetylene and hydrogen under different pressures (C.R. LXVIII 810)

K. Birnbaum (Annalen der Chemie und Pharmacie CLI, 147) found
that acetylene was formed when I acts on acetate of silver.

Berthelot (C.R. LXIX 563) studied the effect of heating the sulpho
hydrocarbon salts with caustic potassa. He found the ethylsulphosate
of potassa gave acetylene
C₂H₅SO₃K + KHO = C₂H₂ + H₂O + S K₂O₃
So did the ethylendisulphosate of potassa
C₂H₄S₂O₆K₂ + 2KHO = C₂H₂ + 2H₂O + 2SK₂O₃
