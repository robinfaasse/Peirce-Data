We now perfectly comprehend the order of arrangement of the real numbers. We can imagine points to be so arranged, or instants of time, or anything else which we can imagine to be arranged in a row that is not sparse. We cannot imagine peacocks to be so arranged, but we can comprehend what such arrangement would be. The only difficulty about the imagination is that we cannot picture a row of peacocks in which each peacock did not have a next neighbor. There is no such difficulty in conceiving that the objects which are so arranged, instead of being [del]dots[/del] [ins]points[/ins], are beginningless and endless rows of points, or are lengths along a line. There is, at any rate, nothing [del]absurd[/del] [ins]impossible[/ins] in supposing that if a lapse of time were divided into two periods, and each of these periods into two, and each of these four into two, and so on without end, the resulting parts would be intervals of time, still. They would, however, be innumerable and arranged like the real numbers.
