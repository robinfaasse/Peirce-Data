[u]Hints toward the invention of a Scale-table[/u], by C. S. Peirce

A system of logarithms is a set of numbers in a table one for each natural number, such that, [ins]different[/ins] pairs of natural numbers having the same ratio correspond to pairs of logarithms having the same difference. Thus, since 14:21 = 22:33, log 14 - log 21 = log 22 - log 33.

A table of antilogarithms is a table in which the numbers are entered for constant intervals of the logarithms. In other words, it is a geometrical progression.

A logarithmic scale is one having numbers set down at distances from the origin measured by their logarithms, so that the operation of addition is replaced by measurement along the scale.

Logarithms were invented in [date]1614[/date] by Napier, and the logarithmic scale in [date]1624[/date] by Edmund Gunter. Things patentable are combinations of ideas. What is the difference between a table of antilogarithms and a logarithmic scale?
