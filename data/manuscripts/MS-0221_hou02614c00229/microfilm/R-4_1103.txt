By C. S. Peirce

A system of logarithms is a set of numbers in a table, one for each natural number, such that pairs of natural numbers in the same ratio have logarithms with the same differences. Thus since 14 : 21 = 22 : 33, we have log 14 - log 21 = log 22 - log 33.

A table of antilogarithms is a table [del]in[/del] [ins]with[/ins] which you enter the logarithm and find the number. In other words, the logarithms are tabulated at equal differences, instead of the numbers.

A logarithmic scale is a scale having numbers set down at distances from their origin measured by their logarithms.

Logarithms were invented in [date]1614[/date] by Napier, the logarithmic scale in [date]1624[/date] by Edmund Gunter. Things that are patentable are combinations of ideas.
