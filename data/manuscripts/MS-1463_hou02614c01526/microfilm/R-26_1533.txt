Notes on Strong's [u]Why the Mind Has a Body[/u].
An admirable title.

[pn]p. 1.[/pn] Why should "parallelism" be attributed to Clifford? I am
sure it was a familiar idea to me long before he wrote, and I
believe that Wundt in his first ed. of [u]Menschen und Thierseele[/u]
adopted it.

[pn]p. 2.[/pn] I can't see much distinction between "parallelism" and the
"double aspect theory." The latter certainly implied the former,
and the former must take a peculiar development to avoid the
latter.

[pn]p. 4.[/pn] As to the conservation of energy, there are two things to be
said. Energy may be conserved in each single action of
forces, and yet it may be that there are infinite series of
actions in which it is only conserved if a materialistic
theory of mind be admitted. But Strong is quite right in his
implication that [del]mind[/del] [ins]to give[/ins] [ins]mind[/ins] the power to deflect a moving
body either violates the principle of action & reaction or the
principle of energy.

[pn]p. 6.[/pn] It is not true that interactionism necessarily supposes a
hiatus in the physical series.
