[pn]p 7[/pn] As to this question being "partly at least one of fact,"
meaning presumably phenomenal fact, no doubt it is
so; and that part of the question ought to be isolated so
as to be wholly such. But the facts are not of such a
nature [del]that there is any near hope of bringing the
question to the test of experiment. If there were, it would
be a strictly scientific question.[/del] On the contrary, in regard
to such facts as are involved, we have now, and shall for
an indefinite future, have to depend upon loose and
uncertain analogies. These analogies, such as they
are, are in my opinion against the author's position.
In that sense, it is not a question of fact. That is to
say, no [ins]unquestionable[/ins] fact [del]corroborated by any experiment[/del] can at present be adduced which has
any decisive bearing upon it.

volitions."

ought in logic to assume that "the brain event exhausts itself in
producing the sensation so as to have no physical effects."

On the contrary, it might be [ins]analogous[/ins] [del]similar[/del] to the action of a
heat-engine which cannot exhaust itself in the me-
chanical effect.
