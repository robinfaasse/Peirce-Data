[u]Chap IX Of relative terms.[/u]

There are some reasonings in order to analyze which it is necessary to consider a proposition not in the simple form [del]a is b[/del] [ins]a is b[/ins], but in the form [ins]a is b to c[/ins]. For example, 'Every man is indebted to a woman'. This brings us to the subject of relative terms. A relative term is one which names nothing taken by itself but only in conjunction with another term its correlate. Such are "father of -", "lover of -", "identical with -" etc. [del]We may express these by single letters and write their correlates directly after them so that l x may denote "lover of x"[/del]

In studying [del]relatives[/del], we shall do well to begin with those of individuals. It is true there are no individuals, strictly speaking, but nevertheless it is most useful in logic to consider what their properties would be if they existed.
