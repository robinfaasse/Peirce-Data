In the syllogism in [del]Baraba[/del] [ins]Barbara[/ins],
Every S is M; Every M is a P;
Hence, Every S is a P;
the minor [u]term[/u], S, occurs only as subject,
the major [u]term[/u], P, only as predicate. It
is, therefore, evident that since P may
be any term whatever, it may be either
affirmative or negative, and since S may
be any term whatever, it may be either
universal or particular. We, thus, have
three other modes of syllogism which with
Barbara make up the four Aristotelean
moods of the [u]first Figure of Syllogism[/u].
