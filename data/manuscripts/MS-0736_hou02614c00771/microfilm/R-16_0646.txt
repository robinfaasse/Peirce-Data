[u]Rule I[/u]

If [u]a[/u] and [u]b[/u], then [u]a+b+[/u].

This is the [u]rule of combination[/u]. It is plain that we cannot reason, unless one can combine different premises; and [u]a+b+[/u] expresses no more than that [u]a[/u] and [u]b[/u] are both true at once. To show this, we use a diagram invented by Mr. Venn.

[fig]Venn diagram with four intersecting ellipses labeled with numbers 1 to 16 inside a rectangle[/fig]
