[u]Chapter V[/u]

[u]Dilemmatic Reasoning[/u].

A mode of inference which differs essentially from syllogism is the following:

If S is not true, P is true;

But if S is true, P is true;

Hence, P is true.

This depends upon the principle of excluded middle, so called because it implies that there is no medium between truth and falsehood. This principle is usually stated: "everything is either true or false," or it may be stated: "what is not not true is true."
