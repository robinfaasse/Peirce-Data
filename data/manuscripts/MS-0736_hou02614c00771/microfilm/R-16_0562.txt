The syllogistic of De Morgan better deserves attention. Instead of extending the distinctions of quantity to the predicate, he extends the distinctions of quality to the [del]predicate[/del] [ins]subject[/ins]. That is to say, he adds to the four forms of categorical propositions noticed above, these others:
Every not-S is P, or Everything is either S or P.
Every not-S is not P, or Every P is S.
Some not-S is P, or Some P is not S.
Some not-S is not P, or [del]Not everything is either S or P.[/del] [ins]Something is neither S nor P.[/ins]
