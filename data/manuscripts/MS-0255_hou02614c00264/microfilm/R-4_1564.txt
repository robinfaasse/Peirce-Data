[fig]Large X mark[/fig] [u]Of the Nature of Measurement.[/u]

[u]Definition[/u] . A [u]Quality[/u] is [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] respect in which one thing is [del]said[/del] more or less than another.

[del]Scholium[/del] [ins]Gloss[/ins] . The Latin word [u]qualitas[/u], from which our English word [u]quality[/u], has been derived, was invented by Cicero about 50 B.C. [del]to[/del] [ins]to[/ins] express [del]that[/del] [ins]the[/ins] idea described in the definition. [del]But[/del] [ins]use of the[/ins] [del]The[/del] word has never been very carefully restricted to that precise meaning, which is called the [u]predicamental[/u] meaning, because it is the meaning assigned to the [del]corresponding[/del] Greek word by Aristotle, when he reckoned [u]Quality[/u] as one of his ten [u]Predicaments[/u], or fundamental kinds of things that can be talked and thought about.

[u]Illustrations[/u] . [u]Agreeableness[/u] is a quality; because one thing is more or less agreeable than another. [u]Danger[/u] is a quality; because one situation is more dangerous than another.

[u]Definition[/u] . [u]Measurement[/u] is the assignment of numbers to objects in such a way that anything which is [u]more[/u] than another in a given respect will have a larger number assigned to it.

[u]Illustration[/u] . Thus, the market value, or price for which things could be sold may be said to measure the desire people have for them. If a given [ins]grey[/ins] horse will sell [del]for[/del] on a given day, at a given place for $500, but if it were bay would sell on the same day at the same place for $600, this shows how much more people desire bay horses than grey horses.
