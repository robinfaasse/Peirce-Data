The properties of Euclidean space are as follows:

[u]I. Properties of the Connections of parts of Space.[/u]

1. Space is continuous.

2. Space is tridimensional.

3. Space has no [del]singular[/del] surfaces, lines, or points, from which its mode of extension is different from its mode of extension from neighboring surfaces, lines, and points.

4. Space has no shell-like or annular parts.

5. Space is perissid; that is, there is [ins]are[/ins] [del]an[/del] innumerable multitudes of unbounded surfaces any three of which have [del]at[/del] odd number of points in common.

[u]II. Properties of Paths of Light.[/u]

[del]6. There is a triply continuous multitude of surfaces, called planes, any two of which intersect have a ray, or possible free path of light, and nothing more, in common, and any two rays in one plane have just one point in common[/del]

6. Through any three points there is just one plane surface; and [del]any two plane's[/del] the place common to any two plane surfaces is a ray, or possible path of light; and any [del]ray[/del] plane and ray [ins]not in that plane[/ins] have just one point in common.

[u]III. Properties of the Motion of Rigid Bodies.[/u]

7. In measurement by rigid bodies, there is a firmament, or infinitely distant surface, which is plane.
