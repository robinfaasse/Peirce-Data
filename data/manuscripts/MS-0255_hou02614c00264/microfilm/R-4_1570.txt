[u]Scholium[/u]. In order to [del]deal with[/del] [ins]measure[/ins] modular quantities, [del]precise[/del] [ins]exactly[/ins] or approximately, it is necessary to have some method by which we can pronounce whether one difference on the line of variation is equal to another, or not. If one difference is wholly included within another, we [del]pronounce[/del] [ins]shall, in all cases,[/ins] hold the latter to be the greater. Therefore, the method of comparison must be such as to be consistent with that. But we require a method which shall pronounce differences which entirely outside one another to be equal, or [ins]and when they are[/ins] unequal shall decide which is the greater. For this purpose, we must, either actually or virtually, have some standard which we can move along the line of variation. The displacements of this standard must be such that it shall always give the same result. That is to say, its pair of extremities must have a single degree of freedom in moving along the line; and in every motion, the two extremities must [del]be moved[/del] [ins]move[/ins] [del]in[/del] the same direction. [ins](But we shall see presently, that the last condition may be subject to exceptions.)[/ins] Nothing further can be insisted upon. We cannot, for example, insist that the standard shall always remain of the same length; for there is no way of ascertaining its length unless by means of another standard, & "the same length" is a phrase without meaning, unless it means how some standard or standards might be displaced.
