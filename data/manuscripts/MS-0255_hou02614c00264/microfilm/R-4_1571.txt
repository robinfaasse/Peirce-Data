[u]Definition[/u]. The number expressing the modular quan-
tity of a stretch is taken to be equal to the sum of the numbers
expressing the lengths of all the parts into which it can be
separated.

[u]Division[/u]. Measurement upon a circuit is of three
kinds, as follows:

1[ins]st[/ins], [u]Elliptical Measurement[/u], where the quantity of the circuit
is a finite number of times that of the standard. Consequent-
ly, the standard can be carried all round the circuit. Consequent-
ly, too, the circuit is divisible into halves, quarters, etc.

circuit is an infinite number of times that of the standard.
Consequently, there is a state, [ins]called the absolute state, up[/ins] to which neither extremity of the
standard can ever be quite brought. There is only one such; other-
wise it would easily be shown that there would be more than
one state [del]of a given m[/del] [ins]whose difference[/ins] from a given state was equal to a
given modular quantity; and this we have seen is not the
case.
