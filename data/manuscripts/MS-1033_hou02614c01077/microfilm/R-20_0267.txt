[u]Solubility of Acetylene and of Other substances in Acetylene[/u]

Edmund Davy, the discoverer of acetylene, remarked that, water at 18°C absorbs its own bulk of acetylene. Berthelot in 1866 remarked that water absorbs about its own volume of H₂C₂. Villard in 1895 ([u]Comptes rendus[/u] CXX. 1262) states that the coefficient of solubility of the gas at 0° and a pressure of 4.65 atmospheres is 1.6

Berthelot says bisulphide of carbon and hydride of amylene dissolve each its own bulk; refined petroleum 1½ times its bulk; oil of turpentine and CCl₄ 2 times; amylic alcohol and styrolene 3½ times; chloroform and benzene, 4 times; glacial acetic acid and absolute alcohol, 6 times their respective bulks.

[date]1877 Nov 2[/date], Cailletet ([u]Comptes rendus[/u] LXXXV. p 851) notes that liquid acetylene is highly soluble in water and that it dissolves paraffin and fats.
