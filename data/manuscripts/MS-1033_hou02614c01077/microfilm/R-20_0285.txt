[pn]Acetylene[/pn] [u]Part II. Properties of Acetylene[/u].

[u]Composition[/u]. [del]In[/del] [ins]In[/ins] 1836, when Edmund Davy discovered acetylene, the letters of chemical formulae [del]were applied to the "equivalents," i.e. what we should now call the atomic weights divided by the valency; so that H=1 and C=6. Avogadro's Law was not known, according to which[/del] in perfect gases of all kinds there is [del]at any given temperature[/del] the same number of molecules per unit of volume. There was, therefore, no satisfactory way of determining the [del]size of[/del] number of atoms in a molecule. Davy correctly determined the composition of the gas, as consisting of 1 part hydrogen and 12 parts carbon.

[u]Constitution[/u]. Davy estimated the density of the gas as 0.917, which is substantially correct. [del]It is[/del] [ins]Gas[/ins] therefore 13 times the density of hydrogen, and its molecular composition is H₂C₂.

Taking carbon as quadrivalent, its graph is H-C≡C-H.

The heat of formation of a triple bond between two carbon atoms is zero. (See the review of this subject dated [date]June 1894[/date] by William Sutherland in the London, Edinburgh, and Dublin Philosophical Magazine and Journal of Science [5] XI. 1.)
