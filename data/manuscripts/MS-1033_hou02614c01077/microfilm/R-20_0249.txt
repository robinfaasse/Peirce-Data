In the same year E. Paterno and A. Peratoner (Gazzetta chimica italiana XX 670 and in other memoirs) discussed the iodisation of acetylene, endeavoring to prove that the solid and liquid diiodacetylene differ as fumaric and maleic acids, that is by their steric formulae, the solid being by far the more stable, but the proof was somewhat defective, although they do make it probable.

About this time (or rather in [date]1890[/date]) there was a controversy between Schützenberger and Berthelot as to whether water could pass through glass under the influence of the silent discharge. Berthelot opposed the idea. But some of Schützenberger's experiments are difficult to explain, and should be borne in mind. There must be something extraordinary behind them. (Comptes rendus CX 889)

In February [date]1890[/date], Edward H. Keiser of Bryn Mawr had written most the first of several interesting papers on derivatives of acetylenes. He showed that the explosive compounds of copper and silver are $Cu_2C_2$ and $Ag_2C_2$. But there is another far more explosive silver compound [del]C2Ag2NO3H[/del] [ins]$C_2Ag_2\cdot AgNO_3$[/ins] (there is a mercurous acetylide $Hg_2C_2$ which is explosive, and a mercuric acetylide $(HgO)_3(C_2H_2)_2$ which is not strictly explosive.

At the same time R. T. Plimpton and Travers were working in the same direction but with less clear and decided results.

In [date]1890[/date] Poleck and Thümmel produced another mercury compound, a greenish black substance, which they call acetilone-mercury. It does not explode on percussion, but does so violently when heated to 150°. The formula is very complicated according to them. Nobody can have much faith in the formula.

In [date]1891[/date] J H Gladstone showed that both the refractivity and dispersive power of acetylene indicate double linking. [u]H-C=C-H[/u].

In [date]1892[/date] Vivian B. Lewes brought out (Transactions of Chemical Society [date]1892[/date] p 322) his theory of the luminosity of flames, making it depend on the production of acetylene. There can be no doubt that this increases the luminosity of all hydrocarbon flames. But his theory after much discussion is, on the whole, rejected.
