[date]1878 Feb 25[/date] R. Hermann's researches on Atomic Volumes concern us lightly. (Journal für praktischen Chemie, Neue Folge. XVII. 49)

[date]1878 Feb. 4[/date] Mascart (Comptes rendus LXXXVI. 321 and 1182) measures index of refraction of acetylene and compares it with that of other gases.

J. Coquillion ([date]May 13[/date]) studies the combustion of acetylene, which he says has two phases.

[date]1879 Aug 25[/date] Gerard Ansdell of Dewar's laboratory (Report Sheffield Meeting British Association for the Advancement of Science p. 309) and Chemical News XL. 136 (b) has a paper on the effects of pressure and heat on acetylene. His results differ enormously from those of Cailletet. He finds the critical point 37°.05. Hermann's calculated specific gravity for solid acetylene is about double Ansdell's observations of the liquid.

Dr. Hans Jahn [date]1880 Apr 15[/date] (Sitzungsberichte of the Vienna Academy LXXXI. Abth. ii. p. 756) called attention to the action of zinc dust.

[date]1880 May 31[/date], Berthelot (Comptes rendus XC. 1240) presented a memoir on the heat of combustion of hydrocarbons. This makes it clear that the heat of combination of acetylene is a large negative quantity, a very important and far reaching fact about it.

In [date]1881[/date] Lemoine (Comptes rendus XCIII. 514) considers the effect of light and heat on acetylene.

Kutscheroff showed that water will combine directly with acetylene

Berthelot (Comptes rendus XCIII 613) shows that acetylene can be exploded [u]per se[/u] by fulminating quicksilver.
