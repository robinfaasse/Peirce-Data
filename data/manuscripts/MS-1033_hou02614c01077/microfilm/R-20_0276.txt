In [date]1883 Dec 10[/date] Frédel presented to the French Institute (Comptes rendus xcvii. 1371) a paper by R. Cazeneuve in which that chemist announced that iodoform intimately mixed with moist silver powder, produces acetylene even in the cold. Of course, the reaction is precisely analogous to the above. But it is a curious instance of two solids forming a gas in the cold though neither are excessively violent [del]agents[/del] reagents. He stated that the action was improved by mixing the silver with copper precipitated in powder from blue vitriol CuSO₄ by the action of zinc (in short the powder often produced in a Daniell's battery). Also that mercury, zinc, and iron act in the same way on iodoform. He stated that a liquid is formed besides the metallic iodide. There is a fuller account in the [u]Bulletin de la sociéte chimique[/u] [2] xli. 106.
