Lewes shows pretty conclusively (Chemical News LXV. 153) that the acetylene is produced simply by the effect of heat and that the oxidation has nothing to do with its formation.

[date]1892 Oct 17[/date] Maquenne (Comptes rendus CXV. 558) proposes a better way of preparing acetylene than any except Davy's yet given. It is more scientific than Wöhler's. He mixes pure carbonate of baryta, magnesium in powder, and purified animal black and heats to bright redness in an iron bottle, so making BaC₂

P. Villard (Comptes rendus CXX 1262) examines the properties of acetylene. He gives -81° as its melting point. He goes down to much lower temperatures than Ansdell. His pressures are about 2 atmospheres greater, which he attributes to the purity of his acetylene. He does not give the specific gravities of the liquid. He says crystals of acetylene do not act on polarized light.
