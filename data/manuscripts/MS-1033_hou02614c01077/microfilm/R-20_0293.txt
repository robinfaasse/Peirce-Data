For he finds that just as much is formed whether the gas is burned with 15%, with 20%, or with 25% of air.

5. On [date]1862 March 24[/date], Berthelot astonished the world by showing that acetylene can be produced by the direct synthesis of Hydrogen and Carbon. The young chemist of today has little conception of the sensation that discovery produced. The positive discoveries of science are recorded in journals where the student can read them; but prevalent ideas are not recorded. The more prevalent they are, the more they are matters of course, the less likely they are to get definite announcements. At that time, I had almost lived in the chemical laboratory for ten years. I can testify from continual intercourse with chemists that it was the universal opinion that chemistry was impotent to produce organic bodies. Today, "organic chemistry" means only that branch of chemistry which studies the compounds of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, with the occasional introduction of nitrogen, sulphur, chlorine, bromine, iodine, and other elements having properties of a strictly systematic regularity. [del]Some[/del] [ins]Many[/ins] chemists define it as the chemistry of carbon compounds, a definition first given by Kekulé, shortly after Berthelot's announcement.

Berthelot made the synthesis of acetylene from C and H, the first step in a series of transformations by which bodies of indefinite complexity were built up, some of them identical with those produced in the living organism.
