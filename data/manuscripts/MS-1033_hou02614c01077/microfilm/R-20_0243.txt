In 1858, Adolphe Perrot (Comptes rendus XLVII. p. 350) passed
the electric spark through the vapor of alcohol and through the vapor
of ether. He thus made acetylene, but failed to recognize its
true nature.

In 1859, Berthelot (L'Institut for 1859. Also, his note of
April 30, 1860, in Comptes rendus L. 805.) made acetylene by passin[del]g[/del]
olefiant gas mixed with the vapor of alcohol (or of ether, of alde-
hyde, or of wood-spirit) through a red hot tube. He named the g[del]as[/del]
[del]vide[/del] acetylene or quadricarbide of hydrogen. Berthelot investigated it
energetically and published various papers, several of which are collect[del]ed[/del]
in the [u]Annales de Chimie et de physique[/u] [3me série] LXVII. p. 52. He
soon had his attention called to the previous discovery of the gas by
Davy, but unfortunately paid no attention to Davy's method of
preparation of it.

Before Berthelot's first note was presented to the Academy of
Sciences, 1860 April 27, Victor Sawitsch in Würtz's laboratory
showed ([u]Bulletin de la Société chimique[/u]) that monobromethylene
treated with caustic alkali would give acetylene. Subsequently
(1861 Jan 28. [u]Comptes rendus[/u] LII. 157) he showed that the monobrom-
ethylene is first converted into dibromethylene, and then, with loss
of HBr into acetylene. Upon this was founded one of the usual
methods for [del]prep[/del] preparing acetylene. About the same time
(1861 Jan 25 [u]Bulletin de la Société chimique[/u], 1861, p. 12) Miosni-
Koff showed that acetylene might be prepared by leading the vapor
of monobromethylene into a cooled ammoniacal solution
of nitrate of silver. A grey precipitate of acetylide of silver is
thrown down and this treated with hydrochloric acid gives off
acetylene.
