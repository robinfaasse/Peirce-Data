Acetylene

Is acetylene poisonous? Berthelot in 1866 ([u]Annales de Chimie[/u] [u]et de physique[/u] [[u]4[/u]] [del]X[/del] [ins]IX[/ins]. 417) says it is no more poisonous than ordinary hydrocarbons, but that its formation is a sign of incomplete combustion and thus of the presence of the highly poisonous CO.

But in 1868, Bistrow and Liebreich ([u]Berichte der deutschen[/u] [u]chemischen Gesellschaft[/u] 1868. p. 220) showed that acetylene unites with the red blood-corpuscles just as carbonic oxide does. Acetylene is however more readily removed from the red corpuscles than is carbonic oxide, and therefore persons poisoned by this gas would have a better chance of ultimate recovery. This is a not unimportant consideration, whether it be viewed as [del]an objection to acetylene[/del] [ins]the introduction of[/ins] or [del]an[/del] [ins]as an[/ins] advantage of it, that persons who blow out their gas would be less likely to be removed from this sphere of activity.

In 1881 Hatton, in the [del]Transactions of the Chemical Society[/del] [u]Journal of the Chemical Society[/u] [del]xxx[/del] states that acetylene does not seem to exert any influence whatever upon bacteria.
