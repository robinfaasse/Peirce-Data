Refraction and dispersion of light are easily measured to a high degree of accuracy, and they seem to afford very reliable indications of the molecular constitution of bodies. [del]They have generally[/del] [ins]Suffice it to say that they[/ins] have generally been applied to liquids. But Mascart, 1878 Feb 11 (Comptes rendus LXXXVI [del]1878[/del] [ins]p. 321[/ins] and May 13, p. 1182) presented to the French Academy [ins]of Sciences[/ins] tables giving the refractive power of many gases and vapors referred to that of air as unity. If we put [u]c[/u] for the effect of a Carbon atom and [u]h[/u] for that of a hydrogen atom, we have the equations
$$\text{c} + 4\text{h} = 1.57$$
$$5\text{c} + 12\text{h} = 5.82$$

These give $\text{h} = .206$ $\text{c} = .746$; so that $\text{c} = 3\text{h}$. Ethylene, propylene, amylene, and benzine have double bonds; and the last (according to the common theory) has three of them. If [u]d[/u] be the effect of a double bond, the number for Benzene gives
$$6\text{h} + 6\text{c} + 3\text{d} = 6.20$$
