[u]Name[/u]. In connection with the constitution of acetylene we
record its names; since chemical names are intended to express the
constitutions of the species of substances. Davy called the gas bicarburet
of hydrogen, which is correct. Gmelin, in his Hand Book of Chemistry,
(Cavendish Society's translation, [u]VIII[/u]. 150) names it Klumene: "because
the primary nucleus is obtained from a potassium-compound", potassium
in German being Kalium. It was a foolish name. Berthelot, on his redisco-
very of the gas, proposed to call it quadricarburet of hydrogen, which
was wrong; and but also proposed the name acetylene, which has
been accepted. Namely, just as we call C₂H₄ which is derived from
ether (C₂H₅)₂O by subtracting hydrogen from its hydrocarbon nucleus,
ethylene, so I suppose he conceived that C₂H₂ might be derived from
acetic acid (C₂H₄O₂ by subtracting hydrogen from its hydrocarbon nucleus.
Acetylene is also sometimes termed ethine. The principle is that C₂H₆
is called ethane, C₂H₄ ethene or ethylene, and so C₂H₂ should be ethine,
according to the order of the vowels, [u]a[/u], [u]e[/u], [u]i[/u]. This is a good name well-
recommended. But it is seldom used.
