Plimpton has rather an elaborate paper on the haloid compounds.
Sabanejeff has a greater paper on acetylene derivatives (Annalen

In [date]1884[/date] Berthelot and Violle made important experiments on
the force of different kinds of explosions. See Berthelot's great
work on explosives, and also Comptes rendus xcviii, 545, 601.
The explosion of acetylene is shown to be terribly violent. These
experiments were continued the following year.

In [date]1885[/date] Dewar measured the critical point of acetylene,
finding it 37°.0 with a pressure of 68 atmospheres. (Chemical
News)

In [date]1886[/date] Bellamy (Comptes rendus c. 1460) made curious ex-
periments on the effects of different metals in exploding
mixtures of acetylene and air.

In [date]1887[/date] J. W. Brühl (Zeitschrift für physikalische Chemie I. 30
made an important investigation of the influence of single and multiple
bonds upon refractive power. He finds positional-isomers have sub-
stantially the same refractivity but saturation-isomers different.

Thomsen gives a formula for the heat of combustion of any hydrocar-
bon showing that single, double, and triple bonds have different
effects (Zeitschrift für physikalische Chemie I. 369)
