At this time Berthelot pronounced acetylene not to be particularly poisonous (_Annales de chimie et de physique_ [4] IX. 407.) No doubt it was an error. For [del]Bishoff and Liebreich[/del] (Berichte der deutschen chemischen Gesellschaft, 68 p. 220) showed it combines with red blood corpuscles just as CO does, only the combination is looser.

He finds the best test for acetylene is a drop of ammoniacal cuprous chloride.

In 1868 he found that acetylene unites directly with nitrogen to form prussic acid under the influence of the Ruhmkorff spark. (_Comptes rendus_ LXVII, 1141)

In 1870. (_Comptes rendus_ LXX. 256) Berthelot gave a general method for the synthesis of organic acids founded on acetylene. Thus, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ acts on KHO to make $\text{C}_2\text{H}_3\text{KO}_2$ (acetate of potassa) etc.

In [date]1872 Feb 16[/date] Jungfleisch (_Bulletin de la Société chimique_ [2] XVII. 145) described his apparatus of for preparing acetylene by the method of Berthelot from the imperfect combustion of lighting gas.

[date]1874 March 12[/date], P. de Wilde (_Berichte der deutschen chemischen Gesellschaft, 1874, p. 352_) [del]thinks[/del] finds the easiest way to prepare acetylene is by acting upon $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{Cl}_2$ by caustic lime or soda. When we consider that this requires ethylene and chlorine to be made and stored beforehand, it gives a lively sense of the difficulties of other methods.

[date]1874 Jan 26[/date] the Messrs Thénard, father and son, announced (_Comptes rendus_ LXXIV. 219) that under the electric effluvium acetylene is converted into a horny substance.

[date]1877 April 9[/date], Truchot (_Comptes rendus_ LXXXIV 714) examined the effects of induction sparks passed through liquids raised nearly to the boiling point.

[date]1877 Nov 5[/date] Cailletet (_Comptes rendus_ LXXXV 851) announced the liquefaction of acetylene.
