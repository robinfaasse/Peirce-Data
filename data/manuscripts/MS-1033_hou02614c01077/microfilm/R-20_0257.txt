The heat of formation from the elements is[/del] equal to a sum composed of [del]a number for each[/del] bond numbers, one for each bond of the compound, and this sum is diminished by a similar sum for each molecule of the elements. If the bodies considered are not all in the gaseous state, the heat required to bring them into that state has also to be taken into account in reckoning up the heat of formation. For an endothermic compound the heat of formation from the elements is a negative quantity, because heat has to be imparted from surrounding bodies, instead of being conveyed to them. Each bond has a value in reckoning the heat of formation depending on the nature of the two substances, and if two atoms are connected by two or three bonds that makes a difference which has to be ascertained experimentally.

The heat of formation of acetylene is ascertained by burning it, and so converting it into water and carbonic anhydride, according to the formula
