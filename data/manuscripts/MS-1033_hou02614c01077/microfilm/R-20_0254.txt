To say that a body, A, is attracted by a body B is to say that if A moves away from B it will (other things being equal) move slower and slower, while if it moves toward B it will move faster and faster. Thus, a stone, which is attracted by the earth, if it is moving away from the earth, in consequence of being thrown up into the air, moves slower and slower, till it stops; but when it comes down it moves faster and faster. And if, like a coasting sled, it is deflected from the downward direction, that makes no difference. It continues with all the speed it has so gained, until either by rising again or by friction its motion is lost. If a body in moving from one point, P, to another, Q, gains a certain velocity, then in being carried back over the same path it will lose it again.
