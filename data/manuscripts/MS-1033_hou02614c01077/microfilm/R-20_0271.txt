Acetylene

4. Berthelot originally made acetylene by passing through a red hot [del]tube[/del] olefiant gas mixed with the vapor of alcohol, of ether, of aldehyde, or [ins]of[/ins] wood spirit. He [del]probably[/del] [ins]no doubt[/ins] regarded the ethylene chiefly as a [del]medium[/del]

In [date]1862 March 3[/date] (Comptes rendus LIV. 515) he showed that acetylene was formed (though with some difficulty) by heating marsh gas.

[date]1864[/date] de Wilde (Bulletin de la Société chimique [2] I. 402) stated that acetylene is freely formed when chloride of ethylene is passed through a tube heated to dull redness.

[date]1864 Dec 3[/date] F. de Wilde presented to the Belgian Academy (Bull. de l'Académie royale de Belgique [2] XIX. 93) a paper in which [del]he[/del] says that reasoning from Berthelot's discovery that olefiant gas [del]passed[/del] through a red hot tube is partly converted into acetylene he inferred that acetylene would be a product of the imperfect combustion of ethylene, and upon experiment found that it was so. He remarked that, this being the case, it must largely account for the illuminating property of the olefiant flame. It was evidently de Wilde's idea that it was the heat and not the oxidation which made acetylene a product of the imperfect combustion of ethylene.

[date]1866 Jan 8[/date] Berthelot (Comptes rendus LXII. 94) read a paper [del]on[/del] the formation of acetylene in imperfect combustions; and he showed that it is generally a product of such combustion. For he found [del]it in[/del] the products of the imperfect combustion of ethylene, chlorhydric ether, propylene, methylic ether, marsh gas, ether, amylene, "hydride of amylene," benzene, acetone, methylformic ether, and other bodies.

On [date]Feb 17[/date] following de Wilde wrote to the French Academy (Comptes rendus LXII. 400) claiming priority in this matter. To this claim Berthelot (Ibid. p. 459) replied that it was naturally to be expected that acetylene would be a product of the incomplete combustion of ethylene (since it was known to result from the heating of ethylene), but that it was quite another thing to find that it is a general product of incomplete combustions, even of such bodies as benzene, naphthalene, [del]etc.[/del]
