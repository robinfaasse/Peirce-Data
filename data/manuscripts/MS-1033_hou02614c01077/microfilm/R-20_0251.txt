Willgerodt (Berichte der deutschen chemischen Gesellschaft XXVIII. 2107) says that acetylene prepared from carbide of calcium is contaminated with phosphine, and proposes to pass it through bromine water to purify it.

1895. Feb 14, Lewes presented to the Royal Society (Proceedings LVII. 450) his strongest defense of his theory of flames.

1895 and 1896 were marked by the discovery of numerous metallic carbides of Y, Ce, Ba, U, Li, Ca, Th, Mn, Zr, V, and W.

1895. Sep 2, C. Bohn (Zeitschrift für physikalische Chemie XVIII. 219) discussed flames, and argued that no Carbon spectrum is known.

1895 Sep 9 L. Maquenne (Comptes rendus CXXI. 424) read a memoir on the explosive resolution of endothermic bodies into their elements, in which he showed that at ordinary low pressures such an action is not propagated more than a few feet in acetylene gas unless the shock is very considerable, due for instance to a gramme of fulminating mercury.

1895 Dec 30 Henri de Chatelier (Comptes rendus CXXI. 1144) read a masterly memoir on the imperfect combustion of acetylene.

The year 1896 was marked by papers treating acetylene as a body easily obtained. Thus, Violle (Comptes rendus CXXII, 79) proposed to use a fixed area of acetylene flame as a standard photometric unit, an idea which will no doubt find acceptance.

The temperatures of flames and high temperatures generally, received considerable attention, and good progress toward settlement of many of those questions very important in their bearings on illumination by acetylene were made.

Finally, in [del]1896[/del] [ins]1896[/ins] Oct 5, Berthelot and Vieille took up the question of the dangers of acetylene illumination (Comptes rendus CXXIII. 523). They showed that these dangers were very serious, and demanded the most careful investigation and scrutiny, but that, at the same time, they were not of such a character as to stand in the way of this method of illumination, provided they were investigated and provided against.
