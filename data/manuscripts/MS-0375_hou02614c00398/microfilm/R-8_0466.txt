[u]On Reality[/u]

I term that nothing, predication concerning which [del]predi[/del] no predication can have any meaning. You can therefore predicate what you please of it [del]as a matter[/del] [ins]in[/ins] form, and the sentence will not be false, because it will be meaning-less. Now there are two cases of nothing.

1[sup]st[/sup]. If contradictories enter into the definition of a term, it is necessarily nothing. This [ins]case[/ins] is called [u]absurdity[/u].

2[sup]nd[/sup]. A term may be nothing without any [del]a priori reason why it should be[/del] [ins]independently of[/ins] any logical maxim. This is a case of [u]simple unreality[/u].

We have then [del]nothingness essential and nothingness informational[/del] some terms determined to be nothing by their [u]essence[/u] or the ideal starting-point of information, others determined to be nothing in the progress of information. And finally we assign an ideal goal of information and we may suppose others to be determined to be nothing only then, and not before.
