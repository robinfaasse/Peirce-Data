[del]The[/del] The first essay in the book is called Origin and
Validity. Its chief purpose is to enforce the elementary
remark that Kant's Critic of the Pure Reason is
mainly logical and not psychological. This is certainly
true; and not to understand this is to miss the whole
purport of that great book. At the same time, the
logical propositions [ins]of the work[/ins] are frequently supported by psycho-
logical discussions, and to such an extent that the
details of the theory would fall to the ground were
the psychology not admitted. Kant's stock argument,
which he applies to a hundred problems, is accepted by
Mr Ritchie in the most unguarded way and without
sufficient [del]and[/del] reflection. Mr. Ritchie states it,
in a very inadequate form, as follows: "If knowledge
be altogether dependent on sensation, knowledge is
impossible. But knowledge is possible, because the
sciences exist. Therefore, knowledge is not altogether
dependent on sensation." If there were nothing in the
