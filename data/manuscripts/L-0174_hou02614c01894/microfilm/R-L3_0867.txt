I should regret exceedingly if any impression went abroad that [del]an[/del]
any one was trying to avoid any responsibility, or that any one con-
sidered the observations a failure. I was always impressed with the
great zeal and earnestness that you showed in connection with this
work, both before I went North and after my return. I am sorry that you
should have considered anything in my remarks was of a recriminating
nature. I think, looking at the thing impartially, you can readily see
that it would have been out of place in this memorandum to have put
in anything which should bear upon your 'good will' towards this work.
It was obvious to me that there were lively differences of opin-
ion between the assistants of the Coast Survey, and as an official not
at all connected with it, but at the head of a Government bureau, I felt
that it would be improper for me to touch upon anything other than the
facts connected with the statements set forth in your report. The
statements made in the second paragraph in regard to the head on which
the pendulum had been supported in its oscillations, is, as I consider
it, an unimportant matter, but if it is not, I have plainly stated the
facts in the case. I do not see how any person could consider that you
did not have the greatest good will towards a set of pendulum observa-
tions with which you was intimately associated, and with which was
used a pendulum constructed in accordance with your ideas and under
your supervision. That I was disposed to refrain from a criticism of
your report, is shown by my not touching on several points connected
with the work. The statement in the opening line of your report is
incorrect. The Chief Signal Officer did not apply to the Superintend-
ent, but the Superintendent applied to [del]me,[/del] asking if I would make the
observations, and, as I then understood, this application was made owing
to your interest and zeal in the matter. Mr. Patterson, in his inter-
view with me, did not absolutely say that you had initiated the idea of
these observations, but I inferred it from his conversation; and cer-
tainly the opening of the whole affair was a letter from him to me,
which I brought to the personal attention of the (then) Chief Signal
Officer.
