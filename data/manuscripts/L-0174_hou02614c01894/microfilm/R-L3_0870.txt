[u]Signal Office,[/u]
WAR DEPARTMENT,
Washington City, [date]December 13, 1883[/date]

My Dear Professor Peirce:

Referring to your letter of 28th ultimo, I have the honor to inform you that steps have been taken to ascertain whether the memoranda referred to by you can be inserted or not--I fear a doubtful matter; but if it can [ins]not[/ins] be done at the Government Printing Office, I can make arrangements to have it set up, printed, and inserted in the 750 copies which are distributed through this office.

As I have already written you, I do not construe the letter as being any reflection upon me, but, at the same time, it left matters in some few cases a little doubtful, which, as I thought, called for explanatory words from me.

I am obliged to you for the frank, full, and courteous, manner in which you have written upon this subject.

Very truly yours,

[fig]Signature of Chief Signal Officer[/fig]
Chief Signal Officer.

Professor C. S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.
