**Signal Office,**
**WAR DEPARTMENT,**
[u]Washington City,[/u] [date]November 30, 1888[/date]

My Dear Mr. Peirce:

I have your letter of 27th instant. Regarding the memorandum by me forming page 715 of the Report of the Lady Franklin Bay Expedition, you say, 'I am surprised to find that you conceive that passages in my report imply blame of you or of Mr. Israel. I assure you that this is neither the intention nor the effect of those passages; but had I known that you took that view of them, I would have modified them.' I beg to assure you that I in no ways considered anything in your report as implying blame of either Mr. Israel or myself, but simply looked on it as a conviction on your part that the loss of weight occurred during our retreat when everything was necessarily handled with great rapidity and under many disadvatages. I regret exceedingly that you should have thought that I had any other than the interests of science in view in penning the memorandum. I thought I made it clear to you in my conversation with you, that no accident in any way, shape, or manner occurred to the pendulum. I consider the whole matter simply as a dissent from your hypothesis of a loss of weight.

I have never considered this question otherwise than impersonally and I should in no way have considered the question as involving any imputation upon Mr. Israel or myself. It would have been a grave reflection upon both of us if the scientific world believed that an accident having occurred, the commanding officer failed to fully and clearly set forth the nature of such accident. The whole question for the scientific world to pass upon is whether your hypothesis is correct or not.

I am sure if you consult the original description furnished by me to the Coast and Geodetic Survey, you will find it clearly set forth in writing that I personally superintended every reversal and suspension of the pendulum. I will further state that invariably (with one exception, noted hereafter) Mr. Israel and myself entered and left the pendulum room together during the progress of the work from January 6th to January 29th, 1882. On one occasion, when the decrease of arc of oscillation was so rapid as to show conclusively that the vibration would not continue more than one-fourth the usual time, Mr. Israel suggested that one of the screws might be loose, and on that occasion he entered the pendulum house alone and stopped the pendulum, readjusted the screws, and set the pendulum again in motion.
