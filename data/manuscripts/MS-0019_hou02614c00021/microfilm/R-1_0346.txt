[u]Art. 2. Definition 2.[/u] Let us say that a relation, $i$, [u]universally[/u]
[u]transfers[/u] one collection, $n$, to another, $m$, and write $t_{imn}$, if, and only
if, to every object of $n$ some object of $m$ is in the relation $i$. That is,

[del](6)[/del] $\Pi_{m} \Pi_{n} \Pi_{i} \bar{t}_{imn} + q_{i}$

(6) [del]$\leftarrow$[/del] $\Pi_{m} \Pi_{n} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{s} \Sigma_{r} \bar{t}_{imn} + \bar{q}_{ns} + q_{mr} \cdot r_{irs}$

(7) $\Pi_{m} \Pi_{n} \Pi_{i} \Sigma_{s} \Pi_{r} q_{ns} \cdot (\bar{q}_{mr} + \bar{r}_{irs}) + t_{imn}$.

[u]Corollary 3.[/u] Every relation universally transfers nothing, $0$, con-
sidered as a collection, to every collection. For $\Pi_{s} \bar{0}_{s}$, or $\Pi_{s} \bar{q}_{0s}$; and
multiplying this into (7), we get $\Pi_{m} \Pi_{i} t_{im[0]}$.

[u]Corollary 4.[/u] Every converse of a universal relation [ins]universally[/ins] transfers every
collection to the collection of the universe. For the converse of a universal
relation is defined by $\Pi_{s} \Sigma_{r} r_{irs}$ and the collection of the universe is
defined by $\Pi_{x} \infty_{x}$ or $\Pi_{x} q_{[\infty]x}$.
