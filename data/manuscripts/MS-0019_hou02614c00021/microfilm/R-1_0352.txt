Solution. Let [u]m[/u] be the [del]property in question[/del] [ins]collection, and[/ins] let [u]X[/u][sub]m[/sub] express that it
has the property sought. Then
[math]\Pi_n \bar{w}_{mn} \text{ + } \bar{X}_m \text{ + } \bar{s}_{mn}[/math]
That is [math]\Sigma_n w_{mn} \cdot s_{mn} \prec \bar{X}_m[/math].

We have, then to form [math]w_{mn} \cdot s_{mn}[/math], to eliminate [u]n[/u], as economically
as possible, so as to have as broad an [u]X[/u] as possible.

But after [u]X[/u] has been determined so as to fulfil this condition, it
must then be narrowed, if necessary, so as to fulfil another condi-
tion, namely, that no addition to the universe can [ins]enable[/ins] [del]destroy[/del] the
character [del]of the collection[/del] to proceed from any collection of which it is a part. This addition to the universe will be expressed by
[del]changing[/del] [ins]reducing[/ins] every quantifier to [math]\Sigma[/math] which refers to objects not within
the collection.

Finally, it may be that the first step of eliminating [u]n[/u] was not
economically performed, and that [u]X[/u] requires to be broadened so that
any collection whatever that does not possess [u]X[/u] may, by an addition
to the universe, be made to proceed from a collection of which it
forms a part.
