[u]Third Mode of Division.[/u]

This mode of division is of great importance, but in its present condition, due, as can hardly be doubted, to insufficient study, is irregular. A relation having seeds may be termed a [u]granulation[/u]. The terms [u]repeating relation[/u] for [u]granulation[/u], and [u]non-repeating relation[/u] for [u]extragranulation[/u], [del]due to[/del] first proposed by the author, seem to be generally received. Every [u]juxtasuilation[/u] is a granulation; and every [u]juxtagranulation[/u] is a suilation.

Extragranulations are of subsidiary importance in logic; an essential extragranulation closely approximating [del]than[/del] [ins]in[/ins] its nature to a reference. But this is not to say that they are without importance.

A granulation may contain a spike or it may not. In the former case, it may be termed a [u]spiculation[/u], in the latter an [u]extraspiculation[/u].
