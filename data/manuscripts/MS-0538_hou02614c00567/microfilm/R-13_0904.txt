[del]The fundamental dyadic relation of logic in the order of im-
portance as well as in that of simplicity is that between truth
and falsehood. This relation is the relation of negation [ins]or contradiction[/ins], which is
not quite the same as the existential relation of otherness of individuals.[/del]

The [del]principle of contradiction[/del] [ins]no[/ins] [del]is that every[/del] definite proposition
is both true and false.

The [u]principle of excluded middle[/u] is that every individual
proposition is either true or false.

These two principles may be regarded as constituting the
definition of negation or contradiction. Or, if that is regarded as fa-
miliarly known, the former will define [u]definiteness[/u], the
latter [u]individuality[/u]. Thus, "Some man is rich" and "Some
man is not rich" being both true, makes "Some man" an
indefinite description; while [del]the fact that[/del] "Every man is rich"
and "Every man is not rich" are both false makes "Every
man" a non-individual, or universal term. [del]Or[/del] [ins]In[/ins] the third
place, some writers evidently regard the two principles as expressing
positive truth, or falsity, a position which the present writer must
confess himself unable to comprehend.
