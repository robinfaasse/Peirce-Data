[u]Fourth Mode of Division[/u].

This mode of division depends upon the consideration of [del]the mode of being of the relates and correlates of a relation. It ought, perhaps, to be made the first mode.[/del] The logician, as such, has no concern with [del]the[/del] modes of being except so far as they affect modes of reasoning. What particularly concerns him, in this connection, is the applicability, or inapplicability of these two principles:

The Principle of Contradiction: [del]Ever both[/del] Of nothing definite can any predicate be both true or false.

The Principle of Excluded Middle: Of every [del]thing[/del] individual every predicate is either true or false.

A [del]person[/del] logician does not, as such, care for metaphysical being. Anything which has sufficient steadiness to have anything decidedly false of it, and not true, has being enough for him.
