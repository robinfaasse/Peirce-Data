[del]But now I begin to find myself, like a man that has
taken a long walk and comes at last to a hill from
whence he can see his home at a distance. But I
must be content to walk it. I was in a fine way
for my second part; but the wind changed, and
now I am becalmed. But since I can't steer my
own course, I must be content to follow the tide.
I have two or three things now in my head to do,
which are as good as done; but they must stay. 
I have done all I can; and let the world do its part
now. What does it signify to be always thinking of
things? We may as well let them come when they
can. The business of a man is to be quiet, and
take what the day brings; and let things go
their own way. If we can't do what we would,
we must do what we can. ? over.
Now let me think what I must do. But first let me
think of what I have done. I have done what I
could; and I can do no more. I must leave the
rest to Providence. I have been very lucky in
most things that I have done; and why should I
distrust my luck now? I have been lucky in
my friends too. I have been lucky in everything;
and if I am not lucky now, it is because I don't
deserve it. But why should I not deserve it?
I have done nothing but what I should. I have
not been idle. I have not been extravagant.[/del]
