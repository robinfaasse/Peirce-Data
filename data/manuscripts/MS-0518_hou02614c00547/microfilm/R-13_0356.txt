[del]Identity has at all times been represented in functional algebra by[/del]
1. But to avoid all confusion, it will be best to give this sign a
distinctive appearance, by writing it as a thick vertical line. Other-
ness will then naturally be represented by a plain T, this being
compounded of the signs for identity and negation. Schröder uses $1_1$
for identity and $0_1$ for otherness, which is objectionable, since the rela-
tion of identity to the universe is quite different from that of other-
ness to nothing.

Two alternative copula-signs recommend themselves for special
occasions, as, for example, when the algebra of the copula is em-
ployed. Namely, the sign for $\prec$ is a modified F, [ins]as for "follows,"[/ins] whose upper line
is to extend over the whole antecedent. Thus, we may write [ins]indifferently in place[/ins]
$A \prec B$ and $\overline{A} \text{F} B$,
reading "from A follows B. So we may write indifferently,
$(A \prec B) \prec C$ and $\overline{\overline{A} \text{F} B} \text{F} C$,
reading "from, from A follows B, follows C." The sign for $\overline{\prec}$ is a modified
C, for "leaves contingent," where the upper part extends over the whole non-
consequent. Thus, we may write indifferently
$A \overline{\prec} B$ and $A \overline{\text{C} B}$,
