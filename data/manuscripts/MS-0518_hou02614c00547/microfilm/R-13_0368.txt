I am very far from denying the interest and value of the
problems to which Prof. Schröder has applied himself; though
there are others [del]not[/del] to which I turn by preference. Certainly,
he has treated his problems with great power and clearness.

In respect [ins]to[/ins] individuals, both non-relative and pairs, he
has added some fundamental propositions to those which had
been published. But he is very much mistaken in supposing
that I have expressed contrary views. He simply mistakes my
meaning.

In regard to [del]notation[/del] algebraical signs, I cannot accept any of

serious hindrance to the [del]spread[/del] of promulgation of the new doctrine

own course in using the dagger as the sign of relative [del]multiplication[/del] [ins]addition[/ins]
must be continued, yet I have always given that sign [ins]in its cursive form[/ins] a scorpion-tail
curve to the left; and [del]I think[/del] I will not insist on one form of
curve rather than another. In almost all other cases, in my judg-
ment, Prof. Schröder's signs can never [del]re-[/del] be generally received,
because they are at war with a principle, the general character of
which is such that Prof. Schroeder would be the [del]very[/del] first of all men

led to adopt in regard to their systematic nomenclature. It is that
