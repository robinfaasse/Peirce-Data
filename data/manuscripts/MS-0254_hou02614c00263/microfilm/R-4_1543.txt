[u]Theorem[/u]. [u]In hyperbolic motion there are just two firmamental states, and in both the regions into which they sever the circuit the state-numbers increase toward one of these and away from the other; and the quantity of the whole circuit is not infinite.[/u]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. If the entire series of numbers occupies a limited portion of the circuit of variation, $+\infty$ and $-\infty$ are different. They are, therefore, not the ordinary $\infty$, which is $\frac{1}{0}$. [ins]Such[/ins] [del]This[/del] is, for example, the ordinary infinity of an exponent. For $b^z$, when $z=0$ equals $1$; and if $b < 1$ when $z > 0$ $b^z < 1$ and when $z = +\infty$, $b^z = 0$. On the other hand, $b^{-z} = \left(\frac{1}{b}\right)^z$, and when $b < 1$, $\frac{1}{b} > 1$, and [del]if[/del] if $z = +\infty$, $\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)^z = \infty$. If $z$, instead of being either $+\infty$ or $-\infty$, is $\frac{1}{0}$, then $b^z$ is a quantity such that $\left(b^z\right)^0 = b$.
