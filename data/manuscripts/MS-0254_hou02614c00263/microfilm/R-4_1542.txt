[u]Division[/u] . Measurement is either

A. [u]Parabolic[/u], when the circuit of real numbers corresponds to the circuit of variation of states, or

B. [u]Non-parabolic[/u], when the two circuits are not coincident.

Non-parabolic measurement is of two kinds, viz.: —

a. [u]Elliptic[/u], when the entire circuit of states coincides with a finite part of the circuit of numbers.

b. [u]Hyperbolic[/u], when the entire line of finite numbers occupies but a portion of the circuit of variation, and leaves a portion vacant.

[u]Definition[/u] . An absolute, or firmamental, state is a state to which or from which no member of the metron can change.

[u]Theorem[/u] . [u]In parabolic measurement upon one circuit there is just one firmamental state.[/u]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. In every [del]measurable[/del] [ins]finite[/ins] change of the metron, the station-numbers of all members are changed by the same [ins]finite[/ins] increment. Now, a finite number added to any number gives a different number with one sole exception. Namely, if the number increased is infinite, the sum has the same value. But only one state has a given state-number & therefore, the state of that member of the metron which has $\infty$ for its state-number is never changed. Hence, since every change can be reversed, no [ins]member[/ins] [del]state[/del] is by any measurable change carried to the state whose state-number is infinite.
