Each particle will, therefore, be confined to an ordinary surface,
and each flat film to touching an ordinary surface. [del]These[/del] [ins]Such[/ins]
surfaces will all be of the same sort, namely, they will all
be [u]spherical surfaces[/u]. These two modes of construction give two
descriptions of such a surface, one as [del]the[/del] a surface all whose
points are equally distant from one point, the other as a
surface all [del]of[/del] whose tangent planes are equally inclined to
one plane. It will have to be proved that these two are the
same.

If two particles [ins]of a metrical image[/ins] are fixed at points, or two flat films are confined
to planes, or one particle is fixed at a point while one flat film
is confined to a plane, then the metrical image has one degree
of [del]freedom[/del] [ins]motion[/ins] remaining. In that [del]case[/del] [ins]motion[/ins], every particle must describe
a line; but it will not be an ordinary line, but one whose developable
surface reduces to a plane; that is, it will be a [u]plane curve[/u]. It will
be a particular kind of plane curve, namely [u]a circle[/u], being the
curve all whose points are at constant differences from two fixed
points. It will, thus, be the intersection of two spheres. In the
same motion, [del]each[/del] every flat film will roll over a developable surface;
not an ordinary developable, however, but one whose cuspidal edge
reduces to a point, which is called a [u]conical surface[/u]. It will
be a particular kind of cone, namely a [u]circular or right cone[/u],
that is to say, [del]a cone[/del] [ins]the surface[/ins] every tangent plane to which has a
constant inclination to two planes. That is, it is the surface
bitangent to two spheres.
