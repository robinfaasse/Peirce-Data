(From Graphical Geometry) [u]A moving particle generally[/u] [u]describes an edge of regression, or, if confined to a plane, a[/u] [u]plane curve; and a [ins]moving[/ins] flat film generally envelopes a developable,[/u] [u]or, if compelled to pass through a fixed point, a cone[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. The positions of a moving particle are positions continuously related to instants of time. These instants forming a singly continuous multitude, so do the points. Now a singly continuous multitude of points is a line. And since the points, being innumerable, cannot be separately defined, they can only be defined by a general description; so that the line is either a regular curve, or is composed of portions of regular curves. In like manner, triads of successive positions of a moving particle are continuously related to triads of instants of time, and hence when two of the instants are brought to the third, there is generally a definite limiting position of the plane through them.
