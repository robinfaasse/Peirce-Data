An Argument from a Random Sample, is a method of ascer-
taining [ins]of[/ins] what proportion of [del]the[/del] members of a finite class possess
a predesignate, or virtually predesignate, quality, by [del]taking[/del] [ins]selecting[/ins]
instances from that class according to a method which will, in
the long run, present any instance as often as any other, and
concluding that the ratio found for such a sample will hold
in the long run. Its justification is evident. An Abduction is a
method of forming a general prediction without any positive
assurance that it will succeed either in the special case or [del]usually[/del] [ins]generally[/ins],
its justification being that it is the only possible hope of regulating
our future conduct rationally, and that Induction from past
experience gives us strong encouragement to hope that it will
be successful in the future.
