[pn]8th[/pn], a [u]Rhematic Symbol[/u], [ins]or Symbolic Rheme,[/ins] is a sign connected with its Object by an association of general ideas in such a way that its Replica calls up an image in the mind, which image, owing to certain habits or dispositions of that mind, tends to produce a general concept, and [del]the[/del] Replica is interpreted as a Sign of an object that is an instance of that concept. Thus, the Rhematic Symbol either is, or is very like, what the logicians call a [u]General Term[/u]. The Rhematic Symbol, like any Symbol, is necessarily itself of the nature of a general type, and is thus a Legisign. Its Replica, however, is a Rhematic Indexical [del]Sinsign[/del] [ins]Sinsign[/ins] of a peculiar kind, in that the image it suggests to the mind acts upon a Symbol already in that mind [del]and[/del] to give rise to a General Concept. In this it differs from other Rhematic Indexical Sinsigns, including those which are Replicas of Rhematic Indexical Legisigns. Thus, the demonstrative pronoun ‘[u]that[/u]’ is a Legisign, [del]and[/del] [ins]being[/ins] [del]is[/del] a general type; but it is not a Symbol, since it [del]does[/del] [ins]is[/ins] not signify a general concept. Its Replica draws attention to a single Object, and is a Rhematic Indexical Sinsign.
