[u]An Illustration of Dynamics.[/u]

(1). Let us suppose a particle to be attracted toward a fixed centre by a force inversely proportional to the cube of its distance from that centre. Then we desire to [del]know[/del] ascertain the form of the trajectory or orbit and also the time at which the particle will reach different points. The latter problem is slightly the easier.

(2). To solve these two problems it will be necessary to use the calculus, of which I shall suppose the reader to possess the rudiments, although I shall be prepared that he is thoroughly rusty in its use. As he probably learned what he knows of it by the method of limits, which presents needless difficulties, I shall explain briefly the method of infinitesimals.
