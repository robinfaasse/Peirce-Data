[marginalia]P.O. Milford, Pa. [date]1908 July 30[/date] [pn]1½[/pn][/marginalia]

My dear Golden:
May I venture to believe that you
will not be indifferent to the great
interest I have found in all your
articles of late years concerning the
history of science. I have learned a good
deal from them & this letter is prompted by
particular approval of [del]your paper on[/del]
Progress. Perhaps I don't go quite as far as
you (Kindly forgive that blot which was due
to the circumstance that having been kept up
all last night & having now at 3 P.M. been
working since 3 A.M. at original work, I fell
asleep between two words of this lively & wide-
awake epistle) No doubt the Athenian and -
torians (this doesn't exclude the Greats gathered
in [del]Plato's[/del] [ins]Athens &[/ins] later in Alexandria) were marvellous
bright minds, but was largely due to their being clannish.
