My dear Rolker

I am one of those excessively imperfect beings whom sympathy comforts greatly and I got much comfort from your warm letter.

You are not to suppose that I want any [del]work[/del] [ins]employment[/ins] from publishers except to do hack work. Hack work, conscientiously done, is most respectable. Witness Byron's Hebrew Melodies. I am aware that publishers want, not only hack work, but mediocre work. It must have the precise merits that other work in their line has, and with those merits it is required to have qualities that from higher points of view are faults. For instance, in writing for the American Book Company, it would be absurd to speak of the three races in Europe now recognized by anthropologists. That would be a fault. One must say that excepting Hungarians, Finns, and Basques, all are descendants of ancient Aryans, whom it would be wisest to locate dogmatically in Chorasmia or Margiana. But it is the business of the good hack, to which selfrespect requires him to accommodate himself, to look to the special purpose for which he is employed, and earn his money honestly. When I was employed upon the Century Dictionary, it was the express desire of Roswell Smith that I should impart an exceptional character to my work, and I did it.
