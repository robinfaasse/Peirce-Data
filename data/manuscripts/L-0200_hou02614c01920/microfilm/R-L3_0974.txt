[u]Monist[/u], [date]Jan./91[/date] p. 166 line + 8. you say
"for very slight variations often, for wider
ones rarely" — speaking of Darwin's theory.
[del]That[/del] The view that the wide variations
in individuals of a species were rare
was always a stumbling block to me.
But Wallace has conclusively shown by
measurements of all kinds on birds
& beasts that the variations in the
wings, legs, bones of the individuals
[u]of even a well-marked species are
wide — very wide[/u] — & by no means
small. "Sporting" is not a matter
of 2 or 5 per cent, but of 50 + 80
per cent. This, as an observed
fact, helps Darwinism immensely
