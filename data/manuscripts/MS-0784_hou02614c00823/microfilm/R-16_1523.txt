[u]Cantor's Geschichte der Mathematik[/u] ([u]Vol. III[/u], p. 27)
gives an account of a professed proof of the postulate concern-
ing parallels by one Nasr ed Deen. I slightly modify it
for the present purpose.

C, C', C'', etc., and at these points erect equal perpen-
diculars CE, C'E', C''E'', etc. Now it is evident from
symmetry, and may be admitted, that if the upper extre-
mities E' and E'' of any two of these be connected
by a straight line, the angles so formed at E' and E''
will be equal. Hence, the angles E'' and E will be equal,
E and E''' will be equal, and in short all the angles
