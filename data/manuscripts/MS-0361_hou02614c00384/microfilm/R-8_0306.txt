(Enlarged abstract)

✓ The very first of distinctions which Logic supposes is between [del]a[/del] doubt and [del]a judgment[/del] [ins]belief[/ins], a question and a proposition. Doubt and belief are two states of mind which feel different, so that we can distinguish them by immediate sensation. We almost always know without any experiment when we are in doubt and when we are convinced. This is such a difference as there is between red and blue, or pleasure & pain. [del]But this is[/del] [ins]were[/ins] the whole distinction, it would be almost without significance. But [ins]in point of fact[/ins] the [del]mere[/del] sensible distinguishability is attended with an important practical difference. When we believe there is a Proposition which according to some rule determines [ins]our[/ins] actions, so that [del]the[/del] belief being known, the way in which we shall behave may be surely deduced, but in the case of doubt we have such a proposition more or less distinctly in our minds but do not act from it.
