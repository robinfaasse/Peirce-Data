He is said to have been a dull child, but to have been devoted to
the worship of the Virgin. He is also said to have had a remarkable
turn for [del]mathematics[/del] geometry, which is borne out by some
passages of his greatest work.

He entered the house of the Minorites in Oxford, and Merton
College. His master is said to have been William of Ware, and
the manner [del]he[/del] [ins]Scotus[/ins] alludes to this Doctor makes this probable. This
William of Ware is [del]alluded to[/del] occasionally quoted by Franciscan
writers, [del]and he was very likely[/del] He seems to have been known by
only one writing; and by comparing the quotations, I am
inclined to think he is that William de Marra who wrote
a celebrated, but [ins]now[/ins] almost unknown, "Correctorium fratris Thomae."

At Oxford Scotus seems to have written his strongest works namely,
his Quaestiones subtillissimae on the Metaphysics of Aristotle
and his Oxoniense Scriptum on the Sentences of Peter Lombard.
The last contains a reference to a Bull dated October 1303. He
would naturally have written first of all his Questions on the
Logic of Aristotle and Porphyry.

In [ins]November[/ins] 1304 he was living in Paris and was then created
Doctor Subtilis. He there wrote his Reportata, Collationes, Quod-
libeta, etc. In 1307 he was made a Regent of the University.
