Every possible dissection of history is, of course, more or less artificial. Earth-circling cables have made the very day to be so. But some divisions, however vaguely bounded, are founded on fact. A general movement of thought, action, and feeling, will become absorbing and suppressive of other tendencies and begin to decline usually in from twenty to forty years; and as soon as it is enfeebled another movement will take its place. With a good deal of truth, too, we are accustomed to break history up into developments of two to five hundred years or longer.
