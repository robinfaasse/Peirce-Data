[del]Formation[/del] Certain Fundamental conceptions.

The term [u]ens[/u] (plural: [u]entia[/u]), a present participle formed from [u]esse[/u], was employed by the schoolmen to denote whatever can be mentioned or be the object of [del]any sort of thought[/del] [ins]thought[/ins] and whatever the mind can be directed towards, whether it exist or not, or however absurd it may be. In this sense I shall use the word. The corresponding abstract noun is [u]entitas[/u], or in English, [u]entity[/u]. As in many other cases, the abstract form is frequently used in our language with a concrete signification. It has been usual with us to take [u]entity[/u] in the sense of [u]reality[/u], as when we sneer at the medieval commentators for considering abstractions to be "entities." This sense of the word was recognized as a secondary one in the middle ages, but the broader meaning was never forgotten and all the schoolmen hold that [del]some[/del] [ins]y[/ins]some [u]entia[/u] are [u]entia realia[/u] [del]some[/del] [ins]some[/ins] also are [u]entia rationis[/u].
