An ens in so far as it has a quality may be termed a quale, or the subject of a quality. The relation of a quality to its subject is termed inherence; the quality inheres in its subject. The more qualities an [u]ens[/u] has, the more determinate it is said to be. Red is more determinate than [del]red[/del] colour [del]than[/del] and scarlet than red.

To suppose an ens [del]without a quality[/del] [ins]devoid of every quality[/ins] would be a violation of the first principle of logic, yet such a supposition would appear to be entirely self-consistent. But to suppose a quality not inhering in [del]a subject[/del] [ins]any[/ins] ens, is absurd in itself, and opposed to the very definition of quality. An ens considered apart from its be a quale is said to be taken in its immediacy.
