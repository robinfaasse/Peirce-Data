Professor C. S. Peirce,

Dear Sir:-

We shall be happy to send you two dozen copies of your letter of the 27th. If you have no objection to giving it to us, we should be glad to have a list of the names of professional men to whom you send the book that we may enter them on our records and thus save us the possible chance of sending a second copy to some gentlemen who have already been favored by you.

With reference to the cuts, we would say that all our mathematical authors furnish us copy for the diagrams the same as the MS. for the text. This is a part of the work of preparing a book for the press. It is not, however, necessary that the authors should prepare these diagrams in a style good enough for photo-engraving. We are not accustomed to engrave them that way, but by what is better, the wax process. To be done in this way, the diagrams may be sketched in pencil. The only thing absolutely necessary is that the engraver should be able to see exactly what the author wishes the diagram to show. If there are to be lines of two sorts, one thick and one thin, they must be indicated clearly. If some are to be dotted with long dots and others with short dots, that also must be perfectly clear.
