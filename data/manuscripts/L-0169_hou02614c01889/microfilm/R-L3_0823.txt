Milford, Pa. [date]1899 Nov 18[/date]

My dear Mr. Plimpton:

I learned today on returning to the hotel that that Washington place has been filled by a young fellow of no experience and whose "published papers" would not altogether compare with mine for any five years of my life. After the solemn promise made that no consideration except ability should "have any weight whatever," I consider that a great injustice has been done to me and a great blow has been struck to our office of weights and measures.

This will cause me to take still more interest in the Cyclopaedia of Practical Information. The more I look at it the greater its usefulness appears, notwithstanding some glaring weaknesses. I think you can make enough off it to encourage you to revise it. I shall set to work at once upon a careful examination of it. You express the opinion that the best thing would be to get 25 agents. I think too that that would be highly successful, if I train them & arrange their work. Please send me few agents outfits, - specimen books and I will at once get agents for the road from
