E.G.

March 2, 1895.

Mr. C.S. Peirce,

Dear Sir :-

Referring to your MS. just received, we would say that it seems to be in two parts, one a sort of mathematical philosophy, and the other more properly a text-book. Now the mathematical philosophy would meet with hardly any sale, however valuable it is ( and of its value we have no doubt); but Book V, which you call Metrics, is a proper text-book in Geometry. It is not only a good revision of your father's text-book, but much more than that. Now why cannot we make this suitable for the use of schools and colleges, publishing it by itself, and test the market with it, before undertaking the other?

Awaiting a reply from you , we are

Very truly yours,
