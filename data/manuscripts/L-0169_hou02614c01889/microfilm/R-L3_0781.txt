70 Fifth Ave., New York, May 21, 1894

Mr. Charles S. Pierce
Milford, Pa.

My dear Mr. Pierce:-

I have just got back to town, and find two letters from you-- one arriving this morning, and the other the other day.

Now I am mighty sorry that you are having such a hard time, but I trust it will be only temporary.

In regard to the anti-logarithmic chart, our people say it is a good thing, but they do not see any money in it, and furthermore they find that it doesn't pay us to handle single sheets of paper. You see, it is not a book, and it is so small that it is difficult to keep track of it. I haven't the slightest idea what the value of it is.

I trust you will pardon this delay in hearing from me.

Sincerely yours,
Geo A Plimpton

I wish I could see some way by which I could let you have some money but find at present that we have demands which ofter my own affairs. I am surprised that you still have difficulty raising it way on your property.
