July 10, 1894..

Prof. C. S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.

Dear sir;-

We have just received a report from the gentleman who read the manuscript of your Logic. He is not a philosopher, as Professor Royce is, for example, but he is a very successful teacher of the subject to college students. It is not necessary for us to make any further remarks on the importance of having a text-book reviewed by a practical teacher, after what we took the liberty of writing you, the other day, in explanation of our sending the manuscript to such a person.

The reviewer appreciates in a very high degree the unusual merits of your work, viewed from the standpoint of the philosopher of logic. He remarks, as your own recent letter indicates:

"It is a work for specialists in logic, not a treatise for anyone who has not this special knowledge."

"It is a serious critique of all those matters in logic which, during many years, have seemed to the author incorrectly stated or in need of a better interpretation."
