[date]Aug. 28, 1900[/date]

Mr. Charles S. Peirce

Milford, Pa.

My dear Mr. Peirce:-

Yours of the 26th is at hand. As to the Cyclopaedia, I do not believe it would be worth while for you to come down to talk with me about it. I am afraid that it is a dead dog now anyway. I wrote you that I would sell the books to you for one dollar apiece. I should be glad to sell the whole thing, plates, 1500 copies and all, to your friend for one thousand dollars cash.

As to the manuscript,- I do not recollect writing you that I was having it typewritten. I did acknowledge the receipt of what I supposed was a manuscript. I had the package put into the safe, hoping that I might hear from you in detail regarding it; but when I come to open it, I find that it is an account of the Decennial Celebration of Clark University. So I guess this time you have one on me.

I don't believe it is worth while for you to submit your Editor's Manual to Ginn & Co. Really, nothing is considered unless it is strictly a text-book.

As to cataloguing the books, my house is closed at present, and I doubt if it could be opened before November.

Sincerely yours,
Geo. A Plimpton
H.
