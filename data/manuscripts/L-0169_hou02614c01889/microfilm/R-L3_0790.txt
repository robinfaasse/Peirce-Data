I assent to your proposi-
tion to publish the Metrics alone, as
contained in your letter which I received
this PM. But I do not approve of it.

One book, that on the Fundamental
Properties of Space has the character you
mention of being concerned with the
"philosophy of Geometry." But it has
always been considered as proper to insert
in the elements the Postulates & Axioms.
This book is the same thing in a modern
form. I go on the principle it is wrong

propositions of geometry. At any rate it
must come in somewhere. There is nothing
"philosophical" about it. On the contrary,
it is peculiarly elementary. But by giving
a separate book to it, those teachers who
chose to do so can make it the last pro-
position by simply teaching topology after
metrics. I could insert a suggestion to
that effect in the preface. Let me beg you
to read that over again & to ask yourself if what
I say of it is not true.

The other book which precedes metrics is
Graphics. There is certainly no "philosophy"
about it. It contains merely the elementary
propositions of graphics. Perhaps it
goes too far. About this I was doubtful.
I reached the decision I did by considering
that many pupils will go no further in
mathematics than this treatise & I had
better insert all that will be practically useful
to them & which is not too difficult. I am
decidedly of opinion graphics ought to be
studied, because of its extreme utility, and
because further exercises of the generalizing
power and of the imagination are requisite
to successful teaching of geometry before
