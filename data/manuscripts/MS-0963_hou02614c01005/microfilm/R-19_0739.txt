By the Principles of Association are meant the general rules according to which one idea

has a tendency to suggest another. Most psychologists reduce these to two, the principle of

contiguity or coadjacency and the principle of similarity. The principle of contiguity

is that an idea is apt to be accompanied and followed by those ideas by which it has been

accompanied and followed on previous occasions; and this tendency is the stronger the

oftener such previous association has occurred. The principle of similarity is that

an idea is apt to suggest others similar or analogous to it. Thus, the thought of Niagara

may suggest a hero or anything else that is grand, and so similar to the cataracts, or it

may suggest a crowd of hack-drivers, which is connected with the place in every

visitor's experience.
