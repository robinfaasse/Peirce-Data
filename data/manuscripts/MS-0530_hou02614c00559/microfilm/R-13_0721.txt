The proposition is therefore of a kind which I have expressly
excluded from present consideration. Besides conditionals,
the Conimbricenses admit Causals; as do the [del]Port[/del] Royalists
and their followers. A causal is a proposition [ins]formally[/ins] involving 'be-
cause', or some similar conjunction. Burgersdicius admits,
beside the traditional three species, also Adversatives (propo-
sitions formally involving 'but', or 'although', of some [del]similar[/del] [ins]similar[/ins]
conjunction) and Relatives (propositions with 'where', 'when',
'as', etc). Blambergs list (in the Logica Contracta) is the
same, except that Adversatives are called Discretives. Milton
calls them Discretes. L'Art de Penser admits all six of the
species mentioned.

On the other hand, there has been a general tendency
to drop Copulatives from the list. Since Aldrich, they
have been universally omitted. Mill even produces a
