If no man at present is able to fly, De Morgan would
say that the 'classes' of men and of flying things are each
'an external of' the other or are 'coexternals', and that
the power of flying is 'repugnant' to humanity. Now
[u]external[/u] is a common word in philosophical terminology,
and has several well-recognized acceptations; but not
this one. Nor is it advisable to adopt it; for here existing
terminology is rich. The most familiar and unambiguous
expression is to say that the classes in question are [u]ex-[/u]
[u]clusive[/u] each of the other, or are coexclusive. As for
[u]repugnant[/u], De Morgan could easily have defended his
use of the term by a dozen most respectable authorities.

contradiction. Among these are [del]Hamilton and[/del] Prantl and
