If we term a proposition dualistic which simply admits or
excludes each of these, there are (as Mrs. Franklin
showed) 16 [del]dualistic[/del] [ins]formulae[/ins] propositions. Representing the
copulas or conjunctions by employing transverse
lines to cut off the excluded cases, the 16 copulas will
be as follows:

$S \bar{\vee} P$, [del]Anything is nothing (absurd)[/del] [ins]asserting an absurdity that is other[/ins]
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is both at once $S$ and $P$,
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is $S$ but not $P$.
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is neither $S$ nor $P$.
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is not $S$, but is $P$.
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is either at once $S$ and $P$ or neither $S$ nor $P$.
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is either $S$ and not $P$, or $P$ and not $S$.
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is $S$.
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is $P$.
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is not $S$,
$S \bar{\vee} P$, Anything is not $P$,
