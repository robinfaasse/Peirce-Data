A little further down [ins](cap 12)[/ins] he defines the word thus: "quidquid repugnat, id ejusmodi est, ut cohaerere numquam possit." He also applies [ins]to the word[/ins] comparatives and superlatives, [del]to the word[/del] which are inapplicable to contradiction. * In the second century of our era the word had the same meaning; for Aulus Gellius (xvi. viii. 12) says: "Est item aliud, quod Graeci 'διεζευγμένον' [ins]ἀξίωμα[/ins], nos disiunctum dicimus. Id hujuscemodi est: aut malum est voluptas aut bonum aut neque bonum neque malum est. Omnia autem, quod disiunguntur, pugnantia inter sese oportet."

The opinion here expressed about disjunctives is bad; but it is familiar and ancient. This Stoic doctrine [del]but it[/del] has always been common enough. He certainly could not mean to say that the members of a disjunction [del]must[/del] ought to be contradictories.
