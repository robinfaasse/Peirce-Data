[u]Notation for the General Algebra of Logic[/u]. A proposition in the
general algebra of logic consists of two parts. The first is the series
of quantifiers, or directions for the order and manner of choosing the
individuals of the set to which the [del]oth[/del] second part of the proposition,
called the Boolean, (since it is an expression of the Boolean al-
gebra) is written. The first part collectively may be called the
Hopkinsian, since it was developed in the Johns Hopkins Uni-
versity. According the practice now in vogue (introduced by me
in [date]1870[/date]) the letters Σ and Π are employed for this purpose.
