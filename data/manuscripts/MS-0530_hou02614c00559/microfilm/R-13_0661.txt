It will therefore not be necessary, [ins]or advisable,[/ins] to have independent signs for copulas and conjunctions; for every copula is so closely connected with a conjunction that the notation should show the connection. I will, therefore, at once consider signs of conjunction.

Any conjunction of classes [ins]or of relatives[/ins] can be reduced to a conjunction of propositions, after we have introduced a series of proper names each which are to be assigned, in a certain order, each to whatever individual in the universe, an enlightened defender or an enlightened opponent of the proposition may choose. Thus, to say that every mother loves something of which she is a mother, is the same as to say, "Take any individual, [u]i[/u], then there is an individual, [u]j[/u], such that either [u]i[/u] is not mother of [u]j[/u] or [u]i[/u] loves [u]j[/u]. We have, therefore, first to consider signs for the conjunction of propositions.
