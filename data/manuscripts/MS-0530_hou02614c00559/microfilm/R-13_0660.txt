Prof. O. H. Mitchell, in his epochal paper in the [u]Johns[/u]
[u]Hopkins Studies in Logic[/u], shows that every proposi-
tion may be regarded as a description of the universe.
It follows from this that every 'copula' is of the nature
of a logical sign of combination. Thus, De Morgan's
eight copulas are as follows:

$X))Y$ Every $X$ is a $Y$; i.e. Anything is either not $X$ or is $Y$.
$X\cdot(\cdot Y$ No $X$ is a $Y$; i.e. Anything is either not $X$ or not $Y$.
$X(\cdot)Y$ Everything is either $X$ or $Y$.
$X((Y$ Every $Y$ is an $X$; i.e. Anything is either $X$ or not $Y$.
$X(\cdot(Y$ Some $X$ is not $Y$; i.e. Anything is coexistent with an $X$ that is not $Y$.
$X(\cdot)Y$ Some $X$ is $Y$; i.e. Anything is coexistent with an $X$ that is $Y$.
$X)(Y$ There is something besides $X$'s and $Y$'s; i.e. Anything is coexistent with a non-$X$ non-$Y$.
$X)\cdot)Y$ Some $Y$ is not $X$; i.e. Anything is coexistent with a non-$X$ that is $Y$.
