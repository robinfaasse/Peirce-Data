It would be ridiculous to ignore the usage of twenty centuries in order to adhere to the probable use of some unknown writer. We must then conclude that the proper sense of the word is that given by De Morgan. It will, however, be proper to revert to the original meaning in order to determine an uncertain point in the meaning.

Namely, we ought to call two [del]marks[/del] [ins]predicates[/ins] [u]Repugnant[/u], if [del]they never are[/del] there is, in fact, no individual to which they both apply, whether in their own nature it is logically possible that they should be conjoined or not. For on this point authority is divided; and since the [del]pro[/del] [ins]xy[/ins] had reference to factual existence, not to ideas, we ought to allow this circumstance to decide the doubtful point.
