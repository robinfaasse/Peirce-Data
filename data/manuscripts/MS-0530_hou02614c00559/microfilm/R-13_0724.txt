If 'some antediluvian was not long-lived', De Morgan
would say that the class of antediluvians is 'an exient of' the
class of the long-lived, while the latter class is 'a deficient of' the
former. [u]Exient[/u], though it might better be made an adjective than
a substantive, is a useful contribution to our language to denote
that which extends beyond something else. [u]Deficient[/u] is already
a term of philosophy in two senses; first, as opposed to redundant,
and to denote that which lacks something belonging to a perfect
object of its class; and secondly, in the phrase [u]deficient cause[/u].
