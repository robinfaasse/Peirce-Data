That is 'much' which a man thoroughly well acquainted
with the force of words might call much in the endeavor to
express the truth; and [del]of[/del] [ins]that[/ins] a person who calls such a thing 'much',
it must be admitted [del]that he[/del] speaks the truth, even though the same
thing might also be called little, that is, not much, in an
endeavor by a perfect master of the language to speak the truth.
Thus, the characteristic of the vague statement is that it
may be both asserted and denied with truth. The principle [ins]of[/ins]
contradiction does not apply to it. Consequently, that to
which this principle does apply is not vague, but definite.*
So we have expelled the vagueness from "vagueness."

In so far as a proposition is vague, the information
it conveys is defective. But this result may equally
be due to the proposition's being general. Vagueness
and generality are not to be confused.
