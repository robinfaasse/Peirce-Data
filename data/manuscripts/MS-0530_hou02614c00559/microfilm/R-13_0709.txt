It seems likely that this was the original meaning in
Greek, as it was of [u]contra dicere[/u] in Latin, where it had
a legal flavor. [u]Contradictio[/u] as one word appears in Quin-
tilian, Tacitus, etc., but as a regular term of logic, along
with [u]contradictoriae[/u], first in Boethius (about A.D. 500.)

The words were in universal use throughout the middle
ages. [u]Contradictory[/u] and [u]contradiction[/u] have been used in
the same precise sense by all logicians writing English
(except only De Morgan) beginning with Thomas Wilson
in 1551 ([u]Rule of Reason[/u], Ed. of 1553, Fol 20 a: "contradictorie
propositions;" Fol. 53a: "sentences gainsaying, otherwise
called [del]contradictions[/del] [ins]contradictories[/ins]"). The verb appears soon after 1600.
