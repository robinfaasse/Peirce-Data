[u]καθ' ἕκαστον[/u], translated singular. Singulars, or the loose
logic of modern [del]books[/del] treatises, have been confounded [del]two[/del]
often with individuals, the element of definiteness being
disregarded. Every proposition must refer to something
singular. It evidently will be so if the proposition mentions
any thing with which the utterer and interpreter are both
familiar[ins], and upon which they fully understand one another to have their minds directed.[/ins] It is only when the utterer talks of something
with which the interpreter is not familiar that his talk
becomes vague. Suppose, for instance, that he says, "A man
I knew had blue hair." He refers to the man vaguely, because
the interpreter did not know him, or did not know him
as well, — had not seen him; for if he had, [del]there no informa-[/del]
