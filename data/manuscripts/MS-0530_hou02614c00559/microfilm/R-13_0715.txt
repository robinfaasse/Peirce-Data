Very many show more or less aversion to this use of
the word 'hypothetical'; and many use, either along with
it, or instead of it, some appellation like complex. Thus,
Wilson, whose Rule of Reason appeared in 1551 (but I use
the edition of 1553), speaks of 'Propositio hypothetica, that
is to saie a double Proposition'. Blundevile talks of the
'Compound or hypotheticall proposition'. [ins]Bain and others still approve of this.[/ins] John Milton, a
Ramist, about 1640, treats of 'Axioma compositum, vulgò,
