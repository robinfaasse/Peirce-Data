It will be seen that all the principle classifications of [del]every[/del]
[del]science[/del] [ins]human[/ins] will be given in the book, and the brief explanations
which will accompany these will make it a little syllabus of
human knowledge, so far as general ideas are concerned; so
that in this respect it will be an exceedingly useful book
which people will often look into. I shall do everything to make
it the most useful general [u]vade mecum[/u] ever put together.

At the same time, I shall not forget that its special utility
is to enable people to find [del]the very[/del] best word for a given idea. One
thing requiring attention throughout will be [u]accepted metaphors[/u].
Whatever their origin, whether it is mere custom or goes deeper into human
nature, it is very important to a person seeking expression for his
idea to be reminded that a given kind of metaphor is usual. For
instance, under traits of character he can refer to zoology (occasional)
to habits of growth of plants, creepers, vines, parasites, oaks, pines.) So under
moods of emotion, colors are called to mind, etc.

I shall make great use of little hieroglyphics to express the
general [del]character[/del] [ins]tones[/ins] of [del]a[/del] words, and as to intensity, dignity, [del]or[/del]
favor or disfavor, etc. I shall also have a character to show that
valuable explanations are found in the C.D. and another to
show that the quotations of the C.D. illustrate particularly well
the character of the word.

As everybody who uses Roget exclaims "Was there ever a book so sure not
to tell me what I want to know," so my object will be to make this a book
which everybody will say is the most sure to give something pertinent and valuable.
