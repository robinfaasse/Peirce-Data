(Dictated.)

NEW YORK, [date]April 12, 1890.[/date]

Benjamin E. Smith, Esq.,

Dear sir:

I sent you last evening my suggestions for the words Metagnostic and Metagnosticism. To-day I should like to add under the latter word, so that the last definition shall read as follows: the doctrine of the Ultimate Reality--the sole existence--The All in All.

I also send you definitions of the word Metanoetic, which I urgently desire to have crowded in if possible in any way. I do not extend the definitions to the word Metanoetics, only because I do not desire to crowd this matter upon you over much, feeling that I have been at fault myself in not bringing it to your attention fully at an earlier day.

Permit me to say, however, that I have in mind and have used in my work the additional cognate words metagnosis [del]and metagnoiology[/del]. [ins]The latter words belongs over against[/ins] the word agnoiology, which you have defined as belonging to "the doctrine or theory of ignorance, which seeks to determine what we are necessarily ignorant of". The word I suggest would cover the doctrine or theory of knowledge, and which seeks to determine what we may acquire knowledge of and what may arise in consciousness beyond knowledge considered relatively.

I should like to know your further views in regard to space for all the words I have attempted to define by suggestion; and I should also like to see exactly what you finally settle upon, including the Greek derivatives and their definitions. I don't like the word "after" used by you in the definition of the word metagnostics. I think the word [u]beyond[/u] is better and corresponds more accurately with the word "transcending" used in the definition, and also [del]with[/del] the word metaphysics, which certainly would be better defined as [u]beyond[/u] physics, rather than as [u]after[/u] physics.

I shall be glad to respond to a call for further interview at almost any time. Thanking you for your past courtesy, I am,

Very truly yours,
James A Skilton
