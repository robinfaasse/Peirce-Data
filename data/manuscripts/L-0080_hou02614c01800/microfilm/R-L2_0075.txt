From the Sword and [ins]Trowel[/ins] [del]Journal[/del] for March 1890
by Charles H. Spurgeon.

"The Century Dictionary, an Encyclopedic
Lexicon of the English Language. Prepared
under the [del]direction[/del] superintendence of William
Dwight Whitney Ph.D. LL.D., Professor of Com
parative Philology and Sanskrit in Yale
University" —

This is the dictionary of the century and
it will be a century before it is eclipsed.
To produce it is not merely a labor, but an
Enterprise. We have heard that if the pro-
prietors could have foreseen the vast expense
and the tremendous toil, they would never have
ventured upon the enormous task. We have
Vol. I. now before us. It contains all the words
a mortal man can need to use and a great
many more. Technical terms, such as we
have never met with, are here in profusion.
The explanations are full, though brief, and
were evidently prepared by specialists.
We know of no work which can rival it.
Nothing remains to be desired but the money
wherewith to purchase it. To the literary
man it is a necessity, and no one who can
afford its purchase should view it in any
other light.
C.H.S.
