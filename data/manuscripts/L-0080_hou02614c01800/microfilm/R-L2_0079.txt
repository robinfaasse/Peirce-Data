COMMITTEE ON TERRITORIES,
UNITED STATES SENATE,

March 20th, , 1891.

My dear Mr. Johnson;

I am booked for an address at the Patent Centennial celebration here next month, and in exploring a somewhat vacant brain for material, this, query suggests itself. When did man begin to reckon time[ins], by Centuries[/ins] and how does it come about that we regard a hundred years in the world's life very much as we regard a year in human life, and have a kind of birthday anniversary at the commencement of the new century? As you are one of the Editors of the Century I thought you might perhaps tell me. Were centuries thought of when they reckoned from the building of Rome or in Egyptian or Assyrian chronology, or is this method of computing time one of the modern inventions? Its earliest application so far as my observation goes, commences with the Christain era and we now talk of the first and second and third centuries &c, reckoning from that date. But when did we begin to do that, and why? I know these inquiries are not very practical in their nature, but my curiosity is rather stirred up.

Yours truly,
O. H. Platt

R. U. Johnson, Esq.,
New York.
