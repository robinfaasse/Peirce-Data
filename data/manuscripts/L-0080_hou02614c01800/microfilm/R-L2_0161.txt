take a stand monstrously opposed to the principles of
their own science. The written & spoken languages are
two different dialects. The purposes to which they are
put are so widely different that they cannot be
brought together without rendering one or other or
both less adapted to its special uses. But it is easy
to see which would suffer. Speech influences print very
little. Print influences talk far too much. It is to set the
iron & the earthen vessel afloat together. Print needs
to be stationary, because books continue to be used for
centuries. Talk needs to be plastic because in five minutes
it is all over & the conditions of life are changing swiftly
in our days. Therefore I want to rescue the poor earthen
vessel from bumping up against its iron brother. Hence
I say, let spelling be as it is. Nevermind its being unlike
pronunciation. So much the better.

As to punctuation my experiments show that the present
tendency is not to punctuate enough. People have to
re-read to make sure of the meaning. I would come
much nearer Wilson. But I can draw up rules that
will vastly simplify the system & exhibit its logic.

I don't suppose either of these extensive studies will
ever get into print, — unless I should fall in with
a zealous collaborator.
