There are thirteen different opinions now more or less in vogue as to what logic reposes upon. This diversity arises chiefly from two misunderstandings. In the first place, here, just as through all the walks of life, men cannot see that it is downright nonsense to find fault at what is absolutely incapable of being changed. It is simply silly. To talk of the good or bad logic of mental operations not in the least subject to self-control, is as absurd as to talk of the logic of a comet. But where there is no good or bad logic possible, one [del]thinks[/del] a mental product cannot be said to logically require, or assume, anything as a premiss. Yet quantities of writers reason just like this (I don't mean use this same reasoning, for theirs always involves additional faults): "When a man judges anything to be evident, he virtually assumes that whatever he thinks is infallibly true." In truth, he does not virtually assume anything, because his judgment not being subject to self-control is not open to logical criticism. Nor does he think at all about himself or his thinking. In the second place, people imagine that every fact about stars is a [del]discovery of[/del] [ins]discovery in[/ins] astronomy, and every fact about men is a discovery of anthropology. On the contrary, there are matters of ordinary knowledge about the one and the other which are perfectly indisputable, and were so, before these sciences were ever developed. Logic is made to repose on psychology simply because it recognizes facts of universal human experience. Those facts were not found out by scientific psychology. There is a special branch of science whose business it is to study these facts and describe them in general terms. That science, phenomenology, [del]needs[/del] [ins]no aid[/ins] from logic and logic ought to consult it. But psychology cannot take a step safely, without appealing to scientific logic and therefore it won't do for logic to appeal to it. Some go so far as to make logic depend on metaphysics, although the whole difficulty of metaphysics is that the students of it do not strictly follow the principles of logic. Two sciences cannot reciprocally draw their fundamental principles from one another.
