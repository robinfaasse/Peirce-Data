Now in regard to the cost of it, the only way to avoid its
outrunning all measure is to make it upon a Procrustean
Bed. The work will be laid out into parts. The preparation
of the [del]a[/del] [ins]y[/ins] copy involves three things, the formation of the skeleton,
the filling in of all the words, and the writing of it. Of course
the classification has been the subject of an incredible amount
of labor already. Hence, if I allow 3 months to form the skeleton,
namely, 1 month for the first making of it, 1 month to improve
it, and 1 month to revise it, I shall have a skeleton which
will be excellent except as to very slight modifications of
its smallest articulations which may suggest themselves in
writing the book. I have made a good many trials and studies
as to the method of going through the dictionary and inserting
the words. I know already pretty closely how to do it and how fast
I can work. Still, I should devote a month to learning the trade
before I started on the real business. I should then go through the
Century Dictionary at the uniform rate of 20 pages a day for 6
days in the week. Any falling behind would have to be made up
on Sundays. Still, it would not be safe to allow more than 100 pages
per week, as there might be hitches. I should thus have all the words
of the Dictionary distributed under about 1500 heads, with
certain subdivisions running cross-wise through the whole. I should
have occupied 75 weeks.
