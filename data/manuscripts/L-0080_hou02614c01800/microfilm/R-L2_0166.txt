There are several subjects which might be considered as belonging to [del]our[/del] logic or to metaphysics and might be considered to belong to some other department.

The first of these are two parts of the mathematics of logic. The first is the logic of necessary reasoning. It is more mathematical than logical, embracing the whole of the logic of relatives. But as it is acknowledged that I am one of those who have done most to advance this subject, I should like to have this department.

The other branch of the mathematics of logic is the doctrine of probabilities upon which I have also done much original work; and I should like to have this. It is fully as logical as it is mathematical.

Then there is the logico-mathematical work, the theory of ordinal and cardinal numbers. I am constantly appealed to by the workers in this branch & have made some discoveries in it, one of which is highly important. I have a book on the subject under weigh which I think will be marked by its presenting all that is known with perfect clearness and at no excessive length. The subject is, any way, more logical than mathematical; and I should like to cover this. If your mathematician, however, wishes to take over all these topics in the latter part of Schönfeld's Report, I should only be too glad.

Closely connected with this is the question of the logic of the calculus etc. concerning which my views have been much disputed. I acknowledge that the treatment now usual is sound. But it is so abstruse and intricate that it is impossible to teach the calculus in all its strictness. Now what I maintain is that the method of infinitesimals rightly understood is equally sound and much easier. It is purely a logical question and I have not taken the trouble to make a public reply to objectors who have not standing enough in logic. However, since no stronger ones appear, I intend to publish my reply which will be conclusive. I should like to take this subject as before, namely, [u]The logic of mathematics[/u].
