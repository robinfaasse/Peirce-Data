In regard to the profits to be derived from such a work, I
have been informed by a publisher who took pains to ascertain
the matter, that the sales of Roget are surprisingly large even
at this late day, half a century and more, since its appearance.
And yet Roget is a notoriously unsatisfactory thing, — a laugh-
ing-stock among all who have used it, — and [del]everybody[/del] [ins]has[/ins]
used it who writes at all. Mine would be so infinitely superior
that it would have, I believe, a decided boom at first.

Moreover, there is strong reason to think it would hold its
ground without serious competition for many years. For before
Roget nothing of the sort had been done since the rude attempt
of Bishop Wilkins in the middle of the 17[ins]th[/ins] Century. I don't count
such performances as the Visible World of Comenius which, as its
name implies, was chiefly limited to names for objects capable
of pictorial representation. In other languages it is the same, all
such books are mere translations of Roget. The reason of this
is that the task of classifying all the words of language, or
what is the same thing, all the ideas that seek expression, is the
most stupendous of logical tasks. Anybody but the most accom-
plished logician must break down in it utterly; and even for
