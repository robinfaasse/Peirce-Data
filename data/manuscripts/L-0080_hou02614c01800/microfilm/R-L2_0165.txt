P.O. Milford Pa. [date]1904 July 16[/date]

My dear Mr. Smith:

My departments in the new work being so differently bounded from what they formerly were, it will save vexations [del]disagreements[/del] if I now state just what I should like to cover.

In the first place, in the original dictionary, I gave my ideas of the classification of the sciences. I have since made a [u]much more[/u] thorough study of the subject than anybody seems ever to have done beginning by reading what has been written about by very nearly one hundred authors each with his own idea more or less unlike that of [del]any[/del] of the others. These authors, however, are nearly all of them divisible into those who understand the word science in the ancient sense, [greek]ἐπιστήμη[/greek], scientia, and into those who understand science in the sense of "systematized knowledge." I, however, mean by science the business of scientific men, which is only for some philosophers [greek]ἐπιστήμη[/greek] and only for those who are engaged in making textbooks and other digests is systematized knowledge. In the next place, the majority of those writers are endeavoring, more or less definitely, to classify [u]possible[/u] sciences. I limit myself to classifying science as it now exists or as there is a clear prospect that it soon will be. What we understand by a "natural classification" of possibilities, even when they are of such a nature that we can completely comprehend them, is, I think, out of the question. Thus, algebraic plane curves are of certain orders and classes easily definable. But that is a mere formal division quite unlike the natural classifications of chemistry and biology. But my "sciences," being the businesses of pretty distinct groups of real men, have their real affinities and relations, I recognize only the classification attributed to Comte as a beginning in the right way, & by a very full and careful study, I have worked up an elaborate scheme, of which what I gave in the 1st edition was a preliminary sketch, pretty accurate, considering how much less I then knew about the matter.
