South Bend is located on the St. Joseph River, one and three-quarter miles above the ancient Portage to the Kankakee River, a connecting link
between the basin of the Great Lakes and that of the Mississippi. LaSalle and his followers
made use of this historic highway.

SOUTH BEND, INDIANA, [date]June 9th, 1897[/date]

Mr. C. S. Peirce
New York.

Dear Sir

Your communication of June 5th to Mr Smith
of the Century Co. regarding the "league" used by
Charlevoix, and in use in New France during that
period has been forwarded to me. My attention
was called to this subject a short time ago,
when our society endeavored to harmonize the
distances given by Charlevoix (in "leagues") in his letters
of August 16 & September 17th, 1721 to the Madame la
Duchesse de Lesdiguieres, with the active survey in
miles. (I am informed that the Lenox Library, have the
London edition 1761 of these letters). In the letter of
August 16 he states that "the trading post of St. Joseph,
(on the St Joseph river) is 20 leagues from the mouth
of the river." The actual survey, makes the distance a trifle
over 60 miles. In his letter of September 17th he states
that the Portage from the St Joseph river is 2 1/4 leagues.
that he first followed the edge of the water, then across the
the field into a great prairie - to the source of the Kankakee
river. The active survey of [del]this[/del] [ins](Charlevoix's)[/ins] route makes the distance
6 3/4 miles. (This is Charlevoix' route) Charlevoix made a slight
detour, for he says, he encamped at an extremely beautiful
place called "Le Fort des Renards", a fortified village of the Outagamis
This site is well known to day, it is something like 1/4 mile off
the Portage route, making his route longer than the
Portage route of La Salle, who states in his writing, 17th
of November 1680, (2d trip via St Joseph river) that the Portage is 2 leagues
