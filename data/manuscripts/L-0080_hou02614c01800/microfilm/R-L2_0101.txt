[u]SOUTH BEND, INDIANA,[/u]

The survey of La Salle's portage, from the spot that
the portage leaves the St Joseph river to the source
of the Kankakee, is a trifle over 5 miles. This
harmonizes very closely to Charlevoix distance, allowing
for the detour to the "Fons des Renards" and the distance first
followed along the edge of the [del]H[/del] [ins]p[/ins]rairies.
These distances given above harmonize with the league
of 3.051+ miles, but not with a league the equivalent
to a less number of [ins]English[/ins] miles. You state in your letter to
Mr. Sennett, that "there was no such nice accuracy in road units".
It would seem that in most instances, [del]this[/del] [ins]that[/ins] is a correct conclusion,
but not in the ones I referred to. In sparks life of Marquette
see "Library of American Biography" [del]701-X[/del] [ins]"Harper Brothers" 1854[/ins] [ins]see[/ins] note, page 291, he states
that the league of Marquette, was 3 miles. (40 leagues
120 miles) - I will also refer you to, Vol IV. new
Edition of the Jesuit Relations, edited by R. Thwaites Sec'y.
Wis. Historical Society. See note on page 268 in which he
states that the league, was equal to, 84 Arpents of,
191.85 English feet, (or 3.051+ miles). This is also the conclusion
of the Chicago Historical Society, per Chas. Evans Sec'y - I am in
receipt of letters, from N. E. Dionne Librarian Bibliotheque de la
Legislature, de la Province de Quebec - and from Mr Douglas
Brymner, Public Archivist of the Dominion of Canada - both
of these gentlemen, give the French league as 3.051+
English miles. Thanking you for the attention given
this subject, and hoping to hear from you again
I remain with much respect.

Very truly Geo. A. Baker Sec'y.
