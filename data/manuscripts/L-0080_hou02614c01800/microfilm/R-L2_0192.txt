Mrs. Charles Sanders Peirce
Milford
Pike County
Pennsylvania

My dear Mrs. Peirce:

In accordance with your request for information as to the financial conditions under which we publish books, I am enclosing our printed contract form and am explaining it in general terms.

As a rule, the lowest rate of royalty we pay on an accepted book is ten percent of its retail price; and twice a year we supply the author (or [del]his[/del] [ins]a[/ins] representative) with a statement of the number of copies sold during the preceding six months and how much in royalties is due him, and then the royalties due are paid within the next thirty days. When a manuscript is accepted a contract in duplicate is made out. Both are signed by publisher and author (or the author's legal representative), and each keeps a copy. That is for the protection and guidance of both parties to the contract. If an author dies, his heirs thus have in writing what is due from and to his publisher.

This is the form and rate of most publishers' contracts. There are of course exceptional cases which demand exceptional treatment by contracts. Sometimes a publisher pays as much as twenty percent and (once in a long while) thirty percent of the retail price as royalty. Sometimes he makes an advance payment on royalties of a book to be published in the future. Occasionally, too, an author will waive royalties on the first thousand or two copies sold; and once in a blue moon an author will himself guarantee the publisher against loss on his book. But, as I say, the form enclosed, filled in with a ten percent royalty, would indicate the essentials of most of the publishers' contracts made in this country.

And all this applies to any kind of book. The probable sale of the book or series of books determines the rate of royalty and the question of the advances on royalty. A biography, a book of letters, or a scientific book may command as good terms as a probably popular novel.

I hope that the information here given, despite its necessarily general nature, will be helpful to you.

Sincerely,

Barry Benefield
Book Editor
[ins]Century Co.[/ins]
