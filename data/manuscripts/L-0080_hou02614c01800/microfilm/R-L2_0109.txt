My dear Mr. Smith:

One of the books which I have been working
at all my life, having begun when I was in College and steadily
pursued it since, — although of actual writing upon it I have
done little, — is what I have called an [u]Index Raisonnée of Words
and Ideas[/u]. It is, if you please, a Roget's Thesaurus, with three differences;
that the intellectual feebleness and ignorance of words of Roget is
something perfectly amazing, as any competent person can see
for himself who will devote an hour to the examination of
that work from that point of view, [del]and[/del] [ins]while[/ins] I have been train-
ing myself for the task on a good original basis during all these
years; [del]and[/del] secondly, that the system of philosophy which under-
lies Roget is one utterly obsolete; [del]and[/del] thirdly, that I should intro-
duce a number of important improvements, such as the distinct
indication of usual metaphors, the exact discrimination between
words leaving any exact meanings, the indication of the tone
and style to which other words are appropriate, etc.; and fourthly,
instead of the small selection words in Roget, I should propose to
insert all that are strictly good English, together with as many
more as are requisite for the [ins]exact[/ins] expression of all ideas now current.
