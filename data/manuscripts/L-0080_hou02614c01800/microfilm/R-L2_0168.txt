There may be a question whether epistemology belongs to logic
or to psychology. It unquestionably has to depend upon facts about
consciousness; but a good many such facts are no discoveries of
the science of psychology, and I do not believe that epistemology is
under any absolute need of appealing to that science, although
such appeal is sometimes very convenient. Therefore I think
that epistemology ought to belong to logic, to which it is usually
assigned.

Psychology is not the science of the soul, but is only the
science of the phenomena of consciousness. The subject of
the connection of soul and body belongs neither to psychology
nor to physics, which might as reasonably claim it. It belongs
to metaphysics of which it is the very heart, and is usually so
classed, though many psychologists dwell upon it. The
vocabulary of this subject is large and needs much overhauling
and I regard it as coming into my department.

I do not object to any amount of suggestion in any part
of what I have to look after, but on the contrary should warmly
welcome it. But I think it will be best to have such sugges-
tions laid before me.

I have myself a few little suggestions to submit outside of
my department. One is that that ridiculous list of bad
spellings of the philological society be dropped. It has had
its hearing before the bar of public opinion & the case has gone
against it. Strange that it never occurred to those gentlemen that
the English language not having any completely distinct dialect
for purposes of talk, it is highly desirable to keep speech and print
as separate as possible. Strange also to find a purely descriptive
science quarrelling with the objects of its study. We dont find entomologists proposing
to reform spiders.
