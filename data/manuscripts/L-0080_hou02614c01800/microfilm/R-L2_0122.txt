108 W 89th St. New York [date]1897 July 15[/date]

My dear Mr. Smith:

Herewith you will find papers to elucidate the plan of my [u]Book of Divisions[/u], or [u]Index raisonné of Ideas and Thesaurus of English Words[/u]. There has been unconscionable delay in sending these owing to extraordinary interruptions, the death of an intimate friend and partner in certain affairs, the necessity of my daily going to Wall Street about a sale of property, and also the necessity of my keeping up the work by which I am now earning a partial living. This delay does not indicate that there would be similar delay when I had once regularly taken up the new work.

The papers are intended to show,

1st, that the work would pay if done and what it would be like

2d, that it could be done [del]at[/del] [ins]for[/ins] the estimated expenditure of time [del]and[/del] [ins]and[/ins] that the method of work would be such that [del]monthly[/del] [ins]better still weekly[/ins] or [del]free[/del] [ins]weekly[/ins] examination occupying an hour each, would show that the work had progressed properly to the point calculated, and that, in the event of my death or disability at the end of two months or longer, the work would be so organized that another person could easily complete it.

In my previous letter I gave reasons for believing that the book would be highly profitable for a very long time. These I will not repeat; but I have devoted 20 minutes to running over Roget and jotting down such faults of classification as [del]I[/del] met my eye. You will find these on the sheet marked A.
