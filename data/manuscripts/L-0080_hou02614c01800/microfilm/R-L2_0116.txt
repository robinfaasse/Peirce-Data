Of course everything depends upon the classification
of the ideas. It will be perceived that the fundamental object
of the classification is the ideas, because it is with an
idea that the table, so to speak, is entered. What is required,
therefore, is a [ins]logical[/ins] classification of all ideas. This is the vast work
which has chiefly occupied all the time I have devoted to the
consideration of this projected book. The classification must
be philosophical in the most modern and catholic sense
of the term. It must also be so lucid that a person who knows
nothing of philosophy and won't give one minute to the study
of the classification can use it without the slightest embarrass-
ment. Not an easy problem to find such a classification.

I propose to put first all words describing qualities
of feeling or sensations. Here for example will be the qualities
[del]of the[/del] brought to our knowledge by the "five" senses. The color
names will be accurately given. It will amount to a little syllabus
of the doctrine of chromatics. The qualities of sound will be given.
