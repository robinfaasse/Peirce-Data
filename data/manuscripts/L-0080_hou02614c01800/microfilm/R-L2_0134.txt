[date]September 30, 1897.[/date]

Dear Professor Peirce,

It has been impossible, on account of the absence of the gentleman who decides such matters, to come to a conclusion with regard to your proposition before to-day.

As I have told you, the scheme strikes me personally as one of great merit, and I have no doubt that if the completed manuscript of such a book as you suggest could be placed before our publishers it would receive a very favorable consideration. In the form, however, in which you present it there appeared to be to them so many uncertainties as regards cost, expenditure of time, size, and possible market, that they are unwilling to assume the risk. You have of course thought these details out with great care, and are satisfied with your own estimates; but the practical experience of the office in such matters has shown the wisdom of caution. While, therefore, the publishers recognize the merits of the scheme I am instructed to say to you that it will not be possible for them to take it up in its present shape.

They are obliged to you for the opportunity to consider the matter and regret the delay in giving their reply, a delay which, under the circumstances I have explained to you, has been [del]un-[/del] unavoidable.

I return by express the manuscript which you have sent me.

Sincerely yours,
Benj. E. Smith
