3[/pn] I draw a [ins]plain and[/ins] heavy line between [del]arguments[/del] [ins]reasonings[/ins] that are
merely weak and reasonings that are [del]bad, that is[/del]
that are radically unsound. An argument which shall
bring ever so little sound reason for believing in its con-
clusion, and which does not profess to bring more, nor of
any other kind, than that sound reason which it does in fact
bring, may be ever so weak, but it is perfectly sound. But
an argument, no matter how much [ins]good[/ins] [del]sound[/del] reason it may
bring in support of its conclusion, which professes to bring
more than it really does, or professes to bring reason of a
kind different from that it really brings, is a very [del]trea-[/del]
[ins]cherous[/ins] thing to trust to, and is downright unsound.
