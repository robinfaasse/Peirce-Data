My other division of Deduction is into Necessary
and Probable. By Probable Deduction I mean simply
Deduction concerning Probability or any other function
of the "odds." I have never seen probability de-
fined without a vicious circle; but I define it
thus: Let a generic event occur in endless
repetition and at each occurrence let it be
noted whether or not it is of a given specific
kind. Let two tallies be kept during the occur-
rences; one of those that are and the other of those
that are not of the specific kind. And let the amount
of the former tally be divided by that of the latter after
each generic occurrence. This fraction will neces-
sarily change its value with every occurrence. Now,
unless it never changes from 0 or from 1. Now,
[del]unless[/del] if the specific kind of occurrence depends
on general circumstances that never change but
are not sufficient to prevent the fraction's chang-
ing its value, then every value of the quotient, with
one single exception, will sooner or later cease
to be traversed. I say a value is traversed, when
of two successive values of the quotient one is
greater and the other less than the value, I then
say is "traversed." The one sole value that will
never cease forever to be traversed is the value
of the odds in the "long run." This of course, is sort
of limit, but not a limit according to any defn.
of limit, but not a limit according to any defn.
nition previously given as far as I know.
