[u]Preliminary Theorems[/u].

Since number is a sum of equals, whatever two things are in
numerical ratio are sums of equals. Hence, if the total differences
between two pairs of objects are in numerical ratio, their dif-
ferences in every respect are in the same ratio & vice versa. Thus,
$(A^B - B^A) : (C^D - D^C) = (A^B - B^A)^{XY} : (C^D - D^C)^{XY}$
express that $(A^B - B^A)$ & $(C^D - D^C)$ are in numerical ratio.
From the above it follows immediately that
$(A^B - B^A) : (C^D - D^C) = (A^B - B^A)^{[XY]Z_{\Xi}} : (C^D - D^C)^{[XY]Z_{\Xi}}$
When two differences as the above are in numerical ratio
$A_B = C_D$ for did they not agree in the same respects they would
not be sums of equals. Hence
$(A^B - B^A) : (C^D - D^C) = (A^B - B^A)^{[XY]A_B} : (C^D - D^C)^{[XY]A_B}$

Let [fig]curly brackets grouping the four lines below[/fig] $\left. \begin{array}{l}
(M^P - P^M) : (M^R - R^M) = m \\
(M^Q - Q^M) : (M^R - R^M) = n \\
(M^Q - Q^M)^{P_R} : (M^P - P^M) \\
(M^R - R^M)^{P_R} : (M^R - R^M)
\end{array} \right\}$

Let $(T^E - E^T) : (T^F - F^T) = n$     $(T^G - G^T) : (T^H - H^T) = n'$
$(T^I - I^T) : (T^J - J^T) = n''$     $(T^K - K^T) : (T^L - L^T) = n'''$
$(T^I - I^T) = (T^E - E^T)^{T_I}$     $(T^J - J^T) = (T^F - F^T)^{T_I}$
$(T^K - K^T) = (T^G - G^T)^{T_K}$     $(T^L - L^T) = (T^H - H^T)^{T_K}$
$(T^K - K^T) = (T^I - I^T)^{T_K}$     $(T^L - L^T) = (T^J - J^T)^{T_K}$

Then it will follow from the above that
$(T^E - E^T) : (T^F - F^T) = (T^G - G^T) : (T^H - H^T)$
