[u]Singularities of Pairs of Terminals[/u]

A pair of points or planes may with reference [ins]to[/ins]
to the absolute have one of 6 singular situations.

1[u]st[/u] The two points or planes may coincide. Then the
distances of the points or angles between the
planes is zero.

2[u]nd[/u] one of the points may be on the absolute
or one of the planes tangent to the absolute.
Then the distance of the points or angle between
the planes is infinite.

3[u]rd[/u] both points may lie on one tangent to the
absolute or the lines may intersect on the
absolute. Then their distance [ins]or angle[/ins] is easily proved
to be zero.

4[u]th[/u] They may be harmonically situated relative
to the absolute. Namely, one point on the polar
of the other, or one [del]plane[/del] [ins]plane[/ins] the pole of the other.
In that case, it is easy to see their distance
or angle is a maximum [del]or minimum, since
an infinitesimal change will not alter it.[/del]
