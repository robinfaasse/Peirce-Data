I have invented two different algebraical notations for
expressing such statements briefly and so that the eye can
catch their meaning at a glance. They are explained very briefly
in [u]Studies in Logic[/u], published by Little and Brown, Boston, Mass.

The most important thing to remark about the selection
of subjects is, that if two are taken in different ways, one
as no matter what, the other suitably, it makes a difference
which is taken first. Thus, it is one thing to say that if anything
whatever, A, is taken, then something, B, can be found such
that B counteracts A; and it is much more to say that
something, B, can be found such that, no matter what
thing, A, be taken, B counteracts A. In ordinary language,
[ins]the[/ins] one proposition is, that everything is counteracted by some-
thing or other; the other [ins]is[/ins], that some one thing counteracts
all things.
