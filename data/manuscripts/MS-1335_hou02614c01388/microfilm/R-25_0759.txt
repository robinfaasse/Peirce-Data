Highest on the scale of generality must
be placed mathematics; because mathematics
is the mere study of our own ideas, as they
are, without asking whence they come. It
thus has nothing to do with any facts but
those it draws from within. True, the ideas
themselves may be suggested by circumstances
very special; but mathematics cares nothing
for that. Mathematics is thus like the
contemplation of a beautiful object; except
that the poet does not ask [u]any[/u] questions
while the mathematician does ask
what the relations of the parts of his
ideas to one another are.
