The work naturally divides itself into two parts, the first consisting of angular measures of the deviation of a ray by a diffraction plate, and the second of the linear measures by which the breadth of the diffraction-plate was compared with a metre.

The angular measures were made with a spectrometer, designed by Mr. Rutherfurd and myself, and admirably constructed by Messrs. Stackpole and Brother of New York. This spectrometer is provided with a glass circle, after the plan of Mr. Rutherfurd, and is represented in the plate. [ins]The measures made previous to 1879, were made with another spectrometer of somewhat similar construction.[/ins] [del]The circle is under no strain whatever during the measures and there is absolutely no wear due to use. The clamping was always very light.[/del]
