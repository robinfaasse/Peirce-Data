The deviations were all measured at the minimum
angle of deviation. The ruled surface of the plate was
first placed accurately so as to pass through the axis
of the instrument, (by means of a subsidiary lens
placed over the objective of the telescope. The center
of the field was found by reversing the micrometre,
and the point of the slit which appeared in this
centre was accurately marked. By means of the
reflections of the slit from the two sides of the
diffraction-plate (the telescope being at right angles
to the collimator) the latter was so adjusted that the
mean between its two faces should be parallel to the
axis of the instrument. In the best getters, the
faces were very nearly parallel. The lines on the
plate were then made parallel to the axis, with great
care, by making the spectrum of the marked point
of the slit fall on the centre of the field of the
telescope. The minimum angle of deviation was
found by means of a small plane mirror, fixed
upon the counterpoise of the telescope normal to
its axis. When the image of the slit reflected first
from the diffraction plate and then from this
mirror fell on the cross wires of the telescope the
ray of the spectrum then bisected was at the
minimum deviation. After thus finding the angle
of minimum deviation, the mirror was covered
with a velvet cap during the pointing on the spec-
tral line
