[u]Determination of the relative length of a wave
of light and a metre bar. By C. S. Peirce[/u]

[u]Part I.[/u]

The operations herein described have been undertaken
in order to obtain a check upon the spontaneous
changes of length to which bars used as standards of
length are known to be subject.

The work may be regarded as a natural sequel to
the long labours of Mr. Rutherfurd which have produced
the finest ruling-machine yet constructed, and most of
it has been done in his laboratory with the aid of his
trained assistant Mr. Chapman. In beginning it, I
expected to work in collaboration with Mr. Rutherfurd,
but circumstances have unhappily prevented his following
the work very closely, although I have in several critical
emergencies profited from his invaluable advice.

The work naturally divides itself into two parts, the
first consisting of angular measures of the deviation
of a ray by [del]a[/del] [ins]a[/ins] diffraction plate, and the second of
the linear [del]measures[/del] [ins]measures[/ins] by which the breadth of the diffraction-
plate was compared with a metre.

This spectrometer is provided with a glass circle, after
