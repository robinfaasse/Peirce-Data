D. APPLETON & CO.
72 FIFTH AVENUE,
NEW YORK.

Dear Mrs. Peirce:

As I promised
you, I have been looking
into the matter of the
translation of Dr. Hirsch's
book & I think that
there has been a misun-
derstanding which can be
easily rectified.

Mr. Peirce, as you know, wrote
me that he would be glad
to undertake any translation
we might have. A few
days afterwards, we happened
to have this book & Dr.
Hirsch's list & it seemed
to me a most happy
chance both for him & for
us. Arrangements were
most satisfactorily made
with Mr. Peirce, the only
condition we made was
that the translation should
be satisfactory. Our English
publishers had failed to
supply a satisfactory transla-
tion & we were anxious
to please Dr. Hirsch, as it was
our duty as well, having
accepted his book for
publication. The first
translation was most
satisfactory, but a certain
translation was not, owing
apparently to certain
changes Mr. Peirce had
made in his text. In
great zeal to improve
Dr. Hirsch's work. Naturally
