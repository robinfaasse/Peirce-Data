There are two classes of remains which exhibit to us the workings
of the Egyptian mind, if not upon science, at least upon highly sci-
entific arts, — arithmetic and medicine; for there are several
papyri which relate to these subjects. The medical papyri are the
more primitive and [del]substantially[/del] [ins]in much of their substance[/ins] the older, no doubt; but as manu-
scripts and as books the mathematical papyri are far the most an-
cient. The most important of the latter is the Rhind papyrus of the
British Museum, which has been pretty well translated and expounded
by Dr. August Eisenlohr.* It is an early hieratic MS in relatively
good preservation. It begins as follows:

"Rules for calculating the results (?) of things, the knowledge of everything
that is, of [all] obscurities, [of all mysteries], of all difficulties.
