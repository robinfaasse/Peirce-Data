Some writers[ins]*[/ins] have thought it impossible that the rule should have related to the slope of the arris, or slant-edge, because they say such a rule would not have been practicable for the workmen. They seem to think that the stones of 16 tons weight would be handled like ordinary bricks. [del]Every[/del] [ins]The placing of each[/ins] course of stones occupied a very long time, so that [del]rules[/del] [ins]rulers[/ins] would be specially made to measure the places of each. Every stone was cut for the precise place it was to occupy, and probably had a key, or projection, to fit into a corresponding socket of the stone beneath it. The business was not left to the workmen as the laying of bricks is left to them. Moreover, in [del]the[/del] placing each row of stones, the corner-stones, or something to represent them, would first be set; and a line would then be stretched to mark where the edges of those between them must come. It would, therefore, be precisely the arris-slope, and nothing else, which must be known. But for small pyramids or pyramids built of bricks or small stones, these considerations would not have the same weight.
