The typical Egyptian was a man of great passion and energy and
of penetrating intelligence. In engineering he was gigantic. His
religion, too, so far as it concerned his own salvation, was sincere,
practical, and earnest. But for ideas, philosophical, mathematical,
or even poetical, he had no taste. Every student of things Egyptian
early has his attention drawn to this characteristic of the race, —
for example, by the uncertainty of the Egyptian chronology. Whether
the [u]XIII[/u]th to [u]XVII[/u]th dynasties occupied five or fifteen centuries,
whether the [u]VII[/u]th to [u]XI[/u]th dynasties lasted one century or eight, there
