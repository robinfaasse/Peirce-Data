(Charles S. Peirce, Esqre, Milford Pa. N.Y.)

Dear Sir,

Having received your letter of [date]Oct. 7, 1890[/date], I had
answered it (though not to its full content) imme-
diately, and since then I have also written you a letter,
about the middle of January of the year instant,
wherein I hitherto I have not been favored with
an answer. However I hope that my two letters
have duly reached you, as also the first part of
my vol. 2, that I dispatched in [date]August 1891[/date].

After having in both letters I did not enter into
a certain topic advanced in your former letter,
and thereon I believe to be owing you an expla-
nation yet.

You had the kindness to propose sending me
your important, unprinted additions to the logic
of relatives, [del]with[/del] regretting that I had not been in pos-
session already of some of your (unprinted) exempli,
to insert them.

Fully as I appreciated from the first the confi-
dence you honored me with in making the said pro-
posal, and however highly I should have taxed
your further contributions intended for my
book, I did nevertheless abstain from (as might
have been expected) eagerly availing myself of the
valuable opportunity offered from several reasons,
that it will try to lay here before you.
