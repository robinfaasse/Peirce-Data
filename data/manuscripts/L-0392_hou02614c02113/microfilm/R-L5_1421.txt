My dear Professor Schröder

Relative to the problem from [u]a[/u] < x < y to determine
x, y, if the number of haecceities in the universe exceeds 3 [del]a[/del] [ins]a[/ins] can
be eliminated.

Thus, let A, B, C, D, be four individuals and [u]i[/u] any fifth.

Let x < A:i + A:d + B:a + B:c + c:a + c:c + d:b + d:d + i:a + i:b
y < A:a + A:c + B:b + B:d + c:b + c:d + d:a + d:c + i:a + i:b

It makes no difference about i:c, i:d, i:i, c:i, d:i.

You will see then that [del]a[/del] < x < y, so that [u]a[/u] is eliminated.

I have sent a long and tolerably careful review to Carus. You
know what kind of a man he is. You eat a bushel of salt with him
and you have not reached the conception of what tricks he is capable.
[del]But[/del] what Carus leaves unprinted I shall print in the Bulletin of the
American Mathematical Society, — a most respectable journal &
the the two I shall circulate together. I fear that Carus will peck
my article to pieces & print it so that my real opinion may not
be seen. But if so (it is mere surmise, — I don't like those Pome-
ranians) the correctorium patris Pauli is in my hands.

My wife is dreadfully ill. The doctor says to me brutally, "We
think the thing to be done is to extirpate womb & ovaries." A pretty
speech!
