to be at their disposal after my death.

I beg you to write to the Hon. Abram S.

to say that you consider that I come under the [del]second[/del] head of an

expressions of opinion, I cannot doubt that you will find
this conformed to your belief. I shall, for my part, be warmly
in favor of some of the money being employed to aid the com-
pletion of your great work. I beg you will write without
delay. If you like you may address your letter to Dr. Daniel
C. Gilman, President of the Carnegie Institution. It makes
no difference.

I have done an enormous quantity of work, covering all
parts of logic; but I cannot afford the time to put it into
shape (even if there were means of publication) without
my support as assured.
