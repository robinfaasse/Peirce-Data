My dear Sir,

Being in possession of your two letters of last year (29th April, and the second undated) I can only now express my deep regret that they have so very long remained unanswered by me, especially the second one, which called me to your succour in a certain sense.

But, since, every day has brought its own particular vexations to such an extent, that I have never been left with sufficient energy to meet the demands of my private correspondence. I simply could not answer you before now. It would lead me too far, were I to attempt to enumerate the multifarious obligations laid upon me. Apart from my calling, I have never been spared worries of various kinds, sometimes also I have felt the effects of overwork, and been forced to take my health into consideration. Already my official duties are heavy, and with examinations, sessions, certifications, correspondence, pertaining [ins]to[/ins] appointments of past professors, and so on, by far exceed what can be guessed from our program of my lectures. Moreover the publication of my volumes has drawn upon me a large amount of correspondence domestic and foreign (in different languages!), that makes me regret the want of a secretary, whom I am not in a position to provide myself with. Indeed I experience all the disadvantages of fame without its emoluments.

B. The §45 is not the section § 28 (or § 29), and, as you say, referred to by me in vol. 1 (I cannot find where, just now). And, that does not matter, in as much the reader is there prepared (cf. vol. 2, p. 18 the words: "we shall and also right the sub-classes" — I am sorry not to have underhand to refer the proper paragraph to the subclass of classes of incoming and those restricted to the values 0 or 1, whereupon this §45 will prove a necessary consequence of; in any rate the section following § 28 and mostly in connection with it, is that what I intended to call "paragraph called".

Your assertion that both calculuses are one — considered in the light of your own theory of (binary) relations — would amount to saying: That your "absolute terms" or classes, are called by me "Gebiete" or systems, and logically behaved like relation as (e.g. $a_{11}$), used throughout, namely following the rules and formal laws of those relations, [del]which[/del] [ins]which[/ins] I have originally termed "one-equivalent Relative" (or "most distinguished", "prominent" relations), being equivalent of the values, 0 and 1. And such relations, indeed, are to be distinguished! If you are using, for testing, these values and the behavior of them, you are doing, in the same case, whereof I am speaking, by and with the system, (i.e. the relation, $a_{11}$); this is a very useful identification, of vol. 2 — this, indeed, by the by, I wish you might not overlook).

I should be sorry if the course of your message or not having clearly understood the point, was in any way to be attribute to the deficiencies of my exposition.

And with this I hope that at least one point of our Peirce, wherein you find me unsatisfactory (page 34, 26) is at the last, as between us privately, settled, but before the public only remains yet unaccomplished.
