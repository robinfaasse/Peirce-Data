I express this by writing $\Pi_{i} \ a_{i} \prec b_{i} = \Pi_{i} \ \bar{a}_{i} + b_{i} = \Pi_{i} \{ (a_{i} = 0) + (b_{i} = 1) \}$. To express "either $a = 0$ or $b = 1$" in the sense you mean, I should write $(\Pi_{i} \ a_{i} = 0) + (\Pi_{i} \ b_{i} = 1)$. Thus, everything which can be expressed by the [del]statement[/del] [ins]class[/ins]-calculus is readily expressed by the statement-calculus, if the quantifiers are used. But if the quantifiers are [u]not[/u] used, an ordinary hypothetical proposition cannot be expressed.
