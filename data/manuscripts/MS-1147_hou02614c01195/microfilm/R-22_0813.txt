To say that the value so expressed is the "infinity-eth" or infinitesimal
member of the series of fractions, would be to use a self-contra-
dictory expression. It might be allowed as a figure of speech; but,
in fact, though [del]this[/del] [ins]one[/ins] need not here be insisted upon, it is a sort of
generalization of the highest value in mathematics. No mind
not keen enough to steer its way safely among the rocks and shoals
of such generalizations is fit for the business of the mathematician.

Now, whenever an endless series of objects obeys such a law
that, if all the members of the series from the beginning up to any
member exclusive are known, the law shows what that member
will be, [ins]to[/ins] that series the Fermatian inference can be applied, in
the following form:

[u]First Premiss[/u]. All the members of the series up to the M[ins]th[/ins] inclusive, stand, in their
order, in the relation, R, to one another;

[u]Second Premiss[/u]. If all the members of the series up to the N[ins]th[/ins] exclusive stand
[ins]in their order[/ins] in the relation, R, to one another, and if N > M, then all the members of the
series up to the N[ins]th[/ins] inclusive stand, in their order, in the relation, R,
to one another;

[u]Conclusion[/u]. All the members of the series stand, in their order, in the relation, R,
to one another.
