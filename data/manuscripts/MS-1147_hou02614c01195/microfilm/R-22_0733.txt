[u]Converse[/u] ([del]See[/del] [ins]Converse[/ins]) the relation or relative expression the relation
of the correlate of a dual relation or relative to its relate.
Thus if the individual, A, stands in the relation, [u]r[/u], to the
individual, B, then B stands in the converse relation,
(called "[u]r[/u]-verse" by De Morgan) to A. Peirce usually
abbreviates these expressions by saying that 'A is [u]r[/u] to B'
and 'B is [u]r[/u] to A'. In the case of triadic relations there
is a group of six relatives which can be obtained [del]by[/del] from
any one by alternately using to operations. Thus, we have
[fig]Diagram of relations with arrows and text[/fig] Sortes dat asinum Platonis
Sortes dat Platoni asinum
Plato datur ab Sorte asinum
Plato datur asinum ab Sorte
asinum datur Platoni ab Sorte
asinum datur ab Sorte Platoni
The further extension of the rule is supplied by the known
mathematical substitutions.
