Mnemonic Verses.

Certain traditional verses of logic containing also
mnemonic words. They are found in the compendium of
Shyreswood, who died 1249, [ins]possibly far older.[/ins] but are probably older[del]s[/del], [ins]}[/ins] They come
to us through the celebrated Summulae Logicales of Petrus Hispanus,
whose versions here follow. Those marked S are given also by
Shyreswood, those marked L by Lambertus.

1. Instrumenta novem sunt, guttur, lingua palatum
Quattuor et dentes, et duo labra simul.

[Many copies change the last line, no doubt to avoid requiring the
boys to scan the pentameter, [del]where a d is known for a t[/del]]

2. [del]S.[/del] Quae ca. vel hyp., Qualis ne. vel aff., u Quanta par. in. sing.
["Quae" [ins]inquires ratio[/ins] asks for the substance, 'qualis' what is the quality, 'quanta' what
is the quantity of the proposition. The substance is 'ca', categorical, or
hyp., hypothetical; the quality is ne, negative, or aff., affirmative;
the quantity [ins]is[/ins] u, universal, par, particular, in, indefinite, or
sing, singular.]
