Nor is it easy to show that if any three numbers, A, B, C, are
such that $(1+\frac{A}{B})^3 > 2$, while $(1+\frac{B}{C})^3 < 2$, but $2 - (1+\frac{B}{C})^3 < (1+\frac{A}{B})^3 - 2$,
then $2 < (1+\frac{C}{A+3B+3C})^3 < (1+\frac{A}{B})^3$, and $(1+\frac{C}{A+3B+3C})^3 - 2 < 2 - (1+\frac{B}{C})^3$;
and so if $(1+\frac{A}{B})^3 < 2$ and $(1+\frac{B}{C})^3 > 2$, but $(1+\frac{B}{C})^3 - 2 < 2 - (1+\frac{A}{B})^3$, then
$2 > (1+\frac{C}{A+3B+3C})^3 > (1+\frac{A}{B})^3$, and $2 - (1+\frac{C}{A+3B+3C})^3 < (1+\frac{B}{C})^3 - 2$.

Consequently, if one start with A=0, B=1, C=3, when $(1+\frac{0}{1})^3 < 2$ but
$(1+\frac{1}{3})^3 > 2$, and [del]$(1+\frac{1}{3})^3 - 2 < 2 - (1+\frac{0}{1})^3$[/del] [ins]conceived[/ins], and if we proceed to
[del]form a[/del] [ins]an endless[/ins] series of numbers formed according to the law that each is
equal to the [ins]thrice the[/ins] sum of the [del]two[/del] last and last but one increased by the
last but two, this series being 0, 1, 3, 12, 46, 177, 681, 2650, etc.,
then the cubes of the [ins]endless[/ins] series of fractions
$1+\frac{0}{1}$, $1+\frac{1}{3}$, $1+\frac{3}{12}$, $1+\frac{12}{46}$, $1+\frac{46}{177}$, $1+\frac{177}{681}$, $1+\frac{681}{2650}$, etc.,
will, [del]by the [unclear]Fermatian[/unclear][/del] [ins]from the relation[/ins] inference, all be nearer and nearer to
2, and each one will be intermediate in value between
the two preceding it.
