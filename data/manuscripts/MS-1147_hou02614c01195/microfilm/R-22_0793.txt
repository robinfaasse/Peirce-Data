An operation, F, is such that being performed upon
any two quantities of different values, $x$ and $y$, it gives two
quantities, F$x$ and F$y$, of different values;
$\therefore$ Every equation of the form F$x = y$ has a solution in
the assumed system of values.

If the system of values is not finite but is not more mul-
titudinous than the [del]finite[/del] [ins]cardinal[/ins] [del]integral[/del] numbers, the above inference,
obviously will not hold good (as may be seen by supposing F$x$
$= x + 1$; for the equation $x + 1 = 0$ has no cardinal number
as its solution), but there will always be some [del]operation, F, such[/del] [ins]relation, R, such[/ins]
that the following inference will hold good:

Every value which can belong to a quantity of the form F$x$, [del]but cannot[/del]
[del]belong to a quantity of the form FF$x$, has the property P[/del]
Every value which stands in the relation R [ins]but not in that of R to[/ins]
anything itself R to, a value $v$, has the property P;

R to, but not in that of R to anything itself R to, a value which [del]stands[/del]
[del]in the relation R to $v$ and[/del] [ins]which[/ins] at the same time has the property P, itself
