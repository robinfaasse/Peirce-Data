[del]To prove[/del] For, suppose, first that the line in question is not [del]attached to[/del] [ins]enclosed in[/ins] any oval. Then any point upon it denotes some [del]individual[/del] [ins]thing[/ins] of which something is asserted. To attach that line to an index would simply have the effect of identifying it with some individual designated by a proper name. If it had a name and we knew it, which would not alter the fact expressed, it might be called by that name; and if it had not a proper name, still, as an individual, it would be apt to receive one. Now, suppose the proposition rule had been proved to be valid for every heavy line not enclosed by more than 2N ovals. If I show that it would follow that the rule held good also for every heavy line enclosed in 2N+1 or in 2N+2 ovals, I shall have proved its validity in every case.
