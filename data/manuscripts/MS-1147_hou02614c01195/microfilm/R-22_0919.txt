A collection, [ins]as[/ins] say the As, is said to be "greater" than another, the Bs, and the latter to be "less" than the former, according to the definition given by Cantor in [date]1873[/date] ([u]Journal[/u] [del]von Crelle[/del] [ins]of Borchardt[/ins]. LXXXVII. 258), (here [del]slightly modified in an unessential particular[/del] [ins]taking any[/ins]) if [del]there is[/del] some A which, either relation, S, whatsoever, [del]such that there is some A which, either[/del] does not stand in the relation S to any B at all, or only to a B to which some other A stands in the relation S. That is to say, whatever the relation may be, some A fails to be the [u]sole[/u] A that stands in that relation to any B. It is then a theorem, and a very difficult one to prove, that no two collections are each greater than the other.
