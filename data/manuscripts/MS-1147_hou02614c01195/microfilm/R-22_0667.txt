Seven different methods of attaining this end are frequently resorted
to, and should be carefully weighed. They are by appeals to direct
dicta of consciousness, to psychology, to the usages of language,
to metaphysical philosophy, to special observations, to mathematics,
and to some process of dialectic.

The appeal to direct consciousness consists in pronouncing
certain reasoning to be bad or good, because it is felt to be so.
This is a very common method. Sigwart, for example, bases
all logic upon our invincible mental repulsion against con-
tradiction. In favor of this method it [ins]may be urged[/ins] [del]is to be[/del]

bad reasoning. But it is to be noticed that among the dicta
