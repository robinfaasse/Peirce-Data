To say that the speaker permits every one to be chosen, and
makes his assertion of [u]every one[/u], that is, of [u]all[/u] distribu-
tively. On the other hand, to say that the speaker is to make
the choice, without stating which he chooses, is to say that
he speaks of $S_1$ or $S_2$, or $S_3$, etc. So, from the exemplar
stand-point, to say that a proposition is true of $S_1$ and of
$S_2$ is to say that whether $S_1$ or $S_2$ be chosen, the proposition
is true. That is to say, the proposition "If A is true, [del]C[/del] [ins]B[/ins] is true; and
if B is true, C is true" is equivalent to "If A [del]and[/del] [ins]or[/ins] B is true, C is
true"; and in like manner "If A is true, C is true; or else, if
B is true, C is true", is equivalent to "If A and B are both true,
C is true", so long as the "if" refers to a single state actual state
of things, which is the case when we speak of choosing
examples which [del]our[/del] choice does not affect. For either proposition
is false if, and only if, A and B are both true [ins]while[/ins] C is false.
