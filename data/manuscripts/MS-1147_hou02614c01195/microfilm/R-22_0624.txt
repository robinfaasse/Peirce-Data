erected the word into a technical term of logic in the sense in
which it was used by Blundevile, and also introduced the term
[u]real kind[/u]. As nearly as that master of the art of vagueness,
[del]upon[/del] [ins]to[/ins] which the success of his book was largely due, will permit
us to fix his meaning, it [del]is this[/del] may be stated as follows. The
chief passage is in the [u]Logic[/u], Book I, ch. vii, §4. It must be premised
that whenever Mill spoke of "properties," he had [del]so[/del] in mind
mainly such characters as interest us, and had not considered
how rapidly such properties multiply with increase of knowledge.
