A principle of this chameleon line, at one time
appearing as a ratio essendi, at another as a law of
nature, now [del]acting as a[/del] [ins]a principle[/ins] efficient and now of final
causation, serving equally to invoke an antecedent
for an observed feeling, fact, or thought, and [del]awoke a[/del] [ins]at last[/ins]
consequent from an antecedent, was calculated to please
the German mind. [del]Kant[/del] Wolff, Baumgarten, Selle,
Tiedemann undertook to deduce it as a rigid corollary
from the principle of contradiction. It early became a
subject of study with Kant. In the Critic of the Pure Reason
(1[ins]st[/ins] Ed. pp. 200, 201) he completely confounds it with the principle
of causality. Perhaps this was a mere slip; for in several of
his small writings he takes [del]our[/del] more tenable ground. In the
Logic by Jäsche ([del]1st[/del] Einleitung. [del]VIII[/del] [ins]and §76[/ins]) he makes the principle of
sufficient reason ([del]which he seems to distinguish from the[/del]
[del]Principle of reason[/del], [del]Satz des Grundes[/del], §76) the formal
criterion of the "logical actuality" (logische Wirklichkeit).
