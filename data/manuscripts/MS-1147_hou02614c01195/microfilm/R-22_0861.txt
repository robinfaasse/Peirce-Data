Prof. O. H. Mitchell in his paper [u]On a New Algebra of Logic[/u] ([u]Studies in Logic by Members of the Johns Hopkins University[/u]. Boston: Little, Brown, & Co. [date]1883[/date]. p. 72), among other ideas of extraordinary fertility, first put forth that of a logical universe of two or more dimensions. The idea is essentially different from that of two universes, with which Schröder confounds it. When we treat of a limited universe of marks, we have two universes, some objects being in one and [ins]others[/ins] some in the other exclusively, but none in both. But in the two-dimensional universe, the same object occurs in both. But if we take into account changes in time, any one individual of the dimension of logical extension may extend through all time, and any one instant of time may contain the whole of logical extension.
