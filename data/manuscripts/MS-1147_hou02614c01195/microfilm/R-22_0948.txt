The fallacy of [u]post hoc ergo propter hoc[/u] consists
in any unwarranted conclusion that because one
phenomenon has been observed to follow another
therefore it will follow the other under all indifferent
circumstances. The first tests of medicines must have
been excessively crude. Without doubt, physicians
fell into the error of not defining [del]accurately[/del] exactly,
before making a test, what they would regard as a
success and what a failure. They would go on with
their treatment until, in the case of a self-limited disease,
