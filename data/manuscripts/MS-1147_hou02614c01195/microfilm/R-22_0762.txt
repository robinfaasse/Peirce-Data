The rules of illative transformation are as follows:

I. [u]Any unenclosed partial graph may be erased.[/u]

('Partial' is here to be understood as implying that the same
thing is true, as a special case, of the whole graph.) For let A
be a partial graph, and B the rest of the graph. First suppose
no heavy lines connect A and B. Then the whole asserts that
A and B are true, while after the erasure what is asserted is that
B is true. Now it is a logical principle that if a proposition is
true, everything it asserts is true. Consequently, if A and B are true
B is true. Next suppose A and B are connected with heavy
lines. Let one of these be broken. Then, what is asserted
by the graph so modified is that there is something, X, and
something, Y, [del](which may or may not be identical)[/del] [ins](whether identical with X or not is not said) of[/ins]
which something is true. Before the line was broken, the same
thing was said to be true, and further that X and Y were identical.
Hence, on the principle used as above, any heavy line [ins]connecting a multitude[/ins]
A and B may be broken. But the number of such lines is finite.
