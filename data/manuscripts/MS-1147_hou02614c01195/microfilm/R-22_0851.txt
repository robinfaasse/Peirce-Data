Mnemonic Verses.

The traditional mnemonic verses of Logic are found in the compendium of Shyreswood (Prantl. Geschichte der Logik III p 10), and are probably earlier. They come to us through the celebrated [u]Summulae Logicales[/u] of [u]Petrus Hispanus[/u], who gives them as follows:

Instrumenta novem sunt, guttur, lingua, palatum,
Quatuor et dentes et duo labra simul.
(Not given by Shyreswood.)

Quae ca. vel hyp. Qualis ne. vel aff. u. [del]Quanta[/del] [ins]par. in. sing.[/ins]
(So too Shyreswood.)

Simpliciter [del]feci[/del] [ins]fēci[/ins], convertitur [del]Eva[/del] [ins]ēva[/ins] per acci.
Asto per contra. sic fit conversio tota
Asserit [u]A[/u], negat [u]E[/u], sed universaliter ambae
Asserit [u]I[/u], negat [u]O[/u], sed particulariter ambo.
[In some copies the quantity is corrected by putting
Sunt for the first sed. Not in Shyreswood.]
