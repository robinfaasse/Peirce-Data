Precision is often called [u]precisive abstraction[/u]; but some writers use praecision for any kind of abstraction. The Conimbricenses ([u]In praefat. Isagoges. qu. v. art. 1[/u]) divide abstraction into real and intentional. Intentional abstraction is either negative (by denying a character of an object) or precisive (which takes place "when by simple apprehension of several things in any way connected we cognize only one, the others being pretermitted; as the eye prescinds the color of an apple from its odor or taste. Again, the intellect in this way thinks of a common nature alone, the differences being cast out."
