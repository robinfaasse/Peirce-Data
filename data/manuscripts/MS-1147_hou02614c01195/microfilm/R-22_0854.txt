A modal proposition is affirmative if it contains no negation,
as 'Sortem possibile est currere', or if it contains two as, 'Sortem
impossibile est non currere'. A modal proposition has
two quantities, [u]ex parte subjecti[/u] and [u]ex parte modi[/u]. [u]Ex parte
subjecti[/u], the signs, or signs of quantity, [u]omnis[/u] and [u]nullus[/u],
make it universal, the [u]aliquis[/u] and [u]aliquis non[/u] or [u]non omnis[/u]
make it particular. [u]Ex parte modi[/u] [u]necesse[/u] and [u]non possibile[/u],
or [u]impossibile[/u], make it universal, [ins]possibile[/ins] [del]non necesse[/del], and [u]contingens[/u]
(which may be taken [del]as equivalent[/del] [ins]in the same[/ins] to [u]non necesse[/u] or in
the sense of being at once [u]possibile[/u] and [u]possibile non[/u]) make
it particular.
