[u]Per se, Perseity.[/u] This phrase translates [ins]καθ' αὑτό, καθ' αὑτόν, or ἑαυτοῦ.[/ins] [del]καθ' αὑτό (Sui-herity[/del] [ins]Its[/ins] various meanings are enumerated by Aristotle in several places: I Anal. post. iv. xxii., Metaph. Δ xviii, I i, Z i, v, etc. But what is usually meant is [u]per se primo modo[/u], that is, true by virtue of a definition. But a substance is said to exist [u]per se[/u], and most metaphysicians, ap-parently [ins]including Aristotle,[/ins] consider this as an unrelated meaning, opposed to [u]per alio[/u]. Since, however, a substance exists, [del]if the word[/del] substance would not be applicable to it, this meaning may be considered as only a variation, or derivative, of the first.
