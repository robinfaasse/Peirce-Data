[u]Material Fallacy[/u].

A fallacy which is, in form, correct reasoning. Five such fallacies are generally recognized, two of which are, in themselves, valid consequences. These are the [u]petitio principii[/u] and the [u]ignoratio elenchi[/u]. No argument rises to the dignity of either of these [del]fallacies[/del] [ins]sophisms[/ins], unless the conclusion really follows from the premisses. The other three are bad reasoning. They are the [u]non sequitur[/u], the [u]fallacy of accident[/u], and the [u]fallacy of composition and division[/u].
C.S.P.

[u]Material Logic[/u].

Any logical doctrine which considers the nature of the object reasoned about. The term is of sometimes made a synonym of applied logic.
C.S.P.
