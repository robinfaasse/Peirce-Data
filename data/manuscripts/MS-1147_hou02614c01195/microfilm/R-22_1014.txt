[pn]Quantity.[/pn] [[u]L.[/u] [u]quantitas[/u], a word which appears in the first century after Christ, [del]from[/del] [ins]to[/ins] translates [u]τὸ ποσόν[/u].]

(1). Whether because [u]τὸ ποσόν[/u] is taken in a concrete sense in the Praedicamenta or because [u]ποσότης[/u] is [del]a somewhat[/del] [ins]an uncommon word[/ins], [ins]or for some other reason,[/ins] [u]quantity[/u] is certainly used almost invariably by the old writers in a concrete sense. It would seem as if they had never formed the modern conception. When, for example, they call mathematics the science of quantity, they really mean nothing more than that it is the science of things divisible.
