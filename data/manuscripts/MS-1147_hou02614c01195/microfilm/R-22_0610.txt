[del]In order to get a perfectly distinct understanding
of what is meant by implication, it is necessary to
recognize, that the precise state of the facts to which
the meaning of any proposition limits the universe
of possibilities may be expressed in logical language
in which is shall be the copula of that proposition; and
that it is involved in the meaning of that copula that
it [ins]expresses[/ins] is a transitive relation, so that the dictum de omni
(and also the nota notae, if the latter formula is conceived to
be different from the former) [ins]as well as[/ins] [del]and[/del] every inference which
the dictum de omni [del]that rule would[/del] justify, are implied
in the meaning of the proposition. Accordingly, what is
implied in a proposition, and what can be strictly deduced
from it, are one and the same.[/del] In short, any proposition which
neither requires the exclusion from, nor the inclusion in, the universe of any
[del]state of facts, or kind of object, which such as a second proposition excludes[/del]
[del]likewise excludes or includes, is implied in that second proposition.[/del]
