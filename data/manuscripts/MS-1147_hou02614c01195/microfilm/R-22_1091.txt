[pn]Theory[/pn] [Gr. [u]θεωρία[/u] literally, contemplation, looking on, from [u]θεάομαι[/u], to look on [ins]with a suffix [u]ία[/u].[/ins] Supposed to be connected with [u]θέαμα[/u], to gaze, wonder]

[del]A[/del] [ins]A[/ins] doctrine whose purpose is exclusively the understanding of the subject to which it relates, and not the doing of anything. It thus naturally takes a dyblogistic implication with writers of practical tendencies. But it is a gross misuse of the term to confine it to hypotheses resting upon mere presumption. It would [del]be[/del] do less violence to the true meaning to confine it to established doctrines. In mathematics, it is applied to a systematic and well-rounded doctrine of a [del]somewhat[/del] unitary but large subject.
