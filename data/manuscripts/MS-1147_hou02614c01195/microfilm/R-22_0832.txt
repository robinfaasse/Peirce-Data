[u]The Early, Sponsor Metaphysical Sense of the Terms.[/u]

Aristotle himself remarks that matter was the earliest philo-
sophical conception, all the inquiries of the Ionic philosophers
having reference to the question what the world was made of.
But when we consider that they had no better word than [del]ἀρχή[/del] [ins]ὕλη[/ins],
the beginning, to signify this conception, we see that it must have been
but vaguely apprehended. The writer of this article is one of those who
believe that the ἄπειρον, or Boundless, of which Anaximander took to be
the ἀρχή of the universe was Space, and that Anaximenes took
it in the same sense when he said ἀέρα ἄπειρον τὴν ἀρχὴν εἶναι,
that is, air and space are one and the same. But it must be
admitted that the reasons against this interpretation are weighty.
