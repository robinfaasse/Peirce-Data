[u]Involution[/u]. [L. involu_tionem, a wrapping up, from [del]invo[/del] in-volvo, to roll upon,
from in upon, and volvo, to roll. Same root as Gr. [greek]εἰλύσις[/greek], E. well, spring]
Ger. Involution; Fr. involution; It.

A term borrowed in symbolic logic from algebra, where it means
the raising of a base to a power. In logic, it is used in two widely
different senses. In the first, it is either Progressive or Regressive
Involution. If [u]l[/u]S[u]M[/u] denotes whatever is a lover of a servant of
an individual, M, and if S₁M, S₂M, S₃M, etc. are all the different
individual servants of M, then employing a dot to signify the
conjunction of different descriptions in the same individual subject,
we write
[u]l[/u]ˢ [u]M[/u] = [u]l[/u]S₁[u]M[/u] · [u]l[/u]S₂[u]M[/u] · [u]l[/u]S₃[u]M[/u] · etc.
That is, [del]lover[/del] whatever is a lover of every servant of M; and the
operation which thus [del]unites[/del] [ins]unites[/ins] [u]l[/u] and [u]S[/u] is termed progressive
involution.
