Madame la Superieure:

In sending you a barrel of cider, which [del]James[/del] [ins]we[/ins] have taken the liberty to do, we have shipped it under the name of vinegar, because we thought it would go safer. [del]By[/del] I, therefore, write, so that you may know that it is really cider to drink. [del]And[/del] I [del]write[/del] [ins]trust[/ins] that the barrel of apples got safe to you and that they arrived in good condition. Mrs. Peirce is suffering with a severe reaction from all she did in New York and is not [u]far[/u] able to write. She hopes to do so in a week or two.

We are very much pleased with Marie so far. On Saturday I happened to meet the protestant clergyman and told him how you had given up a girl out of your own house, and I added "Now that will show you there you have an example of what St James means by pure religion and undefiled."

Very respectfully,
C. S. P.
