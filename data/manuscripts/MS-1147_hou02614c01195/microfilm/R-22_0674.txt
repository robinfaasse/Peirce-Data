[u]Logic, Exact.[/u] Ger. [u]Exakte Logik.[/u]

Among the movements of which the great modern movement toward applying to philosophical subjects the methods of successful science, of which this dictionary is the outcome and aims to be an exponent, the movement to place logic among the exact sciences, [del]those sciences which have found means to avail themselves of the continual aid of mathematics to give definiteness to their conceptions and determinacy to their conclusions,[/del] claims treatment at some length, since it is one of those topics of which the literature is small and is largely scattered through the costly publications of learned societies.

There is, strictly speaking, no "school" of exact logic. Mathematics has been applied to problems of reasoning by Pascal (1623-1662), Nicolaus Bernoulli (1687-1759), [del]Euler (1707-1783), Ploucquet (1716-1790), Lambert (1728-1777), La Place (1749-1827)[/del] De Morgan (1806-1871), Boole (1815-1864), and [ins]others; and[/ins] [del]a few men in different countries still[/del] [ins]a few men[/ins] continue the study of the problems opened by the last two named logicians.
