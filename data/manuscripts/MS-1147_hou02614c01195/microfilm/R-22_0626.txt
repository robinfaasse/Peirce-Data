[u]Laws of Thought[/u]. The three Formulae which in many logical treatises
go by this [del]most[/del] inappropriate title are as follows:
I. The [del]Law[/del] [ins]Principle[/ins] of Identity: A is A.
II. The Principle of Contradiction: A is not not-A.
III. The Principle of Excluded Middle: Everything is either A or not-A.

All that the formula of identity properly means is that the copula
is is of such a nature that when the same term is made both subject
and predicate, there can be no falsity in the assertion. Various German
writers, out of a [del]love for phantastic[/del] [ins]respect for traditional[/ins] phrases, irrespective of their
meaning, have used the formula "A is A" to express other principles.
But the [del]wilful[/del] deflection of scientific terms from their proper meanings
is an act of disrespect for terminology and a violation of scien-
tific ethics.
