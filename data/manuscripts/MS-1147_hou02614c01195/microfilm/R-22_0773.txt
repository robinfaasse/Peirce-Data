From the seven fundamental rules follow a great variety of
propositions of which the following may serve as specimens:

Any graph may have two ovals drawn round it.

Two ovals, [ins]one with the other, and[/ins] enclosing nothing but a graph, may be removed.

Whatever transformation is permissible upon [del]any[/del] [ins]every[/ins] unenclosed
graph is permissible upon every evenly enclosed graph, and
its reverse upon any oddly enclosed graph.

If a graph is written outside an oval, it may be erased if
written [del]also[/del] inside that oval, if it be also written there also.

If a graph is written outside any number of ovals, it may
be written also inside any of them.
