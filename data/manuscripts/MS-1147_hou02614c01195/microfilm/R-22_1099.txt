[pn]3[/pn] [marginalia] [del]ject[/del] In the scholastic phraseology, derived from Aristotle, [/marginalia] and continued, in this phrase, to our day, a universal proposition is one which asserts something concerning every individual [del]of[/del] [ins]without exception[/ins] of which the subject term may be predicable. But Kant draws a distinction (<u>Logik</u> by Jäsche, §21) between ‘generale’ (which he elsewhere calls gemeine) Sätze’ and ‘universale (elsewhere allgemeine) Sätze’. The difference is merely that the former are the old universal propositions (often so called by Kant himself) while the latter extend to the whole universe. This distinction is of small importance, since the proposition ‘Every phoenix rises from its ashes’ is equivalent to ‘Take any object whatsoever, and if it be a phoenix, it rises from its ashes’.
