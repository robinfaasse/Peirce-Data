[marginalia]
Whole.
[/marginalia]

No [ins]general[/ins] definite meaning is attached to this word in philosophy. Anything which consists of the union of a plurality of things is called a whole. For example, the cardinal numbers make up a whole, although there is no last number. See Sextus [del]Contlibet[/del] [ins]Adversus Mathematicos[/ins] [del]q. ii[/del] [ins]ix. 341[/ins] and [del]Ockham[/del] [ins]Albertus Magnus[/ins] [del]Logica I. xxvii[/del].
Collective whole, or whole of aggregation (which last has another meaning also)
