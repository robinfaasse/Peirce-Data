[u]Logic[/u] is no exception to the general rule that sciences of
great antiquity first appear in forms of the crudest nonsense.
Aristotle makes Zeno of Eleia the inventor of logic; and for him
and his townsmen it seems to have been a game of paradox. If
he soberly believed there was no such thing as motion, the opinion
betrays a very [del]undeveloped[/del] [ins]rudimentary[/ins] sense of truth. The whole history of
logic shows a gradual growth of this sense. In the age of [del]Pericles[/del] [ins]Zeno[/ins]
there was a sort of paradox-mania in Athens; and the most
mature of the logical dialogues of Plato, the Sophistes, shows
the same feeble grasp of the nature of truth. This small reverence
for fact appears in many dialogues.
