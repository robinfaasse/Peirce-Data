Now if a quantity $w$ is such that $w^{3} = 2$, there will be
found, by simply cubing the algebraical expression $-\frac{1}{2}w + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}w\underline{i}$,
that it too is a cube root of $2$, and so is $-\frac{1}{2}w - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}w\underline{i}$. The real cube
root of $2$ is evidently between $1$ and $2$. We may therefore write
$p$ for $w-1$, $q$ for $-\frac{1}{2}w-1 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}w\underline{i}$, and $r$ for $-\frac{1}{2}w-1 - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}w\underline{i}$,
and $p, q, r$, will be the roots of the equation $x^{3} + 3x^{2} + 3x = 1$. We
[ins](or can easily verify it, if we have forgotten it)[/ins]
know that if the product of two imaginaries [del]is[/del] $P+Q\underline{i}$ and
$P'+Q'\underline{i}$, is $P''+Q''\underline{i}$, then $(P^{2}+Q^{2})(P'^{2}+Q'^{2}) = (P''^{2}+Q''^{2})$; so that
if $\sqrt{P^{2}+Q^{2}}$ is separated as a factor of $P+Q\underline{i}$, the other factor,
[del]$\frac{P}{\sqrt{P^{2}+Q^{2}}} + \frac{Q}{\sqrt{P^{2}+Q^{2}}}\underline{i}$[/del] [ins]together with all its powers[/ins], will always have [del]the[/del] [ins]this[/ins] $\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$ [ins]or[/ins] [del]$\sqrt{P^{2}+Q^{2}}$[/del], that is, the corres-
ponding quantity, equal to unity. This quantity, $\sqrt{P^{2}+Q^{2}}$, is called the
modulus, of $P+Q\underline{i}$. Now if we take any three numbers whatever,
$A, B, C$, we can always find [del]for[/del] there must be three quantities,
$X, Y, Z$, which will satisfy the three equations
$Xp^{2} + Yq^{2} + Zr^{2} = A$
$Xp^{1} + Yq^{1} + Zr^{1} = B$
$Xp^{0} + Yq^{0} + Zr^{0} = C$.
