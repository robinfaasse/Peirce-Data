While modern logic is, in some respects, infinitely superior to
that of the middle ages, in others it is distinctly inferior. For
logic ought to be capable of directing the course of the other
sciences. [del]S[/del] Many writers, indeed, make this its essential office,
as Gerson, Burgersdicius, and Sanderson, quoted above. Lambert, too,
entitles his treatise "Neues Organon, oder Gedanken über die
Erforschung und Bezeichnung des Wahren und dessen Unterscheidung
vom Irrthum und Schein", while [ins][u]H.[/u][/ins] Ritter entitles his "Die philosophische
Methodenlehre für des wissenschaftlichen Denken". Grundling's treatise
is "Via ad Veritatem". Tschirnhausen's "Medicina Mentis". "Catharticon
Intellectus", "Cynosura bonae mentis", "Pharus Intellectus", are titles
of other logics. Isaac Watts says, "Logick is the art of using Reason
well in our Inquiries after Truth". Clauberg defines it as "ars
ratione recte utendi". The Port Royalist makes it "L'Art de Penser".
Schleiermacher: "Die Kunstlehre des wissenschaftlichen Denkens".
