[marginalia]Logical Graphs[/marginalia]

The only indices used in this system [ins]except provisionally,[/ins] are definite indices, which are the proper names of individuals of the universe. The graph does not assert their existence, i.e. their presence in the universe; for this supposed to be a part of that common experience of the writer and reader of the graph [del]which[/del] [ins]their communication supposes.[/ins] In the proposition 'Basil Valentine existed,' Basil Valentine is not an index; for the proposition only means that some man existed who was called by a name spelled like B-a-s-i-l V-a-l-e-n-t-i-n-e.

The rule depends upon the principle that whatever is true of one individual is true of every individual identical with it, or whatever is identical with A is A.

Considered as a part of a definition, the rule asserts that the junction of indices by an unenclosed and uncrossed heavy line has such a signification that whatever is true of the object of the first index is [ins]implied[/ins] asserted to be true of that of the other.
