The modern development of exact logic began with DeMorgan's memoir of October 1846 ([u]Cambridge Phil. Trans.[/u] VIII. 379). The item which remains today the most significant in that paper is its disposition to conceive the quantity of the subject of a proposition arithmetically. It notices the inferential form

Most of the A's are B's;
Most of the A's are C's;
[three dots in a triangular pattern indicating "therefore"] Some B is C.

This is a department which has not yet been sufficiently explored. He also here introduces (p. 380) the conception of a limited "universe of a proposition or of a name."
