Schellbach's apparatus consists essentially of a horizontal steel rod capable of being rotated about its principal vertical axis by means of wheel-work and carrying at its two ends the surfaces upon which the air is to act. The rod is carried in the brass forks which have the form of screw-surfaces with a pitch of 45°. Small rollers whose axes turn in rubies serve to reduce the friction of these guides to an insignificant amount. When the apparatus is set in motion, the resistance of the air acting upon the plates imparts to the steel arms a tendency to be displaced downward in the forks. But since their axis is connected with a brass ball floating on water, there results a certain resistance to the perpendicular displacement of the axis.
