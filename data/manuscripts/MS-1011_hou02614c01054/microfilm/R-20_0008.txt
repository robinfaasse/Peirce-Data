But all experiments depending upon this principle have
been seen to be worthless, since [ins] * [/ins] [ins] S. P. Langley [/ins] proved that
the wind never retains a uniform velocity for a single second,
but consists of rapidly successive puffs.

Langley made the experiment with a very sensitive
Robinson's anemometer with paper cups which marked
every half revolution upon a chronograph. The velocity of
the wind often oscillated several times a second between
zero and 25 metres.

We have here to do with those pulsations (sogenannten Böbby-
bewegungen) in which, as O. Lilienthal showed, the resistance
of the air may mount to 25 times its average value.

[u]* S. P. Langley, "The Internal Work of the Wind" Phil. Mag.
XXXVII, 425.[/u]
