The first idea which suggests itself is to impart to the body
a uniform rectilinear motion and [del]to measure[/del] [ins]determine[/ins] the force required
to sustain it. But this requires a [del]very[/del] complicated mechanism.
The reverse method, that of sending a constant stream of
air against a body at rest, is [del]ill-adapted to the purpose[/del]
since in any artificially produced current, owing to the
friction of the air upon the walls and that of neighboring
strata of air upon one another, the velocities will be
different at different points of its cross-section.
Attempts have been made to avoid these difficulties by
employing [del]natural wind[/del] [ins]meteorological wind[/ins]. The velocity of the wind was to be
measured with an anemometer, and a [del]suitable apparatus[/del]
was to determine the pressure acting upon the body.*
