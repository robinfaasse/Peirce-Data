[u]II[/u]
[u]On the Relative Forms of the Algebras.[/u]
[del]By C. S. Peirce[/del]

Given an associative algebra whose letters are
$i$, $j$, $k$, $l$, etc. and whose multiplication
table is
$i^{2} = a_{1} i + b_{1} j + c_{1} k + \text{etc.}$
$ij = a_{12} i + b_{12} j + c_{12} k + \text{etc.}$
$ji = a_{21} i + b_{21} j + c_{21} k + \text{etc.}$
etc.

I proceed to explain what I call the relative
[del]form[/del] [ins]terms[/ins] of this algebra.

I assume a number of new units, $A$, $I$, $J$,
$K$, $L$, etc. one more in number than the letters
of the algebra and [del]all[/del] [ins]everyone[/ins] but the first, $A$, corre-
sponding to a particular letter of the algebra.
These new units are susceptible of being mul-
tiplied by numerical coefficients and of being
added together[ins]*[/ins]; but they cannot be multiplied
together, and hence are called non-relative
units.
