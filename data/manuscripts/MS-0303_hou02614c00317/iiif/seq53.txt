The Booleān algebra of logic is a mere application
of this kind of pure mathematics. It is a form of
mathematics rather poverty stricken as to ideas.
Nevertheless, it has some features which we shall find
have a certain bearing upon the foundations of pragmatism.
In the first place, although the universe consists
of only two primary substances, yet there will ipso facto
be quite a wealth of abstractions. For in the first
place there will be the universe of which M and B
are the two parts. Then there will be three prominent
relations. Namely 1[u]st[/u] the relation that M has to B
and [del]that[/del] nothing M has to nothing else, and that
nothing but M has to anything, 2[u]nd[/u] there will
be the [del]relation[/del] converse relation that B has to M
and to nothing else [del]x[/del] that nothing but B has to
anything, and 3[u]rd[/u] the relation that B has to M
and to nothing else and the M has to B and to
nothing else. Without counting the absurd
relation that nothing has to anything. That third
relation is the self converse relation of otherness.
Those four relations are [u]dyadic relations[/u]. That
is, considered as abstractions their existence
consists [ins]in[/ins] something being true of two primary
substances. Thus to say that [del]M is other[/del] M is in
the relation of otherness to B is to say the M is
other than B which is a fact about the two
primary substances M and B.
