There are dyadic relations between dyadic
relations. Thus the relation of M to B is the
converse of the relation of B to M and this
relation of converseness is a dyadic relation
between relations.

As an example of a triadic relation between
relations take the relation between the
[del]rela[/del] [u]1[ins]st[/ins][/u], [u]2[ins]nd[/ins][/u], and [u]3[ins]rd[/ins][/u] relations between M and B
That is the relation between first the relation of M
only to B only, of B only to M only, and of other-
ness of M to B and of B to M. This triadic relation
is a case of the general triadic relation of
aggregation. To say that Z stands in the
relation of aggregation to X and Y is to
say that Z is true wherever X is true and
[del]wherever[/del] Y is true and that either X or Y
is true wherever Z is true.
