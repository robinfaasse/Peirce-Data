Multiplication is, in general, best performed by long multiplication rather than
by cross-multiplication. There are, however, many cases in which the operation
can be abridged. Thus, where one of the factors contains in its decimal expression
three or more units in succession, one can by a subtraction somewhat abridge the
work. But, of course, a table of antilogarithms to base 10 is desirable.

Division, too, is, in general, best performed by long division or by logarithms,
though the reciprocals of most low primes are very simply expressed.

[u]The[/u] root of any Algebraic Equation is easily evaluated in the form of a rational
fraction to a high degree of precision, as soon as a value has been obtained whose
modulus differs less from that of the root required than from that of any other
root. For it can then be put into the form $1 = \sum_{1}^{m} a_{i} x^{m-i}$ ; and then assuming any
rough approximations to the first m-1 positive powers of $x$, by calculating, according
to the formula, the successive negative powers of $x$, after a considerable number of such
easy calculations, $x^{-n+1}/x^{-n}$ will be found to be very near to the true value. One
should however avoid allowing any of the $a_{i}$'s to vanish.
