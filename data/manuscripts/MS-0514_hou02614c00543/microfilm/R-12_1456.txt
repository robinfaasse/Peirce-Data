34 Fourth Permission: called the
rule of the pseudograph ([u]pseudon[/u] is the
Greek for 'false', 'pretended', and the like;
and a pseudograph is something which
has the form of a graph and can be
treated like a graph, but lacks the
essence of the graph, which is that it
might be true or might be false, since
this pseudograph does not represent
anything that [del]ever could[/del] [ins]can[/ins] be true.)

When a cut with nothing at all in
its area, [fig]empty circle[/fig], is contained in the
area of another cut, [fig]circle containing B and another circle containing A[/fig], it is
permissible to abolish the whole of the
outer enclosure, with all it contains,
reducing [fig]circle containing B and another circle containing A[/fig] to [fig]circle containing A[/fig] [del]or to B[/del] [ins]instead[/ins]
[ins]with all that is contained in the outer enclosure except the vacant cut can be erased[/ins]
if this vacant enclosure, [fig]empty circle[/fig], occurs
on the sheet of truth, it proves that
there has been some mistake, and
that which was supposed to be true,
(either at the outset or at some
transformation,) is not true; and
everything on the sheet should
be denied [del]by[/del] by making a cut round
the whole of it, or if we prefer, the
whole can be erased by Permission No. 1.
