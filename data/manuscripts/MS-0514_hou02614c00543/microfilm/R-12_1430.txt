To return to our matters, after the first throw of the die, that one of the two tallies (that of A and that of B) that shows zero may chance to continue to show 0 for an indefinite number of throws of the die. But it will not do so forever; and as soon as both tallies have left the value 0, every throw must change the result of C and consequently that of D and of E (for if we write $c$ for C's result when that of D is $d$ we have $d = \frac{c}{1+c}$ and $c = \frac{d}{1-d}$, so that when one changes, so does the other, and when very high numbers are reached any change of $d$ divided by $1-d$ is equal to the simultaneous change of $c$ divided by $1+c$.)
