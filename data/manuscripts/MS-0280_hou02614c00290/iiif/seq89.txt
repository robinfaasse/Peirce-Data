A proper name or term filling the blank of a rheme, is a [u]subject[/u] of
the proposition in which it occurs, according to the writer’s nomenclature.
[ins]A[/ins] The [u]rheme[/u], considered as part of a proposition, is its [u]predicate[/u]. A propo-
sition, according to any one analysis of it, has but a single predicate, but
it is equally legitimate to throw any proper name or substitute for a proper name
into the predicate part of the proposition.
It is “particular”, “singular”, or “universal,” according as it has the character which belongs
to a proper name at the first, second, or third stage of its ideally normal development.
