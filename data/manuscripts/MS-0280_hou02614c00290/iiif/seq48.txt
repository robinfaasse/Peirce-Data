proves nothing except Aristotle's opinion; and it is hardly likely
that he could have given [del]any[/del] [ins]a[/ins] sound reason to support it. The
true reason is that a collection is a single object of a different kind
from the objects that are members of the collection. Now a plural
must be understood as denoting a collection; and the proof of this
[fig]figure[/fig] is that if the plural meant anything else, a skillful logician
ought to be able to say what else it does mean. But nobody has
ever succeeded in doing this.
