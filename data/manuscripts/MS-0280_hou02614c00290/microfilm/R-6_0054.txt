[pn]48[/pn] and all Aristotelian logicians use [greek]πᾶς[/greek] or [u]omnis[/u] in the singular, [greek]πᾶς ἄνθρωπος λευκός[/greek], [u]omnis homo est candidus[/u], never [greek]πάντες ἄνθρωποι λευκοί[/greek], [u]omnes homines sunt candidi[/u]. But that proves nothing except Aristotle's opinion; and it is hardly likely that he could have given [del]any[/del] [ins]a[/ins] sound reason to support it. The true reason is that a collection is a single object of a different kind from the objects that are members of the collection. Now a plural must be understood as denoting a collection; and the proof of this [fig]black circle[/fig] is that if the plural meant anything else, a skillful logician ought to be able to say what else it does mean. But nobody has ever succeeded in doing this.
