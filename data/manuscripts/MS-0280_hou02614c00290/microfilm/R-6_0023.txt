"The meaning of a concept," the great nominalist would have exclaimed, "[u]The concept is meaning[/u]." Accordingly he says, "Datur cognitio distincta notionis indefinibilis, quando ea est [u]primitiva[/u], sive nota sui ipsius, hoc est, cum est irresolubilis, ac non nisi per se intelligitur, atque adeo caret requisitis." He evidently thinks that every indecomposable idea must necessarily be like the idea of [u]blue[/u] or that of [u]grief[/u] in being of such quality as it is, in itself, regardless of anything else. It is amazing that such a mind should have supposed that a simple mixture of sensations could make up any intellectual concept.
