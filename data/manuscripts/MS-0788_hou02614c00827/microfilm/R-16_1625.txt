Thus consider the premises [del]$1 \prec \breve{A} + p \bar{\beta}$ and $1 \prec \bar{\beta} + \gamma$[/del]
and $1 \prec \breve{A} + p \bar{\beta}$. We may write
$1 \prec (\breve{A} + p \bar{\beta}) (\bar{\bar{\beta}} + \gamma) \prec \breve{A} + p \bar{\beta} (\bar{\bar{\beta}} + \gamma) \prec \breve{A} + p (\bar{\beta} \bar{\bar{\beta}} + \gamma)$
$\prec \breve{A} + p (\eta + \gamma) \prec \breve{A} + p \gamma$
which gives the first conclusion; or we may write
$1 \prec (\breve{A} + p \bar{\beta}) (\bar{\bar{\beta}} + \gamma) \prec \breve{A} + p \bar{\beta} (\bar{\bar{\beta}} + \gamma) \prec \breve{A} + p \bar{\beta} \bar{\bar{\beta}} + \gamma$
$\prec \breve{A} + p \eta + \gamma$
which is the second conclusion. The second conclusion
can always be found by substituting $p \eta + \gamma$ for $p \gamma$
in the first, (or $p \eta + \gamma$ for $\bar{p} \gamma$.)

If the premise of the zero order is of the form $1 \prec \bar{\bar{\beta}} \gamma$
and in that of the $1^{\text{st}}$ order $\beta$ is connected by addition
there are two conclusions. Thus
$1 \prec (\breve{A} + p + \beta) (\bar{\bar{\beta}} \gamma) \prec \breve{A} + p + \beta \bar{\bar{\beta}} \gamma \prec \breve{A} + p + \eta \gamma$
and $1 \prec (\breve{A} + p + \beta) \bar{\bar{\beta}} \gamma \prec (\breve{A} + p + \beta \bar{\bar{\beta}}) \gamma \prec (\breve{A} + p) \gamma$

$1 \prec (\breve{A} (p + \beta)) \bar{\bar{\beta}} \gamma \prec \breve{A} (p + \eta \gamma)$
and $\prec \breve{A} p \gamma$
