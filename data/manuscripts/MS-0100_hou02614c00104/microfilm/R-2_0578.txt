[u]Logically correct[/u]

It is by virtue of their relations to one another that
things have position. A point is that whose position
is absolutely determinate; everything else has one po-
sition partly & another partly.

The relative position of one point to another [del]has[/del]
[ins]determines[/ins] two elements [del]posi[/del] distance and direction
so that from one point only [del]point[/del] [ins]one[/ins] has a given
direction and distance. But from any point there
is some point which has any given direction
and distance. The possibility of geometry depends
on the possibility of comparing one distance with
another and one direction with another. For this
purpose it is necessary first to superpose one
space on another & therefore to have something
rigid and moveable [ins](such as a pair of compasses)[/ins] and second to be able to con-
tinue a direction (as with a ruler).
