In the third fig [del]Disamis and Bocardo only involve the[/del] [ins]property of the particular. Thus we have,[/ins] [pn]4[/pn]

Some X is v-X,
All X is Y,
$\therefore$ Some Y is v-X.

The most philosophical way of reducing these moods to the first figure is, therefore, as follows:-

[u]Disamis[/u]
Some S is P,
Any S is M,
$\therefore$ Some M is P.

[u]Reduction[/u]
Any v-S is P,
Some M is v-S,
$\therefore$ Some M is P.

Bocardo [del]is reduced[/del] in the same way, except that not-P is substituted for P. On the other hand, Datisi and Ferison depend upon the second property of the particular and we have,

All X is X,
Some X is Y,
$\therefore$ Some Y is X.

All X is Y,
Some X is v-X,
$\therefore$ Some v-X is Y.

Of the [del]two[/del] moods of Theophrastus, Celantes depends on the properties of the negative, Dabitis on those of the particular, and Frisesomorum on the properties of both.
