[u]On the Figures and Moods of Logic.[/u]

A distinction into two classes is generally vague. Reasoning
[u]à priori[/u] is inferring [del]a[/del] [ins]a[/ins] consequent from an antecedent. Reasoning
[u]à posteriori[/u] is inferring an antecedent from its consequent. There is
clearly a third way of reasoning, which consists in inferring a mutual
connection between the several consequents of one antecedent; this is
induction.

It is important to distinguish three kinds of relation of antecedent
and consequence. The 1[ins]st[/ins] is sequence in the object represented as
it is meant to be understood; as when I say "If the heart beats
the patient lives" where the sequence is from the [del]life[/del] to the hearts beating.
This is cause and effect or in a particular case a law, and reasoning
is with reference to this sequence denominated [del]subjectively[/del] [ins]externally[/ins] à priori, à posteriori
or inductive. The 2[ins]nd[/ins] sequence lies in the representation in so far as
it is thought to be a representation. This is the sequence from the pulse to
the life in the above example. If A, then B; But B; Hence A. In the
major premis the sequence in the representation as it was taken to be a
representation was from B to A, as the reasoning shows. This sequence
is from reason to conclusion through argument. Reasoning is in refer-
ence to this sequence to be denominated internally à priori, à posteriori, or
inductive. The 3[ins]rd[/ins] sequence lies in the representation as an image
in itself. This is the grammatical sequence from protasis to apodosis
