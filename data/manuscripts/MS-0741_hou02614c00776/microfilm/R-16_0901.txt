A universal proposition is said to be [del]unsubjected[/del] when its affirmation concerning the individuals is internally à priori. That is when it asserts a consequent of the individual and not of the class to which he belongs. Thus; there is [del]always[/del] [ins]everywhere[/ins] time. This is a universal proposition and its affirmation is not concerning the members of any class but concerning everything. So; it rains. [del]This is a[/del] particular proposition but yet considered as universal it does not apply to any class but to every thing part of the air is raining.

A universal proposition is synthetic when its affirmation [ins]concerning[/ins] is externally à priori, that is when it asserts of the individual an apodosis.
