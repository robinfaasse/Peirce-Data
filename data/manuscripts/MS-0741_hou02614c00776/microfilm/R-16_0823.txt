Analogy between Logic and Algebra.

The three modes of reasoning in Logic are as follows
1 From the antecedent to infer the consequent which is à priori
2 " the consequent to infer the antecedent [ins]a posteriori[/ins]
3 " the [del]consequent as a facts[/del] [ins]consequent & antecedent[/ins] to infer their connection [ins]inductive[/ins]

In Algebra there are three kinds of operation
1 Addition and subtraction
2 Multiplication and division
3 Involution and evolution

[fig]Three dots in a triangular arrangement[/fig] Every algebraic solution involves [del]the[/del] inverse process
for, from $F[ins]x[/ins] = A$ we infer $F^{[-1]}A = x$
[fig]Three dots in a triangular arrangement[/fig] Of the three inverse processes
1 Subtraction is always valid because it involves à priori
reasoning. That is $a - a = 0$ always
2 Division, whose rule is $\frac{a}{a} = 1$, involves [del]inductive[/del] rea-
soning or the inference from particular to general. Accordingly
it may fail; as when $a = 0$. Thus from $ax = a$ we cannot in-
fer that $x = 1$.
3. Evolution, whose rule is $\sqrt{a^2} = a$, involves à posteriori reasoning
or inference from a value which is a consequent to the quantity which
is its antecedent, hence it fails half the time.
