Problem.
To apply algebra to Logic.

The letters must denote the objects of logical thought; that is, facts or supposed facts.

Numeration applied to these letters will be possible if we make them abstract to some extent so as to disregard their differ[ins]en[/ins]ces. Thus, "two horses" supposes a generalization since we never see the same horse repeated. The same phenomenon cannot occur twice. Hence $2n$ implies that $n$ is a class of phenomena or supposed phenomena and that a repetition of this phenomena with differences that have not been noted has been imagined [ins]or identified[/ins]. What it is that has been abstracted is not noted. It may be substance, as when we speak of two horses or two atoms. It may be [ins]quality[/ins] which is disregarded as when we say, twice as red. It may be [ins]relation[/ins] as when we say twice as powerful. It may be possibility as when we say twice as True. It may be arbitrariness as when we say twice as dependent. [del]Finally[/del] It may be sensation as when we say twice as comprehensible. &c.

So [del]when[/del] $m = n$ leaves it unnoted whether we mean a reference to identity, or [del]some[/del] abstracted quality, or to probability or what.

So $m + n$ implies that a generalization between $m$ & $n$ is made. But what generalization is not noted. All conformities are Negatives. [del]Abstractions of[/del] usage. We either reckon up the substance in both or the intensity or the [del]probability[/del] or something else.
