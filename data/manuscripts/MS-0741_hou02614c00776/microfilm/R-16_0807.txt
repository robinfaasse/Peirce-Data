I number the three propositions of every syllogism thus: [ins]the same syllogism[/ins]
the 1[sup]st[/sup] proposition has the same subject as one and the same predicate as another in
the 2[sup]d[/sup] proposition has only the same subject as one of the others
the 3[sup]d[/sup] proposition has only the same predicate as one of the others
I also call these respectively the [u]à priori[/u], the [u]à posteriori[/u] and the
[u]inductive[/u] propositions, because externally [u]à priori[/u] syllogisms con-
clude with the first, externally [u]à posteriori[/u] syllogisms with the second,
and externally inductive syllogisms with the third. It is important to
observe that the inductive proposition states that relation of protasis
to apodosis from which we argue, that the [u]à posteriori[/u] proposition
determines the existence or non-existence of the object of the protasis
and the [u]à priori[/u] proposition determines the existence or non-
existence of the object of the protasis.
