Consideration of the 3[sup]rd[/sup] Argument in favor of
the [ins]quantification[/ins] of the predicate

M[sup]r[/sup] Jevons pronounces the relation by density to be
simpler than that of inclusion. He has not noticed
the demonstration that has been offered of the contrary.
To say that S is P is saying less than to say S = P for
the latter is equivalent to saying that not only is S P but
P is S. To say that all men are mortals is evidently less
than to say that men & mortals are [del]identical class[/del]
the same class of things. To assert the inclusion of 1 class
in another is therefore to assert less than their identity
so that inclusion is certainly a simpler conception
than identity. This cannot be controverted. M[sup]r[/sup] Jevons
does not explain what he means by saying that identity
is simpler than inclusion nor does he offer any proof of
it. But we may suppose he means this. To say that
S is P is the same as saying that S is identical with
something included under P. If we adopt our type
of proposition S < P there is no essential difference
between subject & predicate but if the typical proposition
is S = P we see such difference. Furthermore, a
former type may be reduced to one of the latter type
