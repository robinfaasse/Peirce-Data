Theorem II. The extensive limits of a concept are not given therein (that is, subjectively) for the object of extension is itself not given in the concept.

Eq. Gr. In the conception of "existing sea-serpent", I know what I mean clearly, that is the intensive limits of the conception are given, so far as it is a clear conception; but whether or not there is such a creature, that is whether there is any extensive object, is not determined by the conception.

Theorem III. The extensive limits of a concept, if there are any, may be found objectively. For since, in observing objects, we recognize the concept when we come across an extensive object of it, we can thus distinguish what is from what is not such an object. And thus, we can approach indefinitely near to [del]an[/del] extensive clearness of conception.

Theorem IV. No approach can be made towards the objective discovery of the intensive limits of a concept. Because [del]all[/del] these limits are known so far as [del]the[/del] concept is clear, and it is only so far that it can be objectively distinguished and recognized.
