No syllogism has its inductive proposition particular while its
à posteriori proposition is negative. If the inductive proposition
is premised a denial of the protasis, so far as the connection
between the existence of the objects of protasis and apodosis
is supposed to be universal. But if we are only able to state
it particularly in affirmative cases there is no implication as
to negative cases. If the inductive proposition is the conclusion
of two premises one of which denies the protasis, we have just as
much reason for [del]making[/del] inferring it universally as particularly.

If the inductive and à posteriori propositions agree in quality, the à priori
proposition is affirmative, and not otherwise. For the affirmation of the
protasis is only relevant to the affirmation of the apodosis and the
negation of the protasis to the negation of the apodosis.

If the inductive and à posteriori propositions agree in quantity
the à priori proposition is universal, and not otherwise.
