[u]Relation[/u]

A proposition is disjunctive when it depends on a condition out
of the representation implied but not stated

Hypothetical when its condition is stated but not included in
the subject

Categorical when it [del]can be[/del] includes its own condition.

***

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\text{B} & \text{B} & \text{B} \\
\text{Not A} & \text{A} & \text{A} \\
\therefore \text{If A, [del]not[/del] B} & \therefore \text{If A, B} & \therefore \text{If A, sometimes B}
\end{array}$$

Those conclusions are hypothetical because the statement of the
condition is essential to them as dependent on these premises
it is logically relevant.

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\text{If A then B} & \text{If A then B} & \text{If A sometimes B} \\
\text{But not B} & \text{But B} & \text{But B} \\
\therefore \text{Not A} & \therefore \text{A} & \therefore \text{A may be.}
\end{array}$$
