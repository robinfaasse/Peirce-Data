I discard extensive judgements [del]are[/del] [ins]for[/ins] reasons I cannot explain here, so that in what follows I shall speak only of intensive judgements; and I cannot stop to show how I explain certain difficulties this gives rise to. What I say here, can easily be applied to extensive judgements if necessary.

In every proposition the subject is represented as determining the predicate; Hence, "A is B" is exactly the same as "If A then B (in H.)" This being premised it is easy to sum up all the possible forms of inference; and if we disregard those differences of [del]quality[/del] [ins]quantity[/ins] and quality (which are the only functions of judgement I shall consider) which the act of inference, we can count nine such [del]figures[/del] [ins]moods[/ins].
