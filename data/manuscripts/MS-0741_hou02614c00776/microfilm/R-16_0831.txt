[u]Propositions of Disquiparance[/u]

Propositions of disquiparance, express a disquiparance. Such as,
"A kills B," "A is killed by B," "A is equal to B," "A is equalled by B," "A
is greater than B." &c.

Such a proposition must have three terms, because relate & correlate
are not in the same position in reference to the ground; and therefore can-
not be thrown together. The third term is [ins]called[/ins] the [del]object[/del] [ins]correlate[/ins].

propositions of disquiparance, because disquiparance is based on a pre-
vious equiparance of its relates.
