[u]On the Conversion of Quantity.[/u]

Let us suppose that there are different objects which can be measured in their different attributes.

Let us further suppose that we can convert a quantity of one attribute into a quantity of another or that in some way the measure of the quantity of [del]every[/del] [ins]any[/ins] attribute can be compared with the measure of the quantity of every other.

It is required to find the mathematical laws of this conversion.

For this purpose, I propose to employ a notation copied from that of quaternions, demonstrating the laws of its application.

Let the capital letters denote the qualities or attributes of the objects to be measured.

Let the measure of a difference between two objects, the kind of difference being taken into account, be denoted thus A-B and also by x, y, etc.

Let (C-D) S [ins]$\frac{\text{A-B}}{\text{C-D}}$[/ins] be the measure of the difference which there is between A & B in those respects in which alone C & D differ.

Let (C-D) V [ins]$\frac{\text{A-B}}{\text{C-D}}$[/ins] denote the difference which there is between A & B in all those respects in which C & D do not differ.

Let T [ins]$\frac{\text{A-B}}{\text{[del]C-D[/del]}}$[/ins] be the number of units of difference between A & B.
