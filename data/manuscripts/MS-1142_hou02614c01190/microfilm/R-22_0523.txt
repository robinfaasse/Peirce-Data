Moreover writers do not make it sufficiently clear in each case whether the two words refer to different objects, or to the same objects regarded as a function of different variables, or whether their difference consists merely in the style of the words themselves. For instance, I open Graham's Synonyms at random and read "[u]Actual[/u] qualifies what is done, and refers to a previous act; [u]real[/u] refers to what simply exists as an object of thought. The former is active, the latter passive in meaning. When we speak of the actual condition of the country, we signify the condition into which it has been brought by previous acts; when we speak of its real, the state in which it exists as an object of contemplation. [u]Actual[/u] is opposed to suppositions; [u]real[/u] is opposed to imaginary, feigned, or artificial. An actual fact, a real sentiment."
