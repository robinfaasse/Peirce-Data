[u]Explanations of Two Map-Projections[/u]
[u]suitable for showing the Territory and[/u]
[u]Possessions of the United States of America[/u]

An ordinary map is to be conceived as exhibiting a
part or the whole of the Earth's surface, as projected
vertically (i.e. in the direction of gravity at each point) upon
upon the [del]mean[/del] surface of the sea, as it would be if it rested
in equilibrium under gravity ([del]including[/del] [ins]i.e. gravitation as modified by[/ins] the centrifugal
force of the diurnal rotation)

A "Conformal, or Orthomorphic, Map-Projection is usually
defined as one that preserves the [del]mag[/del] magnitude of every angle,
meaning at [ins]its vertex, or[/ins] the point where [del]the[/del] [ins]its[/ins] sides meet; but I think a
more useful definition is that it is a map-projection at
[ins]each[/ins] [del]every[/del] point of which the Scale is the same [del]in[/del] [ins]for[/ins] all direc-
tions of measurement, as well east-and-west as north-
and-south, and in all points of the compass. This property
belongs to every conformal map-projection and to none
other; and it seems to me that every map intended
for ordinary miscellaneous purposes should possess as
close an approximation to the conformal character
as the measurements that are made upon it are
likely to have.
