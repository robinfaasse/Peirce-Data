Ethics must appeal to Esthetics in forming its concep-
tion of the summum bonum; and Logic, [del]as[/del] the science
of controlled thought, which is but a species of controlled
conduct, [del]the fact[/del] must rest upon the science of such
conduct. [ins]A[/ins] Logic which does not recognize its relations
to Ethics must be fatally unsound in its [u]Methodentic[/u],
if not in its [u]Critic[/u].
Logic, which began [ins]historically and in each individual still begins,[/ins] with the wish to distinguish
good and bad reasonings, developes into a general
theory of signs. Its three departments are the physi-
ological, or [u]Speculative Grammar[/u]; its classificatory
part, judging particularly what reasoning is good and
what bad, or [u]Logical Critic[/u]; and finally, [u]Methoden-
tic[/u], or the principles of the production of valuable
courses of research and exposition.
In this syllabus, only [u]Speculative Grammar[/u] will
be touched upon.
