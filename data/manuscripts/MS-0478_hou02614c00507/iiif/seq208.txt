[u]Speculative Grammar[/u]. A [u]sign[/u], or [u]representamen[/u], is anything which so stands in relation to a second, called its [u]Object[/u], as to be capable of determining a [del]Third[/del] [ins]third[/ins], called its [u]Interpretant[/u], to be in the same triadic relation to that object [del]to[/del] [ins]in[/ins] which it stands itself. That is to say, the interpretant must be itself a sign capable of determining a sign of the same object, and so on, endlessly.

Signs are divided by two trichotomies. In the first place, every sign is either an [u]Icon[/u], an [u]Index[/u], or a [u]Symbol[/u]. Namely, no sign [del]s[/del] functions as a sign until it has an actual interpretant; but every sign acts [ins]as[/ins] a sign by virtue of a [u]significant character[/u] which is not necessarily dependent upon its having an [u]interpretant[/u], and thus being a sign, nor even upon its having an [u]object[/u], and thus being a reacting thing, or [u]existente[/u].
