Syllabus It has three branches: 1, [u]Speculative Grammar[/u], or the general theory of
the nature and meanings of signs, whether they be icons, indices, or
symbols; 2, [u]Critic[/u], which classifies arguments and determines the
validity and degree of force of each kind; 3, [u]Methodeutic[/u], which
studies the methods that ought to be pursued in the investigations in the
exposition, and in the application of truth. [ins]Each division depends on
that which precedes it.[/ins]
Metaphysics may be divided into, i, [u]General Metaphysics[/u], or
[u]Ontology[/u]; ii, [u]Psychical[/u], or [u]Religious, Metaphysics[/u], concerned
chiefly with the questions of 1, God, 2, Freedom, 3, Immortality;
and iii, [u]Physical Metaphysics[/u], which discusses the real nature
of Time, Space, Laws of Nature, Matter, etc. [ins]The second and third branches
appear at present to look upon one another with supreme contempt.[/ins]
[u]Nomological Physics[/u] is divided into, i, [u]Molar Physics[/u], Dyna-
mics and Gravitation; ii, [u]Molecular Physics[/u], Elaterics and Thermo-
Dynamics; iii, [u]Etherial Physics[/u], Optics and Electrics. Each divi-
sion has two subdivisions. The dependence of the divisions is
well-marked.
