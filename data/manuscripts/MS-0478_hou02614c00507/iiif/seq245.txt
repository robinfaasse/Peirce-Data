Archegetic Rules of Transformation.
The archegetic rules form a code from which all
permissions to transform are deducible. They do not
refer in any way to the Conventions, except to borrow
terms [del]expressive of parts[/del] [ins]descriptive[/ins] [del]parts[/del] of graphs and relations
of [del]such parts[/del] graphs. They constitute a pure mathema-
tical definition of Existential Graphs, [del]without[/del] which
so generalizes the conception of the system that the
graphs are no longer restricted to being representamens
but are any objects [ins]in[/ins] which some condition [ins]general[/ins] or [ins]in some sense[/ins] circumstance
renders certain transformations allowable.
[u]Rule I, Called the Rule of Erasure and Insertion.[/u]
Any heavy line having a part evenly enclosed may
there be broken; and any evenly enclosed [ins]detached[/ins] part of a graph
may be wholly erased with all it encloses.
Within an odd number of cuts [del]already[/del] existing any
graph may be inserted; and any parts of heavy lines
[del]at[/del] on the same oddly enclosed area may be joined
