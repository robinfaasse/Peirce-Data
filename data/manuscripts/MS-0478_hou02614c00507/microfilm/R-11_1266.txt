[u]Archegetic Rules of Transformation.[/u]

The [del]archegetic rules[/del] form a code from which all permissions to transform are deducible. They do not refer in any way to the Conventions, except to borrow terms [del]expressive of parts[/del] [ins]descriptive[/ins] of [del]parts of[/del] graphs and relations of [del]such parts[/del] graphs. They constitute a pure mathematical definition of Existential Graphs, [del]without which[/del] so generalizes the conception of the system that the graphs are no longer restricted to being representamens but are any objects [ins]in[/ins] which some condition [ins]in general[/ins] [del]or circumstance[/del] [ins]in some sense[/ins] renders certain transformations allowable.

[u]Rule I, Called the Rule of Erasure and Insertion.[/u]

Any heavy line having a part evenly enclosed may there be broken; and any evenly enclosed [del]detached[/del] [ins]detached[/ins] part of a graph may be wholly erased with all it encloses.

Within an odd number of cuts already existing any graph may be inserted; and any parts of heavy lines on the same oddly enclosed area may be joined
