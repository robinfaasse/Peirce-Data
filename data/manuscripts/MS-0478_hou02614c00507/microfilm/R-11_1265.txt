[ins]The sheet is[/ins] [del]a[/del] graph forming an unenclosed part of the Entire
[del]Graph of the sheet and connected with the other parts in[/del]
certain ways [ins]defined in the system,[/ins] called the [u]forms of connection[/u] of the system.
Graphs are of three kinds, according to the nature of the
forms of connection of their parts; namely, [u]Alpha Graphs[/u],
[u]Beta Graphs[/u], and [u]Gamma Graphs[/u]. Alpha Graphs are
graphs [del]which[/del] [ins]whose parts[/ins] have no other forms of connection than that
of standing side by side detached from one another; but they
may have [u]Cuts[/u], which, with their [u]Places[/u], [u]Areas[/u], and [u]Enclosures[/u],
are defined in a purely geometrical way as in the Conventions.
Of these, [ins]the[/ins] [del]Enclosures[/del] alone are Graphs. Every Area has its Entire
Graph, like the sheet, be it only a blank. A partial graph
[del]repl[/del] which has no parts connected by Forms of Connection
of the System is a [u]Spot-graph[/u]; and any Replica of it is a
[u]Spot[/u]. [ins]No cut passes through a spot.[/ins] [u]Beta Graphs[/u] differ from Alpha Graphs
