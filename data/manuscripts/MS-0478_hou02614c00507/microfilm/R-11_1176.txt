[u]Additional Deontic Rules for Beta Graphs[/u]

[u]Rule I[/u]. Any evenly enclosed line of identity may be broken
(though no hook can be left without any attached line of iden-
tity[del],[/del] and the point where a ligature emerges from a cut is to be considered
as a hook of the enclosure of the cut) and two lines of identity on
the same oddly enclosed area can be joined by a line of identity
running from one to the other.

[u]Rule II[/u]. This rule applies to Beta Graphs, provided that the copy
of an iterated graph have all its hooks attached to the very same
ligatures as the corresponding hooks of the original replicas,
these ligatures being extended inward for the purpose (but never
out beyond their original [del]pt[/del] areas). [del]And[/del] No ligatures [del]are to[/del] [ins]must[/ins] be
severed or joined to others, in the process. The rule also applies
to portions of lines of identity, as graphs. In particular, it allows
[del]loose ends of[/del] ligatures [ins]to be[/ins] extended [del]life[/del] inwards through cuts,
or, so extending, to be retracted outwards. Hooks of cut-
enclosures that have no connections within, cease to be
hooks, and are to be regarded as outside the enclosure.
