But crystallography is rather an offshoot from chemistry, to which
it furnishes a few facts, but hardly a principle. It is highly mathe-
matical and depends also on elaterics. Biology might be regarded
(although, as a matter of fact, no such view is taken) as the chem-
istry of the albuminoids and of the forms they assume. It is probable
that all the differences of races, individuals, and tissues are chemical,
at bottom. At any rate, the possible varieties of albuminoids are
amply sufficient to account for all the diversity of organic forms.

Pure chemistry seems, [del]at present, to consist of, 1, Physical[/del] Chem-
istry, consisting of the old chemical physics and the modern
chemical dynamics; 2, Organic Chemistry, Aliphatic and Aromatic;
3, Inorganic Chemistry, consisting of the doctrine of the elements,
their atomic weights, periodicity, etc., and the doctrine of com-
pounds.

Biology is divided into, 1, Physiology, and, 2, Anatomy. Physi-
ology is closely allied to chemistry and physics. Anatomy is divided
into many distinct fields, according to the nature of the forms
studied.

Descriptive Physics is divided into, 1, Geognosy, and, [del]2[/del] [ins]3[/ins], Astronomy.
Both have various well-known subdivisions.

Psychology is most naturally divided, according to the methods it
follows, into, i, [u]Introspectional Psychology[/u]; ii, [u]Experimental Psychol-[/u]
[u]ogy[/u]; iii, [u]Physiological Psychology[/u]; iv, [u]Child Psychology[/u].

This division only admits those parts of psychology which investi-
gate the general phenomena of mind. Special psychology belongs
to classificatory psychics. Both Experimental and Physiological
Psychology are dependent upon Introspective Psychology. But it
is hard to say which of them derives most from the other. Child
psychology depends on all the others. Psychology is too young a
science to have any further living divisions than such as are here
admitted.

Classificatory Psychics is divided into, i, Special Psychology, itself
consisting of, 1, [u]Individual Psychology[/u]; 2, [u]Psychical Heredity[/u]; 3,
[u]Abnormal Psychology[/u]; 4, [u]Mob Psychology[/u]; 5, [u]Race Psychology[/u]; 6,
[u]Animal Psychology[/u]; ii, [u]Linguistics[/u], a vast science, divided according
to the families of speech, and cross-divided into, 1, [u]Word Linguistics[/u];
2, [u]Grammar[/u]; and there should be a comparative science of forms
of composition; iii, [u]Ethnology[/u], divided into, 1, the [u]Ethnology of[/u]
[u]Social Developments[/u], customs, laws, religion, and traditions; and, 2,
the [u]Ethnology of Technology[/u].
