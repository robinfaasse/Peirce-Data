[marginalia]Syllabus[/marginalia] It has three branches: 1, [u]Speculative Grammar[/u], or the general theory of [del]the nature and meanings of signs, whether they be icons, indices, or symbols;[/del] 2, [u]Critic[/u], which classifies arguments and determines the validity and degree of force of each kind; 3, [u]Methodeutic[/u], which studies the methods that ought to be pursued in the investigations in the exposition, and in the application of truth. Each division depends on [ins]that which precedes it.[/ins]

[u]Metaphysics[/u] may be divided into, i, [u]General Metaphysics[/u], or [u]Ontology[/u]; ii, [u]Psychical[/u], or [u]Religious, Metaphysics[/u], concerned chiefly with the questions of 1, [u]God[/u], 2, [u]Freedom[/u], 3, [u]Immortality[/u]; and iii, [u]Physical Metaphysics[/u], which discusses the real nature of [u]Time[/u], [u]Space[/u], [u]Laws of Nature[/u], [u]Matter[/u], etc. [del]The second and third branches appear at present to look upon one another with supreme contempt.[/del]

[u]Nomological Physics[/u] is divided into, i, [u]Molar Physics[/u], Dynamics and Gravitation; ii, [u]Molecular Physics[/u], Elaterics and Thermo-Dynamics; iii, [u]Etherial Physics[/u], Optics and Electrics. Each division has two subdivisions. The dependence of the divisions is well-marked.
