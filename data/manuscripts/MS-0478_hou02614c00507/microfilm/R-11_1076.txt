[u]Sundry Logical Conceptions[/u]

[u]Phenomenology[/u] is that branch of science which is treated in Hegels [u]Phänomenologie des Geistes[/u] (a work far too inaccurate to be recommended to any but mature scholars, [ins]though perhaps[/ins] [del]but a very[/del] [ins]the most profound[/ins] [del]deep[/del] [del]written[/del] [del]great work[/del]), in which the author seeks to make out what are the elements [ins]or, if you please, the kinds of elements[/ins] that are invariably present [del]to the mind[/del] in whatever is, in any sense, in mind. According to the present writer, these [u]universal categories[/u] are three. Since all three are invariably present, a pure idea of any one, absolutely distinct from the others, is impossible; indeed, anything like a satisfactorily clean discrimination of them is a work of long and active meditation. They may be termed [u]Firstness[/u], [u]Secondness[/u], and [u]Thirdness[/u].

[u]Firstness[/u] is that which is such as it is positively and regardless of anything else.

[u]Secondness[/u] is that which is as it is in a second [del]something[/del] [ins]to a first[/ins] [del]it is[/del], regardless of any third.
