As for your Ladyship's Essay on
Time, you probably sent it to me be-
cause I mentioned that I had been
at work on Time & I might have added
on Space. But I must explain that
my scope in philosophy is extremely
narrow. I was bred in mathematics
and in the severest branches of physics,
and with the exception of my con-
tributions about Tychism & one paper
declining the idea of the [del]Differency[/del] [ins]Schmoll[/ins]
I have confined myself to endeavoring
to prove what could be scientifically
proved & be removed entirely from
the domain of opinion. As to Time
and Space, we have to distinguish,
first, our feelings connected with
them. With these I do not concern
myself. I imagine they are widely
different with different persons.
Secondly, our experience of Time and
Space, a most difficult question,
not in my opinion ripe for being
answered indubitably. It is only with Time & Space in their
third aspect, as [u]concepts[/u], that I deal with them; concepts
capable of exact definition. That is as much as to say,
that I seek to analyze logically relations of time & space.
