To Josephine. "Know, Madam, that the rules of morality
were not meant for such men as I," — such men being in particular
sovereigns. That is, their private morals are comparatively of little
importance. It was one of the faults of Napoleon that his ideas
were too bourgeois. He was too good a family man and too
[del]unreliable[/del]
[del]unscrupulous[/del] politically.

I desire to aid you in your study of Existential Graphs.
And to do this I will [ins]first[/ins] illustrate how it can be used to draw
conclusions from given premises, although it is to be remembered
that it is not particularly adapted to such work.

<u>Example 1</u>. Take the following premises:
If <u>a</u> is true, so is <u>b</u>.
If <u>a</u> is true, so is <u>d</u>.
It cannot be that <u>a</u> and <u>c</u> are both true.
If <u>c</u> is true, so is <u>b</u>.
If <u>c</u> is true, so is <u>d</u>.
Either <u>b</u> or <u>d</u> is true.
