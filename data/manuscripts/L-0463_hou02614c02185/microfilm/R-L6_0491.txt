My dear Lady Welby

Nona day has passed since I received your last letter that I have not lamented my inability to write that day to you, and promised myself that it should soon be done. A hundred domestic [del]causes[/del] [ins]causes[/ins] (the friction of everything in country life on this side of the Atlantic is most serious) have conspired with an inconsiderate contract I made to get some work on a supplement to the Century Dictionary done within a certain time to give no instant to myself when I did not fall asleep. I have tried to persuade myself that you being a dweller in the country like me, would have faith enough in your friend to be sure that some impossibility was at work. For we ought to drink in from nature a faith that we can with demand of the citadin. I might have dropped just a line of explanation; but I was always saying to myself "Oh, next Sunday there will be time to write the letter I want to write," and now so much time has blurred my idea of what it was I wanted so much to write.
