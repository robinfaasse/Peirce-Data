ideas of Firstness, of Secondness, and of Thirdness. By Firstness I
mean being such as it is, [ins]positively and[/ins] regardless of anything else, without any
reference to anything else. The typical ideas of Firstness are feelings.
Red, — say, the scarlet of your royal liveries, — is such as it is, indepen-
dently of blueness or any contrast. I mean the quality of the red itself &
not its being perceived or remembered. Many people think of existence,
mass, hardness, etc without analysis, without thinking of them as
[del]fein[/del] ideas, or as meaning that an experiment would have a certain result.
Or at any rate they think they do, and that is enough for ideoscopy. In
fact, the whole of what is in my mind at any instant, or of what is before
my mind, taken just as it instantaneously is without any movement
of thought or consciousness, — the total impression, all else being dis-
missed is an idea of Firstness. Whatever idea is not analyzed but
is thought in its positive suchness is an idea of Firstness. By Secondness
I mean that mode of being, [del](or being thought, & being falsely supposed to be thought,[/del]
are included under the "being" of ideoscopy) which is such as it is relatively
to a second directly, and regardless of any third. The types of ideas of second-
ness is the experience of effort, abstracted from all reference to a purpose.
The effort supposes resistance. It would not be effort if it were not opposed.
The idea of experience, as that cognitive effect which the [del]history[/del] [ins]course[/ins] of life has
compelled, is an idea of secondness. All dyadic relations are secondnesses.
