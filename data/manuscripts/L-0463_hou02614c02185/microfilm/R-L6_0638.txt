Consequently $\frac{x}{3(1+b)} + \frac{\bar{x}}{3(1+c)} = 0$ [del]or $\frac{x(1+c) + \bar{x}(1+b)}{3(1+b)(1+c)} = 0$ or[/del]
or $\frac{x(1+c) + (1-x)(1+b)}{3(1+b)(1+c)} = 0$ That is $x(1+c) - x(1+b) + (1+b) = 0$ or $x = \frac{b+1}{b-c}$
$\left(\bar{x} = \frac{c+1}{c-b}\right)$
Hence $P = \left[ \frac{b+1}{b-c} \cdot \frac{3b+1}{3(b+1)} + \frac{c+1}{c-b} \cdot \frac{1}{3(c+1)} \right] B + \left[ \frac{b+1}{b-c} \cdot \frac{1}{3(b+1)} + \frac{c+1}{c-b} \cdot \frac{3c+1}{3(c+1)} \right] C$
$= \frac{3b}{3(b-c)} B - \frac{3c}{3(b-c)} C$
And by symmetry
$Q = \frac{3c}{3(c-a)} C - \frac{3a}{3(c-a)} A$
$R = \frac{3a}{3(a-b)} A - \frac{3b}{3(a-b)} B$

Now then if there is any quantity $t$ such that $tP + \bar{t}Q = R$, $P, Q$ and $R$
lie on one ray. Take $t = \frac{b-c}{b-a}$ $\bar{t} = \frac{c-a}{b-a}$
$tP + \bar{t}Q = \frac{3b}{3(b-a)} B - \frac{3c}{3(b-a)} C + \frac{3c}{3(b-a)} C - \frac{3a}{3(b-a)} A = \frac{3a}{3(a-b)} A - \frac{3b}{3(a-b)} B$
[u]Q.E.D.[/u]
