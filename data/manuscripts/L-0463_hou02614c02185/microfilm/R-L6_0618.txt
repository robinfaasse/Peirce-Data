In an article dated [date]1867 May 14[/date] (Proc. Amer. Acad. of Arts and Sci. Vol VII p. 295) I defined logic as the doctrine of the formal conditions of the truth of symbols, i.e. of the relation of symbols to their Objects. Later, when I was led to perceive that the lines of demarcation between different sciences, in order to be Real, must be determined by those between the different social groups whose members are devote their lives to the prosecution, the advance of discovery, I saw that for the present, all Coenoscopic study of signs must be one and the same science, & I should certainly have decided to give the logic-book I am writing the title of "Logic, considered as Semeiotic", if I had not feared that everybody would think that title was a translation of "Logik als Semiotik betrachtet", and my contempt for German logic would not allow that.

Your science, Significs, must be intended for that branch of Semeiotic that studies the relation of Signs to their Interpretants, since no usage of language is better established among students of semeiotic than that distinction to which the elegant and correct John of Salisbury noted in the XIIth century, in Book II, chapter xx of his Metalogicus, as "quod fere in omnium ore celebre est, aliud scilicet esse quod appellativa significant, et aliud esse quod nominant. Nominantur singularia, sed universalia significantur." I take this from the Ed. of 1620.

I define a Sign as anything that is so determined by something other than else, called its Object, and so determines something [ins] [u]in a Mind[/u], [/ins] [del] called its Interpretant, [/del] that the latter is thereby determined mediately by the former. The words "in the Mind," are a sop to Cerberus that I make in despair of rendering my more general conception of a sign intelligible to many readers.

I recognize three Universes, distinguished by three Modalities of Being [ins] in the Objects they contain [/ins].

1. The Universe of Qualities, or Ideas, whose Being consists in their Real Possibility in themselves.

2. The Universe of Occurrences, [ins] [del] Single [/del] Existents [/ins] and Single Events, whose Being consists in their Real Actuality.

3. The Universe of Tendencies, and Laws, and Habits; whose Being consists in their Active Necessity (not being Necessitated, but Necessitating.)

Both a Sign, and its Object, and its Interpretant may have either of the three Modalities of Being.
