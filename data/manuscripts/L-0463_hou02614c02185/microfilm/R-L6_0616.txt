[pn]13[/pn] [del]3.[/del] [u]The Trichotomies of Signs[/u]
(as apprehended [del]1903 Dec 24[/del] [ins]1904 Dec 24[/ins])

I. A sign is necessarily present to the mind of its Interpreter. But
there are three sharply distinguished ways in which objects may be
Present: First, as Feelings, like Green, the sound of the French [u]i[/u] in [u]fine[/u]
(which I select because a nice ear can detect two tones in it, though the
Feeling itself, like every Feeling, is, in itself absolutely simple), or the odour of
the "gegenüber" eau de Cologne, or Pity, or the sentiment [del]excited[/del] [ins]produced[/ins] by
the skylarks note in the morning; Secondly, as resisting one's effort or
one's instinctive tendency; as any surprising experience, or any experi-
ence; Thirdly, as one's Habit or Association of Ideas; such as anything
stored away in Memory, or anything Familiar.

Consequently, Signs, in respect to their Modes of possible Pre-
sentation, are divisible ([u]σ[/u]) into,
A. Potisigns, or Objects which are Signs in so far as they are
possible.
B. Actisigns, or Objects which are Signs as experienced, or [ins]as[/ins] single
occurrences.
C. Famisigns, or Objects which are Signs as being familiar.

II. Objects may be presented, First, as mere Ideas or Possibles;
Secondly, as Actually exerting Brute Compulsion; and Thirdly,
as Rationally recommending themselves, or as Habitudes, to
which one is already reconciled.

Consequently, Signs, in respect to the manners in which
present their Objects, are divisible ([u]γ[/u]) into,
A. Descriptive Signs, which we may name Qualisigns, which
present their Objects under the guise of Ideas or Possibles; [del]such are[/del]
[ins]predicates and adjectives[/ins]
B. [del]Demonstrative[/del] [ins]Admonitive[/ins] Signs, like pointing the finger, and saying "that,"
which present their objects under the guise of objects of experience, and
such are Proper Names. They may be named Quidisigns.
