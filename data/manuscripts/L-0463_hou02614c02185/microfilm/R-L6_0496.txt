A Dicent is a sign which is true or false. It is
not necessarily an actual assertion. It may be
asserted, or formally sworn to, or denied, or put inter-
rogatively, or simply stated without assertion, denial,
or interrogation. It may be inquired into, doubted,
judged etc. It is always a possible assertion. An
assertion is a sign which professes to be an index, that
is to have a real relation to its object. A dicent would
seem to be a sign whose signified interpretant is to
represent it as if it were in a real relation to its object.
(Not as really being so, but in that form.)

An argument is a sign which collects assertions
(presumably already known) and offers them to
the interpretant as signs of the truth of the conclu-
sion. Suppose I say that an Argument is a sign
[del]which[/del] whose signified interpretant is to represent it as
if it were a sign. (For the argument is not necessarily
urged any more than the dicent is necessarily asserted.)
I confess that I am not altogether satisfied with these
definitions, but I believe I am on the right track.

Formerly I defined a Dicent as a sign which separately denotes its object, and
an argument as a sign which separately represents its interpretant (the con-
clusion) and these statements are true of the dicent and argument. But they do not
satisfy me as definitions. Every icon is a rheme. Every argument is a symbol.
