follows:
[del]Quali[/del] [ins]1[u]st[/u][/ins] According to the Mode of Being of the Sign itself
[del]Sins[/del] [ins]2[u]nd[/u][/ins] According to the Mode of Being of its Immediate Object
[del]Index[/del] [ins]3[u]rd[/u][/ins] According to the Mode of Being of its Dynamical Object
4[u]th[/u] According to the Mode of Relation of the Sign to its Dynamical Object
5[u]th[/u] According to the Mode of Being of its Immediate Interpretant
6[u]th[/u] According to the Mode of Being of its Dynamical Interpretant
7[u]th[/u] According to the Mode of Relation of the Sign to its Dynamical Interpretant
8[u]th[/u] According to the Mode of Being of its Normal Interpretant
9[u]th[/u] According to the Mode of Relation of the Sign to its Normal Interpretant
10[u]th[/u] According to the Mode of Triadic Relation between the Sign, its
Normal Interpretant and its Dynamical Object.

Of the ten resulting divisions of Signs, I have satisfactorily clear
conceptions of some, [del]but of others not.[/del] I will give them, as well as I
can marking each as either Clear, Middling, or Obscure.

I. (Middling) A Sign may be an Existent Object or Actual Simple Event, which
two kinds of objects I class together under the designation of Occurrence;
Secondly, a Sign maybe merely a [del]mere[/del] possible character; whether
it be a Quality, a Pointto Relation [ins]as on a geological map[/ins], or a Rational Relation. Thus, Red-
ness may betoken something, and so may Selfsacrifice.
Thirdly, a Sign maybe of the nature of a Habit or other Active Ten-
dency. [del]Thus the Habit or disposition to selfsacrifice maybe a
sign in a way in which the mere character in itself is not.[/del] A
word, in the sense in which the word [u]the[/u] which occurs some
twenty times on every page and is always one and the same
word is a sign of the nature of a general habit, while each [del]instance[/del]
[del]word is a sign of the nature of a general habit, while each[/del]
[del]of the 20 or more instances of it is a sign of that word, and is a sign of[/del]
[del]one of the other two classes. The distinction seems to be between a sign[/del]
that is a mere feeling, one that exerts a brute force, and one that has an intel-
lectual value.

II (Obscure) A sign may present its Object [ins]or an experienced object[/ins] as a thing [del]as for example, any noun does;[/del]
or it may present its Object as a predicate, as a verb does; or finally,
it may present its Object as a form of relation, as such words as
"whatever," "some," "when," [del]and other syncategorematics do.[/del]
