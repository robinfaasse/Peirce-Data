II. [del]It is[/del] [ins]It is[/ins] the night of Christmas which I trust is a "merry" one with you. I have been so engrossed with occupations which do seem to me poor, in the sense that almost anybody else could have executed them better than I, that I have not had time to add a word to this letter. For the last ten days, there has not been one in which I have been in bed, or slept at all, more than 3 hours, and it has all been such work as anybody could do. However, I will not permit myself to repine, as long as I cannot help the situation. The world does not think it wants original logical studies, least of all mine. So be it. I know it ought to think otherwise. Objects may be presented in three ways, thus:

1[u]st[/u], as mere Ideas or Possibles; in their Qualities distinct enough perhaps but not definitely this or that; mere somethings that may occur sometime somewhere.

2[u]nd[/u], as actually and brutally compelling attention;

3[u]rd[/u], as Rationally recommending themselves, or as Habitudes to which one is already reconciled.

Consequently, a Sign, in the manners in which they presents [del]their[/del] [ins]its[/ins] Objects to [del]the[/del] its Interpreter, may [ins]be (X) either[/ins]

A. [del]A[/del] Descriptives, telling what the characters of its Object are, or some of them, [del]but not[/del] [ins]but not[/ins] indicating where in the world such an Object is to be found, as the predicate of a proposition does (aside from any assertory force it may have)

B. [del]A[/del] Denotative, or indicative, like a demonstrative pronoun, or "Any", "Some", "Here", "Yonder", "Est qui" [ins]"Theodore Roosevelt"[/ins] etc. a signpost, or an obelisk (which according to Horatio Greenough merely says "Here, on this spot.")

C [del]As[/del] Copulant, may neither describe nor denote its Object but may merely express its logical relations as combining parts of that Object in particular ways; as "if - then", " - is - ", " - would be - ", [del]"is"[/del] "relatively to - for - " etc.
