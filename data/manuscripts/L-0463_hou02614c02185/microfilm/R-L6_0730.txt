DUNEAVES,
HARROW.

My dear Mr. Peirce
I have been very unwell & much hindered in writing
or I would have written sooner to tell you what
Prof. Stout now says. He thinks it "very import-
ant" (I think indeed it is) that your Essay should
appear in his collection -
I am therefore writing to Mr. A. Sidgwick & Mr.
Jourdain (who want [del]their[/del] [ins]their[/ins] contributions published
soon) to ask them to wait till you are ready.
Prof Stout is overwhelmed with work at present
but hopes to write a preface to the book later.
So please let us go on yours at once and
don't let anything worry or discourage you.
I have a feeling that things are going to be
better for you all round: "it is a long lane that
has no turning" says the proverb!
I only wish I could be of more use.
Please tell Mrs Peirce I should love to hear
from her - I quite understand her anxieties!
We all have them when we are devoted to
anyone.
I am sending you my book - But it is only a
plea for what the next & last volume will I
hope prove beyond cavil to be our greatest, most
immediate, most pressing need - Ever faithfully yours
Victoria Welby
