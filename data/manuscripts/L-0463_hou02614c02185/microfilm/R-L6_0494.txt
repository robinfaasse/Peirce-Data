In the secundal idea of dependence, we may think of the dependent either
as it is dependent or as it is in itself independently of the dependence. So
[del]in the idea of a sign,[/del] a sign has two objects, the object as it really is inde-
pendently of being signified or represented, and the object as it is repre-
sented to be. So there are three interpretants; the interpreting sign as it is in
itself, the interpretant as it is determined to correspond with to the object,
and the interpretant as the sign means it to be determined. Signs
may be divided in three ways; first, in regard to their own mode of
being in themselves; second, in regard to their relation to their objects;
and thirdly in regard to their relations to their [del]de[/del] interpretants. The
mode of being of a sign may be that of a mere quality, when I call it a
Qualisign; or it may have an individual existence, when I call it a
Sinsign ([u]Sin[/u] being the syllable found in simul, singular, etc), or it may be
a general type, as when we usually speak of [u]the[/u] being [u]one[/u] word and [u]an[/u]

there are so many "words" on the average, on a page of a given book, when we

I call a Legisign. The difference between a legisign and a qualisign is that
