In order to state the nature of logical analysis, I must make an addition or two to logical terminology.

By a [u]proper name[/u] is usually meant an arbitrary designation of an existent, whether it be real or a fictitious existent. We do not even call somewhat arbitrary designations of [del]the[/del] single historical events, such [del]the[/del] "the conquest," "the reformation," "the French revolution" (as if there had been no other,) "the battle of Waterloo," "the statute of frauds" "Proper Names." Far less do we call names of single subjects the modality of whose being is [del]possibility[/del] [ins]probability[/ins] or that of a habit, disposition or tendency which I call the modality of [del]necessity[/del] [ins]destiny[/ins], Proper Names. But I propose to extend the term to all designations which are essentially names of single subjects, and are not merely names of classes whose multitude happens to be 1. Such is "sun". There happens to be but one; but if there were another, it would be called "another sun", while the French revolution is the name given to a single historical movement, in spite of the fact that the words, if understood as descriptive, apply perfectly to a number of other events. I will also call "the attraction of gravitation" a Proper Name, [del]altho[/del] although it contains the descriptive word "attraction", so that "gravitation" is [del]more[/del] a more regular Proper Name. I also call the abstract designation of a [ins]single[/ins] character, such as "antitypy," "truth", "veracity", [del]a[/del] proper name as long as it is definite. If it be vague, one is puzzled to know what to call it, not because the Definition of a Proper Name as a designation essentially applicable [del]but[/del] to one object and to no other, is vague, but because we do not know what the force of the designation is, any more than if it were in a foreign tongue.
