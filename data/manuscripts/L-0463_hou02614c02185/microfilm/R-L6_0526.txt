ally Priman and the other Secundan. That is, one is the agent
or determining factor, the other the patient, or determined factor
(Of course in innumerable cases there is no truth in this). Also in
a triadic relationship, a definite one of the [del]three subjects[/del] [ins]two[/ins] is
Priman and a definite other is Secundan. This depends upon
how the relation is thought; but a triadic relation is usually
a truth of thought, and there is a certain naturally Primary
way of thinking of it. In the [del]grea[/del] vast majority of languages
there is one most natural way of expressing a dyadic relation
of the ordinary type, which puts one subject in the Nominative
case and the other in the Accusative; as "he loves her" which
is simpler than "she charms him," though probably not so true.
