15[/marginalia] to Hurrah for [u]the glorious revolt of 1688[/u] (if I have the date right) You seem to desire to render ordinary gabble as exact in its terminology as science; but I doubt very much whether the end would be worth the cost of the powder & shot the fight would cost, even if there were any likelihood of its succeeding.

Moreover, if you are going to make such attempt, I would counsel making as sure as possible you are right in the ground you take. I forget which of your charming little Essays, which are as agreeable to read as Bacon's, though not always so judicious, you ask how "aspire" comes to have one of the meanings it has; and you offer [del]as[/del] what [u]might[/u] be the explanation if the facts agreed with it. But you do not attempt to show that your guess is right. It happens not to be so; for, as the [u]Oxford Dict'y[/u] shows the sense [del]to be[/del] explained is about a century older than the sense which you suppose explains it. While you will find there ample reason to see that it comes from the original sense of [u]to pant after[/u]. That meaning which you suppose to be the explaining one comes from mist and vapor breathing up from a fen. I recollect now that the meaning [del]to be[/del] explained was that of [u]desiring eagerly to gain some virtue or perfection[/u].
