ESSAYS ON SIGNIFICS.

It is proposed to bring together a number of representative studies of a critic-
al and constructive character bearing upon the group of topics which Lady Welby
has so long endeavoured to bring into prominence. The word which she has fixed
upon as covering the whole range of these questions is "Significs"---the study of the
use of signs in all their bearings. For this purpose it is desirable to make an
appeal for co-operation to those who have shown sympathy for her work.
As indicating the kind of subjects that will come within the scope of the volume,
the following suggestions are offered.

1. The Analogical Extension of Interpretation from one Field of Experience to
another.

2. Value and Purpose as Factors in Significance.

3. The Function of Metaphor in Thought.

4. The Figurative Element in the Conceptions of Space and Time.

5. The Relation of Mathematical Symbolism to Significance.

6. The Sources of Obscurity and Ambiguity in the Use of Words.

7. The Extent of Voluntary Control over Language.

8. The Means of Developing in Children a true Insight into the Nature of
Language.

Further suggestions as to desirable subjects will be welcomed.

The Essays will be edited by Professor Stout, The University, St. Andrews,
and Dr. Slaughter, Elmwood, Bickley, Kent.
