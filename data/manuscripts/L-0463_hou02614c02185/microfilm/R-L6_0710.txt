Dear Mr. Peirce

Your last wonderful letter haunts me,
not merely with the sense of its command-
ing power of thought and exposition,
and its revelations of some possible
developments of Signifies, but also with
the note of anxiety and trouble in
it. — I do hope that by now your skies
have cleared again, that Mrs Peirce is
better and life smoother —

I have been reading your deeply valued
letter many times, & shall read it yet
again as each time some fresh as-
pect of its rich suggestion opens be-
fore one.
