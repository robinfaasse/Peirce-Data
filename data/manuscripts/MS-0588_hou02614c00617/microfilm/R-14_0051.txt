The volume contains two other papers
relating to deductive logic. In one of these,
Mr. Gilman develops those rules for the
combination of relative numbers of
which the general principles of probabi-
lities are special cases. In the other, Dr.
Marquand shows how a counting machine
on a binary system of numeration will
exhibit De Morgan's eight moods of
universal syllogism.

There are besides two papers upon induc-
tive logic. In the first, Dr. Marquand
explains the deeply interesting views of
the Epicureans, known to us mainly
through the work of Philodemus,
περὶ σημείων καὶ σημειώσεων, which
exists in a fragmentary state in a
Herculaneum papyrus.
