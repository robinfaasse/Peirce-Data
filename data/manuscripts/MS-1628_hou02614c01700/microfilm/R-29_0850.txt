CHAPTER II.

Day broke upon the deserted
banquet-hall of yesterday; still
the Count von Schlossinger paced
its marble floor. The sun was
shining through the Eastern win-
dows, playing and glittering in
the empty glasses. The Count
von Schlossinger stops to listen
to a cheery voice. At the fur-
ther end of the hall a door leads
us into a rich, purple parlor, evi-
dently fitted up by the tasty
S.P.A.F. Junks & Co. [See Adver-
tisement in another column] at one
end of which a golden [del]4[/del] [ins]8[/ins] upon
the tapestry winked and sparkled
like the planet Satan. The ap-
artment had none of that frigid-
ity which we noticed in the little
used banquet-hall, but though
everything reminded me of home,
a silent horror filled the air.

The Count entered, leading a
young lady of an intellectual coun-
tenance and elegant form, his
daughter. Who could guess that
morning's vivacious noble was
night's dark conspirator?
