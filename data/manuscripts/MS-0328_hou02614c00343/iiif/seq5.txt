It is almost needless to say that I do not
regard the [del]principle[/del] [ins]method[/ins] of pragmatism to be an
invention of mine. I think it too fundamental
an idea to be any man's invention. It has its
roots in, — I will not content myself with saying
in the constitution of the Human Mind; — it
has its roots in the very essence of Mind as
mind. I had myself come to have a right
to opinions in philosophy through the study,
first, of Kant, then of Berkeley and other English
philosophers, then of Aristotle, and finally of
the great scholastic doctors, without neglecting
other great thinkers, Plato, Descartes, Spinoza,
Leibniz, Hegel, and the rest. I conceived
