The thought of early and often visits to such places
has made me somewhat familiar with the external
signs of such a catastrophe. I cannot think that any
one of my family or of my friends who has died
within my remembrance has gone to his grave
alive. And yet the possibility of such a case
as this story would seem to indicate is enough to
keep one awake o’ nights. I should like to know
more of it — the particulars of the case.
I hope I am not given to morbid fancies.
I have sometimes thought I was: but I am not sure
of it. I hope it is not so.

I wonder if any one will ever read these
pages — or if they will lie here in this old drawer
until they rot and are thrown away as rubbish.
It is no matter. I do not care. I only write
because I like to do so — because it is a pleasure to
me. I have no ambition to be an author — or at
least I think I have not.

And yet it is a pleasant thing to feel that
some one may some time read what we have
written — that some eye may some time look upon
these lines and some heart may some time feel a
little interest in what we have felt and thought.
It is a sort of immortality — a very humble one,
it is true — but still an immortality.

I have been reading today a very strange
book. It is called “The Mysteries of Udolpho.”
It is a very old-fashioned book — one that was
very popular in its day, but which is now almost
forgotten. It is a very wild and romantic story,
full of ghosts and haunted castles and all sorts of
terrible things. It is very well written, too — and
extremely interesting. I like it very much.

I have also been reading some of the old
English poets. I have always been fond of
them. I like their simple and natural style —
their hearty and honest way of speaking. There
is a freshness and a vigor about them which
I do not find in the modern poets. They
seem to speak from the heart — and their
words have a ring of truth about them which
I like.

I have been thinking today of my mother.
She has been dead many years — but I
can remember her very well. She was a
very good and kind woman — and I loved
her very much. I remember how she used
to sit by the window and sew — and how
she used to talk to me. I remember her
gentle voice and her kind smile. I wish
she were here now. I should like to
talk to her — to tell her all that I
have been thinking and feeling. I
think she would understand me. She
always did.
