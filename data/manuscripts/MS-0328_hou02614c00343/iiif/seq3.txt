[del]Preface. Showing Why Pragmatism ought be investigated.[/del]
In the autumn of [ured]1877[/ured] I took passage for
Plymouth, England. There were only four first-
cabin passengers beside myself, two ladies
travelling separately alone and two gentlemen
travelling separately, equally unprotected. They
found themselves very congenial and paired off.
This left me the smoking room entirely to
myself; and I occupied myself by writing an
article in which I enunciated a logical maxim
for making ideas clear, which I called Pragmatism.
Some twenty years later I found that pragmatism
was attracting so much attention that, in the
opinion of those best qualified to judge, it had
become one of the most important and hopeful
methods of philosophizing of the day. I found
that others were carrying this principle [del]much[/del]
