I had been expecting the early part of January
to be quiet, but found that according to
my calculations there was some probability
of storms during the first ten days, or at
the least of quite severe weather, and
it seemed likely that the 4th would
be a critical date. The disturbance
was not slow in appearing, for by the
evening of the 31st Dec. the barometer
began to fall, and though there was
nothing very marked as yet, it seemed
probable that New Year's Day would
witness some atmospheric changes. On
the 1st there was a good deal of fog in
London and the suburbs, and some rain fell
at intervals, and on the 2nd the barometer
fell rapidly, and by the 3rd there were
reports of gales and storms from all parts
of the coast. On the 4th, the date which
had been specially pointed out, there were
heavy gales of wind in many parts of the
country, and on the morning of the 5th
a most severe frost was experienced in
some localities, the thermometer falling to
within a few degrees of zero. On the 6th
and 7th the frost continued, though in a
less severe form, and there was some
fog, and the 8th and 9th were likewise cold
with some mist and snow in places. On the
whole, the forecast for the first ten days of
the month was fairly well justified. The
remainder of the month, from the 11th to the
31st, promised to be less disturbed, and
though there was some wind and rain on
the 14th and 15th, and again on the 19th
and 20th, the weather on the whole was
not particularly remarkable, and there was
nothing that could be called a storm,
though there were some sharp frosts towards
the end of the month.
