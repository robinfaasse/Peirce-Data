having on one side a full face &
one in profile, as far as I can make
out, and on the other an elephant's
head, referring no doubt to the battle
of Ipsus, B.C. 301, which was decided
by his 480 elephants. The inscription
is just about in a condition to place
one given to these matters.

After this you have another of
Alexander's generals, Lysimachus,
with a head of Mars and a rushing
lion. This was struck between
B.C. 306 & 282.

And finally you have a coin of
Hieron II, the friend and relative of
Archimedes, and struck during
the life time of this philosopher.
He must have been perfectly familiar
with the head and trident of Neptune
on these coins, and it would be
a little singular if he had not
seen and handled some from
the same die that furnished the
enclosed.

It seems, to me, quite strange that
this old trash has come down to our
times, and I must confess it has
for me much more interest
than polished precious stones & minerals.

Please remember me kindly to Mrs
Peirce, and don't take the trouble
to write a note of acknowledgment,
as I shall get your signature on
the return postal-card.

Always sincerely yours
Ogden N. Rood.

C. S. Peirce Esqr
Milford, Pa.
