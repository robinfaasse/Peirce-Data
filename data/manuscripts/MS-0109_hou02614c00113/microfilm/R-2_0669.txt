$S$ and $D$ are integers. They are prime to one another.
For since
$$\begin{matrix}
H = S + D \\
B = S - D,
\end{matrix}$$
any common factor of $S$ and $D$ would be a common
factor of $H$ and $B$, contrary to $\S 4$. By $\S 3$
$$U^2 = SD.$$
Hence, since $S$ and $D$ are prime to one another, each is a
square. If then we use the notation
$$\begin{matrix}
V = \sqrt{D} \\
W = \sqrt{S},
\end{matrix}$$
$V$ and $W$ are integers prime to one another. Thus, in
every Pythagorean triangle the legs and hypothenuse
are of the forms
$$\begin{matrix}
A = 2VW \\
B = (W+V)(W-V) \\
H = W^2 + V^2.
\end{matrix}$$
[del]Since[/del] Then by $\S 5$, of the two integers, $V$ and $W$, one is even
and the other odd. [Several corollaries as to the divisibility
of $A$, $B$, and $H$ might here be drawn.]

In like manner, $H+A$ and $H-A$ are odd numbers, so that
denoting them by
$$\begin{matrix}
\Sigma = H+A \\
\Delta = H-A,
\end{matrix}$$
$\Sigma$ and $\Delta$ are [ins]odd[/ins] integers. Since
$$\begin{matrix}
H = \frac{1}{2}(\Sigma + \Delta) \\
A = \frac{1}{2}(\Sigma - \Delta)
\end{matrix}$$
any common [del]odd[/del] factor of $\Sigma$ and $\Delta$ would be a common factor of $H$
and $A$; so that $\Sigma$ and $\Delta$, being odd, are prime to one another.
Since
$$B^2 = \Sigma\Delta$$
$\Sigma$ and $\Delta$ are, each of them, squares, and if we use the notation
$$\begin{matrix}
V' = \sqrt{\Delta} \\
W' = \sqrt{\Sigma}
\end{matrix}$$
