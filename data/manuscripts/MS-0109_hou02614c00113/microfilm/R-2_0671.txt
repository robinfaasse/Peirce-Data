Treating $\frac{P}{Q}$ as unknown and solving by continued
fractions or otherwise, we obtain a series of approximate
solutions [del]which[/del] [ins]and they[/ins] being put in the form of rational frac-
tions reduced to their lowest terms, [del]may[/del] their numerators
and denominators may be assumed respectively equal
to $P$ and $Q$, and then, by the formulae of §7, Pythagorean
triangles can be obtained [del]the ratio of[/del] whose legs shall
be as near to $r$ as may be desired.

<u>Examples</u>.

1. Take $r = 4\frac{1}{2}$. Then the equation is either
$$(\frac{P}{Q})^2 + 9 (\frac{P}{Q}) = 1$$
or
$$(\frac{P}{Q})^2 + \frac{4}{9} (\frac{P}{Q}) = 1.$$

The approximations to the solution of the first of these are the
ratios of successive pairs of the following numbers
$\begin{matrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 9 \\ 82 \\ 747 \end{matrix}$

Taking $P=1$ $Q=9$ both being odd,
$A = \frac{1}{2} (9+1)(9-1) = 40$
$B = 9$
$H = \frac{1}{2} (81+1) = 41$
$A^2+B^2 = 1600 + 81 = 1681 = H^2$
$\frac{A}{B} = 4.44$

Taking $P=9$ $Q=82$ one being even
$A = 2 \cdot 9 \cdot 82 = 1476$
$B = (82+9)(82-9) = 6643$
$H = 81 + 6724 = 6805$
$A^2+B^2 = 2178576 + 44129449 = 46308025 = 6805^2 = H^2$
$\frac{B}{A} = 4.5007$

Taking the second equation, the first approximation is $\frac{P}{Q} = \frac{4}{5}$. Taking
$P=4, Q=5$, since one of these is even, we have
$A = 2 \cdot 4 \cdot 5 = 40$
$B = (5+4)(5-4) = 9$
$H = 25 + 16 = 41$

[del]Another[/del] [ins]The second[/ins] approximation is $\frac{P}{Q} = \frac{45}{56}$. Taking $P=45, Q=56$
$A = 2 \cdot 45 \cdot 56 = 5040$
$B = (56+45)(56-45) = 1111$
$H = 3136 + 2025 = 5161$
$\frac{A}{B} = 4.536$
$A^2 = 25401600$
$B^2 = 1234321$
$H^2 = 26635921$
