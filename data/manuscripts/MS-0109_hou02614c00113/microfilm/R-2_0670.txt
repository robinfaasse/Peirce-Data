$\S 7$. Let $P$ and $Q$ be any two integers whatever, prime to one another, of which $Q$ is the larger. Then, if either $P$ or $Q$ is even, let
$$\begin{aligned}
A &= 2PQ \\
B &= (Q+P)(Q-P) \\
H &= Q^2 + P^2
\end{aligned}$$
and we have, identically,
$$A^2 + B^2 = 4P^2Q^2 + Q^4 - 2P^2Q^2 + P^4 = (Q^2 + P^2)^2 = H^2.$$
But if both $P$ and $Q$ are odd, let
$$\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2}(Q+P)(Q-P) \\
B &= QP \\
H &= \frac{1}{2}(Q^2 + P^2)
\end{aligned}$$
and we have, identically,
$$A^2 + B^2 = \frac{1}{4}(Q^4 - 2Q^2P^2 + P^4) + Q^2P^2 = \frac{1}{4}(Q^2 + P^2)^2 = H^2.$$
It follows from $\S 6$ that there is no Pythagorean triangle which cannot be thus formed from any two integers prime to one another.
