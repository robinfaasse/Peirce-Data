In the Shemitic languages, for example, every common noun is regarded as a formation from a verb. Even if no such verb exists, it would seem that the Shemites cannot think of a noun except as a part of a verb; for they give it a form as if it were of that nature. Indeed, there are Indo European languages in which the idea of the common noun is not completely hardened. For it is plain that with nouns, full nouns, alone, one could not frame a sentence which should satisfy the mind as completely expressed. Now the majority of languages are destitute of any substantive verb 'is'. In Ancient Egyptian, a pronoun "that" usually takes its place.
