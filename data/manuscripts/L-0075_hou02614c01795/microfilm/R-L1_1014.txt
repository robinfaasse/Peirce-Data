CHICAGO, [date]January 19th, 1903[/date].

Hon. H.H.D. Peirce,

Department of State,

Washington, D.C.

My dear Sir:

I understand that there is some possibility that the Carnegie Institution may concern itself with the matter of the publishing of the contributions of Mr. Charles S. Peirce to the logic of the sciences. Permit me to say that I hope this project may be carried out. So far as the interests of general philosophy and logic are concerned, I do not see any way in which the institution could better further them. If there is any way in which I can properly be of more specific use, I shall be glad.

Yours truly,

JOHN DEWEY.

Dewey lately transferred to Columbia, is a pupil of mine, & one of the shining lights of the philosophy of today, — and highly original as most of my pupils have turned out. I always devote myself to developing [del]that[/del] originality in my pupils & am never better pleased than when they attack my position.
