Dear Sir:

I understand that an application has lately been made to the Trustees of the Carnegie Institute, asking them to devote a portion of the funds at their disposal to the assistance of your brother, Mr. Charles S. Peirce, thus enabling him to devote himself uninterruptedly to the further working out of his views on Logic and Philosophy.

In my opinion no case more worthy of careful and favorable consideration could well be presented to the Trustees of the Carnegie Fund. In the first place, there is an urgent demand at the present time from the side of the natural sciences for some philosophical treatment of methodological questions. This is shown for example, by the work of Karl Pearson in England, and that of Mach and Ostwald in Germany. About a year ago Ostwald started a journal - the [u]Annalen der Naturphilosophie[/u] - to deal with the philosophy of the sciences, including general questions of the theory of knowledge and of scientific methodology. In this country such a book as Professor Brooks "Principles of Zoology" (to mention only a single example) shows that the same need of a philosophical basis has been felt by the scientific workers of America.
