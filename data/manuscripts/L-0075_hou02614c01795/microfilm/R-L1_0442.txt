[u]No 7.[/u] [u]Of the Categories in their Mediate Aspects.[/u]

These two memoirs develope and render clear a considerable number of [del]conceptions[/del] of which I shall make constant use in the [del]remaining[/del] memoirs, and which are of constant use in all parts of philosophy and even in mathematics.

[u]No. 8.[/u] [u]Examinations of Historical Lists of Categories.[/u] My list differs from those of Aristotle, Kant, and Hegel, in that they never really went back to examining the Phenomenon to see what was to be observed there; and I do not except Hegel's [u]Phänomenologie[/u] from this criticism. They simply took current conceptions and arranged them. Mine has been a more fundamental and more laborious undertaking; since I have worked up from the percepts to the highest notions. I examine these systems as well as some others.
