No. 25. [u]Of Mixed Arguments.[/u]

This is a highly important memoir upon a subject of singular difficulty, although at first blush one would not anticipate any difficulty or interest in it.

No. 26. [u]On Fallacies.[/u]

There would be no advantage in devoting a special memoir to a strictly scientific treatment of fallacies in general. It [del]would[/del] [ins]be[/ins] like a chapter in a treatise on trigonometrywhich should treat of possible errors in trigonometry. But since my purpose is that these memoirs should not only be scientific but that they should also be useful, I propose to devote this to Fallacies, because I think, though it is not an attractive subject for a logician, that I can make the discussion very useful. I shall not attempt a strict theoretical development, but shall discuss fallacies under five heads, according to their causes, showing under each head how they come about, how we can avoid them in original reasoning and in controversy, how to [del]detect[/del] [ins]detect[/ins] them and reply to others who fall into them. The five heads are: 1, slips; 2, misunderstandings; 3, fallacies due to bad logical notions; 4, fallacies due to moral causes; and 5, sophisms invented to test logical rules, etc. This will thus be of an entirely exceptional character among the memoirs, more so even than the first.
