The only fault that Kant's distinction has is that it is ambiguous, owing to his ignorance of the logic of relatives and consequently of the real nature of mathematical proof. He had his choice of making either one of two distinctions. Let definitions everywhere be substituted for definita in the proposition. Then it was open to him to say that if the proposition could be reduced to an identical one by merely [del]widening the subjects[/del] [ins]attaching aggregates to[/ins] its subjects and components to its predicate it was an analytic proposition; but otherwise was synthetic. Or he might have said that if the proposition could be proved to be true by logical necessity without further hypothesis it was an analytic one; but otherwise was synthetic.
