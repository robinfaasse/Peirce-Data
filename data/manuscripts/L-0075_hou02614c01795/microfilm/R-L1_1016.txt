No. 31 Nassau Street,

New York, [del]November 21,[/del] [ins]November 21,[/ins] 1905

Andrew Carnegie, Esq.,

Dear Sir:

You will remember I hope, my calling on you to ask the exercise of your munificence in aiding Professor Charles S. Peirce for a very few years to enable him to complete and publish some of the results of his studies on Logic.

You then requested me to furnish you with statements as to the quality of Professor Peirce's work from persons who could speak with authority.

Consequently I enclose to you copies of letters, written by Professors Royce and James of Harvard, Creighton of Cornell and Sheldon of Columbia, prefaced by copies of three letters from President Roosevelt, Secretary Root and Honorable Wayne MacVeagh.

The original letters were written in 1903, when the name of Professor Peirce was proposed for the Secretaryship of your own Carnegie Institute. The originals are probably still on file, having been then written to Dr. Gilman.

I shall of course be glad to answer any other inquiries or doubts, and hope that you can conclude to aid this remarkable man who has had the fate of so many other geniuses.

Respectfully yours,

[Albert Stickney]
