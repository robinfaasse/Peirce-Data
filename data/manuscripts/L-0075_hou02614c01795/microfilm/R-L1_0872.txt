Suppose, then, that, being seated in a street car, I remark a man opposite
to me whose appearance and behaviour unite characters which I am
surprised to find together in the same person. I ask myself, How can
this be? Suppose I find this problematic reply: Perhaps he is an ex-priest.
He is the very image of such a person; he presents an [u]icon[/u] of an ex-priest.
Here is an [u]iconic[/u] argument, or [u]abduction[/u]. The word "belike" is appropri-
ate in expressing its conclusion. He is, belike, an ex-priest; or it is "likely"
that he is one, albeit there is no proper [u]indication[/u] of it. Secondly, it now
occurs to me that if he is an ex-priest, he should be tonsured; and in order to test
this, I say something to him calculated to make him take off his hat. He does
so, and I find that he is indeed tonsured. Here at last is an [u]indication[/u]
that my theory is correct. I can now say that he is [u]presumably[/u] an
