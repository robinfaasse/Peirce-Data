Harvard University,

Committee of the Carnegie Institute.

Gentlemen:

I have just learned with extreme regret that the application of Dr. Charles Saunders Peirce for assistance by the Institute in the execution of his plan of philosophic work has been rejected.

I beg to submit an urgent recommendation for a revision of the judgment in this case. Some few years ago, in common with many others, I looked forward to the completion of a series of works outlined by Dr. Peirce which he proposed to publish by subscription, and which from one of his remarkable attainments in logic and philosophy promised to be amongst the most notable of American productions in those fields, and also much the largest single systematic achievement by any American. The plan failed from want of support -- in my judgment, to the very considerable loss of philosophy. The learning, subtlety, originality and boldness of his suggestions in fundamental philosophy place him among the foremost thinkers in this country in his chosen subjects. His work, in the Algebra of logic, has been treated with great consideration by important Germany writers. I am convinced that a highly valuable talent will be passed by and a great scientific work discouraged, if all support should be withheld from his undertaking.

Very respectfully yours,
Dickinson S. Miller.

[date]January 3, 1903[/date].
