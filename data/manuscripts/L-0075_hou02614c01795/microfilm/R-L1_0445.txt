[u]No. 11. On the Logical Conception of Mind.[/u]
This memoir is here placed [ins], or perhaps better before No 9,[/ins] for the sake of perspicuity of
exposition. The matter of it will have to be somewhat transformed
at a later stage. If the logician is to talk of the operations of
the mind at all, as it is desirable that he should [ins], though [ins]it is[/ins] not[/ins]
scientifically indispensible, then he must mean by "mind"
something quite different from the object of study of the psy-
chologist; and this logical conception of mind is developed
in this memoir and rendered clear. [ins]* [See footnote p. 17½.][/ins]
