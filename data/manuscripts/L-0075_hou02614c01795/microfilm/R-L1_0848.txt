[u]33. On Metaphysics.[/u] In this memoir I defend essentially Aristo-
telian opinions which give room for the real being and agency
of ideas, distinguishing an [u]esse in futuro[/u] from an [u]esse in
praeterito[/u] and an [u]esse in praesente[/u], and also a mode of
action substantially Aristotle's final causation as well as
physical action which is substantially his efficient causation.

[u]34. On the Uniformity of Nature.[/u] There is a group of questions be-
longing to metaphysics, yet specially interesting to the logician
connected with the uniformity of nature. I propose in this memoir
to discuss all of these and to treat them historically.

[u]35. On Nominalism and Realism.[/u] This is another metaphysical ques-
tion interesting to the logician [del]upon[/del] [ins]about[/ins] which I think that modern
philosophers have erred greatly.

[u]36. On the Reality and Nature of Time and Space.[/u] I hold, with
Newton, that Time and Space are real entities. I discuss the
question of whether they are so or not, and then consider their
real properties.
