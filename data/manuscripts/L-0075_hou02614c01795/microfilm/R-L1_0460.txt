[u]On Fallacies[/u].

There would be no advantage in [del]making[/del] [ins]devoting[/ins] a special memoir to a strictly scientific treatment of fallacies in general. It would be like a chapter in a treatise on trigonometry which should treat of possible errors in trigonometry. But since my purpose is that these memoirs should not only be scientific but [ins]that[/ins] they should also be useful, I propose to devote this to Fallacies, because I think, though it is not an attractive subject for a logician, that I can make the discussion very useful. I shall not attempt a strict theoretical development, but shall discuss fallacies under five heads, according to their causes, showing under each head how they come about, how we can avoid them in original reasoning and in controversy, how detect [ins]them[/ins] and reply to others who [ins]fall into[/ins] [del]use[/del] them. The five heads are; 1[sup]st[/sup], slips; 2[sup]d[/sup], misunderstandings; 3[sup]d[/sup], fallacies due to bad logical notions; 4[sup]th[/sup], fallacies due to moral causes; and 5[sup]th[/sup], sophisms invented to test logical rules, etc. This will thus be of an entirely exceptional character among the memoirs [ins]more so even than the first.[/ins]
