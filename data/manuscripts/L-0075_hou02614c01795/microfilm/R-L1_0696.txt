[u]27. Of Methodeutic[/u]. The first part of this memoir must be devoted to gaining a precise conception of the nature of methodeutical logic. Arguments are not here examined as to their validity. It is assumed that they are true. But just as critical logic inquires whether a sign refers to its intended ultimate object, the reality; so methodeutic inquires whether, granting that it is true, it [del]ref[/del] determines its intended ultimate interpretant; that is to say, whether it is pertinent to the general purpose in view.
