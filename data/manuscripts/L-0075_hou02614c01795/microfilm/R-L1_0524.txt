[u]Induction[/u] is reasoning which professes to pursue such a method that, [del]being persisted in, the special application of it (when it is applicable)[/del] [ins]if persisted in,[/ins] must at least [del]indefinitely[/del] [ins]indefinitely[/ins] approximate [del]to[/del] the truth [ins]about the subject in hand,[/ins] in the long run. [u]Abduction[/u] is reasoning, which professes to be such, that in case there is any ascertainable truth concerning the matter in hand, [del]this reasoning[/del] [ins]this [del]general[/del] method of reasoning[/ins] must [ins](though not necessarily each special application of it)[/ins] eventually approximate to the truth.

Of these three classes of reasonings [u]Abduction[/u] is the lowest. So long as it is sincere, [del]without which[/del] [ins]and if it be not[/ins], it does not deserve to be called reasoning, [u]Abduction[/u] cannot be absolutely bad. For sincere efforts to reach the truth, no matter in how [del]mistaken[/del] [ins]wrong[/ins] a way they may be commenced, cannot fail ultimately to attain any truth that is attainable. Consequently, there is only a relative preference between different abductions; [del]and[/del] the ground of such preference must be economical. That is to say, the better abduction is the one which is likely to lead to the truth with the lesser expenditure of time, [del]and[/del] vitality, etc.
