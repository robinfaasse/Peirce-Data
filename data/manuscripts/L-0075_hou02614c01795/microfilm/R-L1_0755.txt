[/marginalia] ([u]observe that Kant spells this word, borrowed from the English of Hobbes and Locke, with a C[/u]) the question "How are synthetical judgments [u]à priori[/u] possible?" I notice that Paulsen in his book on Kant remarks that Kant never considered the question how are synthetical judgments [u]a posteriori[/u] possible, and says that if he had done so he would have been forced to say that there are not synthetical judgments [u]a posteriori[/u]. But this is not true. Kant does consider the question on page 8 of his first edition; and answers it in a totally different way from that described by Paulsen. True, he does not go into it minutely; but he does go into it far enough to show that he would have answered it in the general manner of my memoirs. That page of the [u]Critik[/u] is a pregnant one; but it is in strictest harmony with Kant's general position. Kant's precise question now comes before us for answer.
