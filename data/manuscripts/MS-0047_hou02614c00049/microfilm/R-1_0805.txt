Proof of the Fundamental Proposition of Arithmetic.
By [u]C. S. Peirce[/u]

The proposition is that the order of sequence in which the things of any collection are counted makes no difference is the result, provided there be [del]any order[/del] [ins]any order[/ins] of counting in which the count can be completed.

I wish to use this language. Suppose there is a class of ordered pairs such that PQ is one of them (QP may, or may not, belong to the class). Then, supposing $\lambda$ signifies this class of pairs, I say that P is $\lambda$ of Q and Q is $\lambda$'d by P.

Suppose a [del]certain[/del] collection of things, say the As is such that whatever class of ordered pairs $\lambda$ may signify, the following conclusion shall hold. Namely, if every A is $\lambda$ of an A, and if no A is $\lambda$'d by more than one A, then every A is $\lambda$'d by an A. If that necessarily follows, I term the collection of As [u]finite[/u]. That is the sense in which I use the word [u]finite[/u].
