We are in possession of no other way of solving problems with plural solutions than by proceeding backwards, either from an assumed solution to a datum (say, for example, to one of the coefficients of the equation) or from parameters to both solution and datum. At least, this is true of the algebraic part of the solution. Indeed, it is precisely the essence of the method called Analysis, a term not coextensive with algebra.[ins]*[/ins] There may be other modes of solving such problems, — very likely are; but none has hitherto been developed. As for the arithmetical part of the solution, barring exceptional cases, it has to proceed by successive approximations. Perhaps it is only in such problems that approximation is ever indispensible, although [ins]even when [del]not[/del] strictly necessary[/ins] it is frequently the most convenient method.
