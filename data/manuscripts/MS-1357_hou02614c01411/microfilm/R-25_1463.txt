§ 6. The distribution of weight may be conceived to be
expressed as a function either of $s$ or of $x$. I shall
give the formulae for both methods. In the one case, we
shall deal with
(27) $k = dw/ds$,
in the other with
(28) $\kappa = dw/dx$.

[u]Formulae for $k$[/u]. The radius of curvature is
(29) $\rho = ds/d\tau$
so that
(30) $k\rho = dw/d\tau$
and by differentiating (14)
(31) $k(ds/d\tau) = k\rho = Q \sec^2 \tau$
$Q = k\rho \cos^2 \tau = k_H \rho_H$
Hence, by (5)
(32) $q = k\rho \cos \tau$

[u]Formulae for $\kappa$[/u]. The radius of curvature is
(33) $\rho = \sec^3 \tau / D_x^2 y$.
By differentiating (24) we get by (28)
(34) $Q^{-1} \frac{dw}{dx} = \frac{\kappa}{Q} = \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2}$,
and by (33)
(35) $\frac{\kappa\rho}{Q} = \sec^3 \tau$
whence
(36) $Q = \kappa_H \rho_H$
and
(37) $q = \kappa\rho \cos^2 \tau$.
