<u>I. The Special Funicular Polygon.</u>

[fig]Diagram of a cord suspended between two towers with hanging weights[/fig] §1. A cord whose ends, A and [u]$\Omega$[/u], are attached to two towers, is stretched by N weights. Required its figure and tensions.

<u>Notation</u>. Number the weighted points in their order along the cord from A to $\Omega$, the numbers running from 1 to N. Number the stretches of cord between which are the weighted points in the same order beginning with zero. Thus stretch [u]n[/u] will run from point [u]n[/u] to point [u]n+1[/u], while point [u]n[/u] is between stretches [u]n-1[/u] and [u]n[/u].

Measure y vertically upwards and x horizontally so as to increase in going from A to $\Omega$. The origin [del]will[/del] [ins]will[/ins] usually be at A; but sometimes it will be taken at H, the [del]lowest[/del] point of the cord, when it hangs in a positive festoon. If a stretch is horizontal, [del]the[/del] the point H is the end of that stretch nearest A; that is, the stretch H is horizontal.

[fig]Diagram of a hanging cord with points A, H, and Omega labeled[/fig] When the origin is at H accents are attached to the variables measured from it.

Subscript letters and numbers are [ins]sometimes[/ins] affixed to the variables to indicate the number of the particular points or stretches to which they refer.
