The method of moments is somewhat analogous. For
a beam of infinitesimal flexibility resting on two sup-
ports, nothing could be happier. Even when there are
more than two supports, Clapeyron's theorem of three
moments enables us to use this method. But as the
structure becomes more complicated, and especially
when the effect of flexures upon the moments have
to be taken into account, it is far better, in my opinion,
to [del]abandon[/del] leave moments out of account until the
deformations have been ascertained. If the deformations
are insensible, each piece can be considered separately, and
the usual graphical methods are [del]for[/del] in most cases per-
fectly satisfactory.

though they do not take place so swiftly that the momentum
