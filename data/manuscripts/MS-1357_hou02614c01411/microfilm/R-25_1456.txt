I. The Special Funicular Polygon.

[fig]Diagram of a funicular polygon suspended between two towers, labeled A and Ω.[/fig] § 1. A cord whose ends, A and $\Omega$, are attached to two towers, is stretched by N weights. Required its tensions and figure.

<u>Geometrical Conditions</u>. Let us number the weighted points in their order along the cord from A to $\Omega$, the numbers running from 1 to N. Let us number the stretches of cord between successive weighted points in the same order, but beginning with 0. Thus, stretch n runs from point n to point n+1; and point n is between stretch n-1 and stretch n.

Take A for the origin of rectangular coordinates. Measure y vertically up (so that in the case of the suspension bridge, the ys will be negative), and measure x [ins]horizontally[/ins] toward the tower carrying $\Omega$.

Let $x_1, x_2, \dots x_n \dots x_N$ be the values of x for the weighted points
