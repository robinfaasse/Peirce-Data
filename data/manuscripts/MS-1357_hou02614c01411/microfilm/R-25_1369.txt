The moving load which the cables are capable of carrying is
equivalent to 11,000 pounds per lineal foot over the entire
structure, and it is assumed that this load is distributed over
the equivalent of six railroad tracks, corresponding to 1833
pounds per foot of track. In estimating the effects of an un-
equal load a weight of 12,000 pounds per lineal foot is taken in
accordance with the provisions stated at the beginning of this
paper. While it may be considered that the load per foot on
one half or one third of a span ought to be more than 10 per cent
greater than the load per foot on the whole span, it must be
remembered that the peculiar conditions of this bridge are such
that it is only under very rare conditions that any considerable
portion of the moving load must be distributed by the stiffening
truss. In fact two passenger trains could cross this bridge
side by side without disturbing the position of the cables be-
yond the limits of deflection which are permissible; it is only
