§ 5. If the weights are continuously distributed, we may
use $w$ with the signification
(14) $w = \sum_0^n w_n$.
Then the above equations become
(15) $\text{Tan } i = dy/dx$
(16) $ds = \sqrt{(dx)^2 + (dy)^2} = \sec i \cdot dx$
(17) $dw = d(q \sin i) = \frac{q dq}{\sqrt{q^2 - Q^2}} = Q \sec^2 i \cdot di$
(18) $d(q \cos i) = 0$
(19) $w - w_2 = Q \tan i$
(20) $q = (w - w_2) \csc i = Q \sec i = \sqrt{Q^2 + (w - w_2)^2}$
[ins]and writing $\xi$ for $\bar{x}$[/ins] (21) $Q = (w - w_2) \frac{x - \xi}{y - y_2}$, etc.

If we use $k$ with the signification
(22) $k = dw/ds$,
and $\rho$ for the radius of curvature; so that
(23) $\rho = ds/di$,
we have
$k \rho = dw/di$,
which, by (19) is
(24) $k(ds/di) = k \rho = Q \sec^2 i$.

§ 6. If the distribution of the load is uniform along the
cord, $k$ is constant and (24) gives
$s [del]\rho[/del] = \frac{Q}{k} \tan i + \frac{w_2}{k}$.
This is the catenary, which is discussed in all the books.
