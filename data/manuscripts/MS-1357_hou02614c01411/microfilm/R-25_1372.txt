A careful investigation of the theory of the stiffening
truss has been made on entirely independent lines by Mr. Charles
S. Peirce and is given in Appendix B. By the use of the for-
mulae which this investigation has developed, the strains in the
stiffening truss have been calculated for three different condi-
tions. In the first of these a moving load of one third the length
of the span (3100 feet) is assumed to occupy the first third of
the 3100 feet. In the second supposition it is assumed to
occupy the second and third sixths of the 3100 feet, the end of
the load being at the center. In the third supposition it is
supposed to occupy the remaining third of the 3100 feet. The mo-
ments developed by these calculations are given graphically on
Sheet 20. They are a satisfactory confirmation of the capacity
of the stiffening truss.
