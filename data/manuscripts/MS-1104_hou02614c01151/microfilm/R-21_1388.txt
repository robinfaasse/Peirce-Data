[u]On a New Class of Observations, suggested by the principles of Logic.[/u] By C. S. Peirce

It is usually admitted that there are two classes of mental representations, Immediate Representations or Sensations and Mediate Representations or Conceptions. The former are [del]abso-[/del] completely determinate or individual objects of thought; the latter are partially indeterminate or general objects. Granting that both these classes of objects exist, [del]it becomes a question of extreme difficulty[/del] the question [ins]of the prin-[/ins] [del]in what respect[/del] ciple of Individuation [del]or[/del] or of the respect in which the individual differs from the general becomes one of extreme difficulty. Duns Scotus after a masterly criticism of all the attempts at answering it, puts forward the theory that the distinction is a peculiar one [del]not referable to any general class of distinctions or[/del] without any general character, and therefore itself individual, presenting this peculiar aspect of individuality.
