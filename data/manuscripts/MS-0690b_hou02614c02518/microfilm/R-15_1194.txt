different terms by saying that it tests a hypothesis by sampling the
possible predictions that may be based upon it. Predictions are
not units; for they may be more or less detailed. One can say
roughly that one is more significant than another; but no approach
to actual weighing of their significance can, in most cases, be
made. Consequently, we cannot say that a collection of predictions
drawn from a hypothesis constitutes a strictly random sample of all
that can be drawn. Sometimes we can say that it appears to consti-
tute a very fair, or even a severe, sample of the possible predic-
tions; while in other cases, we cannot even say that, but only that
it comprises all the predictions which we can as yet draw and put to
the test. Those two classes of cases may be taken as constituting
two species under this genus. We cannot ordinarily hope that our
hypothesis will pass through the fire of induction, absolutely un-
modified. Consequently, we ought not to conclude that it is ab-
solutely correct, but only that it very much resembles the truth.
