flattery laid on with the trowel of the obituary writer, it may have
been at any time in the Corinthian War, that is, from 394, when Theaete-
tus would be old enough to serve, until 387. Or we may suppose that
the dialogue was written and published without the proem, and that
Theaetetus having been killed, perhaps in a fight near Corinth in
368 B.C., a new edition was gotten out with the proem. It is also
within the range of possibility that neither proem nor dialogue ap-
peared until long after the death of Theaetetus. In short, the
range of possibilities is so great that in my opinion we must con-
sider the dialogue as undated.

We have, then, the following highly probable dates:

[u]Protagoras[/u], 392-391 B.C.
[u]Euthydemus[/u], 390 B.C.
[u]Cratylus[/u], 386-385 B.C.
[u]Symposium[/u], 384 B.C. to later.
[u]Parmenides[/u], 367 B.C.
