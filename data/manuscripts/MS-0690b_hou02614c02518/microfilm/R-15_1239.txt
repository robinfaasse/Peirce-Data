We must next consider what calculations we can make as
to the date of Plato's birth from testimonies as to his age at
death, remembering, however, that this method is found by general
experience to give insecure results, men's age at death being a
class of numbers in which ancient testimonies are particularly apt
to be marked by carelessness. All the statements we have thus far
examined are explained by supposing that Plato was born a little
before the 2nd of July, 428 B.C., the Olympic games being held a
little early, and Epaminon holding over as archon a little beyond the
solstice, and that Plato died say in May of 347 B.C. He would
therefore lack but a month or two of being 81 years old at his death.
