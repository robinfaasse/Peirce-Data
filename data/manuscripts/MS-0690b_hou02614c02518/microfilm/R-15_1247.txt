[u]6th Objection[/u]. That there is absolutely no testimony to
Plato's removal to Megara except the two passages in Diogenes Laer-
tius. This is a sound argument, as far as it goes. But it has
little force, because we have no life of Plato written within five
centuries of his time except that of Diogenes; and the incident was
so trifling that nobody ever had occasion to mention it. For even
when Cicero says the Megarics learned much from Plato, he means the
later Megarics did from Plato's writings not that his elder Euclides
learned from a young man who had written, if anything, only the
Alcibiades or something of the same insignificance. The words are
"Post Euclides, Socratis discipulus, Megareus: a quo iidem illi
Megarici dicti: qui id bonum solum esset dicebant, quod esset unum,
et simile, et idem semper. Hi quoque multa a Platone." Here was
surely no place to expect mention of Plato's early sojourn in Megara.
