Such are the grounds upon which the testimony of Plato's
disciple and biographer is set aside. Let us now form a hypothesis
as to the date of the establishment of the Academy. When Plato was
a prisoner of war at Aegina, Aegina must have been at war with
Athens, a state of things which, according to a statement in Xeno-
phon's Hellenica only began late in the year 390 B.C. to end at the
peace of Antalcydas in 387 B.C. This would probably make the open-
ing of the school take place in 388 or 387 B.C. The only other
evidence is contained in the Seventh Epistle, which it is extremely
difficult to believe genuine, yet still more difficult to believe
spurious. At any rate, the thorough comprehension of Plato's life
which it evinces renders it high authority. This letter would have
