markable that Anytus was the manager of the prosecution of Socrates;
and several high authorities inform us that the speech of accusation
delivered at the trial was written by a rhetorician Polycrates, who
would have been paid by Anytus. It, thus, seems quite possible that
Plato put forward this dialogue to further the accusation of Anytus.
Unfortunately, we do not know when that occurred. It is true that
the speech of Polycrates against Socrates contained a reference to
an event which occurred some years later. But I wish to call atten-
tion once for all to the circumstance that nothing is easier than to
insert in a writing, in a new edition of it, a reference to an event
subsequent to the original composition; so that unless we are in
possession of the document, and can examine it, we cannot say that
it is conclusive proof against an earlier date. I do not think that
in the present case such an inference can outweigh the positive
testimony. But, at present, it must be set down as undated; for
it is almost impossible to admit that it can have been written pre-
viously to the Protagoras.
