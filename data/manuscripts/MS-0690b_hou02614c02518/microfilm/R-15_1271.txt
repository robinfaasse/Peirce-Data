Parmenides, though it is said he looked venerable, is represented to
be about 65 years old, at most, περὶ ἔτη μάλιστα πέντε καὶ ἑξήκοντα;
while Zeno was nearly forty ἐγγὺς ἐτῶν τετταράκοντα. Now Plato,
according to our hypothesis would have been 65 in 363 B.C. As to the
age of Spensippus, we only know that he was the son of Plato's prob-
ably younger sister, -- for Plato was the eldest son of three or
more, and therefore, more probably than not, the eldest child; while
on the other hand Spensippus is called γηραιός, or over sixty, when
he died, according to Eusebius in 339 B.C. It follows, if these
data are correct, that when Plato was 65, Spensippus was somewhere
between 36 and 44 years old. There is one other ground for con-
jecture; for considering the eminent powers of Xenocrates, had he
not been considerably younger than Spensippus, he would hardly have
remained eight years in the Academy after Plato's death as under
teacher. But he was thirty-three when Plato was sixty-five so that,
according to this argument, Spensippus must have been near forty.
Thus, this theory of the Parmenides explains every fact, and, as far
as I know, it is the only one which so much as pretends to do so.
