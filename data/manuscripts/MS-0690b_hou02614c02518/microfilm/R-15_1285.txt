and therefore he could not have carried Pythagoras to Babylon.
Where would he be carried? As a distinguished and learned prisoner,
he would probably accompany Cyrus, who betook himself to Ecbatana.
Arrived there, he would have been enslaved without the smallest
doubt, and therefore would undoubtedly have endeavored to escape;
and being a highly ingenious and enterprising man, he would have
proceeded intelligently. Now what was the intelligent way of try-
ing to escape from Ecbatana? To the nearest frontier of Cyrus's
kingdom. But that was to the east. Then, Pythagoras would cer-
tainly go east, by the most feasible route. [ins]What route would that
be? Probably the one subsequently followed by Alexander)[/ins] [ins][marginalia](for such invasions always follow lines of commerce)[/marginalia][/ins] Then we
must suppose that that road was taken by Pythagoras. Where would

of Cyrus, and within the range of Indian influences. Beyond that
point his course must be doubtful. He may have gone on to India,
or he may have turned to the south and sought the nearest coast.
