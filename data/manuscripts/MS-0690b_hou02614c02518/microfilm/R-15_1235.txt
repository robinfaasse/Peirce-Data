The next problem which I shall consider by way of illus-
trating the method I advocate is to determine the chronology of the
life of Plato.

Diogenes Laertius quotes Hermippus, a high authority of
the IIIrd century before Christ, and in another place quotes Apollo-
dorus, a celebrated chronologer, to the effect that Plato died in
the first year of the 108th Olympiad, which began in midsummer
348 B.C. In a third place Diogenes quotes Pha[del]v[/del]orinus as saying
that Plato died in the 13th year of Philip of Macedon. To make the
two statements agree, we must suppose that he died in the early part
of 347 B.C., which is supported by the statement of Seneca that he
died on his birthday. Other good authorities confirm this; there
is no direct testimony to the contrary, and no modern writer doubts
it. We may therefore admit [del]the[/del] [ins]as a[/ins] hypothesis [del],[/del] that it is true.
