Thus, twenty skillful hypotheses will ascertain what two hundred
thousand stupid ones might fail to do. The secret of the business
lies in the caution which breaks a hypothesis up into its smallest
logical components, and only risks one of them at a time. What a
world of futile controversy and of confused experimentation might
have been saved if this principle had guided investigations into
the theory of light! The ancient and medieval notion was that
sight starts from the eye, is shot to the object from which it is
reflected, and returned to the eye. This idea had, no doubt, been
entirely given up before Römer showed that it took light a quarter

have refuted it by the experiment of opening the closed eyes and
