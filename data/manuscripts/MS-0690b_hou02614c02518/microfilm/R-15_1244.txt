<u>2nd Objection.</u> That Hermodorus is discredited by his
ignorance in alluding to those from whom the disciples of Socrates
apprehended trouble as τῶν τυράννων. There can be no doubt that
this is the objection which has weighed most of all with the critics.
But I reply, first, that since Diogenes does not say that Hermodorus
used this expression, the objection has little force. But, secondly
although it would be presumably a blunder of Diogenes, if it were a
blunder, yet since it would be quite like Plato himself to apply
that epithet to the prosecutors of Socrates, it is no blunder, but
on the contrary, tends to show, that Plato's own words have been
nearly adhered to. Τυράννος is a favorite expression of Plato's,
not merely for everybody whom we call tyrannical, but even more
widely for people who bring trouble and death unjustly. It is very
common in this sense in the dialogues, and no reader of Plato can
doubt that he would have given and did give, in conversation, to his
master's persecutors just that ap[del]e[/del][ins]p[/ins]ellation.
