reached Chorasmia from further east; that is, from this very region.
We should, therefore, not be surprised if Pythagoras were in posses-
sion of our figures, of course without the zero, which is not indis-
pensable, if the numbers are written in ruled columns. We shall see
later some ground for suspecting that this prediction, too, though
it is not a positive one, may be correct. Let us now take two
other testimonies. Porphyry, as well as other authorities, say that
Pythagoras was thirty-nine years old when he went to Italy. Let us
suppose this true. Then he would be born 571 B.C. Again Iamblichus
says he was eighteen years old when he first went to Egypt; and
this is [u]a priori[/u] probable; for Ionian youths appear often to have
made the voyage to Egypt in order to satisfy the curiosity belonging
to their time of life. We may readily believe that, then. He
would, then, have gone to Egypt in 553 B.C., and perhaps remained
there just long enough to have been taken prisoner soon after his
return in 546 B.C. Iamblichus says he remained in Egypt 22 years.
