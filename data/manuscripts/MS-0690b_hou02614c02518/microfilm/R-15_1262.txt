For we should expect that, in early life, while Plato's style was
forming, the grade would change rapidly, that it would change more
and more slowly, until, feeling that he had much that he wished to
get said before he died, he would feel that there was no time for
polishing, and the grade would suddenly change and would increase
rapidly as he was approaching fourscore. Still, unlikely as the
hypothesis is, it is the proper one to begin with, because the errors
of its predictions will aid us in forming a new hypothesis, in case
a new one be necessary. For that purpose, we must fix two dates.

There are a few dialogues whose dates may be approximately
fixed with considerable probability. These are the Apology and
Politicus on external grounds; the Protagoras, Cratylus, Symposium,
Phaedrus, Theaetetus, and Parmenides, on internal grounds.

As to the Apology, it seems to me probable that Plato
talked it over with Socrates during that month of waiting for the
trireme and therefore that it would be written in the same summer of
