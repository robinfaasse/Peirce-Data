I shall assume that Lutostawski's treatment of the facts
has been judicious up to the point when the matter becomes a ques-
tion for the mathematician. The first question I have asked my-
self is whether there are any circumstances in the manner in which
the observations have been made which render them of unequal value.
Now, all the observers have taken the long dialogue of the Laws,
which Plato left unfinished at his death, as the standard by which
the lateness of other Dialogues were to be judged. That circum-
stance must create a suspicion that, while their observations of
other dialogues are comparable with one another, the relation of the
style of the other dialogues to the Laws will be measured different-
ly by their results, since they have treated the Laws in an altogeth-
er exceptional manner. Moreover, the majority of the observations
appear to have been made with a view to ascertaining the relation of
the Sophist and Politicus especially, and more generally, the rela-
tion of the Philebus, Sophist, Politicus, Timaeus, and Critias, --
the last of which is also unfinished, -- to the other dialogues.
One must, therefore, suspect that the observations might somewhat
exaggerate the differences of those five dialogues, both from the
other dialogues and from one another. Still, these are only strong
suspicions, not certainties.
