Let us begin with the least uncertain date in the life of
Pythagoras. This is that he went to Italy 532 B.C. So says Cicero;
less precisely so say Aulus Gellius and Iamblichus; and sundry
other authorities support the date by various assertions. We will
begin the building of our hypothesis, then, by supposing that to
be true. The most serious objection to it is that Livy seems to
say that Pythagoras immigrated during the reign of Servius Tullius,
who was assassinated 534 B.C., two years too early. But if we ex-
amine Livy's purpose, we observe that his only interest in the fact
was as an evidence that Pythagoras cannot have aided Numa, the second
King, in his religious reforms. For that purpose, he allows those
who maintain he did aid Numa the earliest possible date for the com-
ing of the philosopher. So it is really not the historian Livy who
lends his authority to so early a date. Perhaps nobody ever said
so. It may easily be that some author said Pythagoras came early,
or perhaps in the first quarter of the third century of the city.
By our hypothesis he would have come in the 22nd year of that cent-
ury. But Servius Tullius was king for the first twenty years of
the century; and consequently Livy does not [u]assert[/u], but [u]admits[/u],
