Yet, somehow, the numbers seemed too equal to be chance results. There was a smell of the kitchen about them. Lutoslawski gives the numbers of different peculiarities of late style for each dialogue. Here we are confronted with a problem in probabilities something like this. An urn contains a given number of black balls and a given number of white balls; and all the white balls are numbered. Now in a given number of drawings, each ball drawn being replaced before the next drawing, what will be the probability of drawing a given number of white balls [u]once[/u] each, and then what will be the probability of drawing a given number of white balls just [u]twice[/u] each, and so on?
