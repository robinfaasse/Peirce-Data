How, then, shall we explain the statement? Simply by supposing
that Iamblichus, who muddles everything, had been informed that his
travels lasted 22 years. And how shall we explain his having been
so informed? By its being the fact. Very well, this then is a
prediction; and a correct one, since from 553 B.C., when he first
left Samos to 531 B.C., when he landed in Italy, is just 22 years.
Thus, everything fits together like a dissected map. It is diffi-
cult to believe that these facts would so fit together if the hypoth-
esis were not true. It almost amounts to an inductive proof.

Let us now enlarge our hypothesis about Pythagoras so as
to explain other facts. It is a fact that Pythagoras has been and
still is regarded as a mystical person. How shall we explain this
belief? The proper hypothesis to try first is that the observed fact
is due to the circumstance that he really was a mystical person.
