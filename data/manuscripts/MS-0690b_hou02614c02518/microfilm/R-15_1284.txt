by the Persians, when could this have occurred? There is, I be-
lieve, only one admissible way in which it could have happened.
Pythagoras was a Samian. We have only to suppose that he was upon
the mainland opposite Samos in 546 B.C. when Cyrus, the master of
Cambyses, invaded and conquered Lydia, and it would very naturally
have happened that he would be taken prisoner there. Let us then,
adopt this hypothesis, and see if we cannot deduce some probable con-
sequences of it capable of being compared with facts, or with already
admitted hypotheses. If he was taken prisoner and remained in the
power of the Persians, what would he do when he escaped? He would
probably go back to Samos, where his property and family were. Is
there any statement to the effect that he did so? Yes, it is said
that he came back, but did not relish the tyranny of Polycrates, who
had seized the power and that that was the reason he went to Italy.
Supposing that to be true, how long would he have been away? An-
swer: from 546 the date of his capture to 532, the date of his ar-
rival in Italy, was 14 years. But, then, we must allow him time to
become discontented at home, and to make up his mind to emigrate, and
to decide where to go, and to sell his property, and [ins]to[/ins] make the voyage.
Two years seems a middling estimate for all this business. He
