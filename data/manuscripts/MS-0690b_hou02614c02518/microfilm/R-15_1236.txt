adopted ought to explain [u]all[/u] the testimony, that which it supposes
false as well as that which it supposes true. Apollodorus is quoted
by Diogenes as saying that Plato was born in the 88th Olympiad and on
the 7th day of the month Thargelion. This seems to be made up of
two independent pieces of information, probably derived from differ-
ent sources, the one as to the Olympiad, the other as to the birth-
day. Now Plato was, by his followers, regarded as a sort of incar-
nation of Apollo, whose birthday was said in Delos to be the 7th
Thargelion; and both Plutarch and Apul[ins]l[/ins]eius tell us that Plato's
birth was celebrated on that day. It was, therefore, his [u]jour de[/u]
[u]fête[/u] simply, and the odds are 364 to 1 that his actual birth fell on
some other day. According to Ideler's tables, the 7th Thargelion
ended at the evening of May 22 in 429 B.C., of May 9 in 428 B.C., and
of May 27 in 427 B.C. The 88th Olympiad would regularly begin July
2nd, 428 B.C.; but Ideler admits that it may sometimes have come a
month earlier. Still, only the year 427 B.C. will answer if Plato
was really born on the 7th Thargelion in the 88th Olympiad. If,
however, as might perfectly well be, he were really born later in
the same season, especially if as late as July, then 428 B.C. would
explain the testimony of Apollodorus rather better than 427 B.C.
