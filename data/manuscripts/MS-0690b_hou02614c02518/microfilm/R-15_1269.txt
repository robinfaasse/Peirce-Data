The Parmenides appears to me to be so obviously the reply
of Plato to the criticisms of his new pupil Aristotle upon the doc-
trine of ideas that I can only marvel that there are critics who do
not see that it is so. We find here the very same objections that
are urged against the same doctrine in Aristotle's Metaphysics; and
it elicits them from the mouth of whom? Of Aristotle! Only, since
Socrates has necessarily to be introduced as the defender of the doc-
trine that had been originally put forth in his name, -- and in put-
ting it forward Plato, I doubt not, was confident that it was the
logical outcome of the opinions of Socrates, and [ins]that it[/ins] [del]most[/del] likely had been
his master's actual opinion, -- since, therefore, Socrates had to be
introduced, it was requisite to pretend that the Aristotle was an-
other obscure individual of that name among the contemporaries of
Socrates.
