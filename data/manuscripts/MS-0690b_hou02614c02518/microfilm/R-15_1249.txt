According to that same 7th Epistle, the third voyage to
Syracuse would have taken place 361 B.C. We must adopt that hypoth-
esis.

The only predictions which I see how to draw from these
hypotheses,—and they are far from being necessary, though some pro-
portion of them may be expected to be verified,—are that some allu-
sions to his different experiences might probably be found in Plato's
subsequently written dialogues. Such as these predictions are, I
will number them. First, we might expect allusions to the Heraclitan
philosophy of Plato's youth, as soon as the intensity of the impres-
sion he received from Socrates was abated. Second, after his so-
journ in Megara, we might expect allusions to the peculiarly trivial
disputations for which the Megarics are still celebrated. Most of
those critics, by the way, who accept the removal to Megara, includ-
ing Grote, think that his stay there would be less than a year.
