Lutosławski's second category seems to be composed entirely of
peculiarities occurring more than once in the dialogue in question,
but not often enough to be deemed important. It must occur oftener
in a long dialogue to be deemed important than in a short one. The
consequence will be that if a dialogue is increased in length, it
will have category II increased at the expense both of category I
and of category III. In the limited time at my disposal I have not
solved the problem of deducing from Lutosławski's numbers three in-
dependent methods of determining the degrees of stylistic development
of the different dialogues, so as to ascertain the probable errors
of the final results. But I have proved that, in case the numbers
of peculiarities of a dialogue is large, the total number of differ-
ent peculiarities will be proportional to the square root of the
length of the dialogue. Now fortunately, in several cases Lutos-
ławski has given the numbers for groups of dialogues as well as of
single ones. He divides the Republic into five parts, and subdi-
vides two of those five parts; and he gives the numbers for the
whole, for the five parts, and for each subdivision. The following
are the sums of the numbers in his five categories in those cases:
