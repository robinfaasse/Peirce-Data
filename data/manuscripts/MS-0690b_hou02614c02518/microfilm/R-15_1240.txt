Most [del]of[/del] [ins]our[/ins] modern critics, as Grote, Ueberweg, Zeller, Steinhart, and
Windelband prefer 427 B.C. The reason is that they hold that by
simply assuming a testimony to be false they are absolved from giv-
ing any explanation of it, while I require a hypothesis to explain
all the testimonies. At the same time, it would not be surprising
if my hypothesis were wrong by a year, or even two. It merely
stands as a source of experiential predictions to be tested. But
at present I see no way of deducing any experiential predictions
exact enough to test it. It is true that in the dialogue entitled
the Parmenides, Plato intends in a veiled and delicate manner that
Parmenides shall be understood to represent him, Plato; and for
that purpose alters the age of Parmenides, and makes him 65 years
old. Now, I believe I can fix the date at which that dialogue was
written within a year. But not to speak of several uncertainties in
both items, that his Parmenides being meant for Plato, and the year
in which it was written, I do not think it would have suited Plato's
