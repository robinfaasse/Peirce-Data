This table gives satisfactory dates for all the dialogues
which we were able to date, excepting that it probably puts the
Apology too late and the Politicus also too late. There would be
no obvious mathematical curve which would satisfy these better.
But we may suppose that to make a somewhat regular curve, the dates
for the body of the Republic are a year or more too early. I have
corrected the early dates in the first column. This would make the
Cratylus a year later than the date assigned. We must suppose that
the stylistic grade is so uncertain at present that the probable
error of the date of a dialogue, even after the connection of the
stylistic grade with the time is absolutely fixed, is nearly two
years. We may suppose, for example, that the whole Republic was
composed in its present order, by dating Book I in 389 B.C., and
the fragments $b_{1}$ later in the same year.
