But if he did, all traces of it had disappeared when one Thrasyllus,
or Thrasybulus, made the statement that Plato published his dialogues
in senseless tetralogies. Upon one point nearly all critics are
now agreed, namely, that the Laws was his last work. Diogenes says
that Plato left it unfinished at his death; and it is unfinished.
The year after Plato's death, Isocrates, in his 13th Philippic,
speaks of "the works on laws and politics that have been written by
the sophists;" but this need not mean Plato's book.
