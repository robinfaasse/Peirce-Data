            Let us now turn back to the predictions which we based
upon our general hypothesis concerning Plato's life and see how far
they are verified. [ins](I wish to say that these were genuine predictions written out verbatim as here given long before I had any idea how the calculations were to be made or how they would turn out)[/ins] Our first prediction was that we should find
that there was first an intense impression made by Socrates, after
which traces of Heroclitan influence would show themselves.
            We find, if we are to trust our chronological table at
all, that for ten years after the death of Socrates, Plato devoted
himself to picturing his character and mind.  For we may hold that
the first seven dialogues are entirely of that nature, -- that is to
say the [u]Apology[/u], [u]Euthyphro[/u], [u]Crito[/u], [u]Protagoras[/u], [u]Charmides[/u], [u]Euthydemus[/u],
and the first part of the [u]Republic[/u].*  Perhaps we may add the [u]Meno[/u].
I suppose the [u]Gorgias[/u] belongs late in 387 or in 386 B.C.  The [u]Laches[/u]
is the sole military dialogue.  It falls into the last part of the
Corinthian War.  And one of our predictions, -- not, of course, a
positive one by any means, -- was that we might find that Plato re-
turned to Athens and served at the beginning of that war.  If he
did so, it must have been after writing the [u]Euthyphro[/u] and before
the [u]Protagoras[/u] and [u]Charmides[/u];  and the [u]Laches[/u] would perhaps have
been written while Plato was a prisoner of war in Aegina.  But
after those dialogues, the very first which he wrote was the Herac-
litan dialogue the [u]Cratylus[/u].

*[ins]Perhaps this is too positive. There is absolutely no proof that the Charmides, for example, is historical; yet, I know not why, that impression was so strong upon me that it led me to ask whether all the early dialogues were not so. It is my belief they were: I cannot resist it. There is nothing to prevent our supposing Socrates met Protagoras. But it must be remembered that Plato was highly imaginative & drew little line of demarcation between fancy and fact.[/ins]
