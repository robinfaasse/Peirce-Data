totle's writings;  but that, from the nature of things, could only
be Strabo's surmise.  We are at liberty, on our side, if we think it
best to do so, to suppose that it was because Aristotelianism was a
little outside the general current of Greek thought.  Nevertheless,
if in place of imperfect notes by students, the school had had the
works of Aristotle which we have, we too may surmise, if we find
reason for it, that the school would have been somewhat better able
to stem the current of thought, and not have been so swept into it
as it seems to have been.
          We are also asked how, upon this hypothesis can the cross-
references have been inserted?  But to make a difficulty of this
does seem a little childish.  The manuscripts had been upon the
average six years in the school at Aristotle's death, and remained
there for 35 years longer.  Was not this time enough to insert any
cross-references which had not been inserted in the first writing?
