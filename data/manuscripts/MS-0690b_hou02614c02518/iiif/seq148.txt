The [u]Cratylus[/u] contains a reference to the closing of the
gates of Aegina before nightfall, a minute fact which would hardly
have come to Plato's mind very long after his enforced stay there
in 387 B.C.  We may infer that it was probably written in 386 or
385 B.C.

In the [u]Symposium[/u], Aristophanes says, "Formerly, as I main-
tain, we were one; but now, because of unrighteousness, we have been
dispersed by God, like the Arcadians by the Lacedemonians."  That
sounds as if the dispersion of Mantinea were a rather recent event.
It took place in the autumn of 385 B.C.; so that any date from 384
onward would be likely for the [u]Symposium[/u].  Some critics suppose a
different event was alluded to; but the most famous is the most
probable.

The [u]Phaedrus[/u] is now generally regarded as the most exactly
dated of all the dialogues.  Cicero in his Orator seems to recog-
nize, though this is not so clear as it might be, that [ins]at[/ins] the close of
this dialogue Plato refers to [ins]the[/ins] Panegyric of Isocrates.  But it is in
another passage that Thomson has discovered a pretty clear reference
to a passage of that oration, which was published in 380 B.C.  Namely,
Isocrates mentions an artifice of rhetoric of relying upon probabil-
ities to make small things appear great and great things small;  and
says it is something new, that former rhetoricians had not mentioned.
