due to the summation of many small effects. But now on the other
hand, take a question of history. We do not care to know how many
times a witness would report a given fact correctly, because he re-
ports that fact but once. If he misstates the matter, there is no
cooperation of myriad causes. It is on the contrary, due to some
one cause which, if it cannot often be ascertained with certainty,
can at any rate be very plausibly guessed in most cases, if the cir-
cumstances are closely inquired into; and it is most pertinent to
the business of the historical critic to consider how a mistake, if
mistake there be, might credibly have arisen. A mere general ratio
of true statements to false, would be utterly insufficient for his
purpose, even if it really existed. But it does not exist. In
