that Pythagoras was a mystic?   I suppose it is due to three facts;
first, that his theory of numbers seems to be excessively mystical;
[del]and[/del] secondly, that his doctrine was mostly secret;   and thirdly,
that he seems to have sought to cover himself with a veil of mystery.
But we have seen that the theory that he was a mystical person cannot
be entertained for an instant.   How then shall we explain these
three [ins]supposed[/ins] facts?   Let us begin with the last.   It is said, -- and it
makes little difference whether it was true or not, so long as there
was so much approach to truth in it to make it universally believed,--
that he lectured behind a curtain, because only his most intimate
disciples must see his face;   also, that his disciples were not al-
lowed to mention his sacred name, nor to question his dicta.   The
αὐτὸς ἔφη must end dispute.   Also that he exhibited, or, at any
rate had, a golden thigh.   How shall we explain these facts?   If
it were a modern man, the matter would be simple.   He would be a
charlatan.   But charlatan [ins]is?[/ins] was a very harsh word.   Edward Palmer,
the Persian and Arabic scholar, was a man of almost miraculous at-
tainments;   and there was a personal charm about him that far ex-
ceeded that of any man I have ever met.   It was due partly to the
exquisite sweetness and purity of the man, partly to his [u]esprit[/u] and
peculiar intellect, but largely to the childlike modesty and sim-
plicity of his nature and complete absence of anything like egotism.
