No polishing, revision, or even considerable alterations would affect
the stylistic grade sensibly, because [ins]This[/ins] [del]it[/del] depends mainly upon minute
peculiarities of expression of which Plato would hardly be conscious,
and to which his attention would never be directed. Now the Laws
must have been in hand for many years, beginning only a year or two
after the Republic was completed. The Politicus, on the other hand,
would have been written in a few months; for the style is quite
unlabored. It is therefore, not extravagant to suppose the Politicus,
or whatever dialogue was last written, was composed within a year of
Plato's death, since we have various testimonies that Plato worked
to the very end of his life. As for the Critias, which is unfinish-
ed, perhaps it remains so because Plato felt that it was not very
important either from a philosophical or from a literary point of
view. He cannot possibly have foreseen how famous it would become.
The [u]Protagoras[/u] appears to contain allusions to Xenophon's
[u]Memorabilia[/u]; but the date of that work is not known, further than
that it was some years after the death of Socrates, perhaps from
395 to 392 B.C. It was while it was fresh that it would be likeli-
est to be noticed by Plato. Moreover the Protagoras introduces
Alcibiades as a disciple of Socrates; and in my opinion the Pro-
