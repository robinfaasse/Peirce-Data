The [u]Prolegomena[/u] of Olympodorus, a celebrated Neo-Platonist of the
VIth century, says that Plato was born while Pericles was living;
but this statement might easily have arisen out of an exaggeration.
Diogenes reports that Hermodorus, -- meaning, without
doubt, an immediate disciple of Plato, -- says that Plato was 28
years old when he removed to Megara after the death of Socrates,
which death took place not far from June 1st, 399 B.C.  This would
require Plato to be born after June 1st, 428 B.C.
Diogenes further says that Plato was six years younger
that Isocrates; while the life of Isocrates in Plutarch's works
says the difference was seven years.  Now Isocrates was born in the
first year of the 86th Olympiad.  The only two ways of reconciling
these two statements are, first, to suppose that Isocrates was born
in the early part of the Olympiad year and Plato in the latter part
of the third year of the 87th Olympiad, that is, in May or June, of
429 B.C., and, secondly, to suppose Isocrates born in the latter
part of the year and Plato in the early part of the fourth year of
the 87th Olympiad.  But since neither of these hypotheses agree with
other testimonies, it will be better to suppose that one or both of
the statements about the difference of age are erroneous computa-
tions, such as are frequent in ancient authors.  If Plato were born,
