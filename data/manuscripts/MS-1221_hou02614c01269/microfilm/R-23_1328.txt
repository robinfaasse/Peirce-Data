of motive. For instance, one of the principal pauses
of speech is between the subject and predicate of an
assertion; as,
"Rome | shall perish."

We do not ordinarily put any point in that place, in writing
but when certain causes, which will presently be noticed
cause punctuation to be fuller than usual, one of
the first additional commas to be inserted is between
the subject and the predicate; as in the following
examples:

Integrity of understanding, and nicety of discernment,
were allotted to Dryden.

The world that is outward, material, is but a shadow.

Exalted, tender, beneficent, is the love that woman inspires.

Riches, health, pleasure, are curses to the foolish.
