[u]It must not be supposed that material things are the only[/u]
[u]sort of subjects of propositions.[/u] When Abraham Lincoln
said, "it is hard to deceive all the people all the time," his
subjects were persons and times. He might have said,
"Every man is deceived at one time or another, and
sometimes all men are deceived at once, but at
every time there is some man who is not deceived."
That is [del]precisely[/del] expressed [ins]as follows:[/ins] Take any object, A, whatever;
then, an object B can be found such that either A is
not a man or B is a time at which A is deceived;
and an object, C, can be found such that, no matter
what object D may be, [del]C is a time[/del] either D is not
a man or C is a time at which D is deceived; and
no matter what object E may be, an object, F, may be
found such that either E is not a time, or it is a
time at which F is [del]not[/del] man and is not deceived.
