A plane is a non-singular surface having the
following characters

1[sup]st[/sup] every [del]two[/del] pair of points [del]with[/del] upon it is topically
like every other pair of [del]p[/del] different points upon it.

2[sup]nd[/sup], it is such that a non-singular line [ins]indefinitely extended[/ins] can be
drawn upon it which cannot shrink [del]to[/del] [ins]toward[/ins] nothing in
it, but one such line having been drawn, no second
such line not cutting the first can be drawn.

Prop. 1. A [del]point[/del] [ins]particle[/ins] can move in a plane from any
point of it to any point of it.

For a [del]point[/del] particle can move in a plane [ins]from one point to another[/ins], since [del]the plane[/del] [ins]it[/ins] is
a surface. Hence, since all pairs of points are alike, [del]the[/del]
particle can so move from any point to any point [ins]on[/ins] [del]of[/del] the plane.

Prop. 2. [del]A[/del] [ins]An unshrinking[/ins] line can be drawn through
any pair of points of the plane.

For such a line can be drawn, and it passes through
a pair of points. Hence all pairs of points being alike,
such a line can be drawn through any pair of points.
