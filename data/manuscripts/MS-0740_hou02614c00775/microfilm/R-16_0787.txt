Suppose then that we take any objects $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$

$A - B = (A - C) + (C - B) = [(A - D) - (C - D)] + [(C - E) - (B - E)]$

which expression is therefore entirely independant of $C$, $D$, & $E$.

Now suppose we have $a = A' - B'$ and $c = E' - B'$. As $A$ $B$ $D$ & $E$ are

entirely indeterminate, we may take $A - D = A' - D'$ and $E' - B' = E - B$

The above expression remains independant of $C$. Let $C$ be

so taken that $C - D = E - C$; which is always possible on account

[marginalia]Co-identity[/marginalia] of the continuousness of the relations. Still, the expression

remains independant of the value of $C - D$; for let another value

$D''$ be substituted for $D$, then if [del]it is[/del] [ins]we[/ins] only [ins]such[/ins] that a new value

may be taken for $A''$, so that $A'' - D'' = A - D$, [del]the same[/del] [ins]and if[/ins] a new value

$B''$ may be taken for $B$, such that $B - D'' = B - B''$ and for $E$ such

that $E - E'' = D - D''$, the conditions are all fulfilled. If then we

let $C - D = t$ we have

$(a - t) + (t + c)$

independant of the value of $t$ so long as one object can be

correlate on the grounds $a$ and $t$ and one on the grounds $b$ and $t$.

But if this cannot happen the expression cannot be interpreted

at all. Now suppose $t = a$ then $(a - t) + (t + c) = a + c$.
