The only premiss which is held previously to the conclusion
is that "this thing is thus". We may however assume that "what-
ever has this name is thus", and at the same moment in-
fer by hypothesis that, "this thing has this name." Such a
hypothetic process is at once the only one which can yield
that conclusion. The major proposition "whatever should have
this name would be thus," is inferred at the same moment
by induction from the same fact together with the hypo-
thetic inference.

But such a process, it will be said, is a [u]petitio principii[/u].

the premisses, if one of the premisses can only be inferred
from its following from the conclusion, the argument falls
to the ground; because proposition cannot reciprocally
follow from each other. But induction and hypothesis
do not require the conclusion to follow from the premisses
and therefore a [u]petitio principii[/u] is not fatal to them. Each
