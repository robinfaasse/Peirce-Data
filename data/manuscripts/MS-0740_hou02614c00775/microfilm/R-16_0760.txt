By a simple conception, I mean any mental representation whose content is not merely unanalyzed in this or that state of confused consciousness but is absolutely unanalyzable. We will not now inquire whether there are any such ultimate parts of the content of a representation; but it is necessary to assume them provisionally, in order to consider the mere matter of that which is connoted independently of the comprehension or amount of aggregation of that matter to form different compound representations.

A simple conception would be an intelligible predicate. It would not therefore be an intuition or mere impression, for impressions are in themselves incomprehensible. Yet it would be applied to its subject on account of some character recognized therein, for otherwise its application would not make a cognition.
