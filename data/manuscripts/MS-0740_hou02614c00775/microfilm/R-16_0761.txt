The [del]obvious[/del] objection to this doctrine is that hypothesis re-
quires that the predicate of the conclusion shall be [ins]the[/ins] subject
of one of the premisses; namely, of that general principle
upon which the hypothesis proceeds in explaining the fact.
But the conception of this principle and the hypothetic sub-
suming under it may be simultaneous; and, then, as the
hypothesis is the motive for adopting the principle, the con-
ception may properly be said to be derived from the hypo-
thetic inference. It will be shown in the next section that
the major proposition may be taken without any foundation
in fact, except that it gives support to the hypothesis.
