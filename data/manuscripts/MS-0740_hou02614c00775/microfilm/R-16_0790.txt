Thus far we have considered quantitative continuous grounds of relationships, generally. Let us now take up, first commutative, and second, noncommutative grounds.

Take $A-B=a$ and $B-C=b$, then if the relations are [del]continuous[/del] [ins]commutative[/ins] $A$ must be related upon the ground $b$ to a possible correlate, which may be denoted by $B'$. $A$ is related to $B$ and $B'$ to $C$. $A$ and $B$ are relates to $B'$ and $C$ respectively as correlates. But $A-B'=B-C$. Hence, by the law of proportion $B'-C=A-B=a$. Hence $(A-B')+(B'-C)=b+a$. But by the law of addition $(A-B')+(B'-C)=A-C$. Hence $b+a=A-C$. But $a+b=(A-B)+(B-C)=A-C$. So that $b+a=a+b$.

It could be shown by similar reasoning that $\alpha\beta=\beta\alpha$ and that $\alpha+\beta=\beta+\alpha$.
