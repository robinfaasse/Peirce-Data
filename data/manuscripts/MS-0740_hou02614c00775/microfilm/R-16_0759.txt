[u]§ 1. Hypothetic Character of Simple Conceptions.[/u]

Any one who should adopt the hypothesis that light
is ether-waves, in order to explain the phenomena of po-
larization, would be inferring the minor proposition [ins]of[/ins]
the following syllogism:—

Ether-waves would be polarizable,
[u]Light is ether-waves;[/u]
Light is polarizable.

The observed fact follows from the assumption and
is therefore explained by the latter. The hypothetic inference
consists [del]of[/del] [ins]in[/ins] the substitution, in place of the incomprehen-
sed predicate of polarization, of an intelligible predicate
of which the capacity for polarization is necessarily predi-
cable. Thus, the essence of hypothesis is the assignment
