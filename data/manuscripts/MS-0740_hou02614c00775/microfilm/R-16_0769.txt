The second proposition of Kantian aesthetic is that [del]the[/del]
conceptions of space and time belong to sensibility, not to
understanding. Sensibility is "the power by which objects
are given to us;" "the capacity of attaining representations
through the mode in which objects affect us."

The third proposition is that space and time are
intuitive. According to his custom, Kant employs several
definitions of intuition. The following quotations present
them all. "All representations have, as representations, their
objects, and may, themselves be the objects of other representa-
tions. Appearances (Erscheinungen) are the only objects which
can be immediately given to us, and that [ins]in them[/ins] which [del]then[/del] refers
immediately to the object, is called intuition." "In whatever
mode and by whatever means a cognition refers to objects,
that whereby it immediately refers to them and at which
all thought aims as means, is intuition." "Intuition is a
singular representation."
