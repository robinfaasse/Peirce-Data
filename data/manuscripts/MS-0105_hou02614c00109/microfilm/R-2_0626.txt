[u]On Real Curves. First Paper.[/u]
By C. S. Peirce

The object of this paper is to commence the [ins]^[/ins] [ins]y[/ins] investigation of the real parts of [del]the[/del] plane curves, without reference to their imaginary parts. For [ins]nearly[/ins] everything commonly stated, about the singularities of curves is, for every such purpose as that of hydrodynamics, not only inapplicable, but false.

I shall, [del]at the outset[/del] in this paper, limit myself to [ins]really[/ins] unicursal curves, as containing all the general shapes.

Let a curve be considered as generated by the motion of a point along a line in one direction, the line turning round the point in one direction. At a cusp, the point remains stationary while the line makes a semirevolution, so [del]that[/del] that all lines through a cusp are tangents, and the cusp differs infinitely little from a minute loop with a node.
