I believe it is generally held by physiologists
that it is the nerve terminals alone which determine
whether a sensation shall be chromatic or acoustic
or other. But the phenomenon of recollection
seems to show that the brain may excite or
alter a sensation. At any rate, it must be the
grey matter which feels; and it seems to me [ins]as[/ins]
plausible that the general activity of the brain
while we are awake which causes the feeling
we call "immediate consciousness." I find it
difficult to accept James's theory that it is
the sensation of breathing, since I find both
consciousness and self-consciousness to be
facilitated by holding the breath. I have to an
unusual degree a way of holding my breath in
thinking especially in making logical analyses.
I am little aware myself of doing so, but [del]years[/del] [ins]many[/ins]
ago an ingenious secretary of mind remarked
the phenomenon and made some observations
of it. [del]I think there is some connection between this
habit and my power of logical analysis.[/del] It seems
from the observations of that secretary that when
I was in [del]the height of my powers[/del], I often held my
breath for as long as two minutes without being
aware of it. As well as I can ascertain I do not,
now that I am growing old, do so for more than
about a quarter of a minute. Ideas do not now
rush upon me in such mobs; but they no longer
stumble over one another as they used to do, but
are much more orderly; and I think a good deal
more effectively than I did when in the fullness of
life.
