§4. In order to complete the fulfilment of the purpose of this section, it will be necessary for me to define, or at least to describe, the system of non-negative [del]real[/del] [ins]real[/ins] rational values. For that purpose I must first define a system of pure quantitative values; and that definition must be prefaced by a definition of a system of values in general; and that in turn by definitions of a real [u]ens rationis[/u], and of an [u]ens rationis[/u] in general. While I am about it, I had better explain a few terms of logic.

The first word I will explain is [u]Feeling[/u], as I use it, and nearly as many psychologists use it. Look, please kindly oblige me by actually exerting yourself to do the various things I may ask you to do, since if you do not, but content yourself with imagining you do it, the truth will be as completely shut off from you as it was from the medieval alchemists, who never described a dozen experiments in sequence that one at least was not a mere fancy; yet many and many readers will, I am quite sure, be so ignorant of the difference between words and things, between perceiving and fancying, that they will not only substitute the latter for the former in one instance out of a dozen, but rather in a dozen instances out of every baker's dozen. — Look, then, I pray you at a brilliant scarlet or vermillion object, and try to make out what that redness, that scarletness or vermillionness consists in.
