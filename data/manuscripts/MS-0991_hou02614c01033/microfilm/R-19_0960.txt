<u>Categories</u>.

Those are called <u>Homonyms</u>, which have [del]a single[/del] [ins]only a[/ins] common name, but the reason for which name is different; as Landscape is [del]both[/del] the country and the painting. [Aristotle gives [u]ζῷον[/u], [del]an[/del] [ins]meaning both[/ins] animal [del]and or[/del] [ins]and[/ins] image, and instances man and painting.] For the name only of these is common, but the reason for the name is different; since if [del]any[/del] one were to state which each is a Landscape, he would give a different reason for each.

<u>Synonyms</u> refers to those both whose name & the reason itself are common, as animal is both the man and the ox. For the man and the ox are called by a common name animal, and the reason is the same; for should anyone furnish the reason of each, why it is that each of these animals is such, he would furnish the same reason.

<u>Paronyms</u> refers to such as have the [del]appellations[/del] [ins]derivations[/ins] of their names from something from which they differ in case (or part of speech), as [del]the[/del] grammarian from Grammar, and the bravo from braveness.

2.

Of things [del]spoken[/del] [ins]said,[/ins] some are spoken connectedly and others without connection. Now, those connectedly are such as <u>a man walks</u>, <u>a man conquers</u>, and those without connection such as <u>man</u>, <u>ox</u>, <u>walks</u>, <u>conquers</u>.
