[u]Light.[/u]

[u]Light[/u] is that which excites the retina of the eye. Physically considered it consists of vibrations. There is a continuous sequence of different wave lengths, each having its amplitude. The intensity of the sensation depends on the amplitude, [del]the[/del] [ins]its[/ins] quality upon the wave-length. But an increased amplitude always modifies the quality of the sensation, making it yellower. Two lights of very different physical composition may produce identical sensations.

The color of light is its power of producing a quality of sensation [ins]upon a normal human retina.[/ins] The color of a body is the [del]power[/del] color of the light which is irregularly reflected from it in diffused daylight of medium intensity. When the intensity of illumination is increased, the color of the light reflected is yellower, [del]but[/del] and [del]intensity[/del] [ins]the intensity of the[/ins] sensation is greater; but we do not say that the body changes color, or that its color becomes more luminous.

Homogeneous light, that is, light of a single wave-length, is the most highly colored, that is, produces sensations the most distinctive in quality. When the wave length is the greatest that can penetrate the humors of the eye and reach the retina, the color is [u]red[/u]. When the waves are of medium length, the color is [u]green[/u]. When the waves are the shortest that can ordinarily affect the retina, the color is [u]violet[/u]. When red light and green light are mixed, the color is [u]yellow[/u], verging toward red or green, according to the predominance of one or other constituent.
