If the two views are so nearly alike why should the new one be pressed? What are its advantages? I answer that it has three. 1[sup]st[/sup] It is philosophically more perfect. A definition of a science should not include conceptions foreign to that science. For instance, according to the generally received view space is the form of the external sense. If this be true, it would not be false to call geometry the science of the formal laws of the external sense. It would, however, be bad as a definition, because geometry regards its [del]figures[/del] [ins]extension[/ins] simply as [del]objects[/del] [ins]an object[/ins] without any reference to its [del]psychological[/del] [ins]ontological[/ins] character whatsoever.
