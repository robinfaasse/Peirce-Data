Schröder's first two volumes have no very great mathematical interest, and
this is not the place to consider purely logical questions. I shall defend elsewhere
my conviction that the logic of categoricals, his [u]Identitätskalkul[/u], is one and the same
with the logic of hypotheticals, his [u]Aussagenkalkul[/u]. His misunderstanding on this point
would not, I think, have occurred had he attended to my remark in my paper of 1880 that
the hypotheticals require a sign of quantification for their exact expression, so that the un-
quantified hypotheticals which I treat in that paper do not show the characteristics of hypo-
theticals. But connected with that difference of opinion is a difference in our under-
standing of the algebra of logic. Boole assumed that every interpretable expression in
logic [del]is subject[/del] [ins]satisfies[/ins] to a general [ins]quadratic[/ins] equation. He writes it $x(1-x)=0$. That is, every such ex-
pression equals 1 or 0. It makes little difference what two numbers are selected for the
roots; and I prefer to take a positive logarithmic infinity and an absolute zero, so that
the equation ceases to be algebraical. But I [del]had[/del] follow Boole is making every
expression have one or other of two values. Schröder abandons that fundamental
conception. He writes, for example $x \neq 0$ and $x \neq 1$. I express this by writing $\infty \prec \Sigma_A x_A$
$\Pi_A x_A \prec 0$. That is, $x_A$ means that the individual $A$ is $x$, or the possible case, $A$, is
a case of $x$. Aggregating this for [u]all[/u] possible cases, or all individuals, $\Sigma_A x_A$ is not
zero if there is anything that is $x$. But taking the conjunctive combination $\Pi_A x_A$, this
is zero unless [u]all[/u] possible cases are cases of $x$. Since in his third volume, Schröder
finds himself forced to use my quantifiers, as I call the $\Sigma$ and $\Pi$, I cannot
see what he gains by abandoning Boole's fundamental conception. I have oc-
casionally written $a \succ b \succ c$, or the like. But I regard this as a mere abbrevi-
ated expression.
