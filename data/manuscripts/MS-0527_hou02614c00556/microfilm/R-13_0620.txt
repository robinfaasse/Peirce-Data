This is the ninth icon. Next, given any class, there is another which includes all
the former excludes and excludes all the former includes. That is,
$$\Pi_i \Sigma_k \Pi_l (q_{li} \overline{q}_{ki} + \overline{q}_{li} q_{ki}).$$
This is the tenth icon. Next, given any two classes, there is a third which includes
all that either includes and excludes all that both exclude. That is
$$\Pi_i \Pi_m \Sigma_k \Pi_l (q_{li} q_{ki} + q_{mi} q_{ki} + \overline{q}_{li} \overline{q}_{mi} \overline{q}_{ki}).$$
This is the eleventh icon. Next, given any two classes, there is a class which
includes the whole of the first and any one individual of the second which there
may be not included in the first and nothing else. That is,
$$\Pi_i \Pi_m \Pi_l \Sigma_k \Pi_j \{ q_{li} + \overline{q}_{mi} + q_{ki} (q_{kj} + \overline{q}_{lj}) \}.$$
This is the twelfth icon.

[ins]40[/ins] To show the manner in which these formulæ are applied let us suppose we
have given that everything is either true of $i$ or false of $j$. We write
$$\Pi_k (q_{ki} + \overline{q}_{kj}).^{[ins]\times[/ins]}$$
The tenth icon gives $\Pi_i \Sigma_k (q_{li} \overline{q}_{ki} + \overline{q}_{li} q_{ki}) (q_{li} \overline{q}_{lj} + \overline{q}_{li} q_{lj})$
Multiplication of these two formulæ give
$$\Pi_i \Sigma_k (q_{li} \overline{q}_{li} + q_{li} \overline{q}_{lj}),$$
or, dropping the terms in $k$ $\Pi_i (\overline{q}_{li} + q_{li})$.
Multiplying this with the original datum and identifying $l$ with $k$, we have
$$\Pi_k (q_{ki} q_{kj} + \overline{q}_{ki} \overline{q}_{kj}).$$
No doubt, a much more direct method of procedure could be found.
