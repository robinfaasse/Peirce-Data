[pn]377[/pn] The second icon is contained in the rule that the several antecedents of a
consequentia may be transposed; that is, from
[center]$$x \prec (y \prec z)$$[/center]
we can pass to
[center]$$y \prec (x \prec z).$$[/center]
This is stated in the formula
[center]$$\{x \prec (y \prec z)\} \prec \{y \prec (x \prec z)\}.$$[/center]
Because this is the case, the brackets may be omitted, and we may write
[center]$$y \prec x \prec z.$$[/center]
By the formula of identity
[center]$$(x \prec y) \prec (x \prec y);$$[/center]
and transposing the antecedents
[center]$$x \prec \{(x \prec y) \prec y\}$[/center]
or, omitting the unnecessary brackets
[center]$$x \prec (x \prec y) \prec y.$$[/center]
This is the same as to say that if in any state of things $x$ is true, and if the pro-
position "if $x$, then $y$" is true, then in that state of things $y$ is true. This is the
modus ponens of hypothetical inference, and is the most rudimentary form of
reasoning.[ins]x[/ins]

[pn]378[/pn] To say that $(x \prec x)$ is generally true is to say that it is so in every state of
things, say in that in which $y$ is true; so that we may write
[center]$$y \prec (x \prec x),$$[/center]
and then, by transposition of antecedents,
[center]$$x \prec (y \prec x),$$[/center]
or from $x$ we may infer $y \prec x$.

[pn]379[/pn] The third icon is involved in the principle of the transitiveness of the copula,
which is stated in the formula
[center]$$(x \prec y) \prec (y \prec z) \prec x \prec z.$$[ins]xx[/ins][/center]
According to this, if in any case $y$ follows from $x$ and $z$ follows from $y$, then $z$ follows
from $x$.[ins]x[/ins] This is the principle of the syllogism in Barbara.

[pn]380[/pn] We have already seen that from $x$ follows $y \prec x$. Hence, by the transi-
tiveness of the copula, if from $y \prec x$ follows $z$, then from $x$ follows $z$, or from
[center]$$(y \prec x) \prec z$$[/center]
follows
[center]$$x \prec z,$$[/center]
or
[center]$$\{(y \prec x) \prec z\} \prec x \prec z.$$[/center]
