Concerning the squat [u]o[/u]: I do not deny that this form of
[u]o[/u] has been used before this century, but I do not find it in any
good manuscripts. The examples that you quote are archaic, and I
hold that they are of no more authority and no more deserve to be
imitated than the scrawls of the school boy beginning to write.
Here let me make a statement about the [u]o[/u] as it is now made by type-
cutters. It may surprise you to know that the capital [ins]O[/ins] and the
small [u]o[/u] are always made higher than their mates. The top of it
goes above the line and the foot of it below the line. If cut
exactly on the line laid down for other letters, the [u]o[/u] will always
appear smaller. Curved letters touch the standard height at one
point only, and for this reason they have to be made exceptionally
high to mate with other letters that touch them at two or more
points.

I have recently received a letter from a reformer of the
alphabet in Washington Territory, who proposes to reform letters
by rejecting all curves and making all the characters of right
angles. He would give us the squareness of Hebrew without its
picturesqueness.
