Dear Sir:

I fear you do not clearly understand my notions about
types and I am very sure I could not convey them in this letter.
With this I send you some proofs of an article I wrote for the
"Authors' [del]f[/del] Club," which gives my notions more clearly.

I fully agree with you that we have been degenerating in
typography for nearly one hundred years. Baskerville was the
first innovator. The greatest change he made was the thinning of
the thick stroke of a letter and the sharpening of the hair line.
His types, when leaded and well printed on smooth paper, have a good
effect; they are not so good when set compactly, without leads and
carelessly printed.

The worst offender was Bodoni, of Italy, who sharpened and
thinned the lines and elongated the ascending and descending lines,
and tried to imitate copper-plate engraving.
