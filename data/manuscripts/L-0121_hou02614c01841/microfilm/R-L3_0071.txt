My dear Sir:

I do not think that we disagree at all about the main object of print. As I understand the matter, types should be made to be read, and to be read easily. Not only by the matured scholar, but by the small boy. The letters of the alphabet are practically coins, weights or measures and each represents a fixed value. Under no circumstances should one letter be made so as it can be mistaken for another. Somebody, (whose name I wish I could recollect) in giving advice to a writer said, "Before all things Truth." So I say "Before all things, Readability."

This really does not hinder the expression of a proper degree of individuality. Every man's handwriting is, or should be, his own. I have no fault to find with any man's handwriting, however angular or crabbed it may be, if it is distinct and if it is the honest expression of his own personality, but I have small patience with that affectation which purposely and painfully substitutes obscurity and eccentricity for [del]its[/del] [ins]true[/ins] personality. It is the shabbiest kind of affectation.
