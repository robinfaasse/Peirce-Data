recent designers of type have been trying as hard as they could to
imitate the effeminacies of writing masters and copper-plate en-
gravers. A man with imperfect or failing eyesight sees only the
thick strokes of a modern letter. He has to guess that they are
connected with hair lines, for he does not see the hair line.

William Morris, of London has been publishing some books
at the Kelmscott Press in which he has gone back to the oldest
models, avoiding hair lines. Unfortunately, he has copied too
servily, and made his types unnecessarily medieval.

Two type-founders of this country are now getting up a
series of roman types from which the hair lines are entirely ex-
cluded. They are improvements on the accepted styles, but by no
means perfect. I am now at work on a new form, on a type which is
intended to help the reader and not to show the skill of the punch-
cutter. It will be a year or two before I get it out, but I think
that you will admit that it is quite as distinct as any Old-style
letter, even if it is not a copy of any early fashion.
