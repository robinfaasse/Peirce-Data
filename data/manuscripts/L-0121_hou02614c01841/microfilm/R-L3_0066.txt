Dear Sir:

I cannot trace your book in Hain, although I spent some
time in looking it up, nor do I know who Leonhardus was. Probably
a punch-cutter. I have a recollection of meeting this name before
but I cannot trace it. Probably he was the same punch-cutter who
cut the beautiful Basle types for Froeben and Ammerbach. William
Morris in one of his early publications of the "Kelmscott Press,"
gave a re-production of a face of type which he and the "Chiswick
Press" called the "Basle" type, but which I call "Early Italian."
There certainly was in that city one or more punch-cutters of high
merit.

The ugly full-faced types of Arabic figures, which you
probably dislike, are accredited to the type-founder, Robert Thorpe,
of London, who first showed them as the mates of his fat-faced roman
about 1803, but I am not sure that he was the inventor of the fat
face. I think the suggestion and probably the first example was
made in the Didot office, of Paris, when they were trying to rival
the Bodoni house.
