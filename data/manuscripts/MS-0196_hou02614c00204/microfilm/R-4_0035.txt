To double
$$\int_{0}^{x} \frac{d}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}$$
we add more of the little quantities $\frac{d}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}$. Though
$d$ has the same value for each, $x$ is growing longer as
each one is added, and so $\sqrt{1-x^{2}}$ grows smaller, and
$\frac{d}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}$ larger and longer. Consequently, we do not
have to double $x$ to double $\int_{0}^{x} \frac{d}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}$. But each
addition of $\frac{d}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}$ to the arc [del]adds to[/del] [ins]changes[/ins] the quantity
$$\sqrt{1-x^{2}} + x i$$
