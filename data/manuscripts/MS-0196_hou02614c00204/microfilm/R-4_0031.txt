[u]Chapter II. The Addition Theorem[/u]

By looking at the last figure, it will be seen that, by the similarity of triangles

$$\frac{dx}{d\theta} = \frac{y}{1} \qquad dx : d\theta = y : 1$$

$$\frac{dy}{d\theta} = -\frac{x}{1} \qquad dy : d\theta = -x : 1.$$

[del]The first equation is instantly seen[/del]
This is also seen algebraically, remembering that

$$d\theta = \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$$

and $y = \sqrt{1-x^2}$.

The second equation

$$d\sqrt{1-x^2} / \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} = -x$$

results from the value obtained in the last chapter for $d\sqrt{1-x^2}$, namely

$$d\sqrt{1-x^2} = -\frac{x \cdot dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$$
