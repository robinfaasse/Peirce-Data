Three or more points on one ray are commonly said
to be collinear, but in this book the word [u]coradial[/u] will be used.

An [u]inscribable[/u] polygon is one which can be inscribed
in a circle.

The [u]sine[/u] of an angle is the ratio of the leg opposite
an angle of that value in a right triangle to the hypothenuse.

[del]The cosine of an angle is the ratio of the leg adjacent
an angle of that value in a right triangle to the hypothenuse.[/del]

The [u]tangent[/u] of an angle is the ratio of the leg opposite
that angle in a right triangle to the leg adjacent.

The [u]secant[/u] of an angle is the ratio of the hypothenuse
to the leg adjacent an angle of that value in a right triangle.

The [u]cosine[/u], [u]cotangent[/u], and [u]cosecant[/u] of an angle
are the sine, tangent, and secant, respectively, of the com-
plement of that angle.

The [u]versin[/u], or [u]versed sine[/u], of an angle is unity minus
its cosine. The [u]haversin[/u] of an angle is the square of the sine of half the
angle.

[u]Notation[/u]

The following abbreviations are usual

[u]sin[/u] for "sine of,"
[u]cos[/u] for "cosine of,"
[u]sec[/u] for "secant of,"
[u]cosec[/u] for "cosecant of,"
[u]tan[/u] for "tangent of,"
[u]cot[/u] for "cotangent of."

Also $\sin^2 x$, $\tan^2 x$, etc are written instead of $(\sin x)^2$, $(\tan x)^2$, etc.
