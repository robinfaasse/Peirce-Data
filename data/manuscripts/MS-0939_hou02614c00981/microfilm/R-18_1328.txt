Now as to essential modes of being, I remark that there is
no distinction more indispensable to clear discussion
than this. There are three grades of complexity of being.
Namely, in the first place we can conceive of an object
as being such as it is positively in itself [del]with[/del] regardless of
any relation. Next, we can conceive of something which
has no being except in relation to a second object, but
exists in that relation regardless of any third. Thirdly,
we can conceive of an object which is such as it is in a
triadic relation to a second and for a third. There is no
more complicated mode of being unless it be a composite
mode, for the reason that every tetradic or higher order
of relation is equivalent to a compound of triadic relations
