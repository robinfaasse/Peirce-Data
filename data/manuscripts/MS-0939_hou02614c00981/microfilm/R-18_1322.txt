"bodies" are simply the relatively movable [del]the[/del] wholes in our
percepts. Clearly, then, I cannot admit that all our
reasonings take "bodies" for granted; and as for "body",
that appears to me an "abstract" way of thinking of "bodies".

But I note a great difference between a "percept" and a
"perceptual judgment". The latter is a mental description
of a percept, in language or other symbols. Our thought
is, in my opinion, fortified by various reasons, an appeal
in symbols of the self of the present to the self of the
immediate future. It is conducted, if not in propositions,
in something of the very much of that nature. I do not
think it is, in its movement, propositional; but we
are continually drawing up "propositions" or "judgments"
