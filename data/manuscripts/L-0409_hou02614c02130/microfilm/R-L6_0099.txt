Jan. 27, 1900.

Dear Mr. McClure:
Mr. Charles S. Peirce, of the National Academy of Sciences, very well known as eminent in logic and meta-physics, for his practical acquaintance with the whole theory of weights and measures, and a man capable of treating almost any branch of mathematics successfully, asks me to suggest his name to you in case you should need such services in the Encyclopaedia, which he understands you are preparing.
Mr. Peirce has been already employed in the Century Dictionary on definitions cognate to the subjects I have referred to, and you will find his name mentioned under a variety of heads in their statement.
I really think you will find no one who will do some of these things as well as he. I do not, however, ask you to rely upon my opinion alone, but to take that of others, and I think you will find it confirmed by those competent to speak.

Very truly yours,
(Signed) S.P.Langley

S. S. McClure, Esq.,
141 E. 25th St.,
New York City.
