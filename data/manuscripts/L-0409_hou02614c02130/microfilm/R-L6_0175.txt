When I agreed to look for your
passage for you if you would
send me the treatise, what I
meant was that you should
send me the book to keep. I
had in view the Clarendon Press
edition, the price of which is
nine shillings. If you think that
my services in the matter are
worth that sum, I should be glad
to receive that volume. Otherwise,
let it go as it is.

I have written for you a minute
criticism of the fundamental sec-
tions of the [del]our[/del] sceptical part, show-
ing just what the faults of the
reasoning are. But I think it
is probably more minute and
lengthy than you would care
for and therefore I will not send
too, and I on my own means
have now what was in my
mind. If was a thing that had
attracted a good deal of atten-
tion and nobody had discovered
that it was ambiguously ex-
pressed. It has also happened to
me repeatedly to be misinter-
preted when it seemed to me im-
possible and to be accused of errors
by important men which only
existed in their own gross mis-
readings. So it won't greatly
surprise me if some small error
is found in some translation of
mine, notwithstanding my utmost
care.
