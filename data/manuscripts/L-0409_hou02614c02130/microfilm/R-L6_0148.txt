If only one hypothesis suggests itself, we go to work to deduce
from it all the necessary consequences we can think of
(such is the logical order of procedure) and then we experi-
ment to see what proportion of these are verified, or how near
they are verified. This procedure is deduction followed by a
sort of induction. The logic of hypotheses comes to a close
if we can think of but one possible explanation
of the facts.

But in many cases (including all cases of testimony)
various hypotheses suggest themselves. Often we find ourselves
already in possession of evidence concerning some of them;
but let us begin by supposing we knew nothing at all about
any of them. Suppose we find we can think out, say, [del]32[/del] [ins]32[/ins]
