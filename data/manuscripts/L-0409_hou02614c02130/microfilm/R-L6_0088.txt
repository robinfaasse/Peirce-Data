Washington, D.C.,

January 17, 1894.

My dear Mr. Peirce:

I have read with interest the announcement of your proposed
work, "The Principles of Philosophy". While I know competent judges
believe your reading in certain fields of philosophical thought to
be perhaps the most extensive of [ins]that of[/ins] any living writer, I can also say
that the manner in which you have assimilated what is most material
to your purpose in the past, has in no way encumbered the activity
of your own mind, and that you are equally recognized as one of the
most learned of students and one of the most original thinkers
in the subject.

I am glad, then, to know of the probability of the appearance
of the work you contemplate, and from the syllabus you have prepared
I shall expect a valuable addition to the thought of
the past, and to that of the present.

With every wish that it may have the success I am sure it
will deserve, I am,

Very sincerely yours,

S. P. Langley
