Milford Pa [date]1901 May 20[/date]

My dear Prof. Langley:

Your sending me a copy of your former letter seems to suppose that I have not read it with sufficient attention.

But I think I did so. I think the difficulty — mind, it is not for my interest to create imaginary difficulties, — is that you ask me to treat of the "Laws of Nature" with special reference to Hume's argument about miracles, when in fact that argument has nothing to do with Laws of Nature, was not understood so by Hume, nor by the philosophers of Hume's day [del]or since[/del], nor by the general public at Hume's day. [ins]If he had thought the gist of his argument lay in the definition of a miracle, he would not have put it into a footnote.[/ins]

Now I will go ahead and write the article. But I cannot, in 4000 words, adduce full proofs of what I say. I can only mention some facts tending that way, & state that careful investigation has shown me that there is really no connection between the two.

Hume's argument attracts no attention now, because all the German critics having pushed similar argumentation so much further. What is called the "higher criticism" of ancient history (by a quite natural deflection of the term "higher criticism") consists in that. I do not think the method sound; & think its dangers are shown by archeological explorations. I shall assume that an article of this sort will satisfy you, unless I hear to the contrary.

Very respectfully
C. S. Peirce
