It has thus been amply proved [del]that, no matter what number[/del] B
may be, if [del]P[/del] [ins]p[/ins] is a prime number, $B^p - B = M \times p$, [del]where M is a whole[/del]
[del]number.[/del] Adding B to both sides of the equation, $B^p = B + Mp$. Dividing
by B, $B^{p-1} = 1 + \frac{M}{B} p$ [del]and since $B^p - B = (B^{p-1} - 1)$[/del]

It has thus been amply proved that [del]B^p - B = M x p, where M is some whole[/del]
[del]number,[/del] and, since the left hand side of the equation is divisible by B,
being $B(B^{p-1} - 1)$, the right hand side, which is the very same number
must be equally divisible by B; so that we might write $B^p - B = N \cdot B \cdot p$ where
N is some whole number. Dividing the whole equation by B, we have
$B^{p-1} - 1 = Np$. [del]or $B^{p-2} = 1 + Np$.[/del] Thus, taking B = 10 and p = 7, $10^6 - 1 = 7N$
