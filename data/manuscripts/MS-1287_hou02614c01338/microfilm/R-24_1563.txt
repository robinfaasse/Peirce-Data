[u]Lecture I.[/u]

Let us begin by taking one rapid glance
backward at the history of [del]the[/del] human
knowledge. Let us for this purpose reckon
time in eras of five hundred years, periods
long enough to completely revolutionize ideas.
For remember that 500 years ago Geoffrey
Chaucer was writing the Canterbury Tales.
However, we will make our first backward
step resting-point only 400 hundred years
back so as to strike a [ins]usual[/ins] whole number of
500 years of our reckoning of time.

In the year 1500 modern science was
not begun. Copernicus had just taken
his degree at the University of Padua.
In northern Europe men were scarce
beginning to wake up to the futility of
scholastic logic. All the arts of life
were such as would seem barbarous to
us. Men were still wearing gowns or
robes. Women wore steeple crowned hats
