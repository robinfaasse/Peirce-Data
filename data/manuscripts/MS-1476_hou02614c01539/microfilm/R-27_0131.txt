tence, which modifies its mode of being, as opposed to a law, for example,
which is real but has no existence. However, I cannot yet set forth the general
doctrine, [del]because I should need the maxim of pragmatism.[/del] But I can say, that
what [del]cannot be controlled[/del] [ins]is not[/ins] a subject of praise or blame. Now to say
that a proposition is false, is to blame it. Consequently, whatever we uncontrollably
have to think and believe (that is, to act upon the basis of it) must be
allowed to be true, in the negative sense that we have nothing to do but
submit to the acceptance of it. Such are our percepts and our whole
naïve world, the world of common sense. Try or struggle as you may; you
have got to believe it and you may as well do so with a good grace.
