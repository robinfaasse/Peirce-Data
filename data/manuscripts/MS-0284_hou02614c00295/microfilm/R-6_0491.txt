Now that different logical terms have different
valencies in the same sense, is the [del]chief[/del] first
teaching of the logic of relations. A [u]rheme[/u] is
a blank form of proposition, such that when
each blank is filled with a proper name,
the result is a proposition. The [u]valency[/u] of
a rheme is the number of proper names that
have to be inserted in it to make a [ins]complete[/ins] proposition.

'Esau sold his birthright to Jacob for
a bowl of pottage.' This has no valency; we
call it a medad ([greek]μηδέν[/greek], nothing + [greek]ιδα[/greek] the
Acc. sing. of the patronymic termination, the
issue of nothing.)

The following are [u]monads[/u], that is, they have
one valency each since [del]one[/del] the insertion of one
proper name converts each into a proposition.
" — [del]sold[/del] [ins]bartered[/ins] his birthright to Jacob for a mess of pottage."
