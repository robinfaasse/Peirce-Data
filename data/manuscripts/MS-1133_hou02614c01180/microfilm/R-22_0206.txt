Instead of [del]their[/del] acting on the moment's impulse, devotion may cause action [del]for[/del] [ins]from[/ins] a general rule or reason, provided there is a retroconsciousness. This sympathetic devotion may have for its object an estate or a tree; so that there is no [del]essential[/del] aim at pleasure essentially involved. One acts to benefit something that one loves. Of course, many psychologists have been so obtuse as to think it an objection to say that we tend a plant because it gives us pleasure to see it in a flourishing condition. This is a [u]hysteron proteron[/u]. But even if it were not so, it would be quite aside from the purpose, unless the person is supposed to make a calculation of his own pleasure. For our inquiry into motives is not an inquiry into the springs of action, but into the end which presents itself, as desirable, to the mind of the agent. But devotion does not even require retro-consciousness: the vague personification of people in general, — one's corps, or club, or [del]our[/del] nation, — provided the agent does more than merely act on a general reason or rule, but aims at bringing about a definite general state of things.
