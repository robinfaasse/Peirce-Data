A perfect analysis of motion requires that the photographs
should be taken at as short intervals as may be, yet for as long
a time as possible. If we merely make the rotation of the
shutter disk faster, the number of images will, it is true, be
augmented; but the animal's locomotion not being thereby
accelerated, the result will be that the photographs
will be taken so close together that they interfere with
one another, and produce the confused effect seen in Fig. 7.
