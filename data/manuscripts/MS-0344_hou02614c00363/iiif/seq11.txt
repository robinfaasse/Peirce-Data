Let us now endeavor to express upon Boole's system
the three fundamental laws of logic - those, namely, of Iden-
tity, Contradiction, & Excluded Third.

In order to express the law of Identity or [u]all A is A[/u]; let
us first express [u]All X is Y[/u] and then substitute A for both
X and Y. [del]All[/del] To say [u]All X is Y[/u] is [del]the[/del] same as to say that the
class X is identical with the class which has the comprehension
of X & Y together or X = XY. Now substituting A for X & Y we
have A = A^2. Now does this express the law of identity? Unless
the formula [u]A is A[/u] means that A is B that is that A is something
or in other words that every logical term [del]is[/del] capable of an affir-
mative predicate or that it is real; I say unless the law of i-
dentity means this then [u]John is John[/u] does not represent the
law at all. For [u]John is John[/u] means absolutely nothing more
than that [u]John is X[/u], or [u]John is[/u]. What the law may be taken to
mean is this:- that all the individuals composing a class have
the [ins]class[/ins] character. Let A denote a certain class
Let [u]a[/u] denote the individuals in it
Let [u]x[/u] denote [del]the[/del] [ins]whatever has the[/ins] class character.
