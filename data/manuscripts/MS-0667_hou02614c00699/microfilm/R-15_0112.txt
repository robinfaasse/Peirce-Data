5:15 [/marginalia] [ins]The Reasoning[/ins] is to throw light upon the question to which it is directed by taking into account some fact about the mind, in which case that fact will become [del]the[/del] one of the Premises of the Reasoning. For the Reader will then recognize, if he does not already, that the Conclusion is drawn, not from the expression in language of the facts embodied in its Premises, but from those very facts themselves, which are, in all cases, external to the thought of them, since it is impossible for a thought to be directed to itself. This is shown by the self-contradictions that follow from the Proposition, "This Proposition [del]now being[/del] [ins]stated[/ins] is false." If it be true, all that it asserts must be true; but it asserts that it is false; therefore, it must be false. On the other hand, if it be false, some part of what it asserts or implies must be false. But it asserts nothing except that it is false. Therefore, [del]if it is false,[/del] [ins]it must either be true, or must imply its own truth and therein[/ins] [ins]alone[/ins] be false.
