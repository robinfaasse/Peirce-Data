An event for me personally highly favorable to opportunities for watching the
effects of Comte's utterances occurred in the very year his great work began to appear:
namely, I was that year born, and as a consequence, [ins]or, perhaps in a better situation that my[/ins]
reader to testify to the gradual but marked improvement in men's reasoning, as Comte's writings
came to win wider respect, until well into the sixties. Yet the reader, even if he has skipped
my footnotes, will not expect, for all the high honour I render him, to find me, swallowing in one
bolus all this singular prophet ever said, as a society of his English worshippers have done. Those
sociétaires, however, command a modicum of my respect, however, for recognizing, with
Comte, a side of Truth to which other nominalists are mostly quite blind, I mean its spiritu-
al, its religious side, which all truth has. But I opine that a man of science ought to be
esteemed, [del]and rated[/del] [ins]and rated[/ins] chiefly as a discoverer, if he be one, and not a mere observer; the latter's merit
