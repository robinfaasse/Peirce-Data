The logic of relatives shows that every proposition has a number of different subjects; and regarding the proposition as a sign [ins]of the relation between[/ins] these subjects, or the ordered collection of them, constitute the object, the external meaning of the sign and of the idea. These different subjects [del]have[/del] [ins]and will, in many cases,[/ins] have a serial order, although this may happen to be more or less indeterminate.

But for all propositions whatsoever, whether they relate to the world of experience, a world of fiction, or a realm of mathematical hypothesis, the first subject in order is one and the same definite individual, which we usually call "the truth." Thus, to say "Hamlet was a little [del]unhinged[/del]" and "The truth is that Hamlet was a little unhinged" are different expressions of the same proposition.
