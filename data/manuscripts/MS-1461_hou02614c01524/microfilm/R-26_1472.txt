But what, then, can be the mode of being of an idea unequivocally false? Accordingly, for Hegel, the false is simply the bad, that which in some respect is out of harmony with its own essence; and since, for him, contradiction is the great activity of the world, he has no difficulty in admitting that an idea may be out of accord with itself. Prof. Royce, however, seems almost to resent the idea that anybody could suppose that he denies the validity of the distinction of truth and falsehood. He pronounces [del]various sundry[/del] doctrines to be false, [del]even[/del] [ins]quite definitely, even[/ins] if we do not quite hear his foot come down; and he holds nothing to be more false than the usual form of stating the distinction [ins]in question[/ins].
