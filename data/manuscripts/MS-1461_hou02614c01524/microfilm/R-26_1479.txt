But we should like to say this to Mr. Royce. Absolute idealism depends,
as Hegel saw that it did, upon assuming that position at the outset.
If your refutation is addressed to students who are already absolute
idealists at heart, it may or may not be serviceable for the develop-
ment of that philosophy. But if it is addressed to realists themselves,
it must conform to the logical principles admitted by realists, or be
nugatory. Now you know very well that realists do not admit that matter
of fact can be apodictically demonstrated. You ought to know, and
do know, if you reflect upon it, that if you drive them into a corner, they
will simply modify the premises so as to avoid the contradiction and
that it is not of the nature of things that no possible loop-hole should
remain in arguing about matters of fact. But the question of
realism is a question of [del]hard[/del] fact, if there is any such thing as [del]hard[/del]
fact; and, therefore, your method never can produce conviction
in the mind of any realist who is a master of his own logic.
