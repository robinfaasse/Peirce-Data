[del]Setting out from[/del] the ideas of Maine de Biran, the sane and careful, he [del]has taken[/del] the logic of modern physics as [u]lucerna pedibus[/u]. His '[u]Logique de l'hypothèse[/u],' published in [date]1880[/date], while not quite satisfying our American logicians, who would like to see the question discussed in the light of the mathematical doctrine of probability, is acknowledged to be one of the justest sketches of the theory of inductive reasoning, [del]which[/del] [ins]and one that[/ins] unfailingly condemns [del]every inadmissible inference[/del] [ins]all the ordinary inductive fallacies[/ins]. The author regards the "recherche de l'unité" as the guiding principle of scientific hypotheses, and as that from which their value and also their vices spring. In the present volume, he continues to insist upon the controlling influence of the same tendency. Philosophy, to satisfy this impulse, has to fix upon a first principle from which an explanation of the data of experience can be deduced.
