Cosmos Club,
Washington, D.C.,
May 4,1896.

Professor Charles S. Peirce:
My dear Sir:

I thank you for your letter of May 2, I admit that our rate of payment is small but it is literally, as a general rate, the best that we can afford to pay. The American Historical Review is not in the least a money-making enterprise. It is supported in considerable part by [ins]a[/ins] guarantee fund[del]s[/del] which is to operate during three years. The height of our ambition will be attained if at the end of that three years we shall even be self-supporting. We seldom pay for articles, and for reviews have established the rate of which I speak. We often have to appeal to the reviewer's interest in history and in the maintenance of a scientific historical review of high grade as a motive to induce him to assist us. I hope this motive will be operative in your case also. At the same time, if you should feel that the sum offered is too far from adequate, I am permitted, in an occasional special case, to offer $5.00 a page instead of three dollars. I will not have the book sent to you until I hear from you again, but I shall sincerely hope that you will find it possible to aid us.

Very truly yours,

J. F. Jameson.
