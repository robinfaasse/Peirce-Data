This gives me the right to make
the following representations:

A writer would be just as likely
to undertake a book-notice gratis as
for $3 per thousand, if not more so; and
having undertaken the task, he would,
on the average, take far more pains
with the gratuitous communication.

Supposing the practice to be not
to pay what is promised, — until the
writer makes himself very disagree-

able, a better knowledge of human nature would be,
to begin by naming a price which, instead of conveying

during the whole of which time the irritation of the
writer at having accepted such a price is on the
increase, a prompt statement on publication, or even
before, that the Review does not find itself in condition
at present to pay the amount, while is very trifling for
so valuable an article (and it probably would be capital)
might cause the writer to bestir himself to help the Review
instead of contemplating it with nausea. —
