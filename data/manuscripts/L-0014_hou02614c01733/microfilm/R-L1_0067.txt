I wish to suggest in the first place
that to offer $3 is so near to offering
nothing that the writer is [ins]nowhere[/ins] just as likely
to write what was wanted gratis as
for $3, & indeed would, if he undertook
to do the work at all, do it on the
average far better & more carefully
for nothing than if he thought "Now
I will put $3 worth of work into this"
or say half an hour. Or you might
offer a year's subscription. But if
you are not going to pay after having
promised to do so, why not pay the writer the compliment
of naming a handsome price for his work, say $30
a thousand, — which would not be large enough to
excite my suspicion? Then write frankly afterwards &
say the Review can't pay at present, instead of making
humiliating excuses.

Both the notices I wrote were pretty poor, I confess.
They were much worse than they would have been if I
were working for love or for my usual rate of $25 per
thousand. My actual expenses in writing the first
were considerable as I was obliged to pay expressage
on a large book & to buy several journals. For the
last one I purchased a large volume & a small one
& sent to Cambridge for a book.
