[del]Art.[/del] [ins]Art.[/ins] 1. By a [u]verb[/u], I mean either a complete proposition or a fragment of a proposition which would be rendered complete by filling up certain blanks [del]attached[/del] [ins]belonging[/ins] to it by proper names. Thus, "— sells — to — at the price of —" is a verb.

A verb having no blanks, and so being a complete proposition, I term a [u]medad[/u]: a verb having a single blank I call a [u]monad[/u]; a verb having two blanks I call a [u]dyad[/u]: a verb having three blanks I call a [del]triad[/del]; the number of blanks of a verb I call its [u]adicity[/u].

I propose to represent a verb by an ideograph, or unanalyzed sign, — generally, by an initial letter, — with [ins]heavy[/ins] lines projecting from it at definite places to represent its several blanks. Thus, [fig]cross-like ideograph with four projecting lines[/fig] may mean "— sells — to — at the price of —" where the four blanks in their order are represented respectively by the lines at the left, at the right, below, and above.

Art. 2 A lightly drawn oval encircling a verb shall [del]have to[/del] [ins]be understood[/ins] to deny it. The encircled verb is the [u]negative[/u] of the verb. Such an oval must have [del]a heavy line[/del] [ins]a heavy line[/ins] projecting from it [del]to represent[/del] corresponding to each such line projecting from the ideograph of the verb. Thus, [fig]circled cross-like ideograph with four projecting lines[/fig] will mean "— does not sell — to — at the price of —"
