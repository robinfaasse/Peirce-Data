[u]Art. 9[/u]. Another class of verbs requiring special study in logic are [u]verbs of second intention[/u]. When we first begin the study of any department of experiential science, we form classes and systems of objects of experience and these give the ordinary verbs, or [u]verbs of first intention[/u]. But when we come to observe the signs themselves which we have so created, we make classes and systems based on the characters of the signs, and these are, [u]verbs of second intention[/u]. We especially remark the [u]dyad of second intention[/u], which is "— has the character (or is the subject of the monad) —" and the [u]triad of second intention[/u] which is "— and — are subjects which can truly fill respectively the first and second blanks of the dyad —"
