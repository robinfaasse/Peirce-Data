§ 1.
[u]Art. 1.[/u] By a [u]verb[/u], I mean either a complete proposition or a fragment of a proposition which would be rendered complete by filling up certain blanks [del]attached[/del] [ins]belonging[/ins] to it by proper names. Thus, "— sells — to — at the price of —" is a verb.
A verb having no blanks, and so being a complete proposition, I term a [u]medad[/u]: a verb having a single blank I call a [u]monad[/u]; a verb having two blanks I call a [u]dyad[/u]: a verb having three blanks I call a [u]triad[/u], etc. The number of blanks of a verb I call its [u]adicity[/u].
I propose to represent a verb by an ideograph, or unanalyzed sign,— generally, by an initial letter,— with [ins]heavy[/ins] lines projecting from it at definite places to represent its several blanks. Thus, [fig]S with four lines[/fig] may mean "— sells — to — at the price of —" where the four blanks in their order are represented respectively by the lines at the left, at the right, below, and above.
[u]Art. 2.[/u] A lightly drawn oval encircling a verb shall [del]have the[/del] [ins]be understood[/ins] to deny it. The encircled verb is the [u]negative[/u] of the verb. Such an oval must have a [del]blank[/del] heavy line projecting from it to [del]represent[/del] [ins]a heavy line[/ins] correspond-ing to each such line projecting from the ideograph of the verb. Thus, [fig]S with four lines inside an oval with four lines[/fig] will mean "— does not sell — to — at the price of —."
