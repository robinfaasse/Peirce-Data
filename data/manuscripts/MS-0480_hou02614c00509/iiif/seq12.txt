[u]Fifth Simplification[/u]. A circle has as many hands as the graph
within it. It is in itself a sign of negation and of grouping. All oddly
enclosed letters are taken negatively, all evenly enclosed letters affirma-
tively. Graphs encircled together are combined together, quite irrespective of
their bonds. Those bonds only identify the [ins]subjects of the[/ins] hands of the graphs, but do not
combine the graphs. It is their being written together in the same enclosures
which signifies combination. Graphs evenly encircled together are
disjunctively combined, graphs oddly enclosed together copulatively com-
bined, irrespective of their bonds. There is, therefore, no reason why one should
restrict ourselves to the conception that a circle must have hands. Why
should not a medad be encircled as well as a relative? Medads are as
capable as relatives of copulative and disjunctive combination.
