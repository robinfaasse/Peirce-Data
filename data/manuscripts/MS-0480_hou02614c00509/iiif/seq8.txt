Art. 9. Another class of verbs requiring special study in logic are [u]verbs of 
second intention[/u]. When we first begin the study of any department of 
experiential science, we form classes and systems of objects of experience 
and these give the ordinary verbs, or [u]verbs of first intention[/u]. But when we 
come to observe the signs themselves which we have so created, we make 
classes and systems based on the characters of the signs, and these are 
verbs of second intention. We especially remark the [u]dyad of second intention[/u], 
which is " — has the character (or is the subject of the monad) — " and
