To say that both [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are applicable to a subject [u]severally[/u] is to say that [u]x[/u] is applicable and that [u]y[/u] is appli-cable, as if to different [ins]definite[/ins] subjects. To say that they are appli-cable [u]jointly[/u] is to say that the utterer of the proposition might (with sufficient knowledge) specify a definite subject [del]to suit his purpose[/del] [ins]allowed by the vagueness of the proposition to be adduced in its justification[/ins], and that to that definite subject both [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are severally applicable. Thus the distinction vanishes when the subject is definite. Even if one member of the copulation is [del]individual[/del] [ins]definite[/ins], the utterer sur-renders his [del]freedom[/del] [ins]liberty[/ins] of choice, and the distinction is necessarily annihilated. Moreover, the [ins]joint[/ins] copulate whole is individual, when it differs from the several copulation; for then the freedom of choice has been retained by the utterer, and cannot belong to the interpreter.
