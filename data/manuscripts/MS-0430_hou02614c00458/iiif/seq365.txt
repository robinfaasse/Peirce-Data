[u]The Vital Definitions of the Algebra of the Copula.[/u]
(64) I. The letter [u]v[/u] denotes any given object that is
(64) [u]α.[/u] definite and
(65) [u]β.[/u] individual.
(66) II. When a term is written upon a certain [u]sheet of assertions[/u], it is asserted, as we shall say, to be [u]scriptible[/u], that is, to be applicable to [u]v[/u]. The signification of [ins]called[/ins] any term is its signification when asserted.
(67) III. The sign [fig]figure[/fig], or the [u]Copula of Inclusion[/u], signifies that the [u]consequent[/u], or term written to the right of it, is at least as applicable to [u]v[/u] [ins]in[/ins] as the [u]antecedent[/u], or term written to the left of it. In other words, to assert the [u]consequence[/u], which is composed of antecedent, copula, and consequent, united, is to assert that if the antecedent is scriptible, so is the consequent.
(68) IV. The term [u]x [fig]figure[/fig] y [fig]figure[/fig] z[/u], whatever [ins]terms[/ins] [u]x[/u], [u]y[/u], and [u]z[/u] may be, is equivalent to the term [u]x [fig]figure[/fig] (y [fig]figure[/fig] z)[/u].
