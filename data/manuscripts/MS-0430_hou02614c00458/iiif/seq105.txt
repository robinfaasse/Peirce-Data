It is to be observed that this last clause of the definition is very
different from say that whatever [u]a[/u] and [u]x[/u] may be, if [u]a[/u] < [u]x[/u], then
[u]a[/u] is true. What is requisite, to give us a right to say that [u]a[/u] is true, is
that [del]for no m[/del] [ins]having choo[/ins] it having been determined what [u]a[/u] is, it makes
no difference what [del]may be[/del] taken as [u]x[/u], in every case [u]a[/u] < [u]x[/u].
But in order to make the matter quite clear, further explana-
tion is requisite. To assert the truth of any proposition, p,
is to say that the logical value of it is [u]v[/u]. To assert its falsity
is to assert that its logical value is [u]f[/u]. To write p < q means
that if p is asserted, we may assert q in place of it, without
[ins]the value of what is asserted[/ins] passing from [u]v[/u] to [u]f[/u]. If then p is false, we may write p < q
without possibility of error. So we may if q is true. If then
[u]a[/u] is true, that is, if [u]a[/u] is false, we may write [u]a[/u] < [u]x[/u], no
matter what the value of [u]x[/u] may be.
