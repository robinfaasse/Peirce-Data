This asserts that something is at once, a man, wounded, and
disgraced. Then the following
[fig]man — (wounded [del]disgraced[/del])[/fig]
will mean that something is identical with a man wounded
but not disgraced. The following
[fig]man — (([del]wounded[/del] disgraced))[/fig]
will mean that something is identical with a man who
is [del]at once wounded and disgraced;[/del] [ins]identical with a thing that is [del]some man is either not wounded or not disgraced;[/del][/ins] [ins]thing that is, some man is either not wounded or not disgraced; while[/ins]
[fig]man — ([del]wounded[/del] [del]disgraced[/del])[/fig]
will signify that something is a man who is not wounded
[u]and[/u] not disgraced. The continuity of the identity is unaffected
by the oval. Then,
