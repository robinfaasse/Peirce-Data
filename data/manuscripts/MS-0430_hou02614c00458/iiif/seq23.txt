The branch of mathematics which studies this
simplest of all possible hypotheses, excepting only
the hypothesis that there is but one object in the universe
from which nothing can be deduced, may be termed [del]dyadics[/del] [ins]dichotomics[/ins].
A postulate in mathematics, is an item of [ins]general[/ins] the [del]hypotheses[/del] hypothesis,
or [del]statement[/del] [ins]definition[/ins] of the imaginary state of things, studied in the
branch of mathematics to which that postulate belongs. [del]Dyadics[/del] [ins]Dichotomics[/ins]
sets out with these two postulates:
[u]Postulate I[/u]. Nothing is both [u]F[/u] and [u]V[/u].
[u]Postulate II[/u]. [u]Everything[/u] (in the universe of [del]dyadics[/del] [ins]dichotomics[/ins]) is [u]either[/u]
[u]F[/u] or [u]V[/u].
