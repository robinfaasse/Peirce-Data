$$v - \frac{(v-p)(v-n)(v-g)}{(v-f)^2}$$

At this point, a signal improvement is almost forced upon us. It is one of the marvels of mathematics how if we proceed in a true mathematical style [del]advanced[/del] [ins]improvements[/ins] do force and new ideas do force themselves upon us. In the forms of copulation and disjunction which we have just obtained, the exponent of $(v-f)$ in the denominator depends upon the number of elements conjoined, being always one less than that number. But when we come to express the assertion that it rains, snows, and hails, at once we have
(1.8) $$f + \frac{(p-f)(n-f)(g-f)}{(v-f)^2} = v \text{ or } \frac{p-f}{v-f} \cdot \frac{n-f}{v-f} \cdot \frac{g-f}{v-f} = 1$$
where there [del]is no more[/del] [ins]is a factor of[/ins] the denominator for every one in the numerator, however many or few they may be. So to express the [u]denial[/u] of the proposition that it either rains, snows, or hails, we write
(1.9) $$v - \frac{(v-p)(v-n)(v-g)}{(v-f)^2} = f \text{ or } \frac{v-p}{v-f} \cdot \frac{v-n}{v-f} \cdot \frac{v-g}{v-f} = 1$$
