[del]By the ultimate [ins]consequent[/ins] I mean that whose place in the consequence is [ins]affirmatively[/ins] described without using the word antecedent;[/del] [ins]I shall usually mean that [ins]element[/ins] whose place in the consequence is [ins]affirmatively[/ins] described without using the word antecedent;[/ins] that is, it is either the consequent element, or the consequent element of the consequent, or the consequent element of the consequent of the consequent etc. [del]It is always an unenclosed letter; and there is only one ultimate consequent.[/del]
Thus in the consequence, [u](a < b < c) < d < e < f < (g < h)[/u] [fig]figure[/fig] [u]i[/u], [u]i[/u] is the ultimate consequent, [u]c[/u] the ultimate consequent of the antecedent, [u]h[/u] the ultimate consequent of the constituent [u]g < h[/u].

But when I speak of an ultimate constituent, I may conceive unnecessary enclosures to be used, and thus call, for example, [u]y < z[/u], or even [u]x < y < z[/u], the ultimate constituent of [u]w < x < y < z[/u].

By a [u]first antecedental element[/u], I mean either a constituent element not [del]the[/del] ultimate consequent [del]or[/del] [ins]proximate[/ins] the ultimate consequent of a constituent. Thus the first antecedental elements of the above consequence are [u]c[/u], [u]d[/u], [u]e[/u], [u]f[/u], [u]h[/u]. [fig]figure[/fig]
By a [u]first antecedental constituent[/u], I mean any proximate constituent other than the ultimate consequent.
