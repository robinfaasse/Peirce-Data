$\begin{matrix} \underline{x} = y & \underline{x} = y & \underline{x} = \bar{y} & \underline{x} = \bar{y} \\ \underline{x} = V & \underline{x} = F & \underline{x} = V & \underline{x} = F \\ \therefore y = V & \therefore y = F & \therefore y = F & \therefore y = V \end{matrix}$

Really the same as the last [del]two[/del] [ins]four[/ins], though they appear different owing to the imperfection of the notation use, are the following:

$\begin{matrix} \underline{x} = \bar{y} & \underline{x} = \bar{y} \\ y = F & y = V \\ \therefore \underline{x} = V & \therefore \underline{x} = F \end{matrix}$

The mathematician always extends and generalizes his notation. Why not write $\underline{x} = \overline{V}$ and $\underline{x} = \overline{F}$, [del]to give up[/del] [ins]which yield the following[/ins] inferential forms?

$\begin{matrix} \underline{x} = \overline{F} & \underline{x} = \overline{V} \\ \overline{F} = \overline{F} & \overline{V} = \overline{V} \\ \therefore \underline{x} = V & \therefore \underline{x} = F \end{matrix}$

The premisses $\overline{F} = \overline{F}$ and $\overline{V} = \overline{V}$ are no new postulates. For a postulate expresses in part the hypothesis of the system. But these are mere empty formulae arising from the superfluities of the notation. For $\underline{x} = \overline{F}$ is but a superfluous form for expressing $\underline{x} = V$; and $\underline{x} = \overline{V}$ of expressing $\underline{x} = V$.
