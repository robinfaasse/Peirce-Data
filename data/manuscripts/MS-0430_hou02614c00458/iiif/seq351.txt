A few corollaries from the above definitions may be set down at once.
(69) The letter [u]V[/u] is scriptible.
That is to say, by (66), it is applicable to that to which [u]V[/u] is applicable
(70) Any term, [u]x[/u], is equivalent to [u]V[/u] [u]−<[/u] [u]x[/u].
For, by (66), the signification of any term is that that which it represents is applicable to [u]V[/u]; and by (67), the signification of [u]V[/u] [u]−<[/u] [u]x[/u] is that [u]x[/u] is as applicable to [u]V[/u] as is [u]V[/u] itself.
(71) Whether a term [u]x[/u], be scriptible or not [u]x[/u] [u]−<[/u] [u]V[/u] is scriptible.
That is, by (67), [u]V[/u] is at least as scriptible as [u]x[/u]. But by (66), [u]V[/u] is scriptible.
(72) Whatever terms [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] may be, supposing [u]x[/u] [u]−<[/u] [u]y[/u] to be scriptible, if [u]x[/u] is scriptible, so is [u]y[/u].
This follows from (67), according to which [u]x[/u] [u]−<[/u] [u]y[/u] means that [del]if[/del] [u]y[/u] is at least as scriptible as [u]x[/u]. It makes no difference what the nature of scriptibility may be, or what the characters of [u]V[/u] may be.
