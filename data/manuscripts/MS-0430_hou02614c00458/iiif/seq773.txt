We now come to the vital definitions of the algebra,
as follows:
We say that an expression is [u]scriptible[/u], if, and only
if, it is permissible to write it so as to indicate that it
is, in some fixed sense, applicable to a certain recognizable
object, both definite and individual, denoted by [u]V[/u].
The sign [fig]figure[/fig], written between two expressions, makes
up an expression which is scriptible, if, and only if,
the following expression, or consequent, is fully as
scriptible as the preceding expression, or antecedent.
This definition is equivalent to the following three
clauses
(64) A. Either [u]m[/u] is scriptible or [u]m[/u][fig]figure[/fig][u]n[/u] is scriptible, whatever
expressions [u]m[/u] and [u]n[/u] may be.
(65) B. If [u]q[/u] is scriptible, then [u]p[/u][fig]figure[/fig][u]q[/u] is scriptible, whatever
expressions [u]p[/u] and [u]q[/u] may be.
(66) C If [u]s[/u][fig]figure[/fig][u]t[/u] is scriptible, then if [u]s[/u] is scriptible [u]t[/u] is
scriptible, whatever expressions [u]s[/u] and [u]t[/u] maybe.
