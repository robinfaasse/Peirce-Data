In the first place, the identity which they think so simple
is [u]individual identity[/u], whereas the sign [fig]$\equiv$[/fig] is the mark
of collective identity. They think that because percepts
are individual that logic must begin with individual
terms. But logic cannot go back to the percept. Logic
is a critical doctrine, and cannot penetrate further back
than criticism can go. It can imagine [del]a primitive[/del] [ins]an immediate[/ins] judg-
ment about a present percept; but it has no forms to
take account of [del]criticize[/del] the process of formation of that
judgment and to criticize its justness. That judgment
has a predicate which is already general; and though its
subject is an individual, yet it may be regarded as general
so long as it remains a subject. Logic thus begins with
generals; and this could be proved in other ways. For exam-
ple, there can be no logic or idea of forming a logic
until there is some notion of an inference; for logic has
for its principal [del]aim[/del] [ins]and[/ins] primary aim the criticism of inference.
