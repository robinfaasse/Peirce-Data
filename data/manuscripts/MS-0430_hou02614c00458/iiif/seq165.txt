[del]To prove[/del] [ins]Demonstrate[/ins] that [del]if[/del] from the assumption that if [u]x[/u] is scrip-
table then [u]y[/u] is scriptible it follows that [u]x[/u] + [u]y[/u] is scriptible,
it employs a proposition, that either [u]x[/u] is scriptible or
if [u]y[/u] is scriptible [u]x[/u] + [u]y[/u] is scriptible, which at first glance
would seem to be [del]utterly[/del] irrelevant. Yet it turns out to be
indispensible. The proof is as follows:

[del]If[/del] Let it be assumed that if [u]x[/u] is scriptible [u]y[/u] is scripti-
ble. We have to prove that, [u]v[/u] being both individual and
definite, it follows from that assumption that [u]x[/u] + [u]y[/u] is
scriptible. As before, we set out from (67), that
[del]Either[/del] [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u] distributively or [u]x[/u] + [u]y[/u] is
scriptible.

But, [u]v[/u] being individual, there is no distinction between
distributive and collective disjunction.

$\therefore$ Either [u]x[/u] is scriptible or [u]y[/u] is scriptible or [u]x[/u] + [u]y[/u] is
scriptible.

But the assumption, otherwise stated, is that if [u]x[/u]
or [u]y[/u] is scriptible, then [u]y[/u] is scriptible.
