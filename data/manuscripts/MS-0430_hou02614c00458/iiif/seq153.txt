If we introduce a Philonian conditional proposition,
which is equivalent to a collective disjunction of the denial
of its antecedent or assertion of its consequent, the above
rules will readily apply. Thus, to say that it is not blowing, but
that if it is raining it is snowing, is the same as to say that
if it is either blowing or raining it is snowing without blowing.
To say that sometimes it both blows and if it rains, snows, is
to say that it either sometimes blows without raining or
sometimes blows and snows. The following require some
