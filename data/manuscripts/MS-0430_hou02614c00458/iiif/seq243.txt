This sign, $\prec$, with no other, except symbols for different
propositions, gives rise to a valuable algebra, which I call
the Algebra of the Copula of Inclusion.
All the rules of this algebra are deducible from the defi-
nition of the sign $\prec$, which is as follows:
(64) If, [del]under[/del] [ins]in[/ins] any supposed state of things, a given proposition, $p$,
is not true unless a proposition, $q$, is true, then $p \prec q$ is true;
(65) and if [del]the pro[/del] $p \prec q$ is true, then $p$ is not true unless $q$ is true.
From (64) we have immediately
(66) If $q$ is true $p \prec q$ is true. That is, any antecedent may be prefixed.
(67) Either $p$ is true or $p \prec q$ is true.
From (65) we have immediately
(68) If $p$ and $(p \prec q)$ are true, $q$ is true.
