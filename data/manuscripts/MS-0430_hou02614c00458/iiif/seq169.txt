Theorem II. The consequence [u]x[/u][u]y[/u][u]z[/u] implies;
1[sup]st[/sup], that if [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are both scriptible, severally, then
[u]x[/u], [u]y[/u], and [u]z[/u] are [del]scriptible[/del] [ins]applicable[/ins] jointly to [u]v[/u]; and if [u]v[/u] is in-
dividual the converse is true, and this statement is equi-
valent to the consequence;

(81) 2[sup]nd[/sup], that if either [u]x[/u] or [u]z[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u] distribu-
tively, [del]while[/del] [ins]and if further[/ins] either [u]y[/u] or [u]z[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u] distribu-
(82) tively, then [u]z[/u] is scriptible; and if [u]v[/u] is definite, the converse
holds, and the statement is equivalent to the consequence;
(83) 3[sup]d[/sup], that if both [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] severally are scriptible, then
(84) [u]z[/u] is scriptible; and if [u]v[/u] is both individual and definite
the converse holds and the proposition is equivalent to
the algebraical consequence.

[del]Demonstration. In order to prove (79) we first assume
[u]x[/u][u]y[/u][u]z[/u] to be scriptible. Now a Philonian conditional
is true if its consequent is true. That is, for example,
granting that [u]z[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u], it follows that
if [u]y[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u] then [u]z[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u].[/del]
