This sign, [fig]symbol for copula of inclusion[/fig], with no other particularly noticeable, except the symbols for different propositions, gives rise to a valuable algebra called the Algebra of the Copula of Inclusion.*

*I first developed this algebra in 1880, American Journal of Mathematics, Vol. IV. pp 24-31.

Those who take up the study of logic because they are interested in religion, those who take up the study of logic because they are interested in psychology, those who take up the study of logic because they wish to learn how to argue a cause effectively, even those who study logic because they are interested in the theory of cognition, may well be at a loss to know what I can mean by calling this algebra valuable, or even interesting. It is true that from their exterior stand-points it seems uninviting. It is a leaden casket. So, to a child, is any study to which he does not take kindly. "Ma, what is the use of jogafy?"
