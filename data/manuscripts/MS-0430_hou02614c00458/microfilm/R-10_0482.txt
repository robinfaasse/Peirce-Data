[u]The Vital Definitions of the Algebra of the Copula.[/u]

I. The letter [u]v[/u] denotes any given object that is
[u]a[/u]. definite and
[u]b[/u]. individual.

II. When a term is written upon a certain sheet of asser-
tions, it is asserted, as we shall say, to be [u]scriptible[/u], that
is to be applicable to [u]v[/u]. The signification of any term is its signi-
fication when [del]asserted[/del] [ins]called[/ins].

III. The sign [ins][fig]copula symbol[/fig][/ins], or the Copula of Inclusion, signifies
that the [u]consequent[/u], or term written to the right of it, is
at least, as applicable to [u]v[/u], as the [u]antecedent[/u], or term
written to the left of it. In other words, to assert the con-
sequence, which is composed of antecedent, copula, and
consequent, united, is to assert that if the antecedent
is scriptible, so is the consequent.

IV. The term [u]x [fig]copula symbol[/fig] y [fig]copula symbol[/fig] z[/u], whatever [ins]terms[/ins] [u]x[/u], [u]y[/u], and [u]z[/u] may
be, is equivalent to the term [u]x [fig]copula symbol[/fig] (y [fig]copula symbol[/fig] z)[/u].

A few corollaries from these definitions may be
set down at once.
