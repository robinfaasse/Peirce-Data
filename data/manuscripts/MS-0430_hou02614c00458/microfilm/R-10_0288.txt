[u]The Vital Definitions of the Algebra of the Copula.[/u]

I. The letter [u]v[/u] may be taken as [del]any noun[/del] [ins]provided that,[/ins]
[u]a[/u]. it is [del]not general[/del] [ins]individual[/ins], That is to say, when it is the subject of
a proposition, the interpreter is allowed no latitude of choice in
finding an instance to which the subject applies, as he [del]is[/del] [ins]has[/ins], for example,
if the subject is '[del]any man[/del] [ins]human being[/ins]', in which case, if he can find a single man
of whom the predicate is not true, he refutes the proposition. 'Any
human being is either male or female' may be true, though it be not
true either that 'Any human being is male' or that 'Any human being
is female'. To say that [u]v[/u] is individual, is to assert that the [u]principle[/u]
[u]of excluded middle[/u] applies to propositions concerning it, i.e. that,
whatever [u]x[/u] may be, either [u]v[/u] is [u]x[/u] or [u]v[/u] is not [u]x[/u].
