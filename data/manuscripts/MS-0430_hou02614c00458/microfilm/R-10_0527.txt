The looser disjunction is distributive

Disjunction is distributive with reference to
copulation, and vice versa, if both [del]operations[/del] [ins]combinations[/ins] are
[del]loose or[/del] [ins]when both[/ins] are close. [del]To[/del] To say that it always either blows
or [del]blows[/del] rains and snows, is equivalent to saying that it
always either blows or rains and always either blows or
snows. That is distributive disjunction is distributive
with reference to several copulations. So, to say that it
sometimes both blows and either rains or snows is the
same as to say that it [ins]either[/ins] sometimes blows and rains
or sometimes [del]either[/del] [ins]both[/ins] blows and snows. That is, joint copu-
lation is distributive with reference to collective distribu-
tion.

[marginalia]the negative of a close disjunction is a close copulation,
and vice versa; and so it is likewise with the loose
combinations.[/marginalia]
