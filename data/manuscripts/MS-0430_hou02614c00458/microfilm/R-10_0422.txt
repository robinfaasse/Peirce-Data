57[/pn]

The following rules hold, whatever propositions $x, y, z, t$ may be.

(69) $x \prec x$

For if $x$ is true, it holds by (66); if $x$ is false, by (67). This is the
[u]Principle of Identity[/u].

(70) If $x \prec y \prec z$ is true, then $y \prec x \prec z$ is true.

For if $x \prec y \prec z$ is true, by (65), if $x$ is true, $y \prec z$ is true;
and [del]by[/del] [ins]again[/ins] by (65), if $x$ is true, then if $y$ is true, $z$ is true.
That is, if $x$ and $y$ are true, $z$ is true. But by (64), $x$ being
true if $y$ is true, $y \prec x$ is true; and again, by (64), $x \prec z$ being
true if $y$ is true, $y \prec x \prec z$ is true [ins]Thus (70) is proved.[/ins]. This may be called the Rule
of Transposition. Namely, antecedents of one consequent can be
transposed.

(71) $(x \prec y \prec z) \prec y \prec x \prec z$.

This follows from (70), by (64)

(72) $y \prec x \prec y$

This follows from (66) by (64). It also follows from (69) by (66) and (70).
