To say that both [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are applicable to [u]v[/u] severally,
is to say that [u]x[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u] and that [u]y[/u] is applicable
to [u]v[/u]. To say that both [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are applicable to [u]v[/u] jointly,
is to say that there is a definite instance which, by reason
of the indefiniteness of [u]v[/u], [del]the utterer of the proposition[/del] [ins]would justify[/ins] and
to which both [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] apply. The distinction only exists
when [u]v[/u] is indefinite. If [u]v[/u] is 'sometimes', then to say that
both 'it rains' and 'it blows' are applicable to [u]v[/u] severally is
to say that it sometimes rains and sometimes blows. To say
that the copulation is applicable jointly, is to say that sometime
it rains and blows at once.

It will be remarked that since [u]v[/u] [ins]must[/ins] be either indivi-
dual or definite (since the choice of instances cannot be
governed both by the defender and the opponent of a proposition)
both distinctions cannot exist in reference to [u]v[/u].
