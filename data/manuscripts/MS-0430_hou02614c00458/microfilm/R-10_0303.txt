But [u]v[/u] being definite, there is no distinction between the several and joint copulation.

[u]∴[/u] It follows from the assumption that either both [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are applicable to [u]v[/u] jointly, or else [u]x[/u] [ins]and[/ins] [u]y[/u] is scriptible.

But, by (68), if both [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are applicable to [u]v[/u] jointly, then [u]x[/u] [ins]and[/ins] [u]y[/u] is scriptible.

[u]∴[/u] It follows from the assumption that [u]x[/u] [ins]and[/ins] [u]y[/u] is scriptible. Q.E.D.

That if [u]x[/u] [ins]and[/ins] [u]y[/u] is scriptible, then if [u]x[/u] is scriptible then [u]y[/u] is scriptible is shown almost immediately. For if [u]x[/u] is scriptible [del]i.e. applicable to v,[/del] then either [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u] distributively. Now, by (70), if [u]x[/u] [ins]and[/ins] [u]y[/u] is scriptible and either [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u] distributively, then [u]y[/u] is scriptible.

The same thing could be proved by (71), with the fact, that if [ins]both[/ins] [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are applicable to [u]v[/u] jointly, then [u]y[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u].
