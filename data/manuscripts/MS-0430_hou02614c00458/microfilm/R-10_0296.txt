The denial of a disjunction is a copulation and
vice versa. The denial of a close combination is a close
combination, and [del]of[/del] [ins]that[/ins] a loose combination is loose.

The combination of $x$ with the combination of $y$ and
$z$, where both combinations are disjunctions or both are
copulations, and where both are close or both are loose,
is equivalent to the direct combination of $x$, $y$, $z$ in the
same manner.

$z$, where [del]the[/del] [ins]it is[/ins] A-combination [ins]is loose[/ins] and the B-combination is
[del]either both close or both loose[/del] [ins]so also[/ins], but where either is a disjunction
and the other a copulation, is equivalent to an A-combination
of two B-combinations, the one of $x$ and $y$, the other of $x$
and $z$. It is so [del]essential[/del] [ins]desirable[/ins], not merely for the purposes of
this algebra, but for reasoning about matters of complexity,
that the reader should be well skilled in these relations, that
[del]I[/del] shall examples of them are desirable.
