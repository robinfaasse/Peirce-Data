Now this is so, irrespective of what the expressions
[del]may[/del] [ins]which[/ins] are designated by the dragon's head ([fig]dragon's head symbol[/fig])
and the dragon's tail ([fig]dragon's tail symbol[/fig]). It will necessarily be so in every
case. Thus, if the proposition holds for all consequences
having fewer than any given number, N, of proximate
antecedentals, it is [ins]hold,[/ins] good for every proposition having
no more than N, proximate antecedentals, and there-
fore for every proposition having a number of proximate
antecedentals that can be reached by successive addi-
tions of unity to N. But it is identically true when there
is no antecedental, and is true by (64) when there is but
one. Hence, it is true for any finite number of ante-
cedentals, that is, for any consequence which can be
written out; for the writing out has to be done antecedental
by antecedental.

This mode of reasoning about finite collections,
called Fermat's reasoning, need not be stated at full length.
