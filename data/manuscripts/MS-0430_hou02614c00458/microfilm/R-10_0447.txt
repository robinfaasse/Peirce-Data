[u]Propositions deduced from B.[/u]

Using subscript numbers simply to distinguish $P_1$, $P_2$, etc. as having different significations,

(69) If $x$ is $q$, then it is also $P_1 \prec q$ and $P_2 \prec P_1 \prec q$ and $P_3 \prec P_2 \prec P_1 \prec q$, and so on, for any finite number of $P$'s.

For, by (65) if $x$ is $q$, it is $P_1 \prec q$; and if $x$ is $P_1 \prec q$, it is $P_2 \prec P_1 \prec q$. And if we write $Y_1$ for $P_1 \prec q$, $Y_2$ for $P_2 \prec Y_1$, and in general $Y_n$ for $P_n \prec Y_{n-1}$, then if $x$ is $Y_n$ other, whatever integer $n$ may be, $x$ is $Y_{n+1}$, consequently if $x$ is $q$, it is $Y_n$ whatever number $n$ may be, and whatever characters $P_1$, $P_2$, $P_3$, etc. may be.
