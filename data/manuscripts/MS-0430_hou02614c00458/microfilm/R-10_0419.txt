[pn](86)[/pn] If $([x \prec y] \prec t) \prec z$ is scriptible, so is $x \prec (y \prec t) \prec z$.

For by (83), to assert that [del]is[/del] $([x \prec y] \prec t) \prec z$ is scriptible
is equivalent to asserting that both $t \prec z$ and $([x \prec y] \prec z) \prec z$
are scriptible, the latter being, by (85), equivalent to asserting
that $x \prec (y \prec z) \prec z$ is scriptible. This last is, by (82), equi-
valent to asserting that either $x \prec z$ or $y \prec z$ is scriptible.
Thus to assert that $([x \prec y] \prec t) \prec z$ is scriptible, is to assert
that either both $t \prec z$ and $x \prec z$ or else both $t \prec z$ and
$(y \prec z) \prec z$ are scriptible.

To assert that $x \prec (y \prec t) \prec z$ is scriptible is, by (82),
equivalent to asserting that either $x \prec z$ [ins]is scriptible,[/ins] or $(y \prec t) \prec z$
is scriptible. And the [del]latter alternative[/del] [ins]both assertions[/ins] is equivalent, by (85),
to asserting that $t \prec z$ and $(y \prec z) \prec z$ are scriptible. Thus,
to assert that $x \prec (y \prec t) \prec z$ is scriptible is to assert that
either $x \prec z$ is scriptible or that both $t \prec z$ and $(y \prec z) \prec z$
are scriptible. But this and more is [del]asserted[/del] [ins]implied[/ins]
in saying that $([x \prec y] \prec t) \prec z$ is scriptible. Hence, (86).
