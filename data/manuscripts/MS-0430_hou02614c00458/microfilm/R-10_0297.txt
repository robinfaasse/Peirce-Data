To say that it is blowing and is either raining or snowing
is to say that it is either [del]both[/del] blowing and raining or both
blowing and snowing. To say that it is either blowing or both
raining and snowing is to say that it is either blowing or raining
and is either blowing or snowing.

To say that it sometimes both blows and either rains or
snows, where there can be no distinction between distribu-
tive and collective disjunction, as above explained, means
that it either sometimes both blows and rains or sometimes
both blows and snows. To say that it always either blows
or both rains and snows, where there can be no distinction
between joint and several copulation, means that it always
either blows or rains and also always either blows or snows.

while the second member would be verified if it blew on every day
[del]but[/del] [ins]except[/ins] Monday, and then snowed: but the only way in which
they can both be true is by its both raining and snowing
whenever it fails to blow.
