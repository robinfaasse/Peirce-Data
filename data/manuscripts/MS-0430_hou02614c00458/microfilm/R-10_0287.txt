By a [u]simple consequence[/u] shall be meant a consequence of which the antecedent and consequent are both elements; as, [u]a[/u] < [u]b[/u]. A consequence not simple is said to be complex.

By a [ins]standard[/ins] [del]prime[/del] consequence shall be meant a consequence of which each [del]proximate[/del] element is either an element or a simple consequence of which the consequent is the same letter as the [del]other[/del] [ins]standard[/ins] ultimate; as,

[u]a[/u] < ([u]b[/u] < [u]c[/u]) < < ([del][u]d[/u][/del] [ins][u]d[/u][/ins] < [u]c[/u]) < [u]c[/u].

The other letters of the algebra except [u]v[/u] shall be termed the ordinary letters.

We are now [ins]prepared[/ins] [del]ready[/del] for the formal exposition of the algebra, the purpose of which is in part to discover the properties of the relation of consequence, or inclusion, which is the fundamental relation of logic; but more especially, to show in what way those properties depend upon one another.
