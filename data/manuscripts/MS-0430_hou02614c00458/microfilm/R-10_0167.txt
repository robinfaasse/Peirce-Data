I will next prove that if an unenclosed part of a graph
is iterated within one enclosure, the [del]denoted[/del] [ins]inner[/ins] occur-
rence of it can be erased. That is, if the graph AB[ins]([/ins]CA[ins])[/ins] can
be written, so can AB[ins]([/ins]C[ins])[/ins]. For the former asserts that A is V,
B is V, but that not both C and A are V. It follows that C is not
V. Now all that the second graph asserts is that A is V, B is V, and
C is not V. Q.E.D.

I will next prove that if an unenclosed part of a graph is
iterated under any odd number of enclosures, the inner occur-
rence of it can be erased. For we have just seen that an unen-
closed part of a graph can be iterated within any even num-
ber of enclosures. It can therefore be iterated just outside
the inmost enclosure of the iteration within odd enclosures,
and then by Rule III applied to what has just been proved,
the inmost iteration can be erased, after which by Rules I
and III, the evenly enclosed occurrence can be erased.
