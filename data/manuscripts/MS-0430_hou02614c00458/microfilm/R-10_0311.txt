[u]Theorem III[/u]. The [del]algebraical[/del] consequence, [del]($x$[/del] ($x \prec y$) $\prec z$, having a compound antecedent is equivalent to [del]a[/del] several copulation of two propositions, as follows:

(86) 1[sup]st[/sup], when $v$ is individual, to either $x$ or $z$ is scriptible, and $x \prec z$, where $x$ is equivalent to the joint copulation of $x$ and $y$;

(87) 2[sup]nd[/sup], when $v$ is definite, to either [del]$y$ or[/del] [ins]the distributive disjunction of $x$ or $y$, or else[/ins] $z$ is applicable to $v$ [del]distributively[/del], and $y \prec z$;

(88) 3[sup]rd[/sup], when $v$ is both individual and definite, to either $x$ or $z$ is scriptible, and $y \prec z$.

[u]Demonstration[/u]. When $v$ is individual, ($x \prec y$) $\prec z$ is, by (74), equivalent to the proposition, "If $x \prec y$ is scriptible, then jointly both $x \prec y$ and $z$ are applicable to $v$," or either $z$ is [del]applicable to $v$[/del] [ins]scriptible[/ins] but not $x$ and $y$ jointly [ins]or[/ins] both $z$ and either not $x$ or jointly both $x$ and $y$. Or, since the joint copulation of an affirmative and negative is not distinguishable from the several, either $x$ is applicable to $v$ but not both $x$ and $y$ jointly, or $z$ is applicable but not $x$, or $x, y, z$ are all applicable.
