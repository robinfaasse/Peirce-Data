The geometer supposes that the line itself moves. That is
quite analogous to saying that [u]x[/u] having been subjected to
an operation of addition, the result (or the operation, for
the difference is not here important) is now subjected to
another operation, say multiplication. To express that,
parentheses are employed, thus: [del]([/del][ins]{[/ins][u]x[/u] + [u]a[/u][del])[/del][ins]}[/ins] < [u]b[/u]. An
algebra without a sign of this import would be mathe-
matically impotent.

In the algebra of the copula, we shall have to deal
with expressions of the form [u]a[/u] < [u]b[/u]. Any such
expression shall be termed an [u]algebraical consequence[/u].
Its [u]antecedent[/u] is [u]a[/u]; its [u]consequent[/u], [u]b[/u]. The antecedent
may itself be a consequence; and this will be indi-
cated by enclosing it in parentheses, thus: ([u]a[/u] < [u]b[/u]) < [u]c[/u].
The consequent may also be a consequence, and,
if so, we may use parentheses and write [u]a[/u] < ([u]b[/u] < [u]c[/u]).
