The second office of the ovals is that of associating the
conjunctions of terms. By this I mean, for example,
distinguishing between such assertions as
"This man is [ins]either[/ins] at once civilized and a redskin or is a negro,"
and "This man is at once civilized and either a redskin or a negro."
This is the office of parentheses in algebra. In Boole's logi-
cal algebra, for instance, if we put T for "this man", [u]c[/u] for
"civilized", [u]r[/u] for "redskin", and [u]n[/u] for "negro", the above
propositions may be written
T [u]<[/u] ([u]c[/u] x [u]r[/u]) + [u]n[/u]
T [u]<[/u] [u]c[/u] x ([u]r[/u] + [u]n[/u]).
The ovals are able to combine these offices because the last does
