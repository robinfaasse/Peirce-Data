[pn](80)[/pn] [u]Theorem II[/u]. The consequence [del]x[/del] [ins]x[/ins] y [del]~[/del] [ins]~[/ins] z implies;

1st, that if x and y are both scriptible, severally, then x, y, and z are [del]acceptable[/del] [ins]applicable[/ins] jointly to v; and if v is individual the converse is true, and this statement is equivalent to the consequence;

[pn](81)[/pn] 2nd, that if either x or z is applicable to v distributively, [ins]and if further[/ins] [del]while[/del] either y or z is applicable to v distributively, then z is scriptible; and if v is definite, the converse holds, and the statement is equivalent to the consequence;

[pn](82)[/pn]

[pn](83)[/pn] 3rd, that if both x and y severally are scriptible, then z is scriptible; and if v is both individual and definite the converse holds and the proposition is equivalent to the algebraical consequence.

[pn](84)[/pn] [u]Demonstration[/u]. In order to prove [del](79)[/del] we first assume x y ~ z to be scriptible. Now a Philonian conditional is true if its consequent is true. That is, for example, granting that z is applicable to v, it follows that if y is applicable to v then z is applicable to v.
