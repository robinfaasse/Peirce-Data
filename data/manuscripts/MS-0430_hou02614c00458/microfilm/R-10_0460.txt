In the above definition, the relation signified by
[fig]curly bracket[/fig] is interpreted by the three conjunctions, [u]if[/u], [u]and[/u], [u]or[/u].
The significations of these are left unanalyzed. We have
to trust to our natural reason to pronounce upon what
logically follows from, — that is, is involved in, — such
interpretations; and this we may safely do, since if, the
only one of the three which could lead to error, is here,
[ins]as[/ins] we shall come to see later, used in a simplified
sense, in which the intricacies of the ordinary "if" disap-
pear. [del]But[/del] It is enough for the mathematician to be
assured of his conclusions; but this is not enough for
the student of the logic of mathematics. He wishes to
know what the nature of the logical sequence is. To
gain a distinct conception of the essence of this wish
will form a serious task to be entered upon later.
