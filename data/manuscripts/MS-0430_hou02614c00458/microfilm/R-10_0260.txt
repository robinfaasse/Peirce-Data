This must be the case, since we have, whatever the values of

(36) $1 - (x + y) = (1-x)(1-y)$.
Hence, any general [del]formula[/del] [ins]equation[/ins], when both sides are negatived
will simply, interchange arithmetical progression and mul-
tiplication, and interchange $0$ and $1$. True, all the letters will
be changed to their negatives; but that makes no difference, since
the general equation is supposed to be true in all cases. Here fol-
low a few such general equations arranged, as Schröder first
arranged them in his [u]Operationskreis[/u], in 1877:

(37) $x \cdot x = x$
$\bar{x} + \bar{x} = \bar{x}$

(38) $x + \bar{x} = 1$
$\bar{x} \cdot \bar{\bar{x}} = 0$

(39) $(x + y) + z = x + (y + z)$
$(\bar{x} \cdot \bar{y}) \cdot \bar{z} = \bar{x} \cdot (\bar{y} \cdot \bar{z})$

(40) $x + y = y + x$
$\bar{x} \cdot \bar{y} = \bar{y} \cdot \bar{x}$

(41) $(x + y) \cdot z = x \cdot z + y \cdot z$
$(\bar{x} \cdot \bar{y}) + \bar{z} = (\bar{x} + \bar{z}) \cdot (\bar{y} + \bar{z})$

(42) $(x + y) \cdot (x + \bar{y}) = x$
$(\bar{x} \cdot \bar{y}) + (\bar{x} \cdot \bar{\bar{y}}) = \bar{x}$
