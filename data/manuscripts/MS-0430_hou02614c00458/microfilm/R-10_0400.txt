[pn](71)[/pn] It follows as a corollary [del]that[/del] from the last two propositions
that the proximate antecedental constituents of a conse-
quence can be transposed in any way, without affecting
its scriptibility. This is called the rule of transposition.

[pn](72)[/pn] If [u]x[/u]~[u]y[/u] and [u]y[/u]~[u]z[/u] are scriptible, then [u]x[/u]~[u]z[/u] is
scriptible.

For, by (64), either [u]x[/u] or [u]x[/u]~[u]z[/u] is scriptible. But
if [u]x[/u] and [u]x[/u]~[u]y[/u] are scriptible, by (66), [u]y[/u] is scriptible;
and if [u]y[/u] and [u]y[/u]~[u]z[/u] are scriptible, again by (66), [u]z[/u] is
scriptible; and if [u]z[/u] is scriptible, by (65) [u]x[/u]~[u]z[/u] is
scriptible. Therefore, in any case, (72) holds.
