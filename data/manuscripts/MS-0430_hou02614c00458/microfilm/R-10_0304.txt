[del]Demonstrate[/del] [ins]To prove[/ins] that if from the assumption that [u]x[/u] is scriptible then [u]y[/u] is scriptible it follows that [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is scriptible, it employs a proposition, that either [u]x[/u] is scriptible or if [u]y[/u] is scriptible [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is scriptible, which at first glance would seem to be utterly irrelevant, yet is turns out to be indispensable. The proof is as follows:

[del]If[/del] Let it be assumed that if [u]x[/u] is scriptible [u]y[/u] is scriptible. We have to prove that, [u]v[/u] being both individual and definite, it follows from that assumption that [u]v[/u] or [u]y[/u] is scriptible. As before, we set out from (67), that

Either [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is applicable to [u]v[/u] distributively or [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is scriptible.

But, [u]v[/u] being individual, there is no distinction between distributive and collective disjunction.

[u]..[/u] Either [u]x[/u] is scriptible or [u]y[/u] is scriptible or [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is scriptible.

But the assumption, otherwise stated, is that if [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is scriptible, then [u]y[/u] is scriptible.
