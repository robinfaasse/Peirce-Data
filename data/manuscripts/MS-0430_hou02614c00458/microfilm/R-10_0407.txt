(81) The expressions [u](x ~ y) ~ y[/u] and [u](y ~ x) ~ x[/u] are equivalent.
For, by (79) and (80), if (x ~ y) ~ y is scriptible, so is
(y ~ x) ~ x. And, therefore, the converse is true. Whence,
by (68), we have [u]((x ~ y) ~ y) ~ (y ~ x) ~ x[/u] and
[u]((y ~ x) ~ x) ~ (x ~ y) ~ y[/u]; and, by (77), (81) holds.

(82) If (x ~ y) ~ z is scriptible, so is (x ~ z) ~ y.
For, (x ~ y) ~ z being scriptible, by (60), if x ~ y is
scriptible, so is z. But, by (64), either x ~ y is scriptible
or x is scriptible. Hence, if (x ~ y) ~ z is scrip-
tible, either z or x is scriptible; when, by (80), (x ~ z) ~ y
is scriptible.

(83) If (x ~ y) ~ z is scriptible, y ~ z is scriptible.
For, by (65), [del]if[/del] [ins]if[/ins] y is scriptible, x ~ y is scrip-
tible; when, by (66), (x ~ y) ~ z being scriptible z is
scriptible. Thence, if (x ~ y) ~ z is scriptible, then if y is
scriptible z is scriptible; and, by (68), (83) follows.
