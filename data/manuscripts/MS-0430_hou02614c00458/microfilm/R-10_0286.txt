[u]ponding and alike[/u].

Two terms are said to be equivalent [ins]for the purposes of this algebra[/ins] if either can replace the other in any position [del]in an algebraical consequence without affecting the scriptibility thereof.[/del] [ins]if[/ins] they are equivalent by virtue of the general definitions of the algebra, they are said to be [u]necessarily equivalent[/u].

An entire consequence is said to be [u]necessarily equivalent[/u] to a collection of other consequences, [ins]and only if[/ins] [del]if[/del] from the scriptibility of it follows [ins]would necessarily follow[/ins] that of each of those others, while from the scriptibility of all of these, the scriptibility of that one follows. We shall see reason to regard the single consequences of the collection as [del]factors of the first consequence.[/del] [ins]consequences of the first consequence.[/ins]

By an [u]element[/u] of a consequence is meant a constituent which has itself no other constituents than itself. [del]Element[/del] An element is a letter, except that its identity consists of its place in the consequence, while that of a letter consists of its quality as a sign. Thus, the consequence [u]x[/u] [u]y[/u] < [u]x[/u] has three elements but only two letters.
