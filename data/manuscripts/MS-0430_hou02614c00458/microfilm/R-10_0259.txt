The first seems the most obvious and simple; [del]and[/del] [ins]for[/ins] nobody
has adopted it. Jevons was the first to show the need of a simple
sign of aggregation in Booleian algebra; and I conceive that it is
our moral duty of acknowledge our obligation to him for this
proposal by employing the sign which he proposed for that
purpose, only so far modified as is necessary. This sign was
⊹. But this is liable to be taken for three characters. I therefore
join the two dots ✛, continuing distinctly to recognize it as
Jevons's sign. Although Jevons proposed this as a sign of the
logical conjunction [del]aggregation[/del] [ins]and not of an arithmetical operation,[/ins]
yet since he used the sign of multiplication, as Boole did,
[del]to represent[/del] [ins]for[/ins] composition, I will use [del]the[/del] [ins]the Greek letter[/ins] $\Psi$, as the mark of
the arithmetical operation, [del]so that[/del] defined by the equation

[pn](35)[/pn] [del]$x \Psi y = 1 - (1-x)(1-y)$.[/del] [ins]$x \Psi y = 1 - (1-x)(1-y)$.[/ins]
We may call this operation numerical aggregation.

Then, as I first showed in [date]1867[/date], we shall have a perfect
balance between multiplication and arithmetical aggregation.
