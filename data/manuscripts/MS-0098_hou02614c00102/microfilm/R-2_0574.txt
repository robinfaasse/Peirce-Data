[u]The Axioms of Geometry.[/u]

Definition. If a [del]proper[/del] point is designated by a letter, or other wise, [del]and is[/del] not defined by its relation to other points, it is said to be arbitrarily designated. Thus if I speak of the middle point between A and B, I define this middle point and arbitrarily designate A and B.

Definition. If a certain geometrical proposition requires [ins]only[/ins] a certain point or points to be [del]arbitrarily[/del] designated in its enunciation, it is, [del]if[/del] true, a property of [del]that point or system of points[/del] [ins]the designated points[/ins].

[del]Definition[/del] [ins]Axiom[/ins]. All points have the same properties. That is, every geometrical proposition which only arbitrarily designates a single point[del], is[/del] [ins]A,[/ins] is equally true if for A be substituted B or any other letter designating a point.

Axiom. The same thing is true of all pairs of points.

Scholium. To say that a distance has a certain measure is not a property of [del]that line only[/del] [ins]the[/ins] [del]measured only[/del] [ins]two points[/ins], because it implicitly refers to a standard of measure.
