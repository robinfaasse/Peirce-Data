[u]The Axioms of[/u]

[u]Axiom 1[/u]. All the points of space have the same properties. Any pro-
position which can be enunciated without the arbitrary designation of but
one point, A, is said to express a property of that point. Then, the truth
of such proposition does not depend on what point A denotes.

[u]Axiom 2[/u]. All pairs of points have the same properties.

[u]Corollary[/u]. The sum of the three angles of a triangle is equal to two right an-
gles. For it is well-known that this is the case unless different lengths have
different properties.

[u]Axiom 3[/u]. Space has three dimensions. That is to say, three numbers are sufficient
to define the position of a point with reference to four known points.

[u]Axiom 4[/u]. Space permits parallel motion. Two systems of points A, B, C etc
and A', B', C' etc. are said to be [del]part[/del] similar when every proposition which can
be expressed without arbitrarily designating any points except those of the first
series, holds equally true when the corresponding designations of the points
in the second series are substituted everywhere for those of the first series.
Equality in the relations of points is a relation such that 1[ins]st[/ins], if A equals B,
B equals A; 2[ins]nd[/ins], if A equals B and C equals D, then A equals C and B equals D;
and 3[ins]rd[/ins], there are not two points whose relation to one third are equal. A
system of points A, B, C is said to receive a parallel motion, when the points
are gradually changed the new set A', B', C' being always similar to the
first and the relations of A to A', B to B', etc. being all equal. Geometry
supposes that there is some means direct or indirect of recognizing paral-
lel motion.
