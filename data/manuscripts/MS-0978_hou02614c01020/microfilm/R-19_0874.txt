[u]Order of Plato's Dialogues[/u]

The stylistic evidence makes the Apology altogether the earliest of the 23 dialogues considered. [del]It[/del] It was probably written before the removal (journey is not the word) of Plato to Megara. (Death of Socrates [date]399 B.C.[/date])

The Charmides, Laches, Euthydemus, Protagoras, Republic I, and Meno display a steady development of style. The Meno and even the Republic I were in all probability subsequent to the Charmides or even the Laches. The stylistically most probable order is that given. The allusion to the bribery of Ismenias shows that the Meno was written [date]395 B.C.[/date] or later. Very likely these six dialogues were written at Megara. [ins]It is supposed that the Euthydemus which seems to refer to the discourse of Isocrates against the sophists was written about [date]390 B.C.[/date] But it appears rather doubtful what the date of that discourse was.[/ins]

Plato now made his travels and underwent adventures, and the Academy was suddenly founded [date]387 B.C.[/date] He naturally would at once set to work to prepare a number of dialogues for instruction in this new school. Now the Gorgias, Cratylus, Republic X, and Symposium are [del]the[/del] almost identical in their stylistic development. The Republic X must, however, have been revised when the whole Republic was put together, and therefore it may probably now appear somewhat later than its first draught. The Symposium, owing to its mention of the recent partition of Mantinea must have been written [del]about[/del] [ins]385 or[/ins] [date]384 B.C.[/date] This agrees with what I have [del]seen[/del] urged above. The allusion in the Cratylus to the early closing of the gates of Aegina is regarded as evidence that this dialogue cannot have been written before [date]387 B.C.[/date] The Gorgias is supposed to contain a reference to the discourse of Isocrates to Nicocles, the date of which is supposed to be [date]376 B.C.[/date] But both data are very uncertain.

Of later and progressively later stylistic development are the Phaedo, Republic VIII, IX and Republic II, III, IV. These dialogues were probably written at leisure [del]say between 380 B.C. and 370 B.C.[/del] but probably all before [date]380 B.C.[/date]

Of decidedly later stylistic development and nearly contemporary are the concluding part of the Republic V. VI. VII, containing the allegory of the Cave, and the Phaedrus. The Phaedrus seems to contain a reference to the Republic as already published; and Thomson had shown that the Phaedrus was written between [date]380 B.C.[/date] and [date]378 B.C.[/date]
