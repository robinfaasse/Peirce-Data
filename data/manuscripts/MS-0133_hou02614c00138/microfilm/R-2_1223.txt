<u>Definition</u>. <u>Coplanar</u> means contained in one plane. It is applied to a collection of two or more rays with or without points exterior to them, to a collection of a ray with two or more points, or to a collection of four or more points.

<u>Definition</u>. <u>Coradial</u> means contained in or containing one ray. It is applied to [del]the[/del] collection of three or more points, to a collection of three or more planes, or to a collection of points and planes amounting to three or more. It might be convenient to extend the meaning of this word so as to [del]embrace[/del] [ins]apply[/ins] to five [ins]or more[/ins] rays cut by one ray, or to a fewer number cutting the common ray of certain points and planes.

<u>Definition</u>. In some books, the word <u>axis</u> is used for a ray considered as the intersection of planes, which are said to be <u>coaxal</u> (or, less properly, <u>coaxial</u>). The word <u>range</u> is used for a ray considered as containing points, In other books the word <u>ray</u> is used in that sense. Points in one ray are in many books called <u>collinear</u>, and rays through one point <u>concurrent</u>. [del]These terms are not used in this book.[/del]

<u>Definition</u>. <u>Copunctual</u> means containing one point, as two or more rays, or four or more planes, etc.
