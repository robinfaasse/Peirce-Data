[u]Peripatetic Talks. No. 2.[/u]

We observe in experience three categories of elements:
[u]1[sup]st[/sup][/u], [u]Feeling-qualities[/u], each of which is [u]sui generis[/u], and is all that it is as soon as there is an idea of it or possibility of it. It is what it is in itself without reference to anything else. It is absolutely simple. It has no individual identity, but is other than another [del]feeling[/del] quality so far as it is different from it. I call its character [u]Firstness[/u], or [del]Monadism[/del].
[u]2[sup]nd[/sup][/u], [u]Will-reactions[/u], each of which subsists between two subjects, and is absolutely here and now. It is a blind exertion of force, without reason, root, or generality. It cannot exist without being opposed: the opposition to it is its life. It is seen equally in will and in sense-impression. We also see similar dyadic relations between pairs both of whose members are outside not only our consciousness but even our sympathy. Thus we see solid bodies in states of stress. I call all this [u]Secondness[/u], or [u]Dyadism[/u]. It is the character of a pair of subjects.
[u]3[sup]rd[/sup][/u], [u]Habit-representations[/u], each of which involves three subjects, the sign, the object, and the sign which [del]is[/del] interprets the former sign. There are some cases in which there is no sign which come under this category. Thus, A causes B to exert a force upon C.
