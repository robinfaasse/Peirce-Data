Prag. 10. Distinctions have to be drawn. [ured]23[/ured] It has been usual to distinguish the immediate object, or the object as it is represented in the sign, from the real object, or the the object as it really is. In like manner we must distinguish three 'meanings,' the 'interpretants,' to use my ordinary word. There [del]is[/del] [ins]are, first,[/ins] the meaning as it is expressed in the sign; second, the meaning, as it is, in fact, produced by the sign; and thirdly, the meaning, as the ultimate logical result that deliberate reason ought to draw from the sign. Finer distinctions than these [ins]I can plainly see[/ins] [del]will, certainly[/del] be required when logic gets to be more closely studied. [ured]24[/ured] Mean-times the effect of a general sign upon conduct must itself be general. As it is expressed in the sign, it obviously is so; [ured]25[/ured] and as it results in conduct, it is a habit; and a habit is a general mode of action. In its ultimate logical form, it will be a general principle or law.
