Book 6. Campbell weighed heavily upon me, and I had not yet come
to perceive that if philosophy is ever to enter the ranks of the sciences,
literary elegance must give way to plainly saying what has to be
said, and that writers who have new concepts of a scientifically
exact kind to express must be encouraged, nay, required to coin
the new words that may be required compactly and conveniently
to express those new concepts, just as freely as the chemist
and the biologist does. Indeed, as late as 1893, when I
might have procured the insertion of the word "pragmatism"
in the "Century Dictionary", it did not seem to me to enjoy such
vogue as would warrant such a step.
