Definition of Time.

1. That which is settled in the veriest actuality of [del]things[/del] [ins]fact[/ins] to be true is past. That which is settled, but not in the veriest actuality of [del]things[/del] [ins]fact[/ins], is future.

To say that in an actual stage of actuality [del]question[/del] [ins]in veriest actuality[/ins] A is settled, and question B not, is to say that A was or will be settled by accomplished fact, while B is still unsettled.

Of course, the [u]matter[/u] of the definition, that is, the meaning of the phrase "in veriest actuality," is extremely vague; but the Form, which is the important thing, is exact.

2. The bare fact that in an actual state of things, a question is not settled [del]to[/del] in the veriest actuality of fact gives room for the possibility that every individual question of a general class of questions must be settled in the veriest actuality of fact without the settlement of that unsettled question, though this may be thereby settled.
