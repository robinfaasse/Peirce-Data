In [u]ast.[/u], [[del]originally[/del] [ins]originally[/ins], any departure from the diurnal motion of the fixed stars], an angular quantity defining the position of a point in a planetary orbit. It is usually (now, always) [del]considered[/del] [ins]taken[/ins] to increase in the direction of planetary motion. In [u]anc. ast.[/u], it was reckoned from the apogee; in early [u]mod. ast.[/u], from aphelion, except in cometary orbits, but since Gauss from the perihelion. [u][del]Coequate[/del] [del]anomaly[/del][/u], the angle at the sun [del]in the plane of the orbit[/del] between a planet and the place of perihelion or aphelion. [u]Eccentric anomaly[/u] ([u]anomalia eccentri[/u]), the arc of the circle circumscribing the orbit between the major axis and the perpendicular to it through the planet. It is now usually [del]considered[/del] [ins]regarded[/ins] as a subsidiary quantity, [ins]u,[/ins] defined by the equation [u]w[/u] = [u]u[/u] - [u]e[/u] sin [u]u[/u], where [u]w[/u] is the mean anomaly, and [u]e[/u] the eccentricity [ins]and its geometrical signification is not considered.[/ins]

[u]Mean anomaly[/u], the angular quantity which is to 360° as the time since the planet left its perihelion (apogee or aphelion) is to the period of revolution. [u]True anomaly[/u], (a) the arc of the zodiac between the place of the apogee and the apparent place of the centre of the epicycle; (b) now, the coequate anomaly reckoned in the direction of planetary motion.
