UNITED STATES COAST AND GEODETIC SURVEY,

WASHINGTON, D. C., [date]MARCH 30, 1891.[/date]

SIR:

Interesting remains of aboriginal art are very generally distributed over the country, and the work of collecting data concerning them is so vast that every available agency should be employed.

Officers and employés of the Coast and Geodetic Survey engaged in field work visit many remote places and may in cases, if so inclined, secure much valuable information in regard to the distribution of several classes of remains, some of which are here enumerated:

Ancient village sites, occurring in many parts of the country.

Ancient shell heaps and mounds of shell, occurring on the sea-coast and along river banks.

Ancient mounds, fortifications, etc., occurring in the Mississippi valley and elsewhere.

Ancient pueblos, cliff and cave ruins, occurring in the Pueblo country.

Ancient soapstone quarries, mica, flint, and copper mines, in the Appalachian region and elsewhere.

Ancient burial places, in all sections.

Caves and rock-shelters, possibly occupied by ancient peoples, occurring in many localities.

Ancient pictographs.

It is especially desired that mounds, ruins, and other fixed remains should be located upon maps. Any information or data that may be gathered bearing upon the subject of archæology will be highly appreciated by me and will in the end be transmitted to the Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution, who will place it to the credit of the collector.

Yours with respect,

T. C. MENDENHALL,
_Superintendent._
