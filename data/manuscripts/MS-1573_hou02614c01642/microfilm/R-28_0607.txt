[del]and a stumbling-block.[/del] The deepest and most powerful
causes of the success of the modern physical and
natural sciences have been of a moral nature. The
greatest of all has been that students of these sciences have
sought the truth in perfect singleness of heart. They have
never aimed at foregone conclusions; and they have always stood
ready to dump their entire load of theory as soon as it was
indisputably fairly and fully contradicted by fact. Another
moral factor of their success has been their selfconfidence.
In place of the ancient [u]acatalepsy[/u], a feeling of being so
unequal to the task of solving Nature's riddles that it was not
worth while seriously to attempt it, the modern scientist has
been animated by an unwavering faith that whatever
natural problem he had in hand would turn out to
have a humanly comprehensible solution. But the
philosophers have been strikingly lacking in these virtues.
