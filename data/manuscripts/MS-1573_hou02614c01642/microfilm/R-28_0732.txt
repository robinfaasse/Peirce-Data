Their system of weights and measures [del]is[/del] [ins]has for its basis[/ins] [del]highly[/del] [ins]a[/ins] logical element of connecting units of space and of mass by water taken as a standard substance, which was copied by the French and modern physicists. But their arithmetic with its mixture of decimal and sexagesimal groupings, which we still adhere to partly because Vlacq (or whoever it was) rendered it difficult to change, is anti-logical and barbarous in the extreme. It is a striking proof of how anti-logical and [ins]yet[/ins] anti-mathematical the human mind is, that although man was given five fingers on each hand, as the strongest possible hint that could leave any intellectual path for him to do, toward a numeration on the base of [u]six[/u] (for having [del]counted[/del] [ins]used[/ins] the five fingers of one hand, to count up to five, the obvious way was to turn down a finger of the other hand, and release those five of the first hand, only putting one down for seven, or 6+1, etc.) nevertheless among all the tribes of humanity, there was no one that had the intelligence to take the hint. It is true that one could only count 35 on the fingers in that way, while another obvious method enables us to count on them to 1023 inclusive; but it is better than our stupid use of them that only enables us to count [u]ten[/u], a stupidity only surpassed by the more elaborate methods of finger reckoning in which the joints are used. The advantage of using a numeration on the base of six is that while it does not require double the number of figures that decimal arithmetic does, but only 16 for every 9 in decimal numeration or 1058 for every 595 of decimal, it has the convenience that every prime number [ins](except 2 and 3)[/ins] ends either with 1 or 5; and division by a number one more or one less than the base of numeration is particularly easy.
