The velocity of radiation of heat is
$$m a^{\theta} (a^{t} - 1)$$

where $m$ depends solely on the nature [ins]& the thermometric scale[/ins]
of the radiating body, and $a$ is a constant
depending solely on the thermometric scale.
For a centigrade thermometer $a = 1.0077$

$\theta$ is the temperature of the enclosure
$t$ the excess of temperature of the ra-
diating body.

$m$ has the following values, which are
expressed in units equal to the heat re-
quired to raise 1000 grains of water
$1^{\circ}$ Centigrade

$$\begin{array}{l c}
\text{For Glass} & 9.566 \\
\text{Dry Chalk} & 8.613 \\
\text{Dry New Red Sandstone} & 8.377 \\
\text{Sandstone} & 8.882 \\
\text{Polished Limestone} & 9.106 \\
\text{Same unpolished} & 12.808
\end{array}$$
