[u]Dedekind's Dirichlet, §23[/u]

I show here the full expressions for the bracketed
functions:
Denote $a$ by $\tau_0$, $b$ by [del]$\tau_0 \pi_0$[/del] $\pi_0$
$\gamma$ by $\tau_1$, $\delta$ by $\pi_1$
$\alpha$ by $\tau_2$, $\beta$ by $\pi_2$
$\eta$ by $\tau_3$, $\theta$ by $\pi_3$, etc.

Then denote by $\sigma_j^i$ a sum of products each
of $i+1$ $\tau$s and $\pi$s; that is if $i$ is [del]odd[/del] [ins]even[/ins] [del]even[/del]
each product consists of $\tau_0$ with [del]all[/del] $\frac{1}{2} i$ other
$\tau$'s and as many $\pi$s, but if $i$ is [del]even[/del] [ins]odd[/ins], it
consists of $\tau_0$ and $\frac{1}{2}(i-1)$ other $\tau$'s and $\frac{1}{2}(i+1)$
$\pi$'s, and therefore [u]including[/u] $\tau_0$ of as many $\tau$'s
as $\pi$'s. No factor of a product occurs to the
second power, and no $\tau$ or $\pi$ in the product
has a higher index than $j$. And the sum $\sigma_j^i$ consists
of all the possible products that follow this rule.
