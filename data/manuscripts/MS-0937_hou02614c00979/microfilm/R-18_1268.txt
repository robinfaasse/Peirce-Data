The fault of this explanation is the capital fault
which attaches to all nominalistic explanations, namely,
that they merely restate the fact to be explained under another
aspect; or, if they add anything to it, add only something
from which no definite consequences can be deduced.
A scientific explanation ought to consist in the assertion
of some positive matter of fact, other than the fact to be
explained, but from which this fact necessarily follows;
and if the explanation be hypothetical, the proof of it
lies in the experiential verification of predictions
deduced from it as necessary consequences. Leibniz's
