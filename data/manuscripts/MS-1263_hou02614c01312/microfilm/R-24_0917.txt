[u]Arithmetic[/u]

The work is entitled "Precept for attaining knowledge of all occult
things," by which is plainly meant rules for finding unknown
quantities.

The book deals chiefly with fractions, and it brings into a strong
light the truth of the assertion made by some Greek writers that
the Egyptians recognized no fractions except those whose nu-
merators are 1. There is, however, one exception [ins]$\frac{2}{3}$[/ins]. Every reader
of the Almagest is familiar with the fact that Ptolemy when he
does not use the Babylonian division by sixtieths, — the unit
being divided into 60 minutes, and each minute into 60 seconds,
etc. — always expressed fractions as sums of fractions with
unit denominators [del]except that he leaves $\frac{2}{3}$[/del]. Thus, if the latitude of a star is $30^\circ 50'$, he
writes it $30 \frac{1}{2} \frac{1}{3}^\circ$.

The book of Aahmes accordingly begins with a table for doubling
fractions. The system of numeration of the Egyptians is the most
thoroughly decimal of that of any people, unless we except the
French. [del]It[/del] Hence, these fractions run from $\frac{1}{3}$ $\frac{1}{5}$ $\frac{1}{7}$ $\frac{1}{9}$ etc.
up to $\frac{1}{99}$. Several mathematicians have endeavored to find
some system in the choice of [del]a[/del] [ins]the[/ins] particular partitions of the doubles
which are adopted in the table of Aahmes. For it is plain that there
would generally be considerable latitude of choice. Thus, $\frac{2}{15} = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{30}$
and $\frac{2}{15} = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{10}$. [del]But[/del] [ins]And[/ins] when more than two terms are admitted (as they
usually must be) the range of choice is still greater. But the Egyptians
had a great aversion to rigid system; and though in some cases Aahmes
seems to avoid high denominators, in other cases he seems to prefer
them. He [del]takes[/del] [ins]adopts[/ins] the way [del]one[/del] [ins]one[/ins] seems natural and pleasing to him.

But what shall we say of the mathematical genius of a people,
who cannot think of [del]any[/del] [ins]a[/ins] fraction with a [del]denominator[/del] numerator
other than 1 except $\frac{2}{3}$? Or still worse, what shall we say of a
people who being able to conceive of those fractions deliberately
prefer to use sums of fractions of numerator 1? The examples mostly
refer to practical cases of Egyptian life. Now imagine the helpless condition
of a man who has to divide 2 cakes among 5 persons knows no better way
than to give each $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{15}$.
