The Egyptians, considering their extraordinary engineering abilities,
were most singularly wanting in all care for scientific truth. It is this
circumstance which has rendered Egyptian chronology so obscure,
contrasting in this respect most strongly with that of Babylonia. The

Nevertheless, the only work of exact science which has been pre-
served to us from a very high antiquity is the Mathematical Papyrus,
which has been published by Eisenlohr in facsimile, with a transla-
tion and minute commentary. This work is contained in the library
of Mr. Plimpton, as well as in every important learned library.

Since Eisenlohr's work, Egyptology has made important advances,
and the translation might now be considerably improved. Special
facts bearing upon its date, etc, have also been discovered. A tolera-
bly good account of all this is given in the second edition of the
first volume of Moritz Cantor's Vorlesungen über die Geschichte der
Mathematik, [ins]contained in the Astor and Columbia libraries.[/ins]
