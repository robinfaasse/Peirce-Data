incautious excess of caution. It would be better to see whether there
is no way in which their veracity can be put to the test. They all
have a great deal to say of the long time which Pythagoras spent in the
distant East. Zeller flatly denies that he ever was in the East; for no
reason except that it contradicts the biographers. He is much inclined
to doubt whether he was even in Egypt. If, then, it can be proved that
Pythagoras was in the East, it will show that some degree of credit is to
be attached to the biographies, and that their authors did have some
information about [del]the[/del] him other than the little that inquirers before them
like Plato and Aristotle were able to gather.
