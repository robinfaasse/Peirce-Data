[u]Chronology of Arithmetic[/u]
with references to the collection of Mr. George A. Plimpton
to the Astor Library, and to other available collections.
By C. S. Peirce.

[u]1.[/u] In order precisely to limit my subject, I define as follows:—
[u]Arithmetic is the Science of Numbers.[/u]
[u]A Number is a systematic name or symbol for a rational quantity.[/u]
[u]Arithmetic is either Theoretical or Practical.[/u]
[u]Theoretical arithmetic treats of the properties of rational quantities.[/u] Its princi-
-pal branches are [u]Mystical arithmetic[/u], including Cabbala, etc. and the [u]Theory[/u]
[u]of Numbers[/u] which treats of enumerational quantity, or whole numbers, with
special reference to their remainders after division. The theory of the [u]Partition[/u]
[u]of Numbers[/u] is a special branch usually treated separately.
[u]Practical arithmetic[/u], is the art of computation with numbers. It has various
branches, such as the art of using the abacus, the art of reckoning with
counters, the art of reckoning on the fingers, etc. Its most important branch
is [u]Vulgar arithmetic[/u], or [u]Algorism[/u], which is the art of computation with the
Arabic figures.
This chronology is confined to Practical Arithmetic and chiefly to Vulgar
Arithmetic.

[u]2.[/u] In high antiquity practical arithmetic was practiced by the
Egyptians, the Babylonians, and the Chinese.

established, he supports by more than a hundred arguments, to the
effect that the Chinese proper were a tribe which about [date]2300 B.C.[/date]
went to China from Western Asia where they had been in contact
with the Accadians or Sumerians, so that the Chinese civilization
was founded upon the Babylonian.
