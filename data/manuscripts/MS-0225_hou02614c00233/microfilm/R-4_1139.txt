[u]Problem[/u]. To determine, in a table of logarithmic sines carried to a certain number of places of decimals, what is the [del]smallest[/del] [ins]least[/ins] angle at which [del]a certain difference[/del] [ins]quantity[/ins] being the difference between adjacent angles in the table) the error in taking out a sine by means of proportional parts will be inappreciable.

[u]Solution[/u]. Let [u]m[/u] be the angle.
$\delta$ the difference of adjacent angles
[u]N[/u] the number of places of decimals

Then $\frac{1}{2} 10^{-N}$ is the least greatest admissible error of the logarithm which will not affect the figures.

Let [u]x[/u] be a positive angle less than $\delta$. Then in taking out [del]$m + \frac{1}{2} + x$[/del] the error will be
$-\frac{1}{2} 10^{-N} = \frac{x}{\delta} [ \log \sin (m + \delta) - \log \sin (m - \frac{1}{2} \delta) ] + \log \sin (m - \frac{1}{2} \delta) - \log \sin (m - \frac{1}{2} \delta + x)$

Supposing N to vary and m and $\delta$ to remain constant

Supposing N and m to vary and x and $\delta$ to remain constant
