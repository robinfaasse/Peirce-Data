[u]R's Ring[/u].

Given $\Omega$ longitude of node
" $i$ inclination to ecliptic
" $\omega$ inclination of ecliptic

[fig]Spherical triangle with vertices Pole of Equator, Pole of Ecliptic, and Pole of Ring[/fig]

$N$ = R.A. of Ring's node on Equator
$\Phi$ Arc of Ring between nodes of Equator and Ecliptic on Ring
$I$ Inclination of Ring to Equator.
Find $N$, $\Phi$, $I$ by spherical trig.

$N + 90^{\circ}$ is R.A. of Ring's Pole.
[del]$90^{\circ} - I$[/del] [ins]$I$[/ins] is Polar distance of Ring's Pole.

[fig]Spherical triangle with vertices North Pole, Saturn, and Ring Pole[/fig]

$\alpha$ = R.A. of R., $\delta$ Dec of R.

***

Solution of $1^{\text{st}}$ Triangle.

[del]$\text{cotan} \frac{1}{2} \Phi = \frac{\cos \frac{1}{2}(i-\omega)}{\cos \frac{1}{2}(i+\omega)} \text{cotan} \frac{1}{2} \Omega$[/del] [del]$\text{cotan} \frac{1}{2} \Psi = \frac{\sin \frac{1}{2}(i-\omega)}{\sin \frac{1}{2}(i+\omega)} \text{cotan} \frac{1}{2} \Omega$[/del]
[del]$N = \Phi + \Psi$[/del] [del]$\Phi - \Psi$[/del] [del]$\tan \frac{1}{2} I = \frac{\sin \frac{1}{2} \Phi}{\sin \frac{1}{2} \Psi} \tan \frac{1}{2}(i-\omega) = \frac{\cos \frac{1}{2} \Phi}{\cos \frac{1}{2} \Psi} \tan \frac{1}{2}(i+\omega)$[/del]

$\tan \Phi = \frac{\cos \frac{1}{2}(i-\omega)}{\cos \frac{1}{2}(i+\omega)} \tan \frac{1}{2} \Omega$     $\tan \Psi = \frac{\sin \frac{1}{2}(i-\omega)}{\sin \frac{1}{2}(i+\omega)} \tan \frac{1}{2} \Omega$

$N = \Phi + \Psi$     $\phi = \Phi - \Psi$


$\tan P = \frac{\sin \frac{1}{2}(I+\delta)}{\sin \frac{1}{2}(I-\delta)} \tan \frac{1}{2}(N-\alpha)$     $\tan Q = \frac{\cos \frac{1}{2}(I+\delta)}{\cos \frac{1}{2}(I-\delta)} \tan \frac{1}{2}(N-\alpha)$

$90^{\circ} + p = P + Q$     $u? = P - Q$     $\tan \frac{1}{2} l = \frac{\sin Q}{\sin P} \text{cotan} \frac{1}{2}(I+\delta) = \frac{\cos Q}{\cos P} \text{cot} \frac{1}{2}(I-\delta)$
