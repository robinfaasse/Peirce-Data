But in the first place, let us
endeavour to determine, if possible,
how the mind acquires the idea
of any thing being more or less.
1. Any thing is more or less by
comparison, and the degree is
expressed by the greater or smaller
distance from any fixed point, or
between any two given points.
2. Any thing is more or less, when
compared with our own perception
of it, which being the effect of its
operation upon our senses, must
of course follow the degrees of force
it exerts.
3. Any thing is more or less,
when we consider the degrees of
force and attention necessary to
its being perceived by us.
4. Any thing is more or less,
when it occupies more or less
of our time, and becomes a
larger or smaller part of our
existence. [del]Any thing[/del] [ins]Every thing[/ins] then,
being more or less only as it is
related to us, we find it impossible
to form any idea of degree, but as
referring to something else.
