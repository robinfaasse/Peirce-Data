Rule of Existential Graphs.
I. Rule of Erasure and Insertion.
i. In even enclosures,
1. Any line of identity may be broken at 
any point. [del]It may be separated at a [ins]branch[/ins] bifurcation fork 
but cannot be separated from a hook of a spot or enclosure.[/del]
2. Any detached partial graph may be 
erased.
ii. In odd enclosures,
1. Any graph may be inserted detached
[ins]Any line may be extended from a loose end[/ins]
2. Any [del]two[/del] lines of identity may be joined, 
[del]a new line forming a fork on each. Any two loose ends of lines 
may be joined[/del]
II Rule of Iteration and Deiteration.
i. On [del]any[/del] even[ins]ly[/ins] enclosed area,
1. Any partial graph may be iterated, 
i.e. copied with identically the same lines of iden-
tity joined to corresponding parts
2. Any line of identity may be deformed 
[del]in any way and even made to reach [ins]the outside of[/ins] a cut 
[ins]though on the inside[/ins][/del] provided its connections are not changed; and
a fork may be made at any point of a line of identity
ii On any oddly enclosed area,
1. Any iterated graph ([del]that[/del] is scribed 
twice with [del]identically[/del] the same lines of identity 
joined to corresponding parts) may be deiterated 
(by the erasure of one such replica)
2. Any line of identity may be 
deformed in any way so long as its connec-
tions are unchanged, and [ins]its extremity[/ins] may even be retracted 
from the outside of a cut [del](but from the inside)[/del] on 
the same area or [del]from[/del] so as to annul a fork. And a 
[ins]line returning into itself[/ins] may be broken.
