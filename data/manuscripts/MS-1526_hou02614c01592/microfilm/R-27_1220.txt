[u]General Idea and Nomenclature of the Game.[/u]

Each player in his turn throws the dice and moves [del]forward[/del]
[del]his[/del] [ins]his[/ins] men in a fixed direction by a number of points
determined by his throw. When they have all arrived at a cer-
tain part of the board, instead of moving them he throws
them off. He can also set back a man of his adversary's by
"hitting" it in a certain way in the course of his play; and
a man which is so set back has to enter a certain part
of the board in lieu of a move, and then move on from
that point. [del]He[/del] [ins]The player[/ins] who gets all his men off the board first
wins. Such is the general idea of the game.

There are various terms which have to be explained before
a more detailed description can be given.

A "pair" of dice, in backgammon, is two dice. To "throw"
the dice the player puts his pair of dice into his box, shakes
it, if he likes, and allows them to fall out upon the
vacant middle part of the board. The "throw" is the pair
of numbers which appears uppermost. This pair of numbers
the player immediately names, pronouncing the higher
number first. If the two numbers are unequal, each number
gives a right to [del]playing[/del] [ins]moving[/ins] a man forward that number of
points. Either number may be played first, and the same
man or different men may be [del]played[/del] [ins]moved[/ins]. If the numbers
