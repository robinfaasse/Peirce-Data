Doublets count as if the number were thrown four times
instead of twice, as it is. Thus, if cings are thrown, one man
may be moved 20 points, or [del]two[/del] [ins]one[/ins] men 15 and another 5,
or two men 10 points each, or one man 10 and two others
5 each, or four men 5 each.

Nomenclature of the Board. The six points on either
side of either tray are called collectively, a "table." The two
tables near either player are called "his tables." One of these,
(Cavendish says preferably the one toward the light) is called
[ins]his "home", or[/ins] "his inner table," or simply "his table" par excellence. The
two players inner tables are opposite one another, and it is
a mere matter of agreement which player shall have the
inner tables to his right and which to his left. The outer-
most point furthest from the bar in the inner table of either
player is called his ace-point. The next is the deuce-point
and so on up to the size-point. The next point after [del]the[/del] [ins]either player's[/ins]
size-point, being the point in his outer table next the bar is
called his bar-point. The bar-point is also called the ace-point
of the outer table, and the [del]fo[/del] others are counted up to the size-
point of the outer table; but these are merely designations
found in some books. Other books count a player's points
up to 12.
