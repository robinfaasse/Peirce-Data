"[u]Laws[/u]" of Backgammon, i.e. Provisions in regard
to [del]the[/del] Violations of the "Rules", or Regular Method of
Play.
Backgammon is not a serious or exciting game; it is
played for nominal stakes; and by the most respectable
people. It is, therefore, neither desirable nor practicable to
impose any penalties whatever. The "laws" can, therefore,
only determine how the game is to proceed in case one of
the rules is accidentally violated.

1. The dice must fall flat upon the board. If one [del]is[/del] [ins]rests[/ins]
tilted [del]against[/del] [ins]or[/ins] no matter how little, [ins]or[/ins] if one rests on [del]a man[/del] [ins]anything[/ins]
but the [del]bottom of the[/del] board, the player must throw again. Some books
say if one die falls in one tray and the other in the other, the
player must throw again. But there is no reason for such
a law; and since the law is the perfection of reason, [del]that[/del] [ins]this[/ins]
cannot be acknowledged to be the law.
