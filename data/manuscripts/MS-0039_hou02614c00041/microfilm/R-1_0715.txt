It is usual and useful to say that a number which
possesses all the hereditary characters of another
is [u]at least as great as[/u] that other. We may write
$g_{il}$ to signify that $l$ is at least as great as $i$,
so that
$$g_{il} = \Pi_\alpha (\bar{q}_{\alpha i} + \bar{h}_\alpha + q_{\alpha l}).$$

The first proposition is thus expressed by saying
[del]that[/del] there is a number that all numbers are
at least as great as, or

$$\Sigma_i \Pi_l g_{il}.$$

A number that all numbers are at least as great
as is called [u]one[/u]. We may write $U_i$ to signify that
$i$ is one, so that
$$U_i = \Pi_l g_{il}.$$
