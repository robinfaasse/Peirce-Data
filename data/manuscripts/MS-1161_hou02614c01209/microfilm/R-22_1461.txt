Cratylus says this, Socrates, that a name's
correctness belongs by nature to each
of the existents.

φύω (in Homer φύω) is one of the
most ancient verbs, (found in Latin <u>fui</u>
"I grew" Eng <u>to be</u>) originally apparently
φῦμι. Some of its tenses are transitive,
'to produce, build', and are formed from φύω.
Others are intransitive 'to grow to be' and
are mostly from φῦμι. Thus, besides the
present φύω, the imperfect ἔφυον (though
Homer has φύεν in 3 sing.), the future φύσω

1st Ao. ἔφῡσα Pass & Mid Fut φύσομαι
all of which are transitive, and also the intrans
Perf. πέφῡκα (though Homer has πεφύᾱσι as
if the present tense of a verb πεφύω, and
Theognis has Subj. 3rd sing πεφύῃ unless
this is a misprint or misreading in [del]L & S[/del] S and
any way it is a [del]pec[/del] [ins]rare[/ins] form) [ins]also Epic part πεφυῖα[/ins]

Other intransitive forms are from φῦμι
2 Ao ἔφῡν (Homer has 3 Sing φῦ) 3rd pl
ἔφῡσαν same as 1 Ao, of course. Opt
φύη or φυίη Inf. φῦναι (Homer φῦμεναι)
Participle φῡς (Attic fem φῦσα) The Subj.
is usually φῠῃ, φύωσι etc with various
readings but Euripides has φυῶ, φυῇ, φυῶσι

In its [ins]Active[/ins] transitive tenses, Pres, Fut, 1 Ao Active
it means 'to produce', and of a country 'to produce',
of men 'to engender', also 'to grow' (a beard) etc.
'to get' understanding

In the Passive and the Intransitive senses of the
Active i.e. 2nd Ao, Perfect, & Pluperfect it
means 'to wax', 'to come into being' of men 'to be born'
πεφυκέναι τινός 'to be descended from'

But the Perf. (sometimes also 2 Ao) 'to be
formed by nature' so and so, and even simply
"to be" 'to have become, to be destined by
nature (with inf) [del]followed by καὶ or πρὸς to be
inclined by nature to, especially with acc. & inf.[/del]
