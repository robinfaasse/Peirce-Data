Obviously, therefore, if $r$ be the ordinal of any stage the numerators of that stage will contain among them $2^{r-1}$ additional to [del]those of the $(r-1)^{\text{th}}$ stage;[/del] [ins]those of the $(r-1)^{\text{th}}$ stage;[/ins] and among these will be contained every integer not greater than $r$ and all of these that are greater than 2 will occur twice. Namely, 1 will be the $(2^{n-1})^{\text{th}}$ numerator, where $n$ is any integer not greater than $r$; 2 will be the $(2^{r-2} \pm 2^{n-2})^{\text{th}}$ [ins]numerator[/ins]; 3 will be the $(2^{r-2} \pm 2^{r-3} \pm 2^{n-3})^{\text{th}}$ [ins]numerator[/ins]

More important are the facts that the only even numerators are those whose ordinals are divisible by 3.
