A collection consists simply in the [del]co[/del]existence in the universe of certain objects. As an individual object the collection is a creation of thought. The relation [ins]of coexistence[/ins] is thought as if it were a receptacle into which the coexisting things of the given description were "contained".

Being thus a creation of thought, a collection has a purpose and the only proper definition of [del]the[/del] [ins]anything[/ins] that has a purpose consists in a statement of the purpose, together, if necessary, with a statement of how it is to be carried out. For such, evidently, is the "idea" of purposive things.

The purpose of a collection is [del]as[/del] to subserve the ends of [del]the[/del] reasoning. Well then, How? First of all looking upon any special relation of coexistence as [del]the[/del] subject leads us to perform an experiment upon it, namely, that experiment called [u]counting[/u], which without the abstraction would never have entered one's head. It would require a genius to comprehend the utility of this experiment without the conception of a collection. It has other utilities; but this one may be taken as the type of all.
