Arithmetic Integers (Euclid's Numbers)

[u]Divisibility of Numbers.[/u] [fig]large X[/fig]

A very convenient way of saying that [del]any[/del] [ins]two[/ins] numbers, A and B,
being severally divided
[del]after division[/del] by any third number, C, leave the same remainder,
is to say that A and B are congruent to each other, for the modulus
C; which we write thus:
A $\equiv$ B (mod C).

To say that [del]a[/del] [ins]any[/ins] number, A, is congruent to zero, for the modulus
C, is [del]to[/del] [del]say[/del] another way of saying that A is exactly divisible by
C.
