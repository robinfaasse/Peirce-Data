The whole question, we repeat, is a question of economy; and economy is a matter to be discussed by careful and dispassionate estimates of advantages and disadvantages. The burden is upon the advocates of the change to prove beyond all reasonable doubt that the annual value in dollars to the American people would so greatly outweigh that of its disadvantages as to pay for the cost of the revolution and justify the hazard of it. Generalities and superlatives are out of place where our national prosperity is threatened, as [ins]we are assured[/ins] it most seriously would be if such a change had any chance of being brought to pass. The metric advocates, if they have any due sense of the responsibility they are assuming, must produce calculations, sober and reasonable, of the annual dollars of gain that would accrue, and of the annual dollars of loss that must be set off against that gain, in order that we may judge whether the step shall be risked, or not. Such calculations could be made by moderate industry. They would be rough; but that would not matter, since the excess of advantage must be enormous, or the change would not be justified.
