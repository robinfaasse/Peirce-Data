The adoption of the metric system would have real advantages. It would also have real disadvantages. Are the former so assured and so great as unmistakably to overbalance the latter? This is a question of how much, a question of economy. Economy is not a matter to be discussed in a passionate and partisan spirit. Above all let us not hear from the one side that the advantages, or from the other that the disadvantages, of the change would be "incalculable." Economy calls for calculation. The calculations may be very rough; but if it can be shown that any assumption of error in them not grossly improbable will leave the advantages distinctly greater than the disadvantages, [ins]after including the cost of the change,[/ins] then the change should be made if no infringement of natural rights be involved. If such infringement [del]be[/del] [ins]is[/ins] involved, it must be shown that public prosperity is so dependent upon the change that such infringement is imperatively required.
