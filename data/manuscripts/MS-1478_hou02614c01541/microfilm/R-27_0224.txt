A question of economy is not a proper theme for eloquence or for superlatives. What it calls for is careful estimates; and we believe that it is possible to estimate in dollars the annual value to the people of the United States of each advantage and disadvantage of the metric system. The estimates must, no doubt, be excessively rough; but it will be sufficiently accurate to decide the question. We shall not undertake to make a definitive estimate, but a preliminary essay toward such a rational consideration of the question, to be improved upon by others. Meantime, even our first attempt at such an estimate will, at all events, be better than wild shouts that the advantages or disadvantages are "incalculable."
