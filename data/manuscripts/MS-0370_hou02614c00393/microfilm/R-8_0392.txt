physicist is perfectly accustomed to conceive of it
as merely the centre of the forces. It exists, there-
fore, only so far as these forces exist. Since, therefore,
these forces exist only by virtue of the fact, that
something will happen under certain circumstances,
it follows that matter itself only exists in this way.
Nor is this conception one which is peculiar to the physi-
cists and to our views of the external world. A man
is said to know a foreign language. And what does
that mean? Only that if the occasion arises, the
words of that language will come into his mind; it
does not mean that they are actually in his mind all
