[date]Feb 28. 1900[/date]

My dear Mr. Peirce,

Your letter of [date]Feb. 27[/date] is received. I bear with great regret of your brother's troubles. You may remember that he made a large number of pendulum observations, of which the results have never been published. All of the papers are now at the Coast Survey Office in Washington. It occurs to me that some arrangement might be made by which this important work may be completed. My relations with the present Superintendent, Mr. Pritchett, are very friendly and if you with [ins]it[/ins] I will write to him. I should advise the payment of a fixed sum, and the agreement by your brother to deliver a definite conclusion in book form as the work is produced, within a specified time. I have a very high opinion of the talent of Mr. Charles Peirce and believe that this work should not be lost.

As regards this Observatory I do not see that I could offer your brother any suitable position. The work is largely routine, at low hourly pay.
