My dear Mr. Peirce,

Your hypothesis regarding the distribution of the stars is very interesting. As I understand it, hyperbolic space is a mental conception and not a physical fact. It is therefore difficult to understand how its adoption as a mutual phenomenon except by a accident coincidence.

To show please I send you by mail an advance copy of Vol XXVI Part I of our Annals which will be gradually distributed shortly. This volume contains the data in studying the distribution of the stars in space.

Just among them according to their spectra. Those of the first type form the Milky Way and are differently distributed from those of the second type. (See Chapter VII)

I cannot account for the different results from the obtained photometrically except that we are here dealing with monochromatic light. ($R = 430$).

I hope soon to have much more material bearing on this subject. The observations of southern stars (in Peru) are completed and nearly reduced. The distribution of Vol XXIV will enable you to carry the discussion of the visual magnitudes. A photographic meridian photometer is now at work here and will provide at all stars to the twelfth magnitude and give absolute magnitudes on a uniform scale.

Very truly yours
Edward C. Pickering
