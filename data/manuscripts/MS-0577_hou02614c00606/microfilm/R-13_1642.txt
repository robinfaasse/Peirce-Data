[pn]Ms 77[/pn] [u]Algebra of the Copula[/u]

Logical quantity has but two values, t the true
and f the false. But one operation is neces-
sary, defined as follows:
$\alpha$. If b is true, $a \prec b$ is true.
$\beta$. Either a or $a \prec b$ is true.
$\gamma$. If a and $a \prec b$ are true, b is true.
This gives the following:
$f \prec t = t$  $f \prec f = t$  $t \prec t = t$  $t \prec f = f$.
Any proposition written is supposed to be true.
In writing propositions parentheses are employed
to enclose compounds to be treated as single letters
in combining them with letters or other such com-
pounds. These may be called [u]clauses[/u]. Parentheses
ending clauses or propositions are omitted, and
the clauses they would have included are not
commonly regarded as such. The last letter of
a proposition or clause is called its [u]consequent[/u].
Its other [ins]immediate parts,[/ins] letters or clauses, [del]not enclosed[/del] are
called [u]antecedents[/u]. Thus in the proposition
$a \prec [ (b \prec c) \prec d \prec e ] \prec f$
