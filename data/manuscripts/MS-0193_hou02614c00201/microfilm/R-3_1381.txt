[u]Syllabus of the Elements of Trigonometry.[/u]

[u]Art 1[/u] [u]Definition of $\sin z$.[/u]
$\sin z$ is a function of $z$ having the following properties:
$1^{\text{st}}$, For each value of $z$, real or imaginary, $\sin z$ has a single value.
$2^{\text{nd}}$, The same general formula for $\sin (c+z)$ in terms of $c$ and $z$ holds whether $c$ be real or imaginary.
$3^{\text{rd}}$, There is a constant quantity, $\pi$ such that
$\sin (z \pm \pi) = \sin (-z)$.
$4^{\text{th}}$, There are no values of $z$ whose sines are equal except those that are deducible from the third property and there is some value of $z$ which satisfies the equation
$\sin z = a + bi$
whatever the value of $a + bi$.
$5^{\text{th}}$, $\sin 0 = 0$.
$6^{\text{th}}$, $\sin \infty = \infty$.
$7^{\text{th}}$, $\sin \frac{\pi}{2} = 1$.

[u]Art 2[/u] From the third property it follows that the equation
$\sin z = \sin c$
is satisfied only by
$z = 2N\pi + c$ or $z = 2N\pi + \pi - c$
where $N$ is any whole number, positive or negative.
