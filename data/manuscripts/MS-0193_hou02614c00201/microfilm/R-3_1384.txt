[u]Syllabus of Trigonometry[/u]

[u]Art 1[/u] [u]Definition of $\sin x$.[/u]
$\sin x$ is a function of $x$ having the following
properties:
$1^{\text{st}}$, For each value of $x$ (real or imaginary) $\sin x$ has a single [ins]determinate[/ins] value.
$2^{\text{nd}}$, The same general formula for $\sin(c+x)$ applies
whether $c$ be real or imaginary.
$3^{\text{d}}$, There is a constant quantity, $\pi$, such that
$\sin(x+\pi) = -\sin x$
$4^{\text{th}}$, The equation
$\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}u\pi + v\pi i\right) = a + bi$
is satisfied by a single set of [del]real[/del] values of $u$ and $v$, in which
$u$ lies in the limits $-1$ and $+1$, whatever the values of $a$ and $b$.
$5^{\text{th}}$, $\sin 0 = 0$
$6^{\text{th}}$, $\sin \infty = \infty$
$7^{\text{th}}$, $\sin \frac{1}{2}\pi = 1$.

[u]Art 2[/u] From the $3^{\text{d}}$ property, it follows that
$\sin(x+2N\pi) = \sin x$
whatever whole number, positive or negative, $N$ may be.
