Besides the points three others must be brought under consideration 1[sup]st[/sup] the government of the present emperor, in particular 2nd the character of the French people, and 3[sup]rd[/sup] the strength of other dynasties and parties.

In order to recognise the value of any inherent weakness or strength it will be necessary to begin by the examination of external circumstances.

In the first place, then, there is no reason why the present dynasty should not, with only moderate strength, last to Kossuth's "resurrection of nations" if it lives till the contending dynasties are forgotten, and this will hardly be more than one generation.

If the present emperor lives till then (which is hardly probable) all is [del]safe[/del] apparently safe; if he dies the legitimists will doubtless make a great attempt upon the throne. It is, consequently, a matter of great importance to decide upon the strength of this party. I have not data enough to open the question at all, but it seems to me impossible that they should exceed in number or in strength the order loving portion of the community. The truth is that the French people generally are tired of these [del]forfitters[/del] revolutions.

To the interests of the commercial and manufacturing classes a revolution is of course in the highest degree destructive, to the [del]peasantry[/del] [ins]ouvriers[/ins] of all kinds it is in most times hurtful, to the peasantry it is especially distasteful; but the classes form a large majority, and for the rest, the influence of the priests brings over many.

There is no aristocracy in France. This has been considered a point of weakness in the present dynasty. Such is not my opinion, however.
