A second character was ruined by flattery, when a
child, and though a quarter of a century has passed over
his head, and though constant efforts to overcome it have
reduced its baneful effects, that same childish vanity
clings to him still, destroys the activity of his benevolence,
and belittles his mind. His conceit is not his own
fault. At an age when judgement was too weak to
resist it, his inconsiderate guardians allowed flattery [ins]conceit[/ins]
to root itself in his mind, and like a parasite plant [del]it[/del]
has sucked out from him his life, and the more he grows,
the more powerful it becomes.

The conceit of my third and last character
lies not so much in thinking too much of himself
as in not subjecting his opinions to a sufficiently
frequent and severe criticism. He is a man of
the greatest tenderness of heart, conscientiousness,
and [del]industry[/del] in the performance of what he
deems his duty; and it is the more to be regretted
that his conceit is of that kind which narrows
his views and will make a bigot and fanatic
of him. The above three pictures are different
lights of the same person - one who if he could
return in this respect (intellectually) and become
the little child which he is morally, would be
blest with a character only to be met with in
novels.
