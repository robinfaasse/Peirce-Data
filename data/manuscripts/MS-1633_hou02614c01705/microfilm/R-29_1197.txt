For my part, (I = the college student, in general)
I am lazy and sleepy enough to be somewhat ad-
dicted to lamplight excitements; accordingly, there-
fore, an exercise of reverent attention and modest -
the only progressive - thought, - in short, laborious
study of science and literature, is what I need. This
is what produces that sweet and strong enthusiasm
we see in the men about us. If a fairy were
to take up a Harvard undergraduate and put him
down on the road to Happiness, I believe, as far as
outward circumstances go, she would not stir him
an inch. The reason is that our Alma Mater will
give us, if we ask her, not amusement merely, nor
interest, but devotion.

Use! Use! Every thing has a use, and man, too,
was meant for use. As long as he has one, as long
as he lives for something, all goes well; when he
does not, this is a vale of tears. The happiness of

the Christian, what does that consist in, but in
the consciousness that he is giving his infinites-
imal aid to God, - is doing his mite in the work
of the universe? He follows perfection, as the ocean
the moon; he gets no nearer, but though he is dashed
against the eternal walls of circumstance, and, su-
perficially, is monotonous and violent by turns, in
his eternal depths there is a tranquillity which
conception cannot grasp. Moreover, he hopes that
toil and patience will earn him a heaven where
these eternal desires shall fructify - perhaps in the
creation of a new universe. And, indeed, what is
hell but an endless ennui?*

* "The searcher for divine truth will agree that faith is the test of truth: the highest
truths cannot be proved. There are two classes of men who err, - those who are convinced too
easily, and those who are not convinced at all: the former have a manifest superiority to the latter.
Let us start then with a reasoning which is above logic, state truths which have only to be conceived."
