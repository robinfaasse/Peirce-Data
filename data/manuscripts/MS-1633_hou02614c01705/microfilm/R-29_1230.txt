of the dust and heat of the city, so that when we
go to Shakespeare "with his wood-notes wild," and
find every thing vivid, vigorous, fresh, we do in-
deed admire him. Still, however, there is but a
small portion even of educated people who

fully appreciate him. His plays are still
acted with the miserable alterations of the
18th century, they are read as somewhat of a
painful duty, and to express the summit
and depth of bad taste Collier his ablest commen-
tator has published those "Notes & Manuscript Emen-
dations."
