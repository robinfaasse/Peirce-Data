But it is not my object to prove
that Poetical Genius and Practical Talent
are compatible, but, taking that for
granted to illustrate it.

For this purpose I shall first take
an acknowledged poet and endeavor to show
that he possessed Practical Talent; and
then I shall take and acknowledged man
of the world and endeavor to show that
he possessed Poetical Genius.

And here I will observe that no
matter how little Practical Talent the
Poet possesses and how little Poetical
Genius the other has, provided it is
certain that they [del]first[/del] [ins]possessed[/ins] these
qualities, the illustration is just as
much an illustration, as though the
former were the greatest statesman and
the [del]latter[/del] the greatest poet that ever

[page break]

lived. As I am not writing a theme
but the correction to one, I must be
brief.

David was remarkably successful, even
where we cannot suppose him to have
had any special aid from Providence.
