Rome had come to the dregs of her
civilization. She was used up, ennuié.
The reason was that [del]there[/del] no sufficient
aim was presented for the life of
man; there was nothing to be hoped for
and to induce an exercise of his noblest
powers. This is a general remark & it
does not apply to Seneca, for instance; but
everyone knows that the number of men
or women who are "fit for [ins]great[/ins] philo-
sophic retirement" is not [ins]great[/ins] under any
circumstances.

Imagine then a [del]noble[/del] race of "ennui-
és", which was to all intents the case,
for the spirit of ennui pervaded the
nation and the young and the old
looked upon life with the same
byronic weariness. Of course they

were [ins]great[/ins] misanthropists, for they saw men
about them eagerly striving either for what
seemed to them an airy nothing or for what
they [del]they[/del] knew to be so by experience. Now
then, Christianity attracts the notice of some
of these men, offering them hopes apart from
the world; at these they caught.

It was the promises they believed in
not the Christianity; that they did not
want nor were they fit for it. It is no
wonder then that in this way arose
the Hermits.
