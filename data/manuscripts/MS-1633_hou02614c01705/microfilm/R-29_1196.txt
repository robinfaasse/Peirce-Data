Thus, we are interested in relics and autographs, because we think we see the traces of a living being there, or love to fancy so. [u]Amusement[/u] is something the same as interest: the only difference of importance is that interest is too much like work for amusement; amusement is the effect of play.[ins]⁺[/ins]

Interest is of two kinds (I do not mean two distinct classes), — the active and the passive. The former requires outwelling thought, the latter delicate attention. The young man destined to be an ennuié must start without either; otherwise, he would never fall into a love of those pleasures which satiate or pall.

He is not a healthy young mind who had not rather even bear vast unhappiness than tear himself away from space & time, by narcotics for example: For thus to tear himself away is to remove himself from the possibility of interest and amusement. And this is the way narcotics do their business: the opium-eaters mind [del]exists[/del], but it no longer [u]remains[/u]; it has lost its function of appliance to objects, — and this is the mind's only use. [Everything which has the same effects as a narcotic is, so far, a narcotic.] As it is with these reliefs of misery, which wrap a man up in nonentity, so it is with earthly joys, which take him away from the fountain of activity, on one side, and nature, the treasury of entertainment, on the other, — the healthy young mind, I say, prefers a little more (alas! usually, very little more) exercise of 'the spontaneities and receptivities' of his being than earthly things can give.
