But I return to Jamblichus, and his
biography of Pythagoras. Not only is this
fearfully careless, but there is a general
fabulous tone about it, — a sort of Jack
the Giant Killer style. The composition is
also negligent in the extreme. For instance,
it contains about half a dozen stories which
are repeated in different chapters; and there
is no apparent order in the narrative. [del]At the
same[/del] Moreover, Jamblichus was not a
Pythagorean himself, and it is quite plain
from various remarks that he lets drop,
that he had no knowledge of the doctrine
except what he had picked up in books.
