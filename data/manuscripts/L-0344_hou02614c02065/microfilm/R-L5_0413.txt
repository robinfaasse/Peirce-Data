Hancock, N.H.
[date]June 8, 1909[/date]

Dear Charles:

I have your note. I am on the hill-tops for the summer, but I go to town Friday, on my way to Charleston, S.C., & will then see see what I can do for your business. My first tho't, when anything is asked of the library, is: We probably haven't got it. I'd generally am right, but if we have, I will send you what I can that you know what is going.

I too have become an abandoned farmer, & since this place is too remote for Boston for me to go in & out every day, I don't go. The summer is a slack time anyway & then the Trustees never buy more than half of the bks. I suggest, preferring more histories of the war of 1812 & of Am. Lit. to the bks. produced in degraded Europe.

I go to Charleston (by sea, bien entendu) to accompany Moorfield Storey who is to make a speech there, at College of Charleston, on Commencement. I told him he ought to have one person willing to applaud. It's just the round trip.

T.S.P.
