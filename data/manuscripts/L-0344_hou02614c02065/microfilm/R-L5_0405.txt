Tokyo: [date]Feb. 15, 1907[/date].

My dear Charley: Yr. letter comes after so long a delay that I fear lest the questions you ask, abt. the Piperian phenomena, may not have object for yr. mind. In fact— if I can be an ignorant phenomenon: one who remembers to put a promised enclosure in an envelope— I will send you back yr. letter, that you may know what I am answering. You ask me if I am convinced that I was speaking w. S. [ins]W.[/ins] P.; yes, I was. You know how that conviction of another person's individuality meets one things that elude definition. The quality in yr. letter that made it you, for ex., say, Strattens, you, that one can understand better than one can define. I felt that I had come into touch with the mind that I knew, working in the more or less sinuous curve that I knew. This I felt at once, [ins]w. S. P.[/ins] I went to the place in absolute skepticism, more than that in a mind disposed to detect deception, full of contempt for all the business. I was not agitated when the other point of view appeared. I had not felt that I had made, or thoroughly understood, this cosmogony.

It is unimportant, but I regretted, to tell you, the enforced change in my opinion. I was not, & am not, one of those eager for more living. I am quite satisfied, & always have been, with the done to-date. The Spectator of late has been crammed full of letters from people hungrier for life than for annihilation.
