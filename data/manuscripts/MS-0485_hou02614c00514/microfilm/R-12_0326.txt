[u]Existential Graphs[/u].

Rules of their Illative Transformation.

[u]Rule I[/u]. The effect of writing a graph is such, that if we are permitted to write a graph, we are permitted to transform any graph that may be written into that graph.

[u]Rule II[/u]. The effect of writing graphs together to form a compound graph is such that any unenclosed graph forming part of a graph can be transformed [ins]in at least every way in which it could be transformed[/ins] as if it stood alone.

[u]Rule III[/u]. The first effect of writing a graph in an oval is, that if a graph [del]can[/del] [ins]may[/ins] be transformed into an oval containing a second graph, then the second graph were it written unenclosed could be transformed into an oval [del]enclosing[/del] [ins]containing[/ins] the first.

[u]Rule IV[/u]. The second effect of writing a graph in an oval is, that if an oval containing a graph [del]can[/del] [ins]may[/ins] be transformed into a second graph, then an oval containing this second might be transformed into the first.
