[u]On Existential Graphs.[/u]
By C. S. Peirce.

[u]§1. What an Existential Graph is.[/u] A [u]graph[/u] is a diagram composed of [u]spots[/u] of different distinctive qualities, of [u]bonds[/u], and of [u]enclosures[/u].

A [u]system of valental graphs[/u] is a system of graphs in which all spots of the same distinctive quality have the same number of bonds attached to them.

A system of logical graphs is one [ins]in each graph[/ins] [del]of[/del] which is susceptible of illative transformations, and must therefore express or partially express an asserted fact. The system will thus be a diagrammatic syntax, if not an entire language.

Two efficient systems of logical graphs are known to me. They are intimately allied. One of these I slightly sketched in [u]The Monist[/u], Vol VII., pp. 161-186 ([date]Jan. 1897[/date]). I call it [del]the System[/del] of [u]Entitative Graphs[/u], because its fundamental symbol expresses an entitative relation. The other is the system herein described. I call it the system of [u]Existential Graphs[/u], because its fundamental symbol expresses [ins]the[/ins] relation of existence. I speak of existence as a relation, because it consists in the occurrence of a nature among a collection of [ins]individual[/ins] objects of experience, — not necessarily all actually experienced, but all destined to be experienced, if the experience [del]were[/del] [ins]to be[/ins] rounded out to completion. Any entitative graph may be converted into the [del]same[/del] equivalent existential graph by, first, enclosing each spot separately and secondly enclosing the whole graph.
