[u]§2. What assertion is[/u]. A [u]sign[/u], or representamen, is anything which brings an interpreting mind consciously into relation to an object.

Signs are of three kinds, [u]icons[/u], [u]indices[/u], and [u]symbols[/u]. An [u]icon[/u] is a sign which is related to its object only by a resemblance or analogy with it. [del]Its only assured object is a mere[/del] [ins]It represents merely as a[/ins] possibility, or idea. An [u]index[/u] is a sign which is related to the mind it addresses only by violently forcing it to attend to the object. It conveys no idea but merely excites attention to a familiar experience. There must be resistance wherever there is force; and the presentation of an index involves a reaction. The [del]mind[/del] [ins]will[/ins] is overborne by a blind force; and the existence of the object here and now is felt. A proper name of an individual object is the best example of an index. A [u]symbol[/u] is a sign which expresses a habit, settled and self-satisfied, the antecedent, or occasion of exercise, [ins]being[/ins] [del]the occurrence in thought of the objects of some indices, while whenever certain indices are presented[/del] [ins]the action of the habit being to transform these indices into an icon with definite parts to which these indices are mentally affixed[/ins]. For example, a picture of a party of men in medieval costumes landing upon a tropical shore is, in itself, a mere icon.
