Existential Graphs
---
Rules of Illative Transformations

Fundamental Rules

[u]Rule I.[/u] Anything written down is asserted, but nothing else.
[u]Rule II.[/u] A graph forming an unenclosed part of another can be illatively transformed as if it stood alone.
[u]Rule III.[/u] If a graph can be illatively transformed into an oval enclosing a second graph, then the second graph can be illatively transformed into an oval enclosing the first graph.
[u]Rule IV.[/u] If an oval enclosing a graph can be illatively transformed into a second graph, then an oval enclosing the second graph can be illatively transformed into the first graph.
[u]Rule V.[/u] A connecting line within an [del]oval[/del] [ins]unenclosed[/ins] oval may be protracted and joined to any line outside the oval.
