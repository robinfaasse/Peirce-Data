On Existential Graphs.
By C. S. Peirce.

§ 1. What an Existential Graph is. A graph is a diagram
composed of spots of different distinctive qualities, of bonds, and
of enclosures.
A system of valental graphs is a system of graphs in
which all spots of the same distinctive quality have the same
number of bonds attached to them.
A system of logical graphs is one [ins]in each graph[/ins] which is susceptible of
illative transformations, and must therefore express or par-
tially express an asserted fact. The system will thus be a
diagrammatic syntax, if not an entire language.
Two efficient systems of logical graphs are known to me.
They are intimately allied. One of these I slightly sketched in
The Monist, Vol VII. pp. 161–186 (Jan. 1897.) I call it the System
of Entitative Graphs, because its fundamental symbol expresses
an entitative relation. The other is the system herein described. I
call it the system of Existential Graphs, because its fundamental
symbol expresses [ins]the[/ins] relation of existence. I speak of existence as
a relation, because it consists in the occurrence of a nature among
a collection of [ins]individual[/ins] objects of experience, — not necessarily all actually
experienced, but all destined to be experienced, if the experience [del]would[/del] were
rounded out to completion. [ins]to be[/ins] Any entitative graph may be converted into
the [del]correct[/del] equivalent existential graph by, first, enclosing each spot
separately and secondly enclosing the whole graph.
