Existential Graphs.

Rules of their Illative Transformation.

Rule I. The effect of writing a graph is such, that if we are
permitted to write a graph, we are permitted to transform any
graph that may be written into that graph.

Rule II. The effect of writing graphs together to form a compound
graph is such that any unenclosed graph forming part of a graph
can be transformed [ins]in at least every way in which it could be transformed[/ins] as if it stood alone.

Rule III. The first effect of writing a graph in an oval is, that
if a graph [del]can[/del] [ins]may[/ins] be transformed into an oval containing a
second graph, then the second graph were it written unen-
closed could be transformed into an oval [del]enclosing[/del] the first.

Rule IV. The second effect of writing a graph in an oval is, that
if an oval containing a graph [del]can[/del] [ins]may[/ins] be transformed into a second
graph, then an oval containing this second might be transformed
into the first.
