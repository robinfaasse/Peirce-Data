Namely, the very germinal bud of Aristotle's
philosophy out of which the whole tree has
grown, — the metaphor is a bad one since it
continues to be the vital spark of that philoso-
phy after it is fully developed, — is the idea
that, besides [u]existence[/u], there is another mode
of being, — a sort of [u]esse in futuro[/u]; and
here and there in his writings a third mode
of being, — and Entelechy which in some
passages is plainly distinct from Energy, or
Existence, — [del]as[/del] appears in the system. It seems
to me that we have not the means of deter-
mining how far Aristotle would have
deliberately admitted a distinction between
Entelechy & Energy. I do not believe that [fig]figure[/fig]
Aristotle himself knew: the matter was not
clear in his own mind. But the distinction
between the two modes of being of Potency
and Energy is perfectly clear. That his
Potency was a sort of [u]esse in futuro[/u] is shown
in many ways. For example his treatment
of future contingents in his doctrine of modals
illustrates it.
