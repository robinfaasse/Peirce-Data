Under this I will say a little about the
investigations into the physiological
side of the question. We have seen that
the question of whether mental phenomena
are states of consciousness or functions of
consciousness, depends on whether they are
functions of the mind or of the brain.
Now as it is generally agreed that the mind
can only function on the account of the brain
it follows that mental states are really
brain-functions. Thus the psychologist must be
conscious of brain-functions as well as of
psychical ones. But this would seem at first
to make our task a doubly difficult one. It
might even lead some people to think that the
psychologist has to know as much of physi-
ology as the physiologist himself. But
this is not really so. All that is necessary
for the psychologist to know, is the connection
between the mental and physical states.
The question of whether such a connection
actually exists must be left for the physiologist
to decide. All that is required from the psychologist
is that he should find out whether any such
connection is apparent in consciousness.
