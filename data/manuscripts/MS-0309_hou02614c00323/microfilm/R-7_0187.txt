In this respect, as in most respects, the great scholastic realists, such as Aquinas and Scotus, adhered to the doctrine of Aristotle, while the Ockhamists who were at odds with that doctrine upon most points of possible divergence were, in particular, very insistent upon their assertion that there is but one mode of being, namely the existence of reacting individuals. From the time of Ockham on, [del]scholasticism became more and more intolerably formalistic and more and more inclined to put its problems into the most trivial forms with the effect of spinning out discussions to an[/del] [ins]inhumanly[/ins] tedious length

These two faults, [del]were[/del] [ins]bad[/ins] [del]bad[/del] enough among the realists, [del]but[/del] were carried to their last extreme in the writings of the nominalists.
