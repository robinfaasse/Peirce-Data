my protests go but a little ways. The
Worcester case is made complicated by
the proposition to consolidate a part of
Clark University and the Polytechnic — to
split the former into a graduate and an
undergraduate department, with separate
heads, and to join the undergraduate
Clark with the W.P.I. The trustees
of the two institutions already are nearly
the same. While there is no hint
of silence to be observed regarding this,
yet I would not like it to be reported
as coming from me. I fancy that
President Hall has not been informed of
what has been proposed. I am not his
admirer and would not care to be his
informer. There is much friction over
the plan of consolidation and the presidency
and this is likely to be still more.

I write this propped in bed with wretched
cold — I fear the grippe; so if you do
not hear from me again
you may know I am not seriously
laid-up.

Now as to logic and its "authority"!
Pardon me if I confess great embarrassment —
To the bottom of my heart I am sincere
when I say I incline to call you the
best thinker in our land — that, curiously
enough, what you are likely to estimate
your greatest merit I incline to call your
greatest defect. I know not how to hold
my face after this confession unless you
graciously pardon me through the fullness of
your own superiority. You have been called
a "skeptic" and "atheist", and been scowled
and spit at, while for you nobody preserved
your self respect. I beg of you then,
extend to me the courtesy of believing, I
preserve reverence for you and respect for
myself, while in the bonds of truth I yet
differ from you. The chief difference
between us will be in our estimate of
logic. When you say that "good reasoning
