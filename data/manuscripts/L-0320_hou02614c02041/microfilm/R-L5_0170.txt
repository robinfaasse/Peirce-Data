219 Commonwealth Ave
Newton Center Mass
[date]July 12th 1904[/date]

Dr. Charles S. Peirce
Milford Penna

My dear Mr. Peirce,

In some mail herewith I send you copy of the Introductory Volume of my Treatise on Conveying, out today from the press.

You may remember that I had some correspondence with you regarding it before I left New York to live here; was in 1901; and had I not been taken ill I should have had the inestimable pleasure of a day with you in Milford.

The work was first planned in seven or eight volumes; and several of them [del]I[/del] have [ins]more or less[/ins] done upon; but I finally concluded to put this volume out now (though it was completed practically three years ago) in order to reap the full benefit of criticism upon it, for other volumes.

There is no other man in America whose opinion of it I should care for anywhere near equally with your own. And when you write me about it, by of you to do so very freely and fully. I hardly expect you to approve even of its basal thoughts; but you are sure to respect it as the mature product of fifteen years of hard thinking and reading.
