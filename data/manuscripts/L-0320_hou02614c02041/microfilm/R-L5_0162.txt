My dear Prof. Peirce,

Pray accept my apology for thinking of you as [del]Charles[/del] [ins]Sprague[/ins] Peirce. It is a mistake, curiously, that I have harbored for years—a mistake surely made by a recluse like myself.

Regarding the Worcester Polytechnic; I have written to one of the Trustees, my warm friend, protesting against the inclination "to elect a money getter" for the presidency, and urging consideration of the distinction you would confer on the chair. There is room [del]should[/del] [ins]as[/ins] much like to see there as you; and I have steadily protested against the "commercialization" of this [marginalia]In spite of our differences, or to cope I anticipate great benefit from your criticisms of my hypothesis, for a pleasant and clear thinker.[/marginalia]

"But if there is such a proposition as the truth about the particular question in hand, that truth is capable of exact definition" I begin to doubt. For "definition" I hold to be a [u]human art[/u], and I am not sure that any human is capable of exactness. But when you go on to say "from that it follows, with the aid of a few matters of everyday experience, that never have been and never will be called in question, that certain logical propositions hold good for the proposition in hand (or any other where there is any truth)" — when you say that—I find myself [del]obliged[/del] [ins]under[/ins] to be sincere to differ from you [del]in toto[/del].

Euclid believed in his axioms—Descartes in his "Cogito ergo sum" I do not share the confidence felt by either. May I not dare then, dear to be a skeptic of your "logic"!!!
