My dear Prof. Peirce,

Pray accept my apology for thinking of you as Charles Sprague Peirce. It is a mistake, curiously, that I have harbored for years — a mistake easily made by a person like myself.

Regarding the Worcester Polytechnic, I have written to one of the Trustees, my warm friend, protesting against the inclination to elect a "money getter" for the Presidency, and urging consideration of the distinction you would confer on the chair. There is no one I should so much like to see there as you; and I have steadily protested against the "commercialization" of this [del]institution[/del] [ins]criticism[/ins] of my hypothesis. But a pleasant and clear thinker, in spite of our differences, as to logic, I anticipate great benefits from your

"But, of course, there is such a preparation as the truth about the particular question in hand, that 'truth is capable of exact definition' I begin to doubt. For 'definition' I hold to be a human art, and I am not sure that any human is capable of exactness. But when you go on to say "from this it follows, with the aid of a few matters of everyday experience that never have been and never will be called in question, that certain logical propositions hold good for the proposition in hand (or any other where there is any truth)" — when you say this — I find myself obliged, under to be sincere, to differ from you in toto.

Euclid believed in his axioms — Descartes in his "Cogito ergo sum" — I do not share the confidence felt by either. May I not, since these classes to be a skeptic of "your" logic!!
