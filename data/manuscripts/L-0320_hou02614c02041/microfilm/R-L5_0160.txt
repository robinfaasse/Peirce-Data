My dear Prof. Peirce.

It is with deep appreciation that I receive your courteous and most kind letter.

First as to meeting you: I regret you can not come here to the dinner I mentioned. But I shall try to see you as soon as possible. Just now am connect-up with engagements; particularly with committee meeting to discuss a successor for Mendenhall who has or is to resign at Worcester. But the moment I see my way clear will come either to Port Jervis or Milford as is most con-venient to you. - Then you, if able, will come to the hotel and we will have as long a 'talk' as you can stand. - Will let you know long enough beforehand to notify me if you can be at liberty.

In any case I know I shall be stimulated and corrected in no small way by whatever you say or think of my 'project', and even if, finally, we have to modify it completely. I take it to be one of the major achievements of it, if successful, to throw great light on the theory of gravitation, of Helmholtz & Kelvin's [ins]illuminating[/ins] and Vortex atoms, and on the Composition and Resolution of Forces. - It is particularly with reference to the assumption (I feel obliged to make from the observed facts of physics, in order to reconcile gravity, atoms, and composites, which I feel dissatisfaction and wish your judgment thereon. I seem to be driven, by much as physics always has been, to make an hypothesis empirically, & 'just to explain' - as Newton did with his gravity.

But enough for the present - - - and this much that you may have a forecast of what my difficulty is. In a word I have been unable satisfactorily to reconcile Newton's first law with the parallelogram of forces and the origin of rotary motions, and I am as little satisfied with the way it works out under my scheme. If looks too artificial. But enough till we meet. I will notify you some days in advance and await your assurance that the day suits.

Very truly yours
Herbert Nichols
