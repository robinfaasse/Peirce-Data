My dear Prof Peirce

I value your favor of the 18th
inst. as the honest word of a great
thinker, and I could not put it in
a higher category of valuation. There
is nothing that so disheartens me as the
apparent absolute contempt in which I
am held in all American academic
circles. It makes me feel that
academic effort is for the most-
part wire-pulling, and that nothing
is cared for that does not advance
individual interest. When I do
get an honest and valuable opinion
I prize it, whether complimentary
or not.

I beg you, however, to note
that my first volume is but a
sketch, an introduction, for which I
intended to furnish my "reasonings"
later. Also, I may say, I
have never myself [del]been[/del] [ins]been[/ins] entirely
satisfied with my account of
one of my "categories" — ie. Lawfulness,
and specially intend to say
much about this in a future
volume. But whether I shall
ever attempt to bring out the
remaining volumes or not is very
uncertain. I am well nigh discouraged.

In any case I wish you
to be assured I have not intended
lightly your own point-of-view, nor
am so blind to it as you suppose.
But purposely demanded that I stick
to my single sketch, at first, and
leave its defence, against such views
as yours, for my other volumes.
