yet may be utterly deceived.

Under such circumstances the one
sensible thing would be to talk the notion
over with some competent critic. But
there is the rub. Without flattery to
you or conceit in myself, I am entirely
convinced that you and I are the only
two persons in this country capable of
fairly grasping the situation. And you
will forgive me if I add I am not sure
that either you or I am competent in
our alone or independently of the other.
I say this first, because I know I am
not as well equipped in mathematics and physics
as I ought to be for the mighty task in hand.
And on the other side, while I know your full
[del]competency[/del] [ins]adequacy[/ins] in mathematics, physics and general
philosophy, I am uninformed as to how far
you have gone into the psychological lore,
regarding our perceptions of space, furnished
by modern brain-psychology. I know you
will not take this remark as an intended
depreciation; but as an honest lack of
knowledge of how much you have been initiated
in and followed this side of the problem.

At this point I ought to re-introduce
myself. I first met you, for a few moments, at
your father's house, when as a mere boy, I visited
him concerning some mathematical calculations I
had made regarding the possible number of different
light-rays based on the geometrical possibilities of atoms.
Your father had approved the paper and offered to
get it published. I have a much prized bundle of
letters from him regarding it. I was never quite
satisfied with it and withdrew it from publisher.

After this I was forced to study for civil-
engineer. Graduated, spent some years as Asst. to Chief
Engr. Penna. R. R. Resigned, studied abroad: then
at Clark University taking Ph.D. there, then Instructor
at Harvard, practically starting the psychological
laboratory there and taking Wm. James' classes during
his year abroad. Since then I have worked wholly
at Space and Motion; save for a short course
of lectures at Johns Hopkins and elsewhere. I met
you twice at Cambridge in [date]91 or 92[/date]; but you
will scarcely remember me.
