The function of a person is twofold, or perhaps [ins]I should[/ins]
say threefold, to work out his own nature and impulses, [del]to[/del]
aid others, and to contribute to the fulfillment of the destiny of
his generation. The ultimate utility of him of his experience,
is to enable him to do this. But experience has no existence
apart from the person to whom it attaches, and therefore
[del]the[/del] its utility to that person is its whole function. But it
tends to [del]essay[/del] lead to that purpose in a particular
way; namely, by furnishing the person premises
of knowledge. Thus, the essence of experience lies in
the manner in which it contributes to knowledge.

Now the manner in which experience contributes to know-
ledge is very peculiar, and has not been generally recognized.
