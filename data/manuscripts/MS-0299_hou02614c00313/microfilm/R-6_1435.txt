[ins]induction[/ins] Finally, I will remark that [del]a weak kind[/del] [ins]an induction[/ins]
of a weak kind may nevertheless be a strong inference, while
an induction of a strong kind may be a weak inference,
owing to the greater or less multitude and variety of the
instances, and owing to be supported or opposed by other rea-
sonings.

In pure mathematics there is, properly speaking, no such
thing as mathematical probability, unless a special hy-
pothesis introduces it. What is called local probability
is again merely a variety of deduction. It follows that the
strong kind of induction has no place in pure mathematics.
For there is nothing like experience there, since there is no
appeal to promiscuous [del]cases[/del] instances of a course of life.
