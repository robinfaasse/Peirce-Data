§ 121. We have now to consider what are called the second and
third figures of syllogism. In Sir Thomas Wilson's * [u]Rule of Reason[/u]
we find this wonderful effort of human reason given as an
example under [u]Ferio[/u] in the first figure

"No extorcioner is godlie.
"Some riche man is an extorcioner.
"Ergo some riche man is not godlie."

The following would be an example of [u]Festino[/u], in the second figure.

No godlie man is an extorcioner.
Some riche man is an extorcioner.
Ergo some riche man is not godlie.

But the following would be an example of [u]Ferison[/u], in the third figure.

No extorcioner is godlie.
Some extorcioner is riche.
Ergo some riche man is not godlie.

* "Sir", not as being a Knight, but as being a clerk.
