§ 120. Besides these forms which are recognized in the Aristotelian
system, there are a variety of others that are not recognized.
Such for instance is the very common case where S is
an individual. More important is the following:

Some M is P;
Some S is some-M (i.e. the only Ms)
∴ Some S is P.

Essentially of this kind is an instance given by Laurentius
Valla (De dialectica, xlvii)
Aliquis locus est Tartarus,
Deus est ubique (i.e. in omni loco)
Ergo, Deus est in Tartaro.

Here is another:
Some visible is a satyr;
[del]Some[/del] [ins]All[/ins] men are the only visibles,
∴ Some men are satyrs.

Still more important is the following
All not-M is P, (i.e. Everything is either M or P)
S is not M;
∴ S is P.
