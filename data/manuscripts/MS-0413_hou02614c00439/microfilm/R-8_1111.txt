[u]Chapter IX.[/u]
[u]The Aristotelian Syllogistic.[/u]

§111. I shall in this chapter set forth the doctrine of [ins]the first part of the first book of[/ins] Aristotle's [u]Prior Analytics[/u] with some modifications which seem to me called for in order to perfect it.
We are to consider the syllogism, that is, [u]Barbara[/u] and its modifications. Barbara is a reasoning like this:
[u]All men who neglect their business go to ruin,[/u]
[u]This man neglects his business;[/u]
[u]∴ This man will go to ruin.[/u]

(Strictly speaking for [u]this man[/u], we ought to put [ins]instead[/ins] [u]all men of a given description[/u] to satisfy the definition of Barbara. But the character of the reasoning is the same.)

It is easy to overrate the importance of syllogism. Most of the older logics do that, in teaching that the substance of all reasoning lies in this. It is also easy to underrate it, as many have done. The truth is it is an essential element of almost all reasoning, perhaps of all.

§112. [del]It[/del] [ins]A syllogism[/ins] is a valid, [del]demonstrative[/del] [ins]eliminative[/ins], [u]complete[/u], and [u]simple[/u] argumentation.

To say that an argumentation is valid is to say that it is as truthful as it pretends to be. It is essential to reasoning, as already stated.
