If, the question being whether or not there is a future life, a disputant thinks to prove it by showing that a man's ideas may live after him, [del]that is, that he may influence posterity,[/del] he commits an ignoratio elenchi; for that is something nobody was ever such a fool as to contest.

The ignoratio elenchi is a fault of pedants and of those who are removed from the concrete facts. It belongs to wordy theorists. It is met with, occasionally, in writers of great power, especially of pedantic ages before the advent of modern science. Examples of it are to be met with in the writings of metaphysicians of no mean capacity.
