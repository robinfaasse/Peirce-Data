We have detected an intrinsic difference between the reasoning of the 2[ins]nd[/ins] and 3[ins]rd[/ins] figures, and in the two figures we have detected two analogous distinctions besides. But these moods were all obtained from the first figure by the principle of contraposition. The question arises, therefore, would a less assumption than the principle of contraposition have sufficed? Looking at the substance and meaning of it, nothing could be less. Substantially, it is no assumption at all. But looking at the form alone as capable of expressing [u]other[/u] meanings, — that is, looking at the matter with the generalizer's eye, less assumption would have sufficed. The principle of contraposition is that if from A follows from B, then from the denial of B follows the denial of A. But it would suffice [del]in the second[/del] to make use of the following formally less assumptions

[del]For [u]figure 2[/u].[/del] [ins]For[/ins] [u]the Second Figure[/u].
If from S is M follows S is P,
then from S is not P follows S is not M.

[u]For the Third Figure[/u]
If from M is P follows S is P,
then from some S is P follows some M is P.
