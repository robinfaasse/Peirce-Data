[u]All patriarchs are men[/u], and [u]all [del]sinners[/del] [ins]men[/ins] are mortal[/u].
[u]∴ If all men are sinners, all patriarchs are mortal[/u].
[u]∴ All patriarchs are mortal.[/u]
This last step is enthymematic. It [del]needs[/del] [ins]wants rigor[/ins], to make it logical, the expression of the leading principle, "All men are sinners". But put [u]men[/u] in place of [del]sinners[/del] [ins]men[/ins], and this becomes a logical principle, not needing to be set forth as a premise. [del]But[/del] It [del]seems[/del] [ins]however that[/ins] logicians recognize no difference between saying, "If all men are men, A is true," and saying directly A is true; and, I suppose they are right. We cannot recognize logical formulae as, properly speaking, assertions.[ins]*[/ins]

An [del]eliminative[/del] argumentation which is decomposable into syllogisms is called a [u]Sorites[/u], or [u]heap[/u].[ins]†[/ins]

***

[ins]\*[/ins] What Kant calls an explicative, [del]ju[/del] or analytical, judgment is either no judgment at all, because void of content (to use his phrase), or else it sets forth distinctly in the predicate what was only indistinctly thought (that is, not actually thought at all) in the subject. In that case, it is really synthetic, and rests on experience; only the experience on which it rests is mere internal experience—experience of our own imaginations. Association by resemblance, and association by contiguity, all lies in that great distinction.

[ins]† Syllogismus continuus is an expression used by Marius Victorinus in the fourth century.[/ins]
