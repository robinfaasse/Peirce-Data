If the middle term is undistributed in both premises, we
have the [del]fallacy of[/del] [ins]fallacy of[/ins] [u]Undistributed Middle[/u]. This is somewhat
similar to the fallacy of double distribution. Thus, the following is
a fallacy of undistributed middle.
All non-men are non-ogres;
All [del]non-children[/del] [ins]non-eaters of children[/ins] are non-ogres;
[u]∴ There are things besides non-men and non-eaters of children.[/u]

Now this is precisely the same argument called above a fallacy of
double distribution. But what is usually understood by a
fallacy of undistributed middle is such a one as the following
All non-men are non-ogres,
All non-eaters of children are non-ogres;
Hence, Some non-men are non-eaters of children.

This, otherwise stated, is as follows:
All ogres are men;
All ogres are eaters of children;
[u]∴ There are things besides men and eaters of children.[/u]
