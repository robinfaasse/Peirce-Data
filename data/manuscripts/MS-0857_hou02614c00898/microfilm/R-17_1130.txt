Thus of the [del]the[/del] two purposes of this course the second is to find the conditions of sound scientific reasoning, while the first purpose is, in view of these conditions and in view of whatever sound reasoning there may be that is not admissible into science, to say what intellectual trust we may [del]reason[/del] accord to the promptings of the religious instinct.

Of course, I necessarily begin with the second problem, that of the validity of reasonings in general. Now there are three kinds of reasoning & three only. I pass over the proof of this as I must in this course pass over the proofs of the majority of my propositions, though I shall [del]tell[/del] furnish hints sufficient for finding these proofs. The three kinds of reasoning may be designated by the letters A, B, C.
