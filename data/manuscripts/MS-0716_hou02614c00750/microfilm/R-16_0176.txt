When, she goes [ins]into the cellar[/ins] for the apples, she takes whatever bowl or basket comes handy, without caring what one, so long as it has a certain size, is clean, and has other qualities, but having once selected it, into that particular bowl she intends to put some apples. She takes any apples that are handy and seem good; but having taken them she means to make a pie of those apples. If she chances to see some others in the kitchen, on her return from the cellar, she will not use them for the pie, unless for some reason she changes her mind. Throughout her whole proceedings she [del]has[/del] [ins]pursues[/ins] an idea or dream without any particular thirdness and thatness, — or, as we say, haecceity, — to it; but this dream she wishes to realize in connection with an object of experience, which as such, does possess haecceity; and since she has to act, and action only relates to this and that, she has to be perpetually making random selections, that is, taking whatever comes handiest.

The dream itself has no prominent Thirdness; it is, on the contrary, [del]the most[/del] [ins]utterly[/ins] irresponsible; it is whatever it pleases. The object of experience [del]as real[/del] [ins]as[/ins] reality is a Second. But the desire [del]which seeks[/del] [ins]in seeking[/ins] to attach the one to the other is a Third, or medium.
