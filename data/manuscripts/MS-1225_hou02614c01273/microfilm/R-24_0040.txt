But as to this vernacular English of
Sayce, educated people have lost it in
great measure. What literary English is
they know; but what vernacular English
is, they don't very well know, and they
have no feeling of affection or admiration
for it. It really is a foreign tongue. This is
the very spirit of the language of us
educated men; and by English we
do not mean the resuscitated vernacular
but our own language. True, we don't
say "It is I," but "It's me". There are traces
of vernacular in our speech, which sprang
out the vernacular. But let a higher mood
seize us and we shall say "It is I," as na-
turally as possible, without the least reflec-
tion or self consciousness.
