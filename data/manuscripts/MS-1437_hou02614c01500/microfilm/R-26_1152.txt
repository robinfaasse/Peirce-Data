But it is easy to see that the animating motive
of Mr. Bacon's aeronautics, as it will be that of
his reader, was just the fun of the thing; and
we venture to opine that the work was quite
as useful to the human race, — we will not say
as it might have been if a Faraday had con-
ducted it, — but as it would have been if Mr.
Bacon's intellectual energies had been more com-
pletely absorbed in the scientific [ins]side of it.[/ins] [del]problems.[/del] For
every ascent is described so simply and without
straining for effect and yet so graphically in all
its details that one might wager a pretty penny
that, supposing the book to remain at hand and
leisure to be granted, it will not go long without
a rereading, by any given person who has
read it once. How many books are there that
would bear that kind of insurance?
