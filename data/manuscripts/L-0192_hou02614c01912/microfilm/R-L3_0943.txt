Boston, 11 November, 1898.

Mr. C. S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.

Dear Sir:

Yours of the 5th came duly to hand and we have looked it through with great interest. We discover that you propose to make three small volumes, but from the estimate you give we see they will each contain about 345 pages. From your description of your work it is evidently scholastic and belongs to a period [ins]of thought[/ins] which rather antedates the present marked interest in science. No college attempts to give in its regular courses and with the use of a text book such work in logic as your plan contemplates. In post graduate and elective courses much of the matter that you describe is presented in the form of lectures while the class has a single and small manual.

It is this small manual that we had in view in asking you to make a logic. We should perhaps have written you more fully as to what our ideas were. We thought, however, it would be best to find out first whether you would consider the making of such a book. There is evidently no money for either you or us in such a book or series of books as you have suggested even upon the assumption that in subject matter the work is above criticism.

Yours respectfully,

D. C. HEATH & CO.

H.
