[del](5)[/del] (6) { [del]\begin{array}{c} M \prec N \\ \therefore (L \prec M) \prec (L \prec N) \end{array}[/del]
or just as well, (7) [del]{[/del] [ins]:[/ins] [del]\begin{array}{c} L \prec M \\ \therefore (M \prec N) \prec (L \prec N) \end{array}[/del]

I call this last the [u]Formula of Contraposition[/u], the appropriateness of which
designation may be illustrated by a leash of examples, thus:

Any man would be an animal;
[del]\therefore[/del] If any animal would be mortal, then any man would be mortal.

[del]Christ's feeding thousands with a few loaves and fishes would be a miracle;[/del]
[del]\therefore If any miracle would be fabulous, then Christ's feeding a host with a dozen loaves and fishes would be fabulous.[/del]
