Every institution [ins][del]because the adjustment of means to ends is beyond human calculation beforehand.[/del][/ins] should be a growth. Its first beginning should, above all, have vitality, and nothing to check its inherent power of growth; and this is more true of a university than of anything else.

Its vitality will consist in its being animated with the kind of ideal which is always bound to stretch beyond itself.

Every prescribed end for a university must ultimately cramp it and become a narrow end. A narrow end for an individual is sure to result in disappointment; but it may or may not defeat itself. A narrow end in a university, where probabilities must work themselves out into statistics, is necessarily bound to defeat itself in great measure.
