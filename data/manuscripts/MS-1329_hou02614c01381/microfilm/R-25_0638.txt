[u]Oxford[/u] "Rises into eminence 12[ins]th[/ins] c.; first styled University 1201; the
oldest of the colleges, University College, founded 1249." The
idea of its existing before 12[ins]th[/ins] c. is absurd. It arose during
that century and attained some considerable size. The
other statements are correct, though that about the
"university" is excessively misleading, that word
then having a totally different meaning. Of course,
the name University College is modern. It is
"The College of the Great Hall." The foundation in 1249
means that a will dated that year gives a sum of
money to support some masters. But the connection
of the college with that endowment seems to be
made out.

[u]Cambridge[/u]. "Rises into eminence about 1200; designation of university
first applied 1231; the oldest of the colleges St. Peter's College,
founded 1257." In 1209, said to have 3000 scholars. In
1231 first officially mentioned, and called university. The
statement about Peterhouse [del]may[/del] [ins]is[/ins] [del]be[/del] correct. Certain
scholars were brought into the Hostel of St. John some
time between 1257 and 1280, and in 1284 were
transferred to Peterhouse, and 1284 is the date
of the charter of the College.
