teach it, unless he has given to the study any one-third as
many days, amid the manifold distractions of a modern college,
as the scholastics used to devote in their cloisters to the
perusal of the relatively few great works their libraries con-
tained.

Would it be very hardy to entertain a suspicion that,
in a department where surpassing ignorance is treated with
relative respect, and where thorough accomplishment is in
no country insisted upon, possible defective scholarship may
sometimes obtain a lodgment even in the most exalted chairs?
