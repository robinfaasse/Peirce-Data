If history be conceived, neither as mere narration of
events, nor as a study of absolute politics, but as an ac-
count of man's development, then the history of the mind is
the main thing, and that of thought must stand at its head.
Very different to different sciences is the importance of
their own chronicles. Thus, though mathematics, in general,
can boast of a long and interesting past, it is neither need-
ful nor usual for mathematicians to know much about it. At
the same time, some exceptional branches, such as the theory
of elasticity, are always studied in the discussions which
developed them. An examination of the beginnings of mechan-
ics throws, as is well known, no little light upon the science
itself; while physicists are little given to concerning them-
selves about early optics or meteorology. In metaphysical
philosophy the history is often held to be more important
than the doctrine.

through the requisite training in philosophy before they would
allow him to teach it. The metaphysics of those days was the-

stand that in Paris, after a man had taken his degree of mas-
