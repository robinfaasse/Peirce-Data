Now that you understand what a series is, and what numerable, enumerable, and [del]simple[/del] [ins]denumerable[/ins] series are, you can explain that there are several [del]kinds[/del] [ins]different series[/ins] of numbers, (which are otherwise called numerals,) each of which is employed in some special way to distinguish the different members of a series from one another. Those numbers which are used in vulgar arithmetic, are [u]ordinal[/u] numbers, [u]cardinal[/u] numbers, and [u]fractions[/u].

The ordinal numbers are adjectives applied to the different members of a denumerable series; [u]first[/u], [u]second[/u] (or next after the first), [u]third[/u] (next after the second), [u]fourth[/u], etc. The words are generally, in all Aryan languages formed by adding [u]th[/u] to the cardinal numbers.
