One which [del]is[/del] [ins]alone[/ins] used in classical Latin the dictionaries recognize. It means clotted, being the past participle of [u]concrescere[/u], to clot. When we speak of a certain mixture of cement and pebbles or larger stones as "concrete", that word "concrete" comes from the Latin [u]concretum[/u], clotted. But there was in medieval Latin a verb [u]concernere[/u], to concern, whose past participle [del]was[/del] likewise took the form [u]concretum[/u], concerned, and there is historical evidence that when grammarians first called the adjectives [del]concer[/del] [u]verba concreta[/u], concrete words, they meant words concerned with the things.
