16[/pn] and remember this: [del]To use[/del] [ins]One must use[/ins] common, vague words
as instinct makes us use them. One cannot do otherwise: it is human
nature. But if a word is precise, it must be that philosophers have
expended great labor of thinking to bestow upon us the precise idea
that word embodies. To use such a word in a wrong sense tends to
confuse human thought; and when God said, "Let us make man in
our image, after our likeness," what is meant is that man has thought
which is like Divine thought. To do anything to render it less
perfect is a sin. The latter writers are apt to think that only
what [u]actually has happened[/u] is true, while in fact that which surely
[u]would happen[/u] under described circumstances is as true and is more
important, because it guides our conduct more directly.
