The proper way to use this algebra is, in my opinion, with a light touch. Considerable can easily be done with it. Much [del]can[/del] be done with it at the expense of [del]much[/del] [ins]great[/ins] puzzling. If anybody is in love with puzzling, this algebra affords him a decent scope. XIII.

Let us now see how our apparatus works practically. Some very fine examples have been given by MacColl of the transformation of multiple definite integrals & by Schröder. But I will merely mention those, and gang my own gait.

The most rudimentary kind of mathematics is that whose system of values comprises but two degrees. This is the very system of demonstrative logic which we have been studying.

Next in the order of rudimentariness, fundamentalness, and abstractness comes arithmetic. This [del]depends on[/del] [ins]springs out of[/ins] the doctrine of multitude. This depends on the consideration of one-to-many relations.
