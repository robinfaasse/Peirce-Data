The Algebra of Dyadic Relatives was well explained by me in
outline in the book "Studies in Logic" and Schröder has developed
it in his third volume at such enormous length that it is not worth
while to devote much space to it here.
The Boolean calculus turns upon the three operations of
negation, aggregation, and composition. There are three [del]somewhat[/del]
imperfectly analogous operations of a relative kind. By a rela-
tive operation I mean such an operation that the relate and cor-
relate of the function are differently ordered from those of the variable.
The non-relative operation of composition or non-relative logical
multiplication is defined thus:
(l·b)ᵢⱼ ⤙ 0 if lᵢⱼ ⤙ 0 or bᵢⱼ ⤙ 0 But
U ⤙ (l·b)ᵢⱼ if both [del]U ⤙[/del] U ⤙ lᵢⱼ and U ⤙ bᵢⱼ
The operation of relative multiplication is defined thus:
(lb)ᵢⱼ ⤙ 0 if [del]as[/del] Πₖ lᵢₖ + bₖⱼ ⤙ 0
U ⤙ (lb)ᵢⱼ if U ⤙ Σₖ lᵢₖ · bₖⱼ
We read lb "l of b"
The non-relative operation of aggregation is defined thus
(l + b)ᵢⱼ ⤙ 0 if both lᵢⱼ ⤙ 0 and bᵢⱼ ⤙ 0
U ⤙ (l + b)ᵢⱼ if either U ⤙ lᵢⱼ or U ⤙ bᵢⱼ
The operation of relative addition is defined thus:
(l † b)ᵢⱼ ⤙ 0 if Σₖ lᵢₖ · bₖⱼ ⤙ 0
U ⤙ (l † b)ᵢⱼ if U ⤙ Πₖ lᵢₖ + bₖⱼ
We read l † b "l except of bs"
