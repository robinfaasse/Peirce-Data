Considering collections, the sum of two quantities A and B, is that which in the [del]general form[/del] [ins]definitions of[/ins] A and B, allows their common part to stand and [del]removes[/del] [ins]is[/ins] that which distinguishes A from B, while retaining what distinguishes A and B from other things. Thus, if A is the collection of P and Q and B is the collection of U, V, and W. A+B is the collection of P, Q, U, V, W. If the order of arrangement is essential in the collections A and B, it will likewise be so of course in A+B. For non-relative logic, or the logic of monadic terms, no other algebraical operation but this is required.

The product of two collections is the collection of [ins]each[/ins] pairs formed out of a unit of one factor joined to a unit of the other factor. Thus, if A=P+Q and B=U+V+W
A x B = (PU) + (PV) + (PW) + (QU) + (QV) + (QW).
