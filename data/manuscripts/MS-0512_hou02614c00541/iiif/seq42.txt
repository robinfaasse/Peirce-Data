I proceed to expound the algebra of Dyadic Relatives.
If we consider a dyadic relative l, [ins]"loves,"[/ins] we notice that it has
a converse, "is loved by", which I write [u]l[/u]. So that
[u]l[/u]ij = lji .
This is a peculiarity of [u]dyadic relatives[/u]. With triadic relatives, we
have two fundamental modes of transposing the indices. I may,
for instance, write
(G l)ijk = lkji
(A l)ijk = ljki

"substitution" as the mathematicians call it) repeated restores the
original [del]of[/del] arrangement, while the latter only does so when it has
been thrice performed. Moreover AG = GA^-1 . These three conditions
