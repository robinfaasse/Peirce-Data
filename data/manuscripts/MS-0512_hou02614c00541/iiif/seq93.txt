Thence we write
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_i \ q_{\alpha i}$$ and $$\overline{\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_i \ q_{\alpha i}}$$
where $$\alpha$$ belongs to the universe of qualities neither absurd nor necessary.
And in like manner
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k \ r_{\alpha i j k}$$ etc. and $$\overline{\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k \ r_{\alpha i j k}}$$ etc
where there is nothing peculiarly necessary nor impossible about any
of the indices. So too,
$$\Pi_i \Sigma_{\alpha} \ q_{\alpha i}$$ and $$\overline{\Pi_i \Sigma_{\alpha} \ q_{\alpha i}}$$
$$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\alpha} \ r_{\alpha i j}$$ etc. and $$\overline{\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\alpha} \ r_{\alpha i j}}$$.
[del]But[/del] And so likewise
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_i \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\alpha \beta i}$$ and $$\overline{\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_i \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\alpha \beta i}}$$
and $$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\alpha \beta i j}$$ and $$\overline{\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\alpha \beta i j}}$$
Since $$\alpha$$ can be any character (not peculiarly necessary nor impossible)
let us so take it that
$$r_{\alpha \beta i j} = r_{\beta i} \cdot r_{\beta j}$$ or $$\overline{r_{\beta i} \cdot r_{\beta j}}$$
and we have
$$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\beta i} \cdot r_{\beta j}$$ and $$\overline{\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\beta i} \cdot r_{\beta j}}$$
But the last proposition would be self-contradictory unless $$j$$ were restricted
to being [u]other than $$i$$[/u]; so that we must write for it
$$\overline{\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\beta i} \cdot ( \overline{r}_{\beta j} + 1_{ij} )}$$
So in the other proposition $$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\beta i} \cdot r_{\beta j}$$, it must be understood
that $$i$$ and $$j$$ must not be the very reverse of one another in all respects
We thus see that such a proposition as $$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_{\beta} \ r_{\alpha \beta i j}$$ will mean
very little when we undertake to bring it down to a special case.
