Into the product thus obtained, it may be requisite to multiply
propositions expressing general properties of the special universes re-
ferred to. This will be the second step.
For example, if there are indices denoting characters, it may
be necessary to take account of the fact that any collection of objects
of the same universe possess some common character possessed by
no other objects.
But the resources of the algebra as hitherto described are not ade-
quate to expressing a proposition so general as this. We may write
$\Pi_i \Sigma_\alpha \Pi_x \ q_{\alpha i} \cdot (l_{ix} + \overline{q}_{\alpha x})$
or $\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_\alpha \Pi_x \ q_{\alpha i} \cdot q_{\alpha j} \cdot (l_{ix} + l_{jx} + \overline{q}_{\alpha x})$
but we cannot express the truth of [u]every[/u] collection whatsoever.
An innumerable collection of such propositions would be required to
express what is wanted. The indices and row of quantifiers enable us
to express at once the equivalent of an innumerable collection of pro-
positions such as a pure Boolian can express. We need, therefore, some
similar [del]xxx[/del] symbols to enable us to express propositions still more
general. I mention the matter under the head of this second step, because
I do not know that I have ever found such [del]proj[/del] symbols needed
for [del]the[/del] special premises derived from observation.
