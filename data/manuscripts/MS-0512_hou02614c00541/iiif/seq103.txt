In [del]non relative[/del] [ins]monadic[/ins] logic we have a mere universe of things which we can
represent by dots, which we can enclose in bounds to represent predicates.
In dyadic logic, we have a universe of pairs which we can represent
by [del]lines[/del] [ins]arrows[/ins] between the dots. In triadic logic, we deal with distributions
each of which requires a diagram of the universe of dots to represent it.
A distribution is a state of things, and we deal with the universe of logi-
cally possible states of things.

It seems to be pretty clear that if there be any satisfactory Algebra
of triadic Logic it must depend on the use of exponents to denote
the logically possible distributions of all the members of one collection
among members of another. But I have never succeeded in per-
fecting it.

[u]XIV[/u].

In order to cut the Gordian Knot, I introduced the use of Greek
letters as indices, they each of them denoting one of the logically pos-
sible sets of things. I call them characters, adopting De Morgan's
dictum that every set of things has some common and peculiar
character. The effect of this is to remove the difficulty from
the algebra and leave it to be considered in itself. For example,
I then easily write $m^d \prec (\Pi a)$ as follows:
$\Pi_\alpha \Sigma_1 \Sigma_2 \Sigma_3 \Sigma_4 \Sigma_5 \Sigma_6 \Pi_\eta \Pi_8 \Pi_9 \Sigma_0$
$r_{\alpha12} \cdot r_{\alpha13} \cdot r_{23} + r_{\alpha45} \cdot m_5 + r_{\alpha6\eta} \cdot d_6 + m_\eta + r_{\alpha 89} + a_0 \cdot \Pi_{\alpha 89}$
But this leaves $\prec$ subject to rules which I cannot fully express
in algebraical form. For example, the maxim of De Morgan.
