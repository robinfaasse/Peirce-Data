The use of "characters" as indices makes it possible to express
any mathematical propositions, if it can be called expressing them
to resort to this device; but when it comes to the deeper problems
for which a calculus of logic ought to be chiefly useful, this method
furnishes little but memoranda. It will be noticed that the ex-
pressions by means of characters are fearfully long and complex.
Still, that cannot be said to be a fault as long as it is the only
method of expression we have, so that we cannot be sure that
anything simpler is possible. This method renders necessary a doctrine
of mathematical possibility outside of the algebra itself.
Let us trace out a little reasoning in this way. Given any two
collections, the P's and the Q's, it is evident that there is a relation
in which every P stands to every Q and nothing else to anything
nor anything to anything else.
$\Pi_\alpha \Pi_\beta \sum_\gamma \Pi_1 \Pi_2 q_{\alpha 1} \cdot q_{\beta 2} \cdot r_{\gamma 12} + \overline{q}_{\alpha 1} \cdot \overline{r}_{\gamma 12} + \overline{q}_{\beta 2} \cdot \overline{r}_{\gamma 12}$
$= (q_{\alpha 1} \cdot q_{\beta 2} + \overline{r}_{\gamma 12}) \cdot (r_{\gamma 12} + \overline{q}_{\alpha 1} + \overline{q}_{\beta 2})$
