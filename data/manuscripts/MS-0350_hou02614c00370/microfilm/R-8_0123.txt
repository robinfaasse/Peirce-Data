Other parts of Dr. Whewells books seem to show that if this dif-
ficulty were urged against his theory he would support it by another
which if proved would bring him out triumphantly. To Mr. Mills
objection that the condition of a hypothesis accounting for all the
known phenomena is "often fulfilled equally well by two conflict-
ing hypotheses", Whewell replies, "I know of no such case in the
history of science, where the phenomena are at all complicated; and
if such a case were to occur, one of the hypotheses might always be
resolved into the other." This seems to mean that there is only one
conception which will colligate one set of facts. That consequently
if there are two conceptions which will colligate nearly the same
facts, they must be species of one general conception which will
colligate the sum of all the facts colligated by both the others. Conse-
quently, that when any hypothesis is broached which will colligate
among others some of the facts required to be colligated, that hy-
pothesis if not true comprehends the true hypothesis [del]mixed[/del] [ins]with[/ins] an ad-
