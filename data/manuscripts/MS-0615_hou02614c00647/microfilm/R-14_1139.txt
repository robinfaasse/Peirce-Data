LOGIC.

Book I. Analysis of Thought.

Chapter I. Common Ground.

§1. Those enormous numbers which some popular writers on science are wont to parade never particularly struck me, partly because a million is a multitude which, though I understand, I cannot imagine; and I disbelieve those writers doing so. The stars visible in the sky make, I guess, about as large a multitude as anybody can directly imagine[del], or with imagining it in two steps as a collection of equal collections[/del] [ins]with the naked eye[/ins].

Now the number of stars that can be seen at once, without such close scrutiny as one can bestow only upon a [ins]very[/ins] small part of the heavens at a time, is the average number of stars above the fifth magnitude that are over 15° from the true horizon; and that number is a trifle less than 500.
