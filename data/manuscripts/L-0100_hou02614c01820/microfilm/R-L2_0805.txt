The instruction of Mr. Peirce is conducted
entirely by means of practical exercises to
be performed by the pupil, with the neces-
sary explanations of how they are to be exe-
cuted, suitable after reflections upon the
nature of the proceeding and why it has
been successful, with criticism of faults,
and with answers to questions and resolutions
of perplexities. The theory of the subject
will not be neglected; but it will never
be divorced from its practical applications
nor touched upon before living examples
have made the necessity and signifi-
cance of it apparent. All education,
broad or narrow, is intended to teach
