# The Art of Reasoning

TAUGHT BY CORRESPONDENCE.

By C. S. PEIRCE, A. M., S. B. (in Chemistry, *summa cum laude*), MEMBER
OF THE U. S. NATIONAL ACADEMY OF SCIENCES, OF THE LONDON MATH-
EMATICAL SOCIETY, etc., DELEGATE TO THE EUROPEAN GEODESICAL
ASSOCIATION, FORMERLY LECTURER ON THE LOGIC OF SCIENCE
IN HARVARD AND THE JOHNS HOPKINS UNIVERSITIES.

Mr. Peirce has for a quarter of a century been a devoted student of
methods of reasoning, and his proficiency in the subject is widely acknowl-
edged. He has elaborated with great care, and as the fruit of much expe-
rience, a system of mental gymnastics and reflections, calculated to develop
genuine practical skill in reasoning.

The exercises are molded to the individual needs of each pupil. His bad
habits in thinking are corrected, and he is made familiar with valuable meth-
ods of attacking problems. The general course embraces three parts. Part
First teaches all that is useful in the old logic, the art of arranging ideas, and
how to make the thoughts clear and distinct. Part Second teaches math-
ematical precision in reasoning, with instruction in the doctrine of chances.
Part Third teaches the art of investigation, how to ask questions, how to con-
sult a library, how to select fair and instructive instances of any phenomenon,
with all the precautions to be used in making induction and hypotheses.

The instruction is conducted by correspondence. Thirty letters are called
a "quarter," calculated to occupy about three months. The entire course usu-
ally requires six quarters. Each lesson gives the pupil work for four hours.

The improvement is at the end of each quarter brought to a positive and
indisputable test, consisting, not in an examination of the memory, but in
the performance of a task of real reasoning in comparison with the per-
formance of a like task at the beginning of the quarter.

Diplomas on the completion of the course.

Circulars sent on application. Letters of inquiry cannot be answered.

TERMS $30.00 PER QUARTER, IN ADVANCE.

Address MR. C. S. PEIRCE, 36 WEST 15TH STREET, NEW-YORK.

The President of a University, one of the ablest and most celebrated men
in the country, writes: "I am not sure that I sha'n't ask you myself to teach
me how to think. I have found it an extremely difficult and painful task."
Many applications are received from highly educated people; but the
instruction is particularly useful to those who have not had a college train-
ing. Instructors by old methods are earnestly invited to acquaint themselves
with this system.
