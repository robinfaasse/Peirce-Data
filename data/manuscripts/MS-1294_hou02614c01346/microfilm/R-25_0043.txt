[u]Egyptian Science[/u]

In New York, once a week, is published a
journal which describes new inventions and
such things. It is called the Scientific American;
because Americans are apt to imagine that
sort of thing is scientific. In the [del]the[/del] [ins]corners[/ins] of this
journal which might otherwise be vacant
to be found receipts for taking out grease spots,
making hens lay, setting horse-shoes, and the
like, and the journal having existed for a
good many years, a collection of these things
was at last published under the title of the
Cyclopaedia of Experimental Science. You might
imagine such a title would deter people from
buying the book; but not at all; the [del]reason[/del] [ins]is[/ins]
people in the United States fancy that that
kind of thing constitutes science. The famed
"lore of the Egyptians" [ins]was[/ins] very much like that.
