Between five and six thousand years after the erection of
the great pyramid, it happened that in the most advanced
country in the world a mountain had to be tunnelled [ins]for a railroad some five miles long[/ins]. Work
was done from the two ends and the two [del]tunnels[/del] [ins]excavations[/ins] actually
passed one another, and the workmen in each heard
those in the other working at their side. Now the [del]mean[/del]
error of the great pyramid angles is 1/15 of a minute of
arc. This is seven inches on ten thousand feet, or on five
miles less than two feet. It is presumable that business
men who undertake [del]to[/del] making a tunnel five miles
long employ as good surveyors as they can find. Yet since
these nineteenth century surveyors made an error about
ten times that of the surveyors of 3600 B.C., the latter
were (10)² or 100 times as expert. But this is not all. Work
had to be begun at those four corners which did not see one
another at the same level. It is quite likely that changes in
the direction of gravity, produced by tilting of the ground or
otherwise, may have deranged this level since that time.
