[ins]Students of the[/ins] [del]The[/del] descriptive sciences, then, have for their principal object, the explanation of the causes of the [ins]individual[/ins] [del]phenomena[/del] phenomena they observe; but they add to this business that of making any other observations which their special instruments and training render them particularly competent to make. Geology has always put its explanatory duty in the foreground;—a circumstance which gives it a dignity which is not shared by such a science as physical geography, a vague subject pursued, on the whole, by relatively inferior men. A descriptive [del]science[/del] [ins]science[/ins] not animated by a definite explanatory purpose can hardly have the unity of a science. Thus, hydrography is cultivated by sending out vessels with a crew of naturalists and physicists of different kinds; and if we subtract from hydrography what these men contribute to their several more definitely defined sciences, what remains is [ins]a[/ins] somewhat miscellaneous parcel of casually collected facts.
