A good description must have a purpose, and must select
facts conducive to it. It is essential, at the very threshold of
logic, to distinguish between a [u]percept[/u], which is what the
senses perceive, and which is an object of study for the intellect,
and a [u]perceptual fact[/u], which the understanding perceives in
the percept, and which is the first fruit of observation. Photo-
graphs, even when they show no more than the eye can see, are
most valuable in sciences of observation; they are stored up
percepts. But they [del]by no means[/del] [ins]in no degree[/ins] enable us
to dispense with scientific descriptions, which are records of
perceptual facts, the basis of all else in science. But a descrip-
tion must have a purpose; that is, it is to record not all sorts
