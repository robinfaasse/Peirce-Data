CHAPTER II. PRELOGICAL NOTIONS
Section I. Classification of the Sciences.
[u]Auguste Comte[/u] it was, as far as I am informed,* who first had the idea of arranging the sciences in a ladder, each leading to the next. From the point of view of logic, at least, this is decidedly the best arrangement; because, in order to prove one thing to be true, if one is to proceed beyond mere perceptual facts, it is necessary to assume the truth of something else, while to attempt to prove this something else by assuming the truth of the former proposition would be to complete a vicious circle. It does not very often happen that there are [del]several[/del] [ins]two[/ins] independent ways of proving the same thing; and when this does happen, one of these ways will usual be more satisfactory or more fundamental than the other. When this is not so, it is to be presumed that time will show that both the general premisses, or principles, are cases under one still more general which would suffice for proving whatever they both prove.
