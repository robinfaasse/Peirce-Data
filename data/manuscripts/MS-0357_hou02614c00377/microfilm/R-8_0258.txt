Most of the classifications of the sciences which have
been proposed rest upon a classification of the things
of which they treat. This method is objectionable for two reasons
first that many sciences treat of everything — as the science
of mechanics, that of geometry, that of chemistry & so forth; se-
cond because the classification of things needs to rest
upon a classification of sciences. The first great division
of things is according to their functions & since it is that
the homologies or likenesses which pervade a whole
kingdom of nature as mouths, stomachs, locomotive
apparatus among animals are functional homologies.
