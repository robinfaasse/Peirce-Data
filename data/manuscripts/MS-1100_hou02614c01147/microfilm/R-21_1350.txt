The apparatus used was an adaptation of a "Fairbanks" post-office scale; upon the end of the beam of the scale was fixed a square enlargement (about 1-2 in. square), with a flat top, which served to convey the pressure to the finger in a manner to be presently described. This was tightly covered with an india-rubber cap, to prevent sensations of cold, &c., from contact with the metal. A kilogramme placed in the pan of the balance brought a pressure of one-fourth of its weight upon the finger. The differential pressure was produced by lowering upon the pan of the balance a smaller pan into which the proper weights could be firmly fixed; this little pan had its bottom of cork and was placed upon a piece of flannel which constantly remained in the pan of the balance. It was lifted off and on by means of an fine india-rubber thread which was so much stretched by the weight as certainly to avoid any noise.
