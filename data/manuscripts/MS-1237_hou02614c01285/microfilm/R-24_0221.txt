[u]Species c.[/u] Some verbs of various signification take
sometimes [u]à[/u] and sometimes [u]de[/u].

[u]Examples[/u]

[u]Consentir[/u]. This generally takes [u]à[/u] but often [u]de[/u], the latter par-
ticularly when the consent is passive, the former when a promise to
help is implied.

L'amour-propre consent bien
à être blâmé.

César lui-même ici consent de
vous entendre.

[u]Entendre[/u]. In some of its meanings this verb can only take an
indicative [ins](or conditional)[/ins] with [u]que[/u], in others only the subjunctive. But when
it gives consent and approval it best takes [u]à[/u]. When it goes
further and implies conspiring it generally takes [u]pour[/u], but
[del]make[/del] [ins]may[/ins] take [u]de[/u]. When it means to suffer or consent to a disagree-
able thing it takes an infinitive [ins]or noun[/ins] without a preposition, as it
sometimes does when it means to know how, though in this case
it generally takes [u]à[/u].

J'entends dire que vous
avez vos bandes noirs à Paris.

Je n'entends point parler
à ceux qui n'écoutent per-
sonne.

On ne veut entendre à
confirmer rien.

Il s'entend à cela comme
à ramer des choux.

Ils s'entendent pour
vous tromper.
