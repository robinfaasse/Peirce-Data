[pn]143[/pn] The periphery of a place is the number of interruptions of dimensionality 2 less than that of the place which are required in order to leave the place such that it can contain no non-singular surface that cannot by a [del]real[/del] continuous motion [ins]without rupture or joining[/ins] within the place approach indefinitely toward the condition of a line.

Thus the periphery of one side of a disk is 0. That of the solidity of a globe is 0. That of the surface of a globe is 1. That of the solidity of a ring is [del]0[/del] [ins]1[/ins]. That of the surface of a ring is 1. That of the solidity of a box is 1. That of a solid with three hollows is 3. The periphery of any line and of any ordinary [ins]solid[/ins] [del]surface[/del] is 0.

The apeiry of a place is the number of interruptions of dimensionality 3 less than that of the place in question which are required in order to leave the place such that it can contain no-nonsingular solid that cannot by a continuous motion, without rupture or welding, within the place, approach indefinitely toward being a surface.

The apeiry of all space is either zero or one according as we suppose its infinitely distant parts to be shaped. It is best regarded as having all [del]its[/del] its Listing Numbers equal to 1.

Every place has a "census-value" which consists of the Census [ins]number[/ins] [del]value[/del] of its points minus that of its lines plus that of its surfaces minus that of its solids.

The Census number of any homogeneous space is equal to its Chorisy minus its Cyclosy plus its Periphery minus [del]its[/del] [ins]its[/ins] Apeiry.
