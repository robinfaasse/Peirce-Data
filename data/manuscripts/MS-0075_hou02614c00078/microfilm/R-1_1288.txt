A multiple algebra is one in which there are units of different [del]kinds[/del] [ins]qualities[/ins] capable of being added and multiplied together. Thus, ordinary algebra is a double algebra the units being unity and [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] square root of negative unity. An associative algebra is one in which the multiplication of the units, though not generally commutative, is always associative. A linear algebra is one in which the number of linearly independent expressions is finite; but this restriction seems [del]of[/del] [ins]to be of[/ins] little moment.
