[pn]II.*[/pn]

Multiplication expresses relation. Thus, [u]i[/u] A expresses the quantity which is related to A in the manner signified by [u]i[/u]. We may then for any algebra imagine another non-relative algebra, the expressions of which can only be multiplicands or products, never multipliers. Let [u]i[/u] be any unit of a multiplying algebra, and let the general expression of the non-relative algebra be [u]a[/u] I + [u]b[/u] J + [u]c[/u] K + [u]d[/u] L + etc. [ins]where [u]a[/u], [u]b[/u], [u]c[/u], [u]d[/u], etc are scalars.[/ins] Let the product of this by [u]i[/u] be written thus,

( A₁ [u]a[/u] + A₂ [u]b[/u] + A₃ [u]c[/u] + etc ) I
+ ( B₁ [u]a[/u] + B₂ [u]b[/u] + B₃ [u]c[/u] + etc ) J
+ ( C₁ [u]a[/u] + C₂ [u]b[/u] + C₃ [u]c[/u] + etc ) K
+ etc.

Then, by an enlargement of the multiplying algebra, we may suppose [u]i[/u] separated into a sum as follows
[u]i[/u] = A₁ [u]i[/u]₁₁ + A₂ [u]i[/u]₁₂ + A₃ [u]i[/u]₁₃ + etc
+ B₁ [u]i[/u]₂₁ + B₂ [u]i[/u]₂₂ + B₃ [u]i[/u]₂₃ + etc
+ C₁ [u]i[/u]₃₁ + C₂ [u]i[/u]₃₂ + C₃ [u]i[/u]₃₃ + etc
+ etc
