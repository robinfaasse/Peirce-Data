That "infinites and infinitesimals are non-existent in
thought, qua infinite and qua infinitesimal," is (1) a propo-
sition in psychology, concerning which mathematicians
are no authority; (2) it is not a proposition concerning
which mathematicians are agreed; (3) it is false; (4) even
if it were true it would have no bearing on the question
of the equation A + i = A.

You say yourself that you have not read, or at least
not deeply considered, G. Cantor's papers. I can see that
other contributions have escaped you. You are therefore
in a situation analogous to that of those mathema-
ticians who 20 years ago had not read, or had not
sufficiently pondered, their Lobatchevsky.

[del]The question[/del] The question does not involve any
great learning in the intricate parts of mathematics,
nor any great power of dealing with mathematical
problems proper. It requires a good comprehension
of the outlines of mathematics, and a very deep
study of logic in general. I am quite confident that
in this subject you will find, as our discussion
goes on, that my ideas are neither antiquated nor
imperfectly coordinated. But it is quite proper you
should express the impression they make upon you
at the outset, and [ins]there is[/ins] [del]not[/del] the slightest offence in it.
