Washington, D.C. [date]5 July, 1889[/date].

My Dear Pierce:

From your note of [date]July 2nd[/date] I infer that I made a very stupid blunder in turning over the Century Dictionary by stumbling over Argus, and forgetting that that was not the correct name of the constellation. As I intimated in one of my letters my examination was very hasty, and not at all critical. I should be sorry to suppose that a few criticisms of details which make up so infinitesimal a portion of the work should injure its sale. I supposed I was also rendering a service by promptly pointing out the advisability of further examination and revision.

At this moment I have not a copy of the Principia within reach; but I cannot suppose that Newton intended the remark which you quote to serve as a definition of axio in the enunciation of the third law.

Yours very truly,
S. Newcomb

C.S.Pierce, Esq.
Milford, Pa.
