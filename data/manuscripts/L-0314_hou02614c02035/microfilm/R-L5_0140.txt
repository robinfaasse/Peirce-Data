January 25, 1904.

My dear Peirce:

Pardon my delay in answering your letter of the 15th. The fact is, I am so much occupied with the Congress and other work that I have wholly dropped out of the field of logic.

It seems to me you must suffer from intellectual loneliness where you are situated, but I do not know of anything to be done to help the matter. So far as getting things printed is concerned, I am in much the same boat with yourself, having a great number of unfinished works, but it would not pay for a publisher to bring them out, and no institution, so far as I yet know, is ready to undertake the publication.

As to foreign associates of the French Academy; there are eight, not merely five. I have not a list of them in the past, and do not know where to look for one. I remember reading some notice, perhaps in the Nation, of De Candolle's book, but never saw a copy, and do not know either the title or the exact contents. Unless the book is in the Astor or the Harvard Library I do not know where we could find anything about it, except by making inquiry at Geneva.

Very sincerely,
S. Newcomb

Mr. Charles S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.
