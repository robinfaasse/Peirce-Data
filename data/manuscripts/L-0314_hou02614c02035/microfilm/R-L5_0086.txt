Milford Pa 1889 Jan 17

My dear Newcomb:

If the following proposition be not too well-known or obvious, possibly the proof I give of it may be worth filling up some corner of your journal with some time.

"Through a point in space let 4 right lines be drawn. Let 4 points, assumed one on each of these lines, be taken as the vertices of a tetrahedron; and let a second tetrahedron be formed in the same way. Then the lines of intersection of corresponding planes of the tetrahedra lie in one plane.
