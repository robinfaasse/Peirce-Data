My dear Newcomb

The following question recently put to me seems to involve fundamental points relating to [del]the[/del] infinity. I should like to see how you would answer it.

[fig]Diagram showing a horizontal line OX with perpendiculars OA, BC, DE, FG and an oblique line OS[/fig] Upon the straight line OX erect an infinite series of equidistant perpendiculars OA, BC, DE, FG, etc and draw the oblique line OS. Then the space included between [del]OAX[/del] [ins]AOX[/ins] and the circle at infinity, bears some finite ratio to the space included in AOS and the circle at infinity; But it bears an infinite ratio to the space included between two successive perpendiculars the base line and the circle at infinity. Hence the diagonal OS must cross all the perpendiculars, contrary to Lobatchevsky. Where is the fallacy?

Yours very truly, C. S. Peirce
