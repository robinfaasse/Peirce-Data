My dear Newcomb:

It is true I am one who write, though your letter is not signed; and [del]it[/del] is always of great advantage to me to have your criticisms. I infer, however, that you have not read the important papers of G. Cantor, which are condensed in Vol 3 of the [del]the[/del] Acta Mathematica. I do not agree [ins]with[/ins] [del]the[/del] whole of Cantor's conclusions, but regard his most fundamental points as established and among others that one can perfectly well reason about infinite quantities. However, I do not derive this opinion from Cantor, but had already before seeing those papers reached demonstrative conviction of this. In fact, the general forms of reasoning and perfectly applicable to infinite quantities, and it is finite quantities which are [ins]peculiar in that[/ins] [del]peculiar in that[/del] certain modes of inference hold in regard to them which do not hold with regard to objects in general.
