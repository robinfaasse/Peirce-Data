Milford [date]1891 Dec 25[/date]

My dear Newcomb

I have for the present given up the idea that anything can be concluded with considerable probability concerning the curvature of space. The best argument I could make was as follows. By [u]long[/u] motion, I mean the component of proper motion directly away from the apex of the sun's motion. By [u]cross[/u] motion the motion at right angles to that. Now I found the cross motion and brightness had remarkably little to do with one another. Separating the stars [ins](I mean the 1267 motion of)[/ins] into two equal sets according to [del]the[/del] magnitude of either of these quantities, I find the other quantity nearly equal for both sets. In point of fact, the intrinsically brighter stars have in the mean probably a greater mass and therefore by the law of action and reaction a smaller absolute motion.
