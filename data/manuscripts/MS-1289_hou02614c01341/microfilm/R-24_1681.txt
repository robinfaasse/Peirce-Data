[del]The most abstract and general sciences are naturally the first to be attacked, and therefore[/del] mathematics, [del]is[/del] [ins]as[/ins] the [u]most[/u] abstract science (since it is really only tracing out the consequences of hypotheses) [ins]first of all[/ins]. Some peoples are drawn more toward arithmetic, others more toward geometry. In either case, a correct method of reasoning is sure to be reached very soon. This early inculcates a great respect for necessary reasoning. The circumstance that mathematical reasoning derives all its excellence from its being diagrammatic is overlooked. In fact, no necessary reasoning but diagrammatic reasoning is worth a pin. In this way, [ins]early[/ins] success in mathematics is highly corrupting to positive science.

[pn]V.[/pn]

An altogether false confidence [del]being[/del] having been created in the power of reasoning within ones own breast without the support of experience, men soon begin to apply such reasoning to matters of conscience; for conscience does not submit its dictates to experience either. One result of [del]that[/del] is that men begin to rationalize about [del]the[/del] sacred matters, especially questions of purity and integrity, — the effect of which is thoroughly corrupting. That, however, does not concern the interests of science. But what is worse, they begin to look to science for light concerning the conduct of life, and to value it in proportion as it affords such light. This leads direct to contempt for all the problems that early science ought to attack.
