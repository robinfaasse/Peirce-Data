The first problem that presents
itself to the writer on the
question of the relation of the
community to the school is
undoubtly the fact that the
conception of the community is
vague and indefinite. For many
the community means only the
nearer neighborhood and that
only as it affects the child.
There is a general and somewhat
superficial notion that the school
is for the purpose of making a
better community. But if we should
pursue our question more closely
we would find it difficult to define
what is meant. It must mean more
than the better training of the
individual student so that he may
at some later time serve the
community in a more or less
efficient way. It must mean
that the school is itself an
organ of the community and
as such performs a direct function
in the interests of that community.
It follows then that the character
and the nature of the
community must determine the
nature and the character of the
school. This is a point that is
very often [del]often[/del] neglected, for we
look upon the school as something
relatively fixed and unchangeable.
