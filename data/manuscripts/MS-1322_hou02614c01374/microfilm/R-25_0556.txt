Thomas Henry Huxley was a comparative
anatomist of high rank in the scientific world. But
he was even more remarkable in his avocations than
in his vocation. His great clearness and energy of
thought disposed him to say what he thought about
all sorts of subjects in the plainest terms. The strength
of his reasoning combined with his prestige as a
scientific luminary made him greatly admired by
the general public; and whenever he was attacked
he was found to be much better prepared for defense
and counter-attack than had been imagined. He was
thus turned into the ways of writing upon subjects with of
which he had not a professionally thorough knowledge,
into controversies, into appeals to the public, and into
practical endeavors, all of which took him away from
the particular work for which, after all, he had the rarest
