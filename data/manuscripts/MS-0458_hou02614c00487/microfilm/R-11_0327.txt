Ladies & Gentlemen:

I am going to devote this evening's curtate hour in endeavoring to convey to you some notion of the conceptions of mathematics.

What is mathematics? [del]The[/del] [ins]Traditionally[/ins], it is defined as the science of quantity; but [del]when[/del] [ins]at the time when[/ins] that definition was first adopted, three words bore entirely different acceptations from those they bear today. Those three words are [u]mathematics[/u], [u]science[/u], and [u]quantity[/u].

By [del]mathematics was meant[/del] [ins]Under mathematics were included[/ins] the four sciences of Geometry, Arithmetic, Astronomy, and Music. But by [u]arithmetic[/u] was not meant what is now so called, but was then called [u]Logistic[/u]. That was not considered as any part of mathematics. The word [u]science[/u] in those days did not mean [del]devotedness to[/del] [ins]what it now means,[/ins] single-minded [ins]and what Coleridge called science, i.e.[/ins] research. Neither did it mean systematized knowledge. It meant comprehension, understanding [del]the[/del] [ins]metaphysical[/ins] principles of things; nearly what we call philosophy.

[marginalia]The arithmetic of those days was a wordy system of naming ratios mainly. This is now deservedly forgotten.[/marginalia]
