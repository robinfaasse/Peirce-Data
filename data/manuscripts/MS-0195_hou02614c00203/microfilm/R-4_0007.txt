§ 3. The exponential function of [del]1[/del] [ins]t[/ins] as variable
is called the Naperian base and is usually denoted
by [del]e[/del] [ins]6[/ins]; but by scholars of Benjamin Peirce by
the character 6. That is, if t is a positive
integer
[u]6[/u]<sup>t</sup> = Exp t.

Take the product of Exp x . Exp (-x)
Exp x = 1 + x + 1/2 x² + 1/3! x³ + etc = [summation]<sub>0</sub><sup>∞</sup> x<sup>r</sup>/r!
Exp (-x) = 1 - x + 1/2 x² - 1/3! x³ + etc = [summation]<sub>0</sub><sup>∞</sup> (-1)<sup>r</sup> x<sup>r</sup>/r!

Product = 1
+ (1/2 [del]x²[/del] - 1 + 1/2) x²
+ (2/4! - 2/3! + 1/2 1/2) x⁴
+ (2/6! - 2/5! + 2/2.4! - 1/3!3!) x⁶
+ etc

But by the binomial theorem all the terms but
the first vanish. Hence,
Exp (-t) = [u]6[/u]<sup>-t</sup>

It is also evident that (Exp 1/t)<sup>t</sup> = [u]6[/u]. Hence
Exp 1/t = [u]6[/u]<sup>1/t</sup>

Hence for any rational fraction s/t
Exp s/t = [u]6[/u]<sup>s/t</sup>

The method of limits extends this result to
irrational exponents.
