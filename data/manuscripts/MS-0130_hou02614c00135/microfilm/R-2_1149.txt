[pn]Ms. 130[/pn] [u]METRICAL GEOMETRY[/u]

[u]Art. . Analysis[/u] . Measures are numbers assigned to different things which are [del]some more, some less, some[/del] [ins]perceived to be[/ins] of the same kind, so that the sum of the measures of [del]the[/del] aggregate parts (parts of the same kind as the whole) shall be the measure of the aggregate whole.

Thus, we measure lengths, turnings at corners, weights, times, temperatures, forces, etc.

The requisite instruments of measurement are two, as follows:

The first instrument of measurement is a [u]Standard[/u]. This is a single thing whose quantity is assumed as the unit of measure. The [u]Yard[/u] is a bar walled up in the Palace at Westminster. The [u]International Metre[/u] is a bar kept in a vault in the Pavillon of Breteuil in the Park of St. Cloud, at Sèvres near Paris. A standard must be something which we have reason to think [ins]sensibly[/ins] unchangeable, under suitable precautions, and after proper corrections.

The second instrument of measurement is the [u]Comparator[/u]. This is some apparatus by which something can be carried about so as to compare the quantity of anything with that of the [del]comparator[/del] [ins]standard[/ins]. Sometimes the standard itself is used for the comparator. In many cases, there are optical instruments by which a combination of images is moved about.
