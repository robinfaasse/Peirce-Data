It will be convenient to suppose that we have a body which
fills all space and which can be so moved about that any place
can be compared with any other on the assumption that the same
part of this body occupies at all times places that are equal. This
body, so used as a standard of measure, we will call a "metron."

[u]Definition[/u], Clause 1. A metron is an imaginary body having
the following properties: 1[sup]st[/sup], The metron fills all space.

[u]Scholium[/u]. We will further suppose that this metron so far
resembled [del]a solid bo[/del] [ins]an ordinary[/ins] solid body that every straight filament of
it remains [del]for[/del] [ins]ever[/ins] straight, and consequently, every flat film
remains forever flat. Measurements with other kinds of bodies would
not be impossible; but we will not look beyond this case.

[u]Definition[/u], Clause 2. 2[sup]nd[/sup], Every straight filament of the
metron remains forever straight.
