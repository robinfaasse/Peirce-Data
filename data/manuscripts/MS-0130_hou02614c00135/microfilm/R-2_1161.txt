[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Definitions[/u] . A [u]translation[/u] is a movement of the metron in which all the flat films containing one ray, called the [u]axis of translation[/u], remain in their planes, while the particles in the axis move from point to point of the axis.

A [u]rotation[/u] is a movement of the metron in which all the particles in one ray, called the [u]axis of rotation[/u], remain at their points, while the flat films containing the axis move from plane to plane round the axis.

A [u]measurable displacement[/u] of the metron is one which can result by taking two particles, [ins]A and B,[/ins] [del]of[/del] however little removed from one another, so long as they are sensibly distinct, and successively carrying A to the [ins]point[/ins] [del]place[/del] of B, by [del]a[/del] translations; and then taking two flat films, $\alpha$ and $\beta$, however little removed from one another, so long as they are sensibly distinct, and successively carrying $\alpha$ to the plane of $\beta$, by rotations.

A [u]limitary displacement[/u] is a displacement which is not measurable but which results [del]from[/del] the limit of a [del]series of[/del] [ins]simple sparse sequence[/ins] of [ins]measurable[/ins] translations followed by a simple sparse sequence of [del]rotations[/del] [ins]rotations[/ins].

A [u]sweep[/u] is the continuity [ins]or multitude[/ins] of planes all or part way round a ray.
