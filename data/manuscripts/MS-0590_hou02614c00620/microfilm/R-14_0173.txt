[u]III. Synthetical propositions à priori[/u]

That geometry contains propositions which in Kant's days were understood in a sense in which they were synthetical [del]pro[/del] judgments à priori, I will not dispute. Such were the propositions that the sum of the three angles of a triangle is [ins]necessarily[/ins] equal to 180°, that there are but 5 regular bodies, etc. Only, in that sense, namely, considered as applicable to the real world, these propositions are false. It is barely possible that the sum of the angles of every triangle is exactly 180°; but nothing more improbable can be imagined. To say the sum is necessarily 180° is certainly false. Euclid's way of proving this is as follows. In his 5[u]th[/u] Postulate, he practically assumes that the sum [ins]of the three angles[/ins] is not less than 180°.
