[u]Chemistry[/u]
"Misc. notes on Chem."

A "Substance" is properly anything having real being [u]per se[/u] (abstraction being made of dependence on a Creator). A "body" is a substance of the physical universe. But in chemistry, two masses are said to be composed of the same "substance" or "body" if their smallest sensible parts have the same properties or if their molecules differ only by the usual effects of pressure and heat, of crystallization, or of ordinary solution. This use of "substance" (which is unfortunate, for "matter" would have been preferable) has passed [ins]into[/ins] ordinary speech. Moreover, if two masses contain each but one kind of matter (as far as chemistry has [del]been[/del] able to discover) and that the same in the [del]two, although their molecules are not alike[/del] they are said to be the same elementary substance in two "allotropic forms." A mere difference of crystalline form constitutes a distinction of allotropic form.

An "[u]elementary substance[/u]" is one whose molecules are not composed of chemically separable parts having different weights or other characters. The kind of matter composing an elementary substance is a "chemical element." [del][[/del]So called after the Four Elements of Empedocles, Fire, Water, Earth, and Air, each of which, according to Aristotle, contributes two of the four qualities, hot, cold, moist, dry, to the bodies which contain them, thus:
[fig]Table of the four elements and their qualities[/fig]
Fire is hot and dry;
Air is hot and moist;
Earth is cold and dry;
Water is cold and moist.
Thus water is contrary to fire, earth to air.[del]][/del] The elements and elementary substances go by the same names.
