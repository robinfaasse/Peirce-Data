Ms 503 [u]Peripatetic Talks. No 4.[/u]

I have invented a system of diagrams called logical graphs. This system has two varieties. One is sketched in the Monist 1897. I call that Entitative Graphs. The other I here set forth. I call it Existential Graphs.

Existential Graphs are based on five fundamental grammatical rules. I say grammatical because the system is a language.
[u]Grammatical Rules.[/u]

I. The sheet of paper used is understood to relate to [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] universe, or body of experiences, common to the writer and reader; and anything significant written upon this sheet [ins]without being enclosed in an oval[/ins] is asserted of that universe, or, so [del]far as it is indefinite, of some suitably chosen individual of that universe, so as to make the because in the proposition show as true.[/del] [ins]far as it is indefinite, of some suitably chosen individual of that universe, so as to make the [del]because in the[/del] proposition show as true.[/ins]
II. Two propositions written side by side are both asserted.
III. A continuous heavy line joining two Indices of individuals, or blanks for such indices, asserts the identity of those individuals.
IV. If a proposition, A, is enclosed in an oval which encloses nothing else except an oval enclosing a second proposition, B, as in [fig]figure[/fig] Fig. 1, the meaning is [del]that[/del] if A be true B is true. And an individual [ins]not definitely specified is to be selected so as to give A the meaning which it would have if it were asserted.[/ins]
V. An [fig]figure[/fig] asserts that that which is false is true.

[u]Consequences of the above Rules.[/u]

1. What does a blank space, considered as a proposition, assert? There is a blank space beside any proposition which may be written, so that by Rule II, it is asserted, as well as the proposition.
