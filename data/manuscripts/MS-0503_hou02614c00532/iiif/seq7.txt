Special Consequences of the Basic Rules.

A [u]relative[/u], or [u]verb[/u], is a sign which needs no more than to have
certain blank places belonging to it filled each with an index of an
individual of the universe of discourse in order to become a complete
assertion. Verbs are to be distinguished according to the [del]multitude[/del] [ins]number[/ins] of
their blanks as follows: [ins](i.e. the blank or blanks are filled out)[/ins]
A [u]medad[/u] is a verb [del]of[/del] [ins]with[/ins] no blank, as "it thunders," "Tom kills Bill's son," etc.;
A [u]monad[/u] is a verb with one blank, as "—sins," "Tom kills —'s son," etc.;
A [u]dyad[/u] is a verb with two blanks, as "—loves—," "—kills Bill's —," etc.;
A [u]triad[/u] is a verb with three blanks, as "—praises—to—," "—gives—to—," etc.;
A [u]polyad[/u] is a verb with more than two blanks, being either triad, tetrad, etc.
