Gentlemen:

I have just received yours of the 21[ins]st[/ins] inst. You can easily imagine the spiral whose equation in polar coordinates is
$$r = e^{\frac{1}{\theta}}$$
or
$$\theta = \frac{1}{\log r}$$

It will twist round the origin indefinitely many times when $r$ is nearly equal to 1. Draw a radius vector, [del]and[/del] travel along it from the origin to a great distance, and your crossings of the spiral will first be an endless series, but afterward will be a beginningless series.

I enclose a foot-note [ins]relating[/ins] to this difficulty by means of a [del]different[/del] [ins]different[/ins] example. Will you kindly have it inserted? & thank you very much
