**The Monist,**
EDITORIAL DEPARTMENT,
LA SALLE, ILL.

June 2, 1896.

Charles S. Peirce, Esq'r,
180 W. 87th St.,
New York City, N. Y.,

Dear Sir:-

Your letter without date is duly received, but I hardly know what to say in reply. Our next number of The Monist is overcrowded and an extension of your article would on that account not be very desirable. On the other hand I wish that [u]your article should be exactly as you want it[/u], and for that reason I leave you at liberty to remodel it and to re-write it just as you see fit. But remember one thing, we must go to press not later than the 10th of June, which leaves a very short margin for making corrections, even if I leave your article for the very last. As to your emendations I give you no instructions, except to do as you see fit, [ins]to alter without extending the article. Consider,[/ins] [del]A[/del] [ins]if[/ins] there is any reason for omitting your article in the present number [ins](by its being belated)[/ins] I shall be unable to accept another article from your pen for the next number, while otherwise there is at least a possibility of doing so. The plan which you submit of writing an article on the subject appeals to me, and I must confess that I wish the present article [del]discussed[/del] [ins]would discuss[/ins] the subjects which you mention. I send this letter off in a hurry so that it will reach you in time. Your article is in the hands of the compositors and I directed that the proofs which came to [del]my[/del] hands be sent to you. I send them to you unread and uncorrected. They may be full of mistakes. [ins](I also enclose MS. for safety's sake by Express, but please return at your very earliest convenience.)[/ins]

With kind regards and best wishes, I remain,

[ins]Please do not add new symbols, since there is no time left for having them cut.[/ins]

Yours very truly,

P. Carus
