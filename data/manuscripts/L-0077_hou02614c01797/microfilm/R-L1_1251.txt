EDITORIAL DEPARTMENT
LA SALLE, ILL.
DR. PAUL CARUS

La Salle, Ill., Nov. 22, 1905.

Charles S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.

My Dear Sir:

In Dr. Carus' absence I am getting your paper on Royce's book ready for the compositor, whom you may know from former experience is prone to using large words when it becomes necessary for him to set by hand irregular copy. He admits that there is nothing about this that he can not do, but there is one particular about which I am not quite clear.

As I understand, you have plainly indicated a distinction between the capital Roman E and the Greek, thus: [fig]handwritten capital E[/fig] and [fig]handwritten capital Epsilon[/fig]; but the type for the large Epsilon is exactly the same as the Roman Cap. so I am at a loss how to express the distinction which you need.

In the formulae you use also the characters [fig]handwritten e with underline, e prime with underline, and e double prime with underline[/fig] By the line underneath you mean, do you not, simply to emphasize the fact that the letter is the Greek minuscule [fig]handwritten epsilon[/fig], in contrast to the italic [u]e[/u], and not that the line underneath is necessary to differentiate that [fig]handwritten epsilon[/fig] from any other.

Sincerely regretting the necessity of troubling you in this matter, I remain,

Yours very truly,
(Miss) Lydia G. Robinson.
