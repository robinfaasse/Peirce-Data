My dear Sir:

I admit that the phrase Virtual Velocities is not quite appropriate to describe the principle known by that name. But we have not the whole of Bernoulli's letter to Varignon. Probably if we had we should see the matter in another light. In fact, Varignon probably did not take in the whole of Bernoulli's meaning, for the term virtual velocity sound precisely as if he had enunciated D'Alembert's principle, or something approaching that. Anyway, we can see exactly how he might have understood the matter so as to make the phrase appropriate. But the phrase Virtual Displacements is utterly inexcusable. Virtual does not mean [u]möglich[/u], as Mach says, and never did. Virtualis is a term introduced by Duns
