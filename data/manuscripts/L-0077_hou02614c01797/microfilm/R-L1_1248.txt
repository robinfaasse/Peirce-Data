I always thought that it would have been strengthened by presenting the synechism which was to root of tychoism in my own mind & which would have shown the significance & connection of all the different articles of my series. [del]Synechism[/del] is a doctrine which may be regarded as beginning in logic by saying that in the nature of things no idea can be exact, so that the principles of excluded middle & of contradiction only refer to an unrealizable state of thought. Then if metaphysical conceptions are only forms of logic in another application, the assumption should be that [del]was[/del] in reality there is no such thing as [del]absolute[/del] exactitude. This gives a particular form to the conception of the continuity of space & time &c. But my article on continuity in that series I regard as quite the most faulty of the [del]2[/del] [ins]4[/ins] or 5. So that after all I could not, at that stage of my studies, have produced an account of synechism which would satisfy me now. It would have appeared to me now as to some extent erroneous & to a much greater extent imperfect.
