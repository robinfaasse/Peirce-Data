By verisimilitude I
mean that kind of recommenda-
tion of a proposition which
consists in evidence which is
insufficient because there is
not enough of it, but which
will amount to proof if that
evidence which is not yet
examined continues to be of
the same complexion as that
already examined, if the
evidence not at hand and
that never will be complete

should be like that which is at hand. All [del]as[/del]
determinations of probability ultimately rest
on such verisimilitudes. I mean that if we
we throw a die [del]3600[/del] [ins]216[/ins] times in order to ascertain
whether the probability of its turning up a six at
any one throw differs decidedly from 1/6 or not,
our conclusion is an affair not of probability
as Laplace would have it, by assuming that
the antecedent probabilities of the different
values of the probability are equal, but is a
verisimilitude or as we say a "[ins]likelihood[/ins]".
