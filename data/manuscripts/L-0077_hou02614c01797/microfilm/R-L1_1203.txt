[date]October 13th, 1898.[/date]

Mr. Charles S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.

My dear Sir:--

Your letter to The Open Court Publishing Co. was forwarded to me, and I responded to it at once by directing our business manager to send you [u]Buddhism and its Christian Critics[/u] and [u]Lao-Tze[/u], another new publication of mine which I expect will command your interest[del]t[/del]. I am very glad to [del]have[/del] [ins]hear[/ins] from an authoritative source a review which speaks very favorably about the book, especially as to the interpretation of the Chinese.

I suppose that [u]The Gospel of Buddha[/u] is in your possession; I send you in this mail a copy of [u]The Dharma[/u].

As to your remark concerning contributions, I must confess that we have had very heavy expenses, and that I could not ask Mr. Hegeler for an extra donation; the sacrifice which he makes for [u]The Open Court[/u] is great enough, and I should not like to increase it even if he were willing to do so. I must also add that although your contributions are valuable from a scientific standpoint, they are not so from a business standpoint, as the circle of readers is naturally quite limited. My endeavor at present is to reach a larger circle, and I am at the same time obliged to economise more than formerly.

With kind regards, and hoping that you are well both in health and otherwise, I remain,

Very truly yours,
P. Carus
