My Dear Sir:-

Your article on "Synechism" and your letter of May 5th are duly received. You are right that the article [del]might be[/del] [ins]is[/ins] suited for The Monist, and I will consider what I shall do with it.

I had a hearty laugh when reading your letter, but I must confess that your objection to having your lines on [u]prayer[/u] published is a disappointment to me. The two articles in which, as you state, you say pretty much the same, are not so plain as you are in your letter, and [del]I[/del] dislike very much indirect statements. I would rather you would call me "a philosophical crank" directly than that you speak in a general way about "cranks" of such and such a nature. It always strikes me as a breach [del]in the[/del] [ins]of[/ins] etiquette [del]of[/del] [ins]in[/ins] a true knight. It amounts to striking an adversary without taking the responsibility.

I should add at the same time that all your critical remarks are evidences that you do not know the aims of our work. After having seen so much of our publications you should know better. You make imputations [ins]especially on our conception of Religion,[/ins] which are miles off the mark.

I am much obliged for your kind invitation, but I do not think that either I or Mrs. Carus shall find the time to accept it.

With kind regards,

Yours very truly,
P. Carus

As to the duality of nature, I have always insisted upon it very strongly. Yet this duality does not make monism impossible. Again, I have always criticized the Unitarians for their objections to the Trinity. There is a "Three-ism" in nature which is very obvious. Neither the duality nor the Trinity make monism impossible.
