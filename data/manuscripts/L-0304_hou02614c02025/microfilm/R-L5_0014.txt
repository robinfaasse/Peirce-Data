My dear Morse:

I have read, — read in my terminology means what most people would call "studied" if they know at all what it is, — and have read with great delight the following ones of your papers which I shall mention in the order in which I found them profitable.

1. Gradual Dispersion of periwinkles.
2. Korean Interviews.
3. [del]A Bubble-blowing insect[/del] [ins]thought to be so but is blowing thing[/ins]
4. Arrow Release
5. Traces of an early Race in Japan
6. Roof-Teles
7. Was [del]his[/del] [ins]Mid[/ins] America peopled from Asia?
8. Old Satsuma
9. So-called "Bow-pullers"
10. Oriental Latrines

I have "perused", or as people would generally say read
Natural Selection & Crime
Spiritualism a Survival. [del][which last I find the logic weak][/del]

They are wonderfully interesting. They would be so merely for what they are intended to show, but they have a double interest in that.

However there is one of your papers, that on Crime, concerning which I once wrote a small thing. I will see if I have a copy & if so I will take the liberty of sending you [del]soon[/del] a copy. I also send a paper containing an article by me on Sir Thomas Browne.

Reading these papers has given me a great desire to see some of your plants, memoirs, to see your Museum, and your "private residence". Mail to me such of these as you can spare, I beg.

I believe that you want returned & also one of two copies of Korean Roof-tiles that you sent. By the way, you were asking what algebra is. Now there has lately been published a book which puts the answer to that as charmingly as anything could. I wish you would get it or try to; or I will send it to you if you will return it. It has a most disgusting title, but the book itself is clear & true entirely. It is [u]Wells's College Algebra[/u] published by Ginn. Your "ageometer" (it sometimes amuses me. I have two hypotheses about it, if you follow my advice, there are one or two little points in it where I want to push you on your guard against)
