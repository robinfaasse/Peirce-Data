Suppose he has $AB$, the length of the
side of an inscribed regular polygon of
$N$ sides. Call $AB = b$. The radius we assume
is unity. Then in the right triangle $OZB$ $OB = 1$
$BZ = \frac{b}{2}$ $OZ = \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{4}}$. Then in the right triangle
$BZC$, $BZ = \frac{b}{2}$ $ZC = 1 - \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{4}}$
$(BC)^2 = \frac{b^2}{4} + \left(1 - \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{4}}\right)^2$
$= \frac{b^2}{4} + 1 - 2\sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{4}} + 1 - \frac{b^2}{4}$
$= 2 - 2\sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{4}}$

But $BC$ is the side of the regular inscribed
polygon of $2N$ sides. Call it $c$. Then
$\frac{c^2}{4} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{4}}$
$1 - \frac{c^2}{4} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{4}} = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{4}}\right)$

Suppose then he starts with $N = 4$ [fig]Circle with inscribed square and axes[/fig]
$b = \sqrt{2}$. We suppose he knows how to
extract square roots with his abacus.
$b^2 = 2$ $\frac{b^2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$ $1 - \frac{b^2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$
