It is said that he systematized geometry.
If by that it is meant that he established
a regular procedure for dealing with problems,
I can well believe it; although to day we are
destitute of any such method. But I think
the man who could discover the 47[sup]th[/sup]
proposition could produce such a method.

But if it be meant that he did that
which we see done in Euclid's Elements,
namely [del]to[/del] arrange the propositions
so that they follow logically from a small
number admitted at the outset, I cannot
believe that Pythagoras did that. For this
passion for demonstration, which is
carried by Euclid to such an [del]senseless[/del] extreme,
seems to me a foible of the Greek mind; while
Pythagoreanism does not seem to have
the characteristics of Greek thought at all.
Besides, the man was built on too large a
scale to take an interest in such crochet-work —
