I have said that Euclid abounds in
bad reasoning. That is true, and the whole
scheme of the book is artificial. Neverthe-
less, the reasoning is sufficiently close
and accurate to be a very valuable discipline
[del]to a[/del] young mind. But owing to its faults
it inculcated bad reasoning too. As far
as I have observed, those who [ins]have diligently[/ins] studied Euclid
in their youth reason much better than those
who have not; and I consider the success
of the English to be in [del]large[/del] measure due to their
reasoning better than [del]others[/del] [ins]their neighbors[/ins]. Still, they have
certain bad habits of reasoning, an artificial
element, which is very noticeable too.
