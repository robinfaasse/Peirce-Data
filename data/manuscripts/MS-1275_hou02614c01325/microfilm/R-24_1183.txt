Archimedes states the proof a little
differently from what I have done, though
the reasoning is essentially the same. He
begins say with [del]twelve[/del] [ins]twelve[/ins] pounds hung equidis-
tant on a balance.
[fig]Two diagrams of balances with weights hanging from them[/fig]

Now he takes seven and hangs them
from the middle point on a balance and
the remaining 5 he treats in the same
way
[fig]Diagram of a balance with points A and B and weights hanging below[/fig]

These balance with the joints A B stiff, and
consequently also when they are moveable. Thus
we see that 7 at a distance of [frac]5/2[/frac] balance
5 at a distance of [frac]7/2[/frac] and so in general.
Now it is true that Archimedes does
not expressly say anything about
the secondary levers. But that certainly was
his thought, and I am sure the reasoning
is perfectly sound notwithstanding Mach's objections.
