[del]this postulate is not self-evident. But[/del] Archimedes never says it is self-evident. Archimedes no more than Euclid ever had any such conception as that of "self-evidence", which is a Cartesian & Leibnizian idea mainly. Archimedes merely says, in a common-sense acceptation, that it is indubitable; and so it is. Dr. Mach says this assumes that it makes no difference whether the arms of the lever have different colors or not, that no occurrence in the neighborhood has any influence, etc. That is a great misapprehension of the meaning of Archimedes, who does not dream of denying that many circumstances may interfere with the equilibrium, but all he means to say is that in the absence of any special cause of disturbance an equal-armed lever equally loaded is in equilibrium.
