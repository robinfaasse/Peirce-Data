      Involution is the tautologous repetition of an assertion; evolution
is the suppression of a repeated condition or alternative. A graph may
be said [u]to be iterated[/u] when it occurs twice, the two individual graphs, called
[u]iterations[/u], having all their corresponding hands connected by nexes. If
these nexes are bonds, the iteration may be said to be direct, and may be
called [u]duplication[/u]. Thus, in Fig. 82, the [u]l[/u] is duplicated; but in Fig. 83
[fig]figure[/fig] the [u]w[/u] is only indirectly iterated. The rule
is as follows: [u]If a graph is iterated, and[/u]
[u]both iterations are evenly enclosed, the inner[/u]
[u]one may be illatively suppressed; if they are oddly enclosed, the outer[/u]
[u]iteration may be illatively suppressed; and any graph may be illatively[/u]
[u]iterated in such a way that after the insertion the old iteration may be[/u]
[u]illatively suppressed by this rule[/u]. Thus, from the medad of Fig. 84, meaning,
