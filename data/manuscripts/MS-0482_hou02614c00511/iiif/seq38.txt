Rule II. Of Letters.
¶ 1. By a [ins]atom[/ins] [del]letter[/del], in the special sense here intended, is meant a graph which is indivisible, except for dots, called [u]hands[/u], on certain sides of it.
¶ 2. Each letter signifies either a complete assertion or a materially significant relative. In the former case, it has no hand:
¶ 3. Each hand of a letter represents a determinate blank of the relative [ins]which the letter[/ins] [del]it[/del] signifies.
¶ 4. By a [u]hand of a graph[/u] is meant a hand of a letter contained in that graph, which hand is not connected by a nex with another hand of a letter contained in that graph.
¶ 5. A [del]graph[/del] without a hand is called a medad and means a complete assertion. A graph with a hand or hands signifies a relative and is termed a monad, dyad, triad etc., according to the number of its hands.
Rule III. Of Circles.
¶ 1. By a [ins]oval[/ins] [del]circle[/del] is meant a lightly-drawn closed line of any shape, intersecting no other such line, together with any hands it may possess. [del]It may be called a circumference when the single boundary alone is meant referred to.[/del]
¶ 2. The whole of the contents of a circle is said to be [u]encircled[/u] by it; and the parts are said to be [u]enclosed[/u] in it, but not [u]encircled[/u] by it. By an encircled graph is meant the graph composed of a graph together with a circle round it alone.
¶ 3. Anything unenclosed, or enclosed within an even number of circles is said to be [u]evenly enclosed[/u]; but if the number of circles is odd everything enclosed in them is said to be [u]oddly enclosed[/u]. The [del]called[/del] [ins]circles[/ins] may be collectively termed an [u]even enclosure[/u] or an [u]odd enclosure[/u].
¶ 4. Graphs which, together with their nexes, make up the entire contents of a circle are said to be [u]encircled together[/u].
