§7. Characters of [u]second intention[/u] are characters brought to our
knowledge by observation of logical forms, not of things. Three
relatives of second intention are of special importance. Namely,
they are the monad, the dyad, and the triad which signify that their
subjects are not one thing. They may be termed the [del]monad[/del] relatives
of non-unity, and may be drawn as dots, or small [ins] round [/ins] black spots.
The monad of non-unity, which [del]means[/del] [ins] signifies [/ins] “— is not anything,”
is in itself absurd; so that its negative is empty. Consequently,
this relative when encircled by itself as an ultimate antecedent
(an antecedent by itself of some [del]part of the[/del] [ins] partial graph [/ins]) is of little use. [del]When[/del] it
is an ultimate consequent, the partial graph of which it is consequent
asserts that nothing has the character signified by its antecedent.
