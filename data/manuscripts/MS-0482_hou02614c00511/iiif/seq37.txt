§11. [spacer] Rules of Simplified Graphs. [spacer] 59

Part A. Definitions.

Rule I. Of Entitative and Existential Graphs.

¶ 1. A [u]relative[/u] is an incomplete assertion which requires only to have certain blanks in it filled up with signs of objects experientially known to the person who is to understand the assertion, in order to complete it.

¶ 2. By a [u]logical graph[/u] is meant a diagram composed of lines and of spots of various colors which is intended so to exhibit the [del]structure[/del] [ins]composition[/ins] of an assertion [ins]or relative[/ins] that its logical relations can be studied.

¶ 3. By a [u]simplified logical graph[/u] is meant a logical graph composed only of [u]letters[/u] of various qualities, of [u]circles[/u] all of one kind, and of [u]nexes[/u] all of one kind.

¶ 4. Every part of a [del]graph[/del] simplified logical graph which can be separated from the rest by merely cutting nexes and taking it out of a circle (if either or both these operations be necessary) is itself a graph.

¶ 5. According to the interpretation which a simplified logical graph is intended to bear it is either an [u]Entitative[/u] or an [u]Existential[/u] [u]Graph[/u]. These interpretations [del]who[/del] will be given in these rules; and where two different statements are put one over the other and connected by braces, the upper refers to entitative, the lower to existential graphs.
