Suppose a marriage is necessarily either a love match or [ins]a certain[/ins]
<u>mariage de convenance</u>. Then it is certainly true that if from a
marriage being a love match it were necessarily to follow that
it would be a <u>mariage de convenance</u> (all the <u>other</u> elements of
the latter being known to be present) then it would be a mariage
de convenance. For if it were a love-match this consequence
would make it a <u>mariage de convenance</u> and if it were <u>not</u>
a love match, it would have to be a <u>mariage de convenance</u>
because there is no third kind of marriage. But if there were
a third kind, this consequence would not follow. Hence, Fig. 20
