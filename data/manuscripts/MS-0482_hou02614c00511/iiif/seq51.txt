Fig. 3 shows four monad H's, a pentad P, a tetrad C, and a triad N. These are
chemical symbols; but although it is likely enough that chemical graphs,
if they were rightly written, are polar graphs of some kind, it is not pretended
that there is any reason for supposing that they really belong to the system
now under consideration. Chemical symbols are simply used to fix the ideas.
Fig. 4 shows one H [del]spot[/del] united to the P making a tetrad. The H is the negative constituent,
the P the positive. Fig. 5 shows the second H combined, as negative constituent, to the HP as positive
constituent, making a triad. Fig. 6 shows the third H, as negative constituent, combined with the H₂P
as positive constituent, making a dyad. Fig. 7 shows the [del]fourth[/del] H combined, as negative constituent to
the H₃P as positive constituent, making a monad. Fig. 8 shows the C, as negative constituent, united
by three bonds to the N as positive constituent, making a monad. Fig. 9 shows the H₄P
combined, as negative constituent, with the CN, as positive constituent, making a
medad.
