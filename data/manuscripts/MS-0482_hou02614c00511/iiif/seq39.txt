¶ 8. A circle encircling a headed line is a monad and signifies
{"— exists." / "— does not exist."}.
¶ 9. A circle encircling a dash is a dyad and signifies {"— is —" / "— is not —"}
¶ 10. A circle encircling a three-way line is a triad and signifies
{"— and — and — are one and the same." / "— and — and — are not all the same."}
¶ 11. An encircled graph precisely denies the relative signified by the
graph that is encircled.
¶ 12. The effect of writing down a number of graphs together
[ins]in the same enclosures[/ins] is to assert their {disjunctive / copulative} conjunction; that is that {some one / every one}
is true. It is the office of the circle, not merely to deny, but also
to group together, the graphs which it encircles together.
