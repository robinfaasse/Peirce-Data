On Logical Graphs.  1898
§1. A [u]graph[/u], in its most general sense, would be a diagram composed 
[ins]1[sup]st[/sup],[/ins] of [u]spots[/u], which are parts treated as points, though they may have distinguish-
able [u]sides[/u], and [u]colors[/u]; or simple distinguishing qualities; 2[sup]nd[/sup], of [u]bonds[/u], or 
parts treated as lines, though may also have sides and colors; 3[sup]rd[/sup] of 
faces, or parts treated as surfaces; etc. A graph of the [del]second[/del] [ins]first[/ins] dimensionality 
has no faces, or higher parts. In a [u]simple-bonded[/u] graph, [del]all the[/del] [ins]every[/ins] [u]bond[/u][del]s[/del] 
is a line with two ends, of which one, at least, is attached to a spot. 
The other is either attached or is a [u]loose end[/u].
The [u]Listing numbers[/u] of a graph may be defined as follows. Let 
a particle have space within the graph to expand in [u]M[/u] dimensions, 
so as to form an [u]unlimited[/u] [u]M[/u][del]-[/del] dimensional continuum, which once 
formed can by no motion[ins] within the graph[/ins] [del]without rupture [del]with[/del] collapse[/del]. or be brought 
[del]into six[/del] Then, the number of such continua each of which occupies some 
point into which none of the others can be gradually moved within the 
graph without rupture is the [u]M[/u][sup]th[/sup] Listing number. The [del]zeroth[/del] [ins]first[/ins] Listing 
number, which I call the [u]chorisis[/u], is the number of separate pieces. 
The second is the [u]cyclosis[/u]. A graph of chorisis 1 may be called a [u]whole[/u]. A 
graph of [del]cyclosis 0 may be called[/del] [u]non-cyclic[/u].
Listing's census-theorem applied to a [del]graph[/del] simple-bonded graph 
of the first dimensionality is, that the chorisis of a graph [u]minus[/u] its 
cyclosis equals the number of spots less the number of [u]connecting 
bonds[/u] (bonds with loose ends not being counted.)
