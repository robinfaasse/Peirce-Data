Besides the Listing theorem, the following second census-proposi-
tion [del]app[/del] holds of valental graphs: The valency of a graph equals
the sum of the valencies of its spots minus twice the number of
connecting bonds. It is a corollary from this that no combi-
nation of artiads can make a perissid. Hence, the distinction
between artiads and perissids is important.

Between [del]these two[/del] Listing's theorem and this second census-propo-
sition [ins]writing[/ins] one may eliminate the number of connecting bonds. Then,
[del]putting[/del] V for the valency of a graph, $\upsilon$ for the sum of the valencies
of any partial graphs into which it may be considered as divided,
X for the chorisis of the graph, $\chi$ for the sum of the chorises of the
parts, K for the cyclosis of the graph, $\kappa$ for the sum of the cycloses
of the parts, we have
$$V-2X+2K = \upsilon-2\chi+2\kappa.$$
