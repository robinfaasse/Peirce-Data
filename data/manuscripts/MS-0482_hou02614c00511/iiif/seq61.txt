§10. There are three rules for the illative transformations of graphs;
that of Insertion and Omission, that of Involution and Evolution, and
that of Development and Elimination. Involution and Development
are special kinds of insertion, evolution and elimination special
kinds of omission.
In this section only the first, or negative, system of graphs will
be referred to; but everything that is said will apply [ins]with a simple change[/ins] to the second, or
positive system. For the rule of contraposition holds in both, to wit:
[u]If any substitution is illatively valid under an even number of enclo-[/u]
[u]sures, its reverse is valid under an odd number, and vice versa.[/u] Now
the same forms of graphs in the two systems signify [del]the[/del] denials, each
meaning of what the other would be after reversing the meaning of
every letter. Hence, whatever is [ins]illatively[/ins] permissible under even enclosures
in either system is so under odd enclosures in the other, and vice versa.
[u]Omission[/u] is the dropping of irrelevant assertions; [u]insertion[/u] is
the addition of unnecessary conditions and alternatives. The rule
is as follows: [u]Under even enclosures any graph may be illatively[/u]
[u]inserted, [ins]each[/ins] any of its hands being annexed to a furcation interpo-[/u]
[u]lated in any of the previously existing bonds; and any oddly en-[/u]
[u]closed [del]bond[/del] [ins]graph[/ins] may be illatively erased, its nexes being cut of at[/u]
[u]the hands of [del]other[/del] [ins]the nearest[/ins] graphs which hold them.[/u] Thus, from the medad
