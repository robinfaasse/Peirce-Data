[u]On Logical Graphs.[/u]

A [u]graph[/u] is a diagram composed of [u]spots[/u] and [u]bonds[/u]; and different spots may be of unlike quality, while each bond is a line with two ends, of which one at least is attached to a spot, while the other may be [u]loose[/u], or unattached. The [u]chorisis[/u] of a graph is the number of points which may move along bonds and through spots without possibility of reaching another such [ins]We may speak of a graph of chorisis 1 as a whole.[/ins] moving point. The [u]cyclosis[/u] of a graph is the number of bonds which must be cut to prevent the possibility of such a [del]moving[/del] point moving round to its starting-point. We may speak of a graph [ins]of cyclosis zero as a non-cyclic graph.[/ins]

A [u]valental graph[/u] is a graph every spot of which has a valency depending exclusively upon its quality, this valency being the number of bonds attached to it; and these bonds are distinguished from one another by being attached to determinate parts of the spot. [It is true that in chemistry such distinctions between the bonds are not yet recognized; but they presumably exist.]
