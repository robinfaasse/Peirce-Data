[u]Rule I[/u]. [u]The principle of identity[/u]. A medad whose positive and negative parts are exactly alike with bonds uniting all like blanks is necessarily true.

[u]Rule II[/u]. Given two medads, if an unencircled bond of each be broken, and the broken ends of the one be joined to those of the other, the veritability of the result will be as high as one or other of the given medads.
