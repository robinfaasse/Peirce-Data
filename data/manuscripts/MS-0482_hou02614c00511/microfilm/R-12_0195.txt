§ 9. The chief illative transformations of graphs may be termed Copulation, or colligation, and Disjunction, or enumeration, Omission and Insertion, Identification and Diversification, Involution and Evolution, and manipulation of second intentions.

All illative processes are subject to the rule of contraposition, which, as applied to graphs, is as follows: [u]If any illative substitution is valid with an even number of enclosures, its reverse is valid within an odd number, and vice versa.[/u]

[u]Copulation[/u], or colligation, is the operation by which two or more premises are collected into a single copulative proposition. It is performed as shown in Fig. 71. [u]Disjunction[/u] is the operation by which separate cases are taken singly for consideration, as in Dilemma.

[u]Omission[/u] is the leaving out of irrelevant matter. [u]Insertion[/u] is the addition of unnecessary conditions and alternatives. The rule for graphs is as follows: [u]Anything may be [ins]illatively[/ins] inserted by an evenly enclosed bond provided the connections of the parts of are not disturbed, and anything [ins]oddly enclosed[/ins] may be omitted under the same proviso.[/u] A triad of diversity does not disturb the connections of the bond into which it is interpolated.
