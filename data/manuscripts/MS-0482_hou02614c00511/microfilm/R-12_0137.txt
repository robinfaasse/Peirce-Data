[u]On Logical Graphs[/u].

A [u]graph[/u] is a diagram composed of [u]spots[/u] and [u]bonds[/u]; [ins]and[/ins] [del]dif-[/del]
ferent spots may be of unlike quality, while each bond is a
line with two ends, of which one at least is attached to a spot,
while the other may be [u]loose[/u], or unattached. The [u]chorisis[/u] of a
graph is the number of points which may move along bonds
and through spots without possibility of reaching another such
moving point. [ins]We may speak of a graph of chorisis 1 as a whole.[/ins] The [u]cyclosis[/u] of a graph is the number of bonds
which must be cut to prevent the possibility of such a [del]moving[/del]
point moving round to its starting-point. [ins]We may speak of a graph of cyclosis zero as a non-cyclic graph.[/ins]

A [u]valental[/u] graph is a graph every spot of which has a
[u]valency[/u] depending exclusively upon its quality, this valency
being the number of bonds attached to it; and these bonds are
distinguished from one another by being attached to determinate
parts of the spot. [ [ins]It is true that in chemistry such distinctions
between the bonds are not yet recognized; but they presumably
exist.[/ins] ]

Valental graphs and parts of graphs receive designations ac-
cording to the numbers of their loose ends, as follows:

$$\begin{array}{cc}
\text{Number of loose ends.} & \text{Name of graph.} \\
0 & \text{medad} \\
1 & \text{monad} \\
2 & \text{dyad} \\
3 & \text{triad} \\
>2 & \text{polyad} \\
2N & \text{artiad} \\
2N+1 & \text{perissid}
\end{array}$$
