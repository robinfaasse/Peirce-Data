Those relatives of second intention, that is, [ins]relatives signifying relations[/ins] brought down knowledge by observation of logical forms, not of things, are of special importance. Namely, they are those which signify that their correlates are not one thing. The monad of non-unity is "— is not anything." The dyad of non-unity is "— is not the same as —." The triad of non-unity is "— [ins]and[/ins] — [ins]and[/ins] — are not one and the same." I designate them by dots with one, two, and three tails.

The monad of non-unity is in itself absurd. Hence, when negatived it is empty, and consequently when encircled by itself is of little use. But when not encircled by itself, its effect is to leave its antecedent absolutely negatived. Thus, as the consequent of a medad, its effect is to assert the non-existence of the antecedent in any subject.
