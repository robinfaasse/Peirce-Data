[u]Rule IV. Of Nexes.[/u]

¶1. A nex is a {[del]heavily-drawn[/del] [ins]dotted[/ins]} line, of which neither the whole nor any part returns into itself, but which is interrupted for a brief space [ins]close to and[/ins] on the inside of [del]each[/del] [ins]circumference[/ins] any circle it may cross, having each end of it either loose, {[ins]or headed with a spot,[/ins]} or at a semisignificant graph, or at a letter, and not [del]have[/del] [ins]in two places[/ins] extending out from the same [del]circle's[/del] circumference. [fig]A small circle with a line passing through it, interrupted inside the circumference.[/fig]

¶2. A [u]simple nex[/u] is one which does not branch.

¶3. A [u]nex-branch[/u] is a part of a branching nex extending from an end to a node or from node to node.

¶4. [del]The[/del] [ins]A[/ins] [u]bond[/u] of a nex is that uninterrupted portion of it which is not enclosed in any circle that encloses any other portion of it.

¶5. Every nex is conceived to be [u]enclosed[/u] in [del]every[/del] [ins]all the[/ins] circles in which its bond is enclosed and in no others; and one nex is said to be [u]outer[/u] relatively to another, said to be [u]inner[/u], when the former is not enclosed in some circle which encloses the latter, while the latter is enclosed in every circle which encloses the former.
