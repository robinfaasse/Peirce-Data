§2. By a [ins]valental[/ins] graph, I mean a graph of which every spot, by virtue of its inherent quality, has a determinate number of bonds attached to it [del]at[/del] [ins]on[/ins] determinate sides of it. Thus, [del]at[/del] [ins]an[/ins] attachment to one side of a spot will be different [del]from[/del] in kind from attachment to another side. For example, the two graphs of Figs. 1 and 2 will represent different states of things, since in the one the pointed sides of the spots are connected [ins]together,[/ins] while in the other the pointed sides are connected with round sides. [No such distinction has as yet been recognized in chemistry; but [del]it[/del] it may be a [u]special[/u] [u]property[/u] of a spot that its different sides are alike. [u]In general[/u], the possibility of their being different must be recognized.] The number of loose ends of a graph, or of part of a graph, is its [u]valency[/u]. Every spot has, in itself, loose ends, although it may be part of a graph in the whole of which there are no loose ends. There is, in general, nothing to prevent one loose end of a spot from being attached to another loose end of the same spot.
