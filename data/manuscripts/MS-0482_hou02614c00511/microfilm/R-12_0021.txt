Listing's census-theorem, applied to a simple-bonded graph of the lowest dimensionality is, that the chorisis of a graph minus its cyclosis equals the number of spots less the number of connecting bonds (bonds with loose ends not being counted.)

§2. A [u]valental[/u] graph is a graph of which every spot, by virtue of its color, or inherent quality, has a determinate number of bonds attached to it on determinate sides of it. An attachment to one side of a spot will be different in kind from an attachment to another side. For example, the two graphs of Figs. 1 and 2 will have different diagrammatic meanings, since in the one the pointed sides of the spots are connected, while in the other the pointed sides are connected with the round sides. [ins]The places of attachment [del]are not attached to spots of the graph by bonds to other spots of the graph or to other ends of the same spot[/del] may be called the [u]ends[/u] of the spot; and those ends of the spots of a graph which [ins]are not attached to other spots of the graph or to other ends of the same spot[/ins] will be called the [u]ends of the graph[/u].[/ins]

The graphs of chemistry are valental graphs, although (so far as they are as yet [del]and[/del] conceived) by a special property the different sides of all the spots are alike. But it must be acknowledged that [del]a[/del] distinctions between the different bonds of a carbon atom (for instance) may in future be made out. The number of [del]loose[/del] ends of a graph, or of [ins]a[/ins] part of a graph, is its [u]valency[/u]. Every spot has in itself [del]loose[/del] ends, although [del]in[/del] the whole graph of which it forms a part there [del]are[/del] [ins]may have[/ins] no [del]loose[/del] ends. There is nothing in the essential nature of a graph to prevent one [del]loose[/del] end of a [del]graph[/del] [ins]spot[/ins] from being attached to another [del]loose[/del] end of the same spot.
