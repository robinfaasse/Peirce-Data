The second proposition going to define being smaller than is,

[fig]Diagram with a circle containing a letter S[/fig] Nothing is smaller than itself.

The third proposition completing the formal definition of being smaller than is, (in a universe of [u]magnitudes[/u])

[fig]Diagram with two overlapping circles containing the letter S[/fig] Take any two magnitudes, then either one is smaller than the other or they are one identical magnitude.

I now proceed to define the [del]series of[/del] whole numbers. The universe is supposed to be a universe of whole numbers.

[pn]1[sup]st[/sup][/pn] [fig]Diagram with a circle containing -S-[/fig] No number is smaller than the number zero.

[pn]2[sup]nd[/sup][/pn] [fig]Diagram with nested shapes containing S and S-S[/fig] Every number has a number greater than it, but not greater than any third number intermediate between them. That is, there is a number next greater than any given number

[pn]3[sup]rd[/sup][/pn] [fig]Diagram with nested shapes and lines labeled possesses[/fig] The jagged line refers to the universe of characters.

Every character that is possessed by a number is possessed by some number that is [del]equal to[/del] [ins]identical with[/ins] or greater than every number that possesses that character.
