Besides the Listing theorem, the following second census-proposition [del]app[/del] holds of valental graphs: The valency of a graph equals the sum of the valencies of its spots minus twice the number of connecting bonds. It is a corollary from this that no combination of artiads can make a perissid. Hence, the distinction between artiads and perissids is important.

Between [del]these two[/del] Listing's theorem and this second census-proposition we may eliminate the number of connecting bonds. Then, [del]writing[/del] [ins]putting[/ins] $V$ for the valency of a graph, $v$ for the sum of the valencies of any partial graphs into which it may be considered as divided, $X$ for the chorisis of the graph, $\chi$ for the sum of the chorises of the parts, $K$ for the cyclosis of the graph, $\kappa$ for the sum of the cycloses of the parts, we have
$$V - 2X + 2K = v - 2\chi + 2\kappa.$$
