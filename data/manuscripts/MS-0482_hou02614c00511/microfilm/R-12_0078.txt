[u]Fifth Simplification[/u]. A circle has as many hands as the graph within it. It is in itself a sign of negation and of grouping. All oddly enclosed letters are taken negatively, all evenly enclosed letters affirmatively. Graphs encircled together are combined together, quite irrespective of their bonds. Those bonds only identify the [ins]subjects of the[/ins] hands of the graphs, but do not combine the graphs. It is their being written together in the same enclosure which signifies combination. Graphs evenly encircled together are disjunctively combined, graphs oddly enclosed together copulatively combined, irrespective of their bonds. There is, therefore, no reason why we should restrict ourselves to the conception that a circle must have hands. Why should not a medad be encircled as well as a relative? Medads are as capable as relatives of copulative and disjunctive combination.
