§ 5. The fundamental illative transformations of graphs seem to be [del]six[/del] pairs of opposite processes which may be named as follows:

[u]1[ins]st[/ins] Pair[/u]: Contraposition, consisting Double Encirclying and Mutual Annulment;

[u]2[ins]nd[/ins] Pair[/u]: Insertion and Omission of graphs;

[u]3[ins]d[/ins] Pair[/u]: Identification and Diversification of subjects, by Joining and Severing graphs;

[u]4[ins]th[/ins] Pair[/u]: Reassociation; composed of Involution and Evolution;

[u]5[ins]th[/ins] Pair[/u]: Fusion and Segmentation of circles;

[u]6[ins]th[/ins] Pair[/u]: Development and Elimination.

It is possible some of these processes can be shown to be derivative.

[u]Double encircling[/u] is substantially a double negation, by the principle of contradiction; [u]Mutual annulment[/u] is [ins]substantially[/ins] the dropping of such double negation by the principle of excluded middle. The rule is as follows:

[u]Any graph may be [del]doubly[/del] [ins]illatively twice[/ins] encircled; and if one circle encircles another, the two circles may illatively annul one another, and be removed.[/u]
