In what does the inconvertible character of this mode of combina-
tion consist? It consists in the antecedent relative having a negative, or
reversed, force. For to say that "A is a lover only of servants of B," is as much
as to say that anything whatever either is not loved by A or else is a servant
of B. When we take for the antecedent a [del]compound relative[/del] [ins]compound of two,[/ins] the negative
character is imparted, not only to both the [del]rela[/del] component relatives of
the antecedent, but also to their mode of combination. Thus, suppose I
say that "A stands in the relation of lover only to their benefactors, [del]only[/del] to
servants of B." That is as much as to say that anything whatever is either
not benefitted by [u]everything[/u] loved by A (that is, is unbenefitted by [u]some-[/u]
[u]thing[/u] loved by A, — where the "loved," having been twice negatived, has the
positive force again) or is a servant of B.
