Secondly, the [del]loose ends[/del] [ins]bonds[/ins] of a dyad of nullity ought to be omitted, together with any triad of diversity directly bonded to the dyad of nullity. For whatever indeterminacy in the completed graph such omission occasions can have no effect upon the meaning. Thus, Figs. 48 and 49

[fig]Fig. 48: Graph with central b and outer circles[/fig]
[fig]Fig. 49: Graph with central b and looped lines[/fig]
[fig]Fig. 50: Simplified graph with b[/fig]
[fig]Fig. 51: Graph with w, b, v and outer circle[/fig]

both mean, [u]Either nobody loves anybody or Everybody benefits everybody[/u]. Hence, Fig. 50 should be written in place of either. So Figs. 48 and 51 both mean "Some wise man loves some virtuous man", and therefore Fig. 52 should replace them both.

[fig]Fig. 52: Graph with w, b, v and nested circles[/fig]

Thirdly, when in consequence of omissions, a graph appears within two circles, one immediately enclosing the other and nothing else, both ought to be dropped as annulling each other. For in such case there are always two graphs either of which maybe so doubly enclosed without affecting the meaning.

[fig]Fig. 53: Graph with w, b, v and multiple circles[/fig]
[fig]Fig. 54: Graph with w, b, v and single circle[/fig]
[fig]Fig. 55: Graph with w, b, v and no circles[/fig]

Thus Figs 53 and 54 equally mean "Every wise man loves all but virtuous men"; and therefore Fig 55 ought to replace them both.
