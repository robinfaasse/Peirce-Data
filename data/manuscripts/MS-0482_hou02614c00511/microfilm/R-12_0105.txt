[u]Omission[/u] is substantially the deletion of irrelevant assertions, [u]In-[/u]
[u]sertion[/u] the attachment of unnecessary conditions and alternatives. The
rule is as follows:

[u]Under even enclosures, any graph may be inserted, under odd omitted[/u].

Hardly ever will anything but a medad be so inserted or omitted.
The rule is seen to be correct, because graphs evenly [del]enclosed[/del] [ins]encircled[/ins] together are
disjunctively combined and graphs oddly encircled together are copulatively
combined. After insertion a junction of bonds is usually desired; before omission
a severance of bonds is usually requisite. The next pair of processes do these.
