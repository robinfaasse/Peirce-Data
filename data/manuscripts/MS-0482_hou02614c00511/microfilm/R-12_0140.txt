§ 2. A [u]valental[/u] system of graph-formation is one in which each spot has, by its intrinsic quality, a determinate number of places at which bonds can be attached. These [del]are[/del] [ins]maybe[/ins] called the [u]hands[/u] of the [del]bare[/del] spot. In general, the different hands of spot will be distinguishable; so that, for example, Figs. 1 and 2 will have different diagrammatic significations. There are two varieties of valental graph systems. In the one, any cluster of spots together with bonds of which [del]at least[/del] [ins]both[/ins] ends [del]of each[/del] [ins]are[/ins] [del]is[/del] joined to some hands of these spots constitutes a graph. The hands [del]to[/del] which bonds are not joined are to be considered as the [u]hands of the graph[/u]. In this case, there is nothing to prevent a graph from forming a part of another graph in which [del]latter two hands of the former graph are joined to the two ends of one bond.[/del] [ins]the total graph will [del]be composed of two partial graphs together with[/del] [ins]will be[/ins][/ins] There is no difference between the properties of a spot and a graph, except that the former is simple while the latter may or may not be so.

In the other variety of systems, a graph consists of a cluster of spots of which every hand is joined to the end of a bond. The other end may or may not be attached to a hand of one of the spots of the cluster. The [u]hands of the graph[/u] are then the hands which are joined to bonds which are joined to no other hands of the spots of the cluster. In this case, we may regard the bonds as not being, strictly speaking, parts of the graph; for thus alone can we regard a graph as part of another graph. No two hands [u]of the graph[/u] can be [del]connected[/del] [ins]joined[/ins] to the two ends of the same bond; and the same thing is true of two hands of the same spot.
