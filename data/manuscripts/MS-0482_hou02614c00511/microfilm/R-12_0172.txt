§ 9. The illative transformations of graphs are of several kinds,
such as Copulation (or colligation) and Disjunction (or enumeration),
Omission and Insertion, Identification and Diversification, Involution and
Evolution, embracing Duplication and Unification as special cases, and the
introduction, transformation, and elimination of second intentions.

All these processes are subject to the principle of contraposition which, as
applied to graphs, is as follows: [u]If any illative substitution is valid within
an even number of enclosures, its reverse is valid within an odd number[/u],
and vice versa.

[u]Copulation[/u], or colligation, is the operation by which two or more pre-
mises are collected into a single copulative proposition. If several pre-
mises have one consequent, the copulated premise will have the same con-
sequent and a disjunctive antecedent. [u]Disjunction[/u] is the operation by
which separate [del]alternatives[/del] cases are taken for separate consideration,
as in the dilemma. In graphs, copulation will be performed as follows.
