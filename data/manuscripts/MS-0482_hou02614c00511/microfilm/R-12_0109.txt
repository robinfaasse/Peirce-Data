The meaning of the third paragraph is this. Suppose there be
two series of circles, as in Fig. 103, and let the nex, [u]i[/u], be within the
nex, [u]j[/u], and the nex, [u]l[/u] within the nex, [u]k[/u]. If, now,
[u]j[/u] and [u]k[/u] be evenly enclosed and [u]i[/u] and [u]l[/u] be oddly en-
closed, nothing prevents the identification either of
[u]i[/u] with [u]k[/u] or of [u]j[/u] with [u]l[/u]. But let [u]k[/u] be identified
with [u]i[/u]. Then, if can no longer be identified with [u]l[/u]. For, [u]l[/u] is within [u]k[/u]
and [u]i[/u] which is identical with [u]k[/u], is within [u]j[/u]. Consequently, according to the
rule [u]l[/u] is within [u]j[/u]. Now [u]l[/u] is oddly enclosed and [u]j[/u] is evenly enclosed;
so that, their identification is forbidden. The reason is that, at first, there is
no reason why either the subject, [u]i[/u], should not be selected before the subject [u]k[/u],
or the subject [u]l[/u] before the subject [u]j[/u]. But either of these successions being
fixed, the other becomes impossible according to the rule that the subjects
of outer nexes are to be designated before those of inner ones.
