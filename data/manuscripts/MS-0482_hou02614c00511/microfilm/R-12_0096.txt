3. By a [u]letter[/u], in the special sense here intended, is meant an in-
complex logical ideograph with a heavy dash attached to it at an
appropriate side for every wanting subject of the relation for which
it stands. A letter would, most in accordance with the idea of the
system, be a conventional picture, like the ideographs of the Egyp-
tian hieroglyphics, but with the addition of dashes; but it is more
convenient to employ initial alphabetical letters.

4. By a [u]circle[/u] is here meant a lightly drawn closed line of
any shape, but without cusps or nodes.

No two circles intersect or touch one another.

No circle runs through a letter.

Every circle has an inner and an outer side; and everything [del]upon[/del] [ins]in[/ins]
the inside is said to be [u]enclosed[/u] by the circle. The whole of what is en-
closed except any heavy [del]dashes[/del] [ins]lines[/ins] which may run up nearly to the circle
is said to be [u]encircled[/u] by the circle. Several parts of a graph which,
except for such heavy lines running up nearly to the circle, make
up what is encircled are said to be [u]encircled together[/u].

Anything enclosed within an even number of circles, or not en-
closed at all is said to be [u]evenly enclosed[/u]; if the number is odd, it is
said to be [u]oddly enclosed[/u].

5. By a [u]line[/u] is meant a heavily drawn line which may have
interruptions in the course of its length just at the insides of circles it
enters, and may have [del]furcations[/del] [ins]a single point of branching[/ins], but of which no part [ins]nor the whole[/ins] can form a
ring.
