Besides the Listing theorem, the following second census-proposition holds of valental graphs. [u]The valency of a graph equals the sum of the valencies of its spots less twice the number of connecting bonds.[/u] It follows, as a corollary, that no perissid can be composed exclusively of artiads; and this constitutes a fundamental distinction between artiads and perissids.

Between Listing's theorem and this second census-proposition the number of connecting bonds may be eliminated. Let a graph be composed of [u]n[/u]₀ medads, [u]n[/u]₁ monads, [u]n[/u]₂ dyads, [u]n[/u]₃ triads, etc.; and let κ₀ be the sum of the cycloses of the medads, κ₁ that of the monads, etc.; and let V, N, K denote respectively the valency, chorisis, and cyclosis of the graph.
