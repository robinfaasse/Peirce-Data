§7. [u]Characters of second intention[/u] are characters brought to our knowledge by observation of logical forms, not of things. Three relatives of second intention are of special importance. Namely, they are the monad, the dyad, and the triad which signify that their subjects are not one thing. They may be termed the [del]monads[/del] [ins]dyads[/ins] relatives of [u]non-unity[/u], and may be drawn as dots, or small [ins]round[/ins] black spots.

The monad of non-unity, which [del]means[/del] [ins]signifies[/ins] "— is not anything," is in itself absurd; so that its negative is empty. Consequently, this relative when encircled by itself as an ultimate antecedent (an antecedent by itself of some [del]part of the[/del] [ins]partial graph[/ins]) is of little use. When it is an ultimate consequent, the partial graph of which it is consequent asserts that nothing has the character signified by its antecedent.
