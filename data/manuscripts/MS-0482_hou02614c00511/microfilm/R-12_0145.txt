$\S 3$. Of systems of graphs where unconnected hands have two states, being either empty or holding the ends of bonds of which the other ends are loose, one class is to noticed. In [u]polar graphs[/u], all combinations of graphs take place in pairs, the two constituent graphs are antithetical, and every bond has its two ends unlike. No two hands of the same graph can be directly [del]united[/del] [ins]connected[/ins], because only antithetical graphs can be combined. The only hypothesis here to be examined is that the hands of all graphs are alike, and that there is no inherent antithesis between graphs; but in the act of combination one of the two combining graphs has its character reversed.
