Although the above described structure of logical graphs is [del]necessary[/del] for the reasons stated, yet certain simplifications are called for upon the principle, that when, [del]upon omitting[/del] [ins]if a certain[/ins] part of the graph be [del]omitted[/del], it is apparent that there is an omission, and [del]for[/del] [ins]when,[/ins] the various ways [del]in which[/del] the graph may be recompleted, all give the same meaning, the omission ought to be made. [del]Six[/del] [ins]Five[/ins] kinds of simplification ought to be made in obedience to this principle; and they greatly change the external appearance of the graph, and obscure the real structure of it, although this must be considered as really unchanged. These [del]changes[/del] [ins]abbreviations[/ins] also have the effect of making it seem that two distinct signs are used, although in fact there is but one.
