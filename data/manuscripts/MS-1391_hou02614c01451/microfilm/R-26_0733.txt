[u]William Gilbert of Colchester, physician of London[/u], on the Loadstone and Magnetic Bodies, and on the great Magnet the Earth. A New Physiology, demonstrated with many Argument and Experiments. A Translation by P. Fleury Mottelay. New York: John Wiley and Sons, 53 E 10th St. [date]1893[/date].

The lode-stone, which Mr. Mottelay will write load-stone, is a mass of oxide of iron of the approximate composition Fe₃O₄, which is susceptible of magnetic induction, though only very slowly, and which has so lain for [del]ages[/del] [ins]eons[/ins] in the telluric field of magnetism, as to have become polarized. Almost all [del]raw[/del] iron ore of this low degree of oxidation, which alone is magnetic, [del]is[/del] shows more or less polarity. The properties of a lode-stone are sensibly different from those of a steel magnet, owing to its great resistance to magnetic induction. It thus presents the phenomena of magnetism under a simpler form.
