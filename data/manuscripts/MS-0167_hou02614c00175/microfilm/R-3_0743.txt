In the French system, on the other hand, a
[u]billion[/u] is a thousand millions, a [u]trillion[/u] a thousand
billions, etc. But practically, billion, etc. are [del]words[/del]
little used.

Some persons say [u]one-and-twenty[/u], [u]two-and-twenty[/u]. We
frequently call a thousand, [u]ten hundred[/u], and count
the hundreds up to [u]twenty hundred[/u], and more. Many num-
bers have special names. Those that are not decimal
will be noticed in another chapter.

[u]Ten[/u] is called a [u]dicker[/u], a [u]decad[/u], a [u]decade[/u] (espe-
cially of days, and of years), the [u]tetractys[/u], the [u]quaternary[/u]
[del]number[/del]. But the last two are not cardinal numbers.

[u]A hundred[/u] is called a [u]century[/u] (especially of years.)

[u]A thousand[/u] is called a [u]chiliad[/u].

[u]Ten thousand[/u] is called a [u]myriad[/u], and a [u]sum[/u] (of nails.)

[u]A hundred thousand[/u] is called a [u]lac[/u] (of rupees, etc.), and
a [u]plum[/u] (of pounds sterling.)

[u]A thousand million[/u] is called a [u]milliard[/u].
