[u]Counting, Measuring, and Decimal Names of Numbers[/u].

The numerals, [ins]or cardinal numbers,[/ins] are a series of words used to count with, [ins]they are[/ins] as, [u]one[/u], [u]two[/u], [u]three[/u], [u]four[/u], [u]five[/u], [u]six[/u], [u]seven[/u], [u]eight[/u], [u]nine[/u], [u]ten[/u], [u]eleven[/u], [u]twelve[/u], etc.

To count a [del]lot[/del] [ins]collection or lot[/ins] of things we connect these numerals successively with the things, one numeral with each thing, — as, for example, by pointing at the things one after another and calling the numerals in regular order as we point. When there remain no more of the [del]lot[/del] unconnected with numerals, the last of these used is the number of things in the lot. This number will be the same in whatever order the things are counted.
