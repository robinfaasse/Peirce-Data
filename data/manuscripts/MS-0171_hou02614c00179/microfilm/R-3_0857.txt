The succession of rational fractions begins by $\frac{0}{1}$. Then follow: $\frac{1}{1}$, $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{1}{3}$ etc to $\frac{1}{12\text{I}_2}$ or $\frac{1}{12(\text{I}_2+1)}$. Call $Q_2$ the [ins]integral part of the[/ins] quotient of $\text{I}_2$ by $2$, if it is divisible, but if not the next higher whole number. Then the succession ends at $\frac{1}{Q_2}$.

Next follow: $\frac{2}{2Q_2-1}$, $\frac{1}{Q_2-1}$, $\frac{2}{2Q_2-3}$, $\frac{1}{Q_2-2}$, $\frac{2}{2Q_2-5}$, $\frac{1}{Q_2-3}$, etc.
this series ending with $\frac{1}{Q_3}$.

Then follow $\frac{3}{3Q_3-1}$ (if $3Q_3-1$ does not exceed $\text{I}_3$), $\frac{2}{2Q_3-1}$, $\frac{3}{3Q_3-2}$, $\frac{1}{Q_3-1}$
$\frac{3}{3Q_3-4}$, $\frac{2}{2Q_3-3}$, $\frac{3}{3Q_3-5}$, $\frac{1}{Q_3-2}$
$\frac{3}{3Q_3-7}$, $\frac{2}{2Q_3-5}$, $\frac{3}{3Q_3-8}$, $\frac{1}{Q_3-3}$
this series ending with $\frac{1}{Q_4}$.
