<u>XIII</u>. Under even enclosures any graph may be erased; and under odd enclosures any graph may be inserted.

<u>Proof</u>. First, let the graph, [u]a[/u], be [del]detached[/del] unenclosed and either detached or attached by any number of heavy lines to the rest of the graph, [u]r[/u]. Then, I say, that all the attachments of [u]a[/u] may be [ins]illatively[/ins] cut through and [u]a[/u] with the fragments of its attachments can be illatively erased. For by <u>III</u> [u]a[/u] with its attachments could be erased were it the whole graph. <tex>$\therefore$</tex> by <u>V</u> it can equally be erased when it is a part of the graph. [del]Q.E.D.[/del]
