[pn]2[/pn] [u]§5. Logical Interpretation of the Basic Rules by means of the Constitutive Conventions.[/u]

I. Some graph cannot be written unenclosed. That is, some proposition is false.

It is quite possible that a mind incapable of conceiving falsity should reason. But such a mind could have no logic. It could only accept, not reject.

II. A graph, once written, may remain. That is, that which is once true is always true.

For the flow of time concerns only our state of knowledge; but the truth is independent of our state of knowledge. This is anti-Hegelian.

III. The graph may be erased. That is, there is no falsity in non-assertion. Another anti-Hegelian position.

IV Graphs that can be separately written can be written together. That is, propositions that are each true are true when combined.

V. Every rule of illative [del]transformation[/del] [ins]insertion or erasure[/ins] applies to every unenclosed graph. For instance, if it is a rule that if A can be transformed into AB, then C can be transformed into CD, it is equally a rule that if AE can be transformed into ABE, then CE can be transformed into CDE.

For every rule permits certain transformations under all circumstances when certain conditions are fulfilled. Therefore when [del]other[/del] [ins]certain[/ins] circumstances are [del]true[/del] [ins]in addition to those conditions[/ins] the rule permits the same transformations.

[marginalia]Every illative transformation can be effected by insertions and erasures, contrary to Mr. Jevons's doctrine that substitution is the fundamental illative operation.[/marginalia]
