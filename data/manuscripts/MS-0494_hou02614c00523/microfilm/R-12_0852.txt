A subject may be specified either by an index or purely
denotative sign which either causes the experience of it
or recalls a previous experience, or by a sign which
describes what the interpreter of the graph has to do to
gain experience of the object meant; and this for the
present may be restricted to two kinds, each having
reference to an individual collection of objects.
One requires a suitable object to be chosen from the
collection, in the interest of the truth of the proposition,
which is called a
[ins]Particular subject.[/ins] The other permits any object to be chosen
from the collection, [ins]even[/ins] against the interest of the truth of the
proposition, which is called a [u]Universal subject[/u].
