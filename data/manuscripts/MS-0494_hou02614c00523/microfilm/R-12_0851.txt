§2. Commentary on §1.

1. I say, we "pretend" the graph is a general description of the recognized state of things, because the purpose of the system is to facilitate the study of logic, not to enunciate material truth; so that we do not care whether the proposition written in the form of the graph is really true or not.

The recognized state of things may be the actual truth, or something else. It is supposed to be perfectly definite, so that every proposition is true or false of it.

The graph is, therefore, of the nature of a verb predicated of this state of things. Although a verb, it may involve merely denotative, [del]and thus[/del] individual elements. Namely, verbs may be divided into [u]medads[/u], or complete assertions, as "it rains", "some man respects every woman"; [u]monads[/u], or assertions which require only the specification of one individual, or subject (sometimes called in grammar an [del]object[/del] [ins]object[/ins]) to be complete, as "respects every woman", "some man respects"; [u]dyads[/u], or assertions requiring the addition of two subjects to make them complete, as "respects", "A gives B to", "gives B to C", "A gives [del]to[/del] [ins]to[/ins]"; [u]polyads[/u], ([del]triads, tetrads, etc[/del]) which require the addition of more than two subjects to make them complete.
