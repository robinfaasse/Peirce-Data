[pn]§4[/pn] [u]Basic Formal Rules[/u]
from which all illative transformations are deducible without reference to the constitutive conventions.

I. Some possible graphs cannot be written unenclosed.
II. A graph, once written, may remain.
III. The whole graph may be erased.
IV. Graphs that might be written separately, may be written together unenclosed.
[del]V. Every rule of illative transformation applies to every unenclosed part of a graph except the oval boundary can enclose, which alone is not itself a graph.[/del] [ins]insertion or erasure[/ins]
VI. Every graph is equivalent to an enclosure containing some graph.
VII. Every graph may have two ovals drawn round it.
VIII. If any graph, A, is illatively transformable [ins]by the insertion of[/ins] into another, B, then [del]an[/del] [ins]by the insertion of[/ins] enclosure containing only B is illatively transformable into an enclosure containing A; provided that no [del]graph[/del] [ins]line[/ins] not connected with an index must be cut by the enclosing oval.
IX. Any graph of the kind called an index may be written unenclosed.
X. Any two indices transformable into one another may be joined by a heavy line[del], the indices being erased[/del], and such a line is a graph.
XI. Every heavy line can have some index attached to it.
XII. The shape of a heavy line, and particularly whether a furcation occurs at one part or another of it, within or without an oval, is unimportant.
