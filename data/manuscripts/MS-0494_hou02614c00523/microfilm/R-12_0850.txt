§ 1. Statement of the Constitutive Conventions of the System.

1. A certain place appropriate to the diagram is to be called the "sheet of the graph", and the whole of the diagram then written is called "the graph". "A graph" is anything which might, in accordance with the system, be written alone on the sheet of the graph, so as to become the graph.

There is a certain recognized state of things, agreed upon beforehand, of which [del]the[/del] [ins]we[/ins] pretend that the graph is a general description more or less vague.

2. A graph that is part of the graph has its full effect in rendering the description less vague.

3. The continuous part of a heavy line is a graph signifying the individual identity of what its parts severally denote. [ins](A "peg" [del]th[/del] at the crossing of two heavy lines shows they are to be regarded as separate.)[/ins] The whole graph may be conceived as connected by a heavy line with an index of the recognized state of things it describes; but this line is not drawn.

4. A general description within a lightly drawn oval is not understood to refer to the recognized state of things, but [ins]is to be taken as other description[/ins] [del]to the imaginary or hypothetical, and[/del] denotes [del]anything you please[/del].

[del]4[/del] [ins]5[/ins]. A lightly drawn line crossing any graphs signifies the falsity of some one or more of them.
