4. The light line crossing out a graph denies the
part it crosses. Thus,

[fig]Diagram showing "kills" crossed out and "loves" uncrossed[/fig]

means, something loves something and does not kill it.

[fig]Diagram showing both "kills" and "loves" crossed out[/fig]

means, something neither kills nor loves something.

[fig]Diagram showing "kills" and "loves" crossed out, and another "loves" uncrossed[/fig]

means, something loves something by which it is either
not killed or not loved.

But I do not use the light line [del]in this[/del] [ins]except[/ins] across
heavy lines to express otherness.

[fig]Diagram showing "Euclid of Megara" and "Euclid the Geometer" connected by a crossed-out heavy line[/fig]

means, Euclid of Megara is not Euclid the geometer,
(but both, as designate individuals, are necessarily
known to exist.)

[fig]Diagram showing "kills" uncrossed and "loves" crossed out[/fig]

means, something kills something and loves something else.
