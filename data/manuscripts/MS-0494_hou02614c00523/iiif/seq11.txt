A subject may be specified either by an index or purely denotative sign which either causes the experience of it or recalls a previous experience, or by a sign which describes what the interpreter of the graph has to do to gain experience of the object meant; and this for the present may [del]be[/del] restricted to two kinds, each having reference to an individual collection of objects. One requires a suitable object to be chosen from the collection, in the interest of the truth of the proposition, which is called a [u]Particular subject[/u]. The other permits [u]any[/u] object to be chosen from the collection, [ins]even[/ins] against the interest of the truth of the proposition, which is called a [u]Universal subject[/u].
