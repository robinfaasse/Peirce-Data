VI. Every graph is equivalent to an enclosure containing
some graph. That is, every proposition is the negative
of some proposition; that is, every proposition is equivalent
to saying that some proposition is false.

VII. Two ovals may be drawn round any graph. That is, if
a proposition is true, it is false that it is false.

VIII. If A is transformable into AB then [fig]B[/fig] is transformable
into [fig]A [fig]B[/fig][/fig]. If A cannot be true unless B is true [ins]too[/ins], then if
B is not true, neither is A.

IX. An index may be written unenclosed. An index is
the sign of an existing individual. The existence
of the sign implies an actual experience of the
existence of the thing.

X. Two mutually transformable indices maybe joined by a
heavy line. That is, there is but one individual de-
noted by each index. Or an individual is indivisible.

XI. Every heavy line can have some index attached to it.
That is whatever is true of something is true of some
individual thing.
