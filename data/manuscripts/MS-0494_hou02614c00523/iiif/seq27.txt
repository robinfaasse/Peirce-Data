§4. [u]Basic Formal Rules[/u]
from which all illative transformations are deducible without reference
to the constitutive conventions.

I. Some possible graph cannot be written unenclosed.
II. A graph, once written, may remain.
III. The whole graph may be erased.
IV. Graphs that might be written separately may be written together unenclosed[del] and make a partial graph[/del].
V. [del]Every rule of illative transformation applies to every unenclosed [ins]insertion or erasure[/ins] part of a graph [del]except the oval boundary of an enclosure, which alone is not its of a graph.[/del][/del]
VI. Every graph is equivalent to an enclosure containing some graph.
VII. Every graph may have two ovals drawn round it.
VIII. If any graph, A, is illatively transformable [ins]by the insertion of[/ins] into another, B, then [del]an enclosure containing only B is illatively transformable [ins]by the insertion of[/ins] into an enclosure containing A; provided that no [ins]graph[/ins] [del]heavy[/del] line not connected with an index must be cut by the enclosing oval.[/del]
IX. Any graph of the kind called an index maybe written unenclosed.
X. Any two indices transformable into one another may be joined by
a heavy line[del]s[/del], [ins]the indices being erased[/ins] and such a line is a graph.
XI. Every heavy line can have some index attached to it.
XII. The shape of a heavy line, and particularly whether a furcation oc-
curs at one part or another of it, within or without an oval, is unim-
portant.
