§1. Statement of the Constitutive Conventions of the System.
____________________________________________________

1. A certain place appropriated to the diagram is to be called the "sheet of the graph", and the whole of the diagram then written is called "[u]the[/u] graph". [u]A[/u] graph is anything which might, in accordance with the system, be written alone on the sheet of the graph, so as to become [u]the[/u] graph.

There is a certain recognized state of things, agreed upon beforehand, of which [del]the[/del] [ins]we pretend that the[/ins] graph is a general description more or less vague.

2. [u]A[/u] graph that is part of [u]the[/u] graph has its full effect in rendering the description less vague.

3. The continuous part of a heavy line is a graph signifying the individual identity of what its parts severally denote. [ins](A "frog" at the crossing of two heavy lines shows they are to be regarded as separate.[/ins] The whole graph may be conceived as connected by a heavy line with an index of the recognized state of things it describes; but this line is not drawn.

4. A general description within a lightly drawn oval is not understood to [del]refer to the recognized state of things[/del] [ins]take other description[/ins], but [ins]to be imaginary or hypothetical, and[/ins] denotes anything you please [ins]to[/ins]

[del]4[/del] 5. A lightly drawn line crossing any graphs signifies the falsity of some one or more of them.
