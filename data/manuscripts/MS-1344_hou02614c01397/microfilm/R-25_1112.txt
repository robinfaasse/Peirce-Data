The first is that much of our thought is not, when it
takes place, reasoning at all, which when recollected
appears to have been reasoning, and which, if recollected
in time, thereby becomes reasoning. This is because no
thought which is not deliberate and controlled can
properly be called reasoning. In the second place, all
our necessary reasoning, including much reasoning
about probabilities, is performed by observing a
real or imaginary construction, or a real object which
is looked upon as if it were a construction and is altered
in the imagination; and all reasoning performed
in this way is essentially of the same kind as the
