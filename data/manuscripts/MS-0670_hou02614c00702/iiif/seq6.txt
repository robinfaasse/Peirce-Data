The fact that according to this syntax any single word is an assertion classes the syntax, but does not characterize it. With that understanding, "man" [ins]by itself[/ins] would most naturally mean what this syntax makes it mean, and the same may be said of "loves," "gives," "sells" etc. Yet "man" might mean "Everything is a man"; and "love," everything loves everything, etc. This latter was [del]in fact[/del], indeed, the first proposal which led up to the syntax here to be described. [del]The former[/del] [ins]That first proposed[/ins] may be named "the Syntax of Entitative Graphs" and [del]that[/del] [ins]which[/ins] is here to be described is "the Syntax of Existential Graphs." [ured]| New ¶ here.[/ured] There are but [del]two[/del] [ins]three[/ins] [del]sage[/del] peculiar signs that the Syntax of Existential Graphs absolutely requires. [del]The first of these is a sign which shall deny a [ins]Graph or[/ins] scribed assertion. Such a sign is required, since "Not man" would only mean, there is something besides what some man, or else, "there is something that is not a man".[/del]
