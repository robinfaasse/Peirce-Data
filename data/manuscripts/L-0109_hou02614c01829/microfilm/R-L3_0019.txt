My dear Prof. Peirce -

The Arithmetic is on its way home, and should have been returned long ago. I wanted to finish it carefully and also to write a letter - for which I have never found a fitting opportunity. I have been a wanderer ever since I left Grey Towers, and [del]only settled to work a few days ago.[/del]

The Arithmetic seems to me admirable, the method beautiful, the rough and simple, perfectly adapted to a child's comprehension - and above all, interesting! For my own part, I should delight in it. The only difficulty likely to arise will be, as I suggested, a lack of docility and patience on the part of the teacher - But, as you very wisely answered, that is a factor without which nothing can be done with any text-book.

I really find there is as much teaching in your book for teacher, as for pupil, and, I think it is quite as much needed in one case as the other. Surely, wisdom is justified of her children - and the best endorsement of the method is in the two illustrious pupils who have been trained by it.

The preface is charming.

I feel a great desire to begin at once the training of a primary class in Arithmetic; and when I do, the suggestions of Clytie Peirce will be carried out as far as I can remember them - I think that is the best answer I can give to your question - do I find it adaptable to the needs of a child's mind and to class work?

I shall take great interest in the rest of the series and hope you will bring out the text-books in time to be available for next year. I am sure they will help us on our way and smooth the road before us -
