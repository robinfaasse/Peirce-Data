All these things ought clearly to be taken account of
by the man whose office it is to teach mathematics, especi-
ally to average pupils. Such a teacher has a hard task indeed
The proportion of boys to whom mathematics comes easy is
small, they may be said to exceptional cases. The teacher
has therefore a problem in psychology before him. What are
the faculties he is to call into play? Into what state of
mind is he to endeavor to put his pupil? These are questions
requiring psychological study. But besides these, logical
questions of extreme difficulty present themselves to him.
What is the real nature of mathematical demonstration? If the
teacher cannot answer that how can he expect to get the
demonstration into the head of his pupil? When I see what
