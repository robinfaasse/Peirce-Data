LOGIC.

[u]Chapter I. Of Thinking as Cerebration.[/u]

Thinking is done with the brain; and the brain is a complexus of nerves; so that thinking is necessarily subject to the general laws of nervous action.

The anatomical elements of the nervous system are three: the afferent nerve, the central cell, and the eferent nerve. The extremity of the afferent nerve is irritable, and when it is irritated something is transmitted along the nerve to the central cell, where some sort of force is generated, in consequence of which a sort of wave travels down the eferent nerve to its extremity, which has the property of setting up some action, such as muscular contraction, secretion by a gland, or a new explosion in another cent[del]r[/del][ins]r[/ins]al nerve-cell.
