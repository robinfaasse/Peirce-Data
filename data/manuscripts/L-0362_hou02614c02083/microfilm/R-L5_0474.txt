THE LIBRARY OF PRINCETON UNIVERSITY.

PRINCETON, N. J., [date]Feb. 15. 1902[/date]

Charles S. Peirce, Esq.
Milford, Pa.

Dear Sir:

Your letter of the 13[ins]th[/ins] inst. [ins]to Dr Richardson[/ins] has been received.
Dr Richardson is in Europe, and we are forwarding your
letter to him. He will doubtless give it his earnest considera-
tion.

In the meantime we are sending by express as requested

(1) Vol 7 of the Presbyterian Review on page 496 of which is
the allusion to Dr Burdin and Saint-Sernin.

(2) Dr Shields' Philosophia Ultima.

This library does not possess any of the works of
Dr Burdin or of any one by that name, and I have not
been able to verify the assertion of Flint.

May I suggest in the meantime, until you hear
more accurately from Dr Richardson, that perhaps the
"Dr Burdin" in question may be Charles Burdin, the
French doctor, born in 1778, died 1856, secretary of the
old Société de Médecine at Paris and a member of the newer
Académie de Médecine. He attended Saint-Sernin in his
last illness. He was the author of [u]Histoire du magnétisme[/u]
