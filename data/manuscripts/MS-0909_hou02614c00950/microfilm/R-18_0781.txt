Besides genuine secondness, there is a degenerate sort, which does not exist as such, but is
only so conceived. Degenerate seconds are of two families [del]are of two families[/del]; for either there
are two independent objects, not coupled together in existence, but only in the mind, or there is
but one object which is second to itself, or to which an abstraction is taken as second. The medi-
eval logicians [following a [ins]hint[/ins] of Aristotle] distinguished between real relations and relations
of reason. A real relation subsists in virtue of a fact which would be totally impossible were
either of the related objects destroyed; while a relation of reason subsists in virtue of two
facts, one of which only would disappear on the annihilation of either of the relates. Such are
all resemblances. Any two objects in nature resemble one another, and indeed in themselves just as
much as any other two; it is only in reference to our senses and needs that one resemblance [del]count[/del]
counts for more than another. Rumford and Franklin resembled one another by virtue of being both
Americans; but either would have been just as much an American if the other had never lived. On
the other hand, the fact that Cain killed Abel is not a mere aggregate of the two facts that Cain
killed somebody and that Abel was killed. But resemblances are not the only relations of reason;
though they have that character in an eminent degree. Contrasts and comparisons generally are of
the same sort. Resemblance is an identity of characters; that is to say, the mind embraces the
resembling ideas together into one conception. Other relations consist in ideas being connected
by the mind in other ways.

Identity is the relation that everything bears to itself. Lucullus dines with Lucullus. We
speak of requirements and motives in the language of forces, as though a man were under compulsion
from without, so with the voice of conscience; and we observe ourselves and our own feelings by a
reflective sense. An echo is my own voice coming back to answer itself. In all these cases, there
is a second object which the mind finds it convenient to regard as set over against itself.
In like manner we may take the abstract quality of an object as a correlate of the object.
