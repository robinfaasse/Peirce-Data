any such [del]entity[/del] [ins]un[/ins]ity, for these [del]things[/del] unities are essentially synthetical, and as such are as rem[ins]ote[/ins]
from the idea of first as possible. The first presents itself as unsubjected and unrelated to any[ins]thing[/ins]
thing that goes before or stands behind, and therefore as present, immediate, fresh, new, initia-
tive, original, spontaneous, free. To be first, it must be alone and indivisible. It must be pre-
sent, then, for if absent, it is thought only by a representation, to which it is other. It must
be immediate, for the same reason. It is fresh and new; otherwise something went before it. It is
initiative, original, spontaneous, and free; otherwise it is determined by a cause to which it is
second. It is also something vivid and conscious, otherwise it would have to be thought as the ob-
ject or second of a feeling. It cannot be asserted. Let that be done, and it has already lost its
characteristic innocence. Stop to think of it, and it has flown. It precedes all assertion, all
differentiation. The discerning intellect may pronounce it manifold; but in its own immediacy
it has no parts, any more than it has unity It is the evanescent vividness of immediate conscious
ness. But every description, and every thought of it, must be false to it, by introducing some
reference to something else, and thus putting it along with something else.
