"How did I slay thy son?" asked the [del]white[/del] merchant, and the genie replied, "When thou threwest away the date-stone, it smote my son who was passing at the time, on the breast, and he died forthright." Here there were two independent facts, first that the merchant threw away the date-stone, and second that the date-stone struck and killed the genie's son. Had the date-stone been aimed at the genie's son, the case would be different; there would have been a relation of aiming, which would connect the aimer, the thing aimed, and the object aimed at, in one fact. What monstrous injustice [ins]and inhumanity[/ins] on the part of that genie to hold that poor merchant responsible for that accident! I remember how I wept at it the day my father first read me the story.
