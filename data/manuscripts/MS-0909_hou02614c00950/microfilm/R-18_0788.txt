Things, as well as processes are thirds. The subject of a sentence is first, the direct object
is second, and the instrument or other circumstance is third.

It will naturally be asked, why stop at the third, why not go on to a conception of fourth,
fifth, etc. The reason is that those numbers offer no new conception, they are mere complications
of the third. The fact that A [del]presents B with a gift C[/del] presents B with a gift C, is a triple relation and
as such cannot possibly be resolved into any combination of dual relations. In the first place the
idea of a combination itself involves the conception of a third, for the combination is something
distinguishable from either of its two or more parts. But even leaving that consideration aside,
the fact that A presents B with C is not a mere combination of the three dual facts that A parts
with C voluntarily, that B receives C, and that A benefits B. Thus, a three cannot be analyzed
