[u]The Locution[/u]. Instead of the clumsy phrase "$P$ stands in the relation
$r$ to $Q$," I say indifferently, as convenience dictates, either
$P$ is $r$ to $Q$,
or $Q$ is $r$'d by $P$.

[u]Definition of Appurtenance[/u]. By an appurtenance, I mean any relation,
[fig]black circle[/fig] $r$, whose essential nature is repugnant to any one subject $P$
being $r$ to two different individual objects or to two mutually exclusive
generals.
