[del]Something is true of whatever can be mentioned.[/del]
Something or other is true of every [u]ens[/u]. For to name,
mention, or any way to direct our thoughts to a thing is to
imply something in regard to it. There must be something de-
finite about it in order that we may thus lay hold of it.
We cannot [del]not[/del] clearly understand this. But it is the first
law of logic.

A [u]quality[/u], in the general sense in which I shall employ
the term, means anything which is true of anything. There are
many terms which are used to express the same thing; as, mark,
character, [del]predicate[/del] [ins]attribute, determin-[/ins]. An [u]ens[/u], in so far as it has a quality,
may be termed a [u]quale[/u], or the subject of a quality. The more
qualities an [u]ens[/u] has the more determinate it is said to be. Thus,
fluidity is more determinate than [ins]mere[/ins] existence, and liquidity
than fluidity. So red is more determinate than colour, and
scarlet than red. The relation of a quality to its subject is termed in-
herence. [del]The quality inheres in the subject.[/del]

To suppose an [u]ens[/u] without any quality would be a violation
of the first law of logic, yet such a supposition would appear to
be selfconsistent. But to suppose a quality [del]without an ens to be-[/del]
[del]long to[/del] not inhering in any [u]ens[/u], is absurd in itself, and op-
posed to the very definition of quality.
