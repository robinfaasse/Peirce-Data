Zöllner and others have constructed what they
call a color intensity curve, which is a curve
showing the manner in which the [del]intensity[/del] [ins]apparent[/ins]
brightness is altered by altering the [del]readings[/del] [ins]setting[/ins] of the
color circle. I have made similar experiments,
and from them I derive the following formula, in
which $\theta$ is the reading of the circle.

$\text{Diff. mag.} = -3.928 + .085575\ \theta - .0004065\ \theta^2$

This has been computed by Cauchy's formula. I have
also calculated a term depending on $\theta^3$ but it is
very small, and the second degree formula gives the
probable error of a single observation $\pm 0.042^{\text{mag}}$
while the third degree formula only reduces it to
$\pm 0.041^{\text{m}}$; so I prefer the former. The following
diagram shows the curves of Zöllner, Rosén, and
