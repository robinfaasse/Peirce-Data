Proper is a peculiar mixed argument, (and not a syllogism, or in-
ference from two premises,) of which, as Qualitative Induction is an
ingredient, [ins]by an error of classification[/ins] [del]Other writers[/del] do not regard Retroduction as an
argument, at all, since it does not justify the assertion of its
conclusion, but only the hope (or fear) that it is true, with
such an embryonic assertion as that passion involves. But the jus-
tification of Induction has been a great problem, and we must
examine together those of the proposed solutions which because they
have been widely accepted, or for any other reason, seem to share with
the trouble. They are those of Aristotle, the Stoics, the Epicureans,
Sir Francis Bacon, Laplace and the mathematicians, Boole, [del]John[/del]
William Whewell, John Stuart Mill, the Père Gratry (de l'Oratoire);
although for my accounts of four of these I am forced to trust to recol-
lections as a particularly treacherous memory can report after a lapse
of 40 years, unaided by the slightest memoranda.
