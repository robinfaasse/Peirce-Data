§114 $\Omega = \mathcal{F}(x+y\sqrt{-}) + \mathcal{F}_{1}(x-y\sqrt{-})$
We have here a case of the integration of equations,
which is a part of the calculus we have not yet
taken up. We study specials cases before we study
the theory, just as it is usual to speak before
studying grammar.
Let
$x_{1} = x+y\sqrt{-}$
$y_{1} = x-y\sqrt{-}$
$D_{x}\Omega = D_{x_{1}}\Omega D_{x}x_{1} + D_{y_{1}}\Omega D_{x}y_{1}$
Which is the usual mode of taking derivatives
of functions which implicitly contain the quan-

tity, according to which we wish to take the de-
rivate, but is explicitly composed of other
functions.
$D_{x}y_{1} = 1$
$D_{x}x_{1} = 1$
$D_{x}\Omega = (D_{x_{1}} + D_{y_{1}})\Omega$
$D^{2}_{x}\Omega = (D_{x_{1}} + D_{y_{1}})^{2}\Omega = (D^{2}_{x_{1}} + 2D_{x_{1}}D_{y_{1}} + D^{2}_{y_{1}})\Omega$

$D_{y}\Omega = D_{x_{1}}\Omega D_{y}x_{1} + D_{y_{1}}\Omega D_{y}y_{1} = (D_{x_{1}} - D_{y_{1}})\Omega\sqrt{-}$
$D^{2}_{y}\Omega = (D_{x_{1}} - D_{y_{1}})^{2}(-\Omega) = (-D^{2}_{x_{1}} + 2D_{x_{1}}D_{y_{1}} - D^{2}_{y_{1}})\Omega$

Add together $D^{2}_{x}\Omega$ & $D^{2}_{y}\Omega$
$4D_{x_{1}}D_{y_{1}}\Omega = (D^{2}_{x} + D^{2}_{y})\Omega = 0$
$D_{x_{1}}D_{y_{1}}\Omega = 0$

Integrate $D_{y_{1}}\Omega = \text{constant} = \mathcal{F}_{y_{1}}$
$\Omega = \mathcal{F}_{y_{1}} + \mathcal{F}_{1}x_{1}$

There is usually much trouble in finding $\mathcal{F}$ & $\mathcal{F}_{1}$.
So much so, indeed, as to render the process
more difficult than that of solving the whole
question without the aid of Laplace's equation.
