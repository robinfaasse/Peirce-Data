§154 The difference of the squares of the radii
vectores from the centre, of two corresponding
points upon the surface of two ellipsoids which
have the same foci, is equal to the difference
of the squares of their semiaxes.
$$\frac{x}{x'} = \frac{A_x}{A'_x}$$
$$x' = \frac{x A'_x}{A_x}$$

§155 The distance of any point upon the surface of
an ellipsoid, from a point upon the surface of
another ellipsoid which has the same foci, is
equal to the distance of the two corresponding
points of the ellipsoids from each other.

[fig]Diagram of two confocal ellipses with intersecting lines and points labeled f, f', g, g', p, p'[/fig]

$$f^2 = -p^2 + (g' - [del]p[/del] [ins]-p'[/ins])^2 + r^2 =$$
$$r^2 + g'^2 - 2g'p'$$
$$f^2 - f'^2 = r^2 + \varepsilon^2 + g'^2 + \varepsilon^2 = 0$$
$$f = f'$$

These theorems, with the next, are from Ivory
— a Scotch-man & a great genius. They are
remarkable theorems; the set, indeed, to which
they belong is without parallel in mathemat-
ical science because they are radically erron-
eous. Analysis, up to this time, has not shown
where the error lies, but has only shown that
there is an error, by application to test cases.

§156 The external level surfaces of an ellipsoidal
Chaslesian shell are those of ellipsoids which have
the same foci with the outer surface of the Chas-
lesian shell.

This theorem is the culminating point of
many investigations on the attractions of el-
lipsoids.
