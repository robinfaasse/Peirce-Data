The work appears to me to be quite certainly a [ins]somewhat advanced[/ins] school text-book of arithmetic. It begins with fractions, and roughly speaking, treats of about the same topics as the latter part of a "high-school arithmetic" of today. I cannot admit the somewhat complicated hypotheses of the MM. Revillout that it is a copy of the arithmetical blotter of a backward school-boy of the time of Amenemhat III. MM. Revillout profess to state only a few of many reasons for this opinion. These few are as follows: First, under each chapter one example is worked out with [del]some[/del] a little more explanation than the others, of which the "ciphering" of the [del]arithmetical[/del] process is set down without any explanation, or very little. Second, these examples are sometimes worked quite wrongly. Third, the [del]ciphering[/del] [ins]computation[/ins] is not infrequently wrong; and sometimes there is in the margin a correction of the figure where the error crept in, without the correction being carried through the rest of the work.
