A great change is coming over the doctrine of probabilities. Mr. Venn happily says that this theory is the great addition which the age of science has made to logic, and that as the ancient logic had to pass through a realistic and a conceptualistic phase before it reached the truth in nominalism, so the [del]notion of[/del] predicate "probable" has been conceived, in youth, as belonging to the singular, objective event ([del]as if Bernoulli's theorem lays it down as a theorem that events occur with a frequency measured by their probabilities[/del]), afterwards as belonging to the conception [del]being in a fact is more or less vivid[/del], finally as belonging only to the denominations under which events are collected together.

Mr. Venn's book is the most nominalistic of any which has yet appeared.
