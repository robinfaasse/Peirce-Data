The pretension [del]that is[/del] [ins]has been[/ins] made, of reduc-
ing the probability of induction to a
matter of calculation; we will, there-
fore, take no account of any difference
between the probability of conclusions
being very false, or a little false. We
will suppose ourselves to be in posses-
sion of the fractions which express the
probabilities of the chance of the truth
of two independent modes of reasoning,
and of nothing else. On the basis of
this knowledge only, it is required to
find the probability of one or other be-
ing true when they conflict. The prob-
ability sought then, is equal to the following
fraction:

[del]The number [or proportion] of questions which one answers truly and the other falsely[/del]
The number [or proportion] of questions which one [ins]answers truly[/ins], the other answers falsely
