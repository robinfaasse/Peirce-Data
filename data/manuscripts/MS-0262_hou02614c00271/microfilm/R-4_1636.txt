As an element moves along the curve from an extraordinary node on an external branch, at first
each element enfilades an element near the end on the return to the node on the internal branch
and as the enfilading element moves forward the enfiladed element moves back, and they must meet
at some point, which will be the external side of an ordinary node, and the two parts of the curve
from the ordinary node to the extraordinary one and back to the ordinary one again must cut every
line in the plane. Of course, there are similar propositions with reference to the bitangents.

Two nodes may be situated with reference to one another in four ways. 1st, The first may be
outside of the second, and the second inside of the first; 2nd, they may be mutually external to
one another; 3rd, they may be mutually internal to one another; and 4th, one branch of each may
be outside and one inside the other. There may be any number of binodes all related to one another
in the first way. Outside of each there will be a ten bitangent for that node, so that there will
be an equal number of binodes and bitangents, and these bitangents will be related to one another
in the same way; and one branch at least, of an outer node must be outside one branch of the
bitangent of the node inside of that node. An element inside of the innermost binode will not be

enfiladed by any element at all; and an element outside the outermost bitangent will not be en-
filaded by any element. There may be any number of nodes all external to one another. Each will
lie inside a bitangent of its own. Any two [ins]of these[/ins] bitangents will either be outside one another or will
intersect. Besides that, there will be four more bitangents for each pair of nodes, two ordinary
and the other two extraordinary. The two ordinary ones will be interior to one another.
