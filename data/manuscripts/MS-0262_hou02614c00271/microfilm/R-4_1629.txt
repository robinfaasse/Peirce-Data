Let us now restrict all our studies, till further notice, to curves having no singularities but binodes and bitangents. Imagine such a curve in process of generation, and not yet complete. The initial tangent of such an incomplete curve, together with the line of junction of the first extremity with the last, divide the plane into two point-regions. We may use the sign + to denote the case in which a point of the curve just after the beginning and a point just before the end of the incomplete curve are in the same region, and - to denote the case of their being in different regions. The initial point of the curve and the intersection of the initial and final tangents divide the plane into two line-regions, and we may use + and - to denote respectively the cases of the tangent just after the first and that just after the last being in the same, or in different regions. Accordingly a symbol consisting of two algebraical signs enclosed in square brackets, the first sign referring to the points, the second to the tangents, will suffice to show the relation of the first element to the last. As the generation of the curve proceeds, one of two things must occur; either the turning motion will prevail over the progressive motion, so that it will pass through a state in which the first element enfilades the last, and this operation, since it reverses the first sign, while leaving the second unchanged, may be denoted by i-[= +]; or the progressive motion may prevail over the turning, so that the incomplete curve passes through a stage in which the first element is enfiladed by the last, and this operation, reversing the last sign and leaving the first may be denoted by j-[+ =].

Let us begin a curve by drawing a binode and then joining the ends. If inner branch is joined to inner, and outer to outer, the binode divides the curve into an inner and an outer part. Considering the two inner ends as the initial and final extremities of the incomplete curve, the symbol is [++] and these ends can be joined without further ado, just like the beginning and end of a curve whose generation has just been commenced. The initial element is therefore enfiladed twice; it must therefore be situated on the inside of a bitangent. It follows that the binode is inside of a bitangent, which it is outside of the binode.
