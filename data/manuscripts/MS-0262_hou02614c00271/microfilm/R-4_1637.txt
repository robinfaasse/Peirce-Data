[u]ON THE REAL QUALITATIVE CHARACTERS OF PLANE CURVES. BY C. S. PEIRCE.[/u]

I consider a plane having only real points and lines, being persuaded that imaginary algebra and geometry are only special varieties, and not truly the general case. Of curves in such a plane, I consider the qualitative characters, that is to say their incidences and coincidences, or more particularly, in reference to each the number of times it is cut by straight lines variously placed, the number of tangents to it from a point variously placed, the numbers of its double and stationary points and tangents, and the arrangement of these. I adopt the usual conception that when in the course of a continuous change, the number of incidences is reduced at a single instant and immediately restored, we are to say that the number is always the same, only at that instant two of the incidences coincide or one is carried to infinity. That is to say the distance becomes either infinitesimal or infinite. Consequently, I have a line at infinity, and need not consider any higher singularities.
