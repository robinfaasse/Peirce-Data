Let us now restrict all our studies, till further notice, to curves having no other singularities but binodes and bitangents. Imagine such a curve in process of generation, and not yet completed. It will be useful to denote the relation of the first and last elements of the incomplete curves by a sign consisting of two square brackets enclosing two algebraical signs of plus or minus followed by one of the four letters A, B, C, D. The two signs refer to the initial element, the letter to the final one. The first sign is + or - according as the intersection, I, of the initial and final tangents is forward or behind the initial point of incidence in the direction of the generation and without crossing infinity (which is a convenient line of reference.) The second sign is + or - according as the line of junction, J, of the beginning and end of the curve, not passing through infinity, is on the concave or convex side of the initial part of the curve. The letter A denotes that the last part of the curve runs away from I, and is concave to J; B that it runs toward I, and is concave to J; C that it runs away from I, and is convex to J; D that it runs toward I, and is convex to J. Thus, the symbol of an oval just commenced is [++A], that of an oval nearly complete is [- + B]. As the generation of the curve is continued, one or other of two things must happen; if the turning motion is in excess of the progressive motion in the generation, I will be carried through the initial incident point or through infinity (whichever it is approaching) causing a reversal of the first sign; or if the progressive motion prevails over the turning motion, J will be carried through the initial tangent or through infinity (whichever it is approaching) so as to reverse the second sign.
