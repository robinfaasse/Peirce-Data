The following notation is useful in considering the [del]situation[/del] situation of points and lines with reference to a curve. Let each point and line be denoted by a letter, and write the letters denoting points in a row in the order in which they are arranged round [del]any point[/del] [ins]the whole[/ins] of the curve, beginning with the direction in which the generator of the curve at that point moves and turning in the direction in which it turns. In another row write all the letters denoting lines in the order in which they are cut by the tangent to the curve at the generating point, beginning at the incident point and moving forward in the direction in which the generation proceeds. In addition to the lines and points which we wish to keep account of, we must also keep account of their intersections and junctions; and also admit to the rows the intersections and junctions of these junctions and intersections. As an example, suppose we wish to take account of the ways in which a curve (as usual, without inflexions or cusps) can wind about among four fixed points. Denote the four fixed points by the letters A, B, C, D; their 6 junctions by ab ac ad bc bd cd; and the 3 intersections of these junctions by abcd, acbd, adbc. We will suppose that the row of points to begin with is A, adbc, D, B, acbd, abcd, C. This line of points does not, naturally, leave the row of lines indeterminate, but postponing the consideration of that, I will simply state the following row of lines: [del]ad, ab, ac, bc, cd, bd[/del] ad, ab, ac, bc, cd, bd. As the generation of the curve proceeds, there are just three things which may happen. 1st, the generating point may cross the line ab, which is the first in the row of signs. In that case, the row of signs would become ab, ac, bc, cd, bd, ad. At the same time, the order of all the points on the line ad would become reversed in the row of points which would therefore become D, adbc, A, B, acbd, abcd, C. Second, instead of this, the generating tangent may traverse the point A, as being the first in the row of points. Then the three lines meeting at A will have their order in the row of lines reversed, so that this row will become ac, ab, ad, bc, cd, bd. The row of points will become adbc, B, B, acbd, abcd, C, A. After this, the tangent might traverse the point adbc, thus reversing the order of the two lines ad and bc. Then the row of points would become D, B, acbd, abcd, C, A, adbc; and the row of lines would become ac, ab, bc, ad, cd, bd. 3rd, returning to the original arrangement, the points acbd and abcd in the row of points being adjacent, and not having their intersection marked in the row of lines. But we cannot know for certain that this can be done without any intermediate changes unless we assure ourselves, in some way that if the junction of these points were inserted in the row of lines it would occupy the first place.
