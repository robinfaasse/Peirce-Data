A science by which things are tested is necessarily a classificatory science. Accordingly, what we have to study, in the first place, is the classification of inferences. As there are several different systems of qualitative analysis based on different classifications of chemical substances but all valid, so there are different valid systems of logic based on different classifications of inferences. The accomplished reasoner must be familiar with [del]more[/del] than one such system.

An inference is the admission of the truth of one fact owing to another fact being true. The [del]first[/del] former fact is the conclusion, the second the premise.

An argument is a body of premises considered as such.

Opinions differ; and there may be some subjects, such as matters of taste upon which men do not come to agreement. But there are subjects [ins]which[/ins] upon [del]a[/del] experience and reasoning, sufficiently long pursued, will produce a settlement of opinion. These are matters of fact. Upon these subjects, alone, has reasoning any validity; for if reasoning does not lead to one result, but leads some men one way and some another, it is self-destructive.

After a question has been started, opinions may for a while differ.
