Ashland House, [date]1894 May 25[/date].

My dear Mr. Plimpton:

The problem of how to make a really good book like Roget's Thesaurus is one I have had in mind for nearly forty years. I have made many experiments from time to time in order to ascertain what principles of classification would give the best results in practice, what the difficulties of the task would be, and what would be the speediest and best way of doing the work. I am confident that the book could be made by one person with aid about equivalent to a fairly intelligent type-writer in 16 months, and with a better assistant (capable of taking the place of the principal in case of death after he was trained to it) it could probably be done in a year, or less. The result would be 5 times as valuable to Roget, whom I use little
