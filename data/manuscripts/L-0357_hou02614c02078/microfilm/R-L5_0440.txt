I. He has not only omitted a host of most
valuable & expressive & excellent words, but he
has also [del]altogether[/del] omitted many necessary ideas.

II. His arrangement is unclear. It depends upon
a philosophy quite out of date. It is too metaphysi-
cal; and the metaphysics is neither modern nor
is it that which presides over the formation of
the words. My arrangement is to begin with
the simplest, most sensuous, most ordinary ideas,
for which we have generally direct names not
reposing on any metaphor. I first take the names
of sensations: colors, sounds, tastes, smells, feelings,
etc. Then, simple actions, walk, strike, eat, etc.
Then, common things, etc. I then take mathematical
& physical ideas. Then the simplest relations of life.
