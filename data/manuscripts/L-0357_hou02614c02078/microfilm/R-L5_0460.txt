My dear Mr. Plimpton:

You used to tell me that you liked getting letters from me, which was very kind in you, especially as I always had some favor to ask. So I have now; but I shall try to ask it so that it shall not be too great a bore to read the letter. A year ago there was a meeting of the National Academy of Sciences in Boston. I got a commission to report it for the Sun and carried Mrs. Peirce with me. There, after consultations with some dear friends of hers, she begged me to letter her come home alone. Some of her friends had persuaded my sister, who owns a students' dormitory and a lodging-house, to allow me to occupy a vacant and partially furnished room until it might be rented. Mrs. Peirce came here and I remained there, which was a very great strain on my health and spirits; for after all my disappointments, due to my having no "pull" in Washington, which in turn is due to my having the habits of a student and a recluse, — as well as to other [ins] & secondary [/ins] causes, — had worked so much on my spirits that I had no pleasure in living. I have none now, and would rather try my fortune in a future life. Only, I must be sure, that I have used my talents first.
