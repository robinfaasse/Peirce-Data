Symbols. The word “Symbols” has already many meanings;
and I shall ask leave to add a new one. Among its
early significations, perhaps the original one, is that
of a watch-word, a somewhat arbitrarily adopted
word or phrase, by which persons of one party recog-
nize one another. It is nearly in this sense that the
church creeds are called Symbols. So a flag is a
symbol, etc. I think, then, that I shall not wrench
the word too much if I use it to mean a sign to
which a general idea is attached by virtue of a habit,
which may have been deliberately instituted or may
have grown up in a natural way, [del]or[/del] [ins]and perhaps[/ins] have been
acquired with one’s mother’s milk, or even by inheritance[del],[/del]
[del]at birth.[/del] heredity.
