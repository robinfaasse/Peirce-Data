The word "I" can never be understood, unless the hearer takes the trouble actually to observe who it is that is speaking. The word "you" cannot be understood, unless [del]he[/del] [ins]one[/ins] finds reason in the attitude, countenance, etc. of the speaker to infer that he is addressing a person who can be seen, or indirectly observed. The words "this" and "that" need to be accompanied by gestures to be quite unmistakable; and those gestures it is which, being observed, are the real indices. The words "who" and "which" refer to names in collocation with them; and it is this observable collocation, not the mere word, which make those names identifiable.
