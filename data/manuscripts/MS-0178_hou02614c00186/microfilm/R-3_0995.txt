[u]Numeration[/u]

A great point is made of the manner of
writing the figures etc.

The figures must be very distinct, free [del]for[/del]
all fanciful forms, and so open that, in case
of error, a figure can have another written
over it without becoming illegible.

1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 0

The figures must be written in clearly marked
columns as evenly as may be.

In the Advanced book, the pupil is required
to state his proceeding and write against
each number what it is, and render the whole
a perspicuous statement.

[u]Addition.[/u]

Addition is by very much the most indispensa-
ble [del]a[/del] part of arithmetic. If one is an accom-
plished adder, one can [del]à la rigueur[/del] go without
knowing even the multiplication table; and at
any rate when addition is thoroughly mastered, it
affords a master-key to all the other operations. It
is also by far the most difficult of the operations
of arithmetic. For all these reasons taken together,
I bestow upon addition more time and exercise
than upon all the other operations taken together,
particularly in the primary book.

is the only really efficient way. It consists of
teaching the children to count rapidly by
