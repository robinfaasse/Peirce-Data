[date]1898 Apr 9[/date].

My dear Sir:

I have to thank you for your paper "On the Continuity of Chance" in which it cheers me to recognize some propositions with which I agree.

I should like to speak an encouraging word to you. Perhaps you don't need it. It is one of the serviceable characters of youth to have faith in oneself. I dare say you expect to do far better than I have done. I hope you may. Born in 1839, I graduated in 1859. I had studied Kant all my spare time for four years. In 1862 I published a philosophical thing. In many respects it was decidedly inferior to the paper you have sent me. I have kept at it hammer & tongs ever since, & if I do not delude myself, I still am strengthening. [del]No doubt you will have the same experience.[/del]

Very cordially
C. S. Peirce

Mr. Ellery W. Davis.
