But the very frequently occurring relations $x \infty y$ and
$x \delta y$ are still only capable of being expressed in a [del]rather[/del]
too complicated way

$x \infty y$ is $x \underline{\overline{y}} \vee \overline{x} \underline{y}$ or $x \overline{y} \vee \overline{x} y$ — [marginalia]wrong too[/marginalia]
and $x \delta y$ is $x \underline{y} \vee \overline{x} \underline{\overline{y}}$ or $x y \vee \overline{x} \overline{y}$

It will be best to retain one or both of them. Moreover, for
logical reasons, the sign $\infty$ is of the greatest importance.
From a logical point of view, we may say, as we shall see,
that it is the most important of any; and even from an
algebraical point of view, if we retain $\uparrow$ and $\vee$ as our
usual signs, [del]and[/del] the most important formula of the algebra,
apart from those of the commutative and associative
principles, requires this sign for its convenient and
clear expression. Namely, this formula is
$(x \uparrow y \vee z) \infty (x \uparrow y \vee z)$
$(x \vee y \uparrow z) \infty (x \vee y \uparrow z)$
expressing a sort of associativeness involving the two signs
