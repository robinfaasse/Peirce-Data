[u]Topic B. Complications of Dichotomic Algebra.[/u]

In 1693, Leibniz first introduced into algebra [ins]a conception[/ins] which, after it had been used by a hundred writers, Sylvester inconceivably imagined that he had invented, and, in consequence, then first gave it a name, the umbral notation. The name is not a particularly appropriate one; for who ever knew of shadows [del]coöperating[/del] [ins]co-operating[/ins] to make up a substantial body? But still, it is the only name in use; and therefore we may as well speak of umbrae, meaning objects which, combined into sets, make up quantities. Umbrae are to quantities what words are to sentences, or what things are to [del]facts[/del] [ins]events[/ins]. [del]A fact[/del] [ins]An event[/ins] is a reaction between facts. There is nothing to prevent a generalization by which the umbrae are themselves considered as quantities also. Then, they are to the quantities they form what a man and woman are to a married couple.
