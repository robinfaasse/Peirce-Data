Adopting these signs, I proceed to state, as definitions,
those fundamental conventions from which all the properties
of the signs, and all the necessary rules and formulae of the
algebra can be deduced. In doing so, I shall, after Schröder,
imitate a practice introduced into geometry, I believe, by
Gergonne (about 1820), of writing reciprocal, related propo-
sitions in parallel columns. The perfect correspondence
between aggregation and composition was vaguely asserted
