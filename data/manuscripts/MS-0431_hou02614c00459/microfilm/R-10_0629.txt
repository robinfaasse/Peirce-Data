That we can equally express every proposition by means of the vin-
culum and $\underline{\lambda}$ is sufficiently shown by the fact that $\underline{x}\cdot\underline{y}$ can be so
expressed. It is,
$(\underline{x}\cdot\underline{\lambda}\cdot\underline{\lambda}\cdot\underline{y}\cdot\underline{\lambda})\cdot\underline{\lambda}\cdot(\underline{x}\cdot\underline{\lambda}\cdot\underline{\lambda}\cdot\underline{y}\cdot\underline{\lambda})$.
It would hardly be worth while, for the sake of this proposition itself, to
show that no other single sign can do this. But for the sake of the
insight it gives into the powers of the signs, it may be well to do so.
Every proposition expressed by no other sign than vincula (and en-
closures) with $>$ asserts that the value of its first letter is V. Thus,
$(\underline{a}>\underline{b})>\underline{c}$ asserts that $\underline{a}>\underline{b}$ is V, that is, is true, and therefore
that $\underline{a}$ is V. And so it obviously will be with any such form.
