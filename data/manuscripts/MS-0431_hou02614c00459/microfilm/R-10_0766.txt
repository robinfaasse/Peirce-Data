Among the minor, but very striking characteristics of
mathematics, may be named the skeletal and fleshless build
of its propositions; the peculiar difficulty of its reasonings;
the perfect exactitude of its results; [ins]their [del]third[/del] generality;[/ins] and the practical in-
fallibility of his conclusions. It is always easy to be exact.
Only, one must abandon all ambition to be certain. It is
equally easy to be certain. One has only to be sufficiently
vague. But to be like mathematics, exact and certain
at the same time, is only possible when we speak
of actual existence, unless we confine ourselves to very
narrow assertions.
