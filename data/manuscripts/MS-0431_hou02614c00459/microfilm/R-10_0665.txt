[u]Definition of the Quantities of a Dichotomic Algebra.[/u]

If [u]x[/u] is any quantity of this algebra, then

No predicate and its negative can
both be true of [u]x[/u]. That is, [u]x[/u] is definite.

Every predicate or its negative
must be true of [u]x[/u]. That is, [u]x[/u] is individual.

Every proposition concerning quantities of the algebra which is
such that it must be true or false but cannot be both is itself a quantity
of the algebra; and no other propositions except [del]those which are primarily[/del]
[ins]quantities of the algebra, and those which relate to the values of quantities of the[/ins]
algebra, are quantities [del]of the algebra[/del].
[ins]Definitions of [u]v[/u] and [u]f[/u].[/ins]

No quantity of this algebra has, at
once, the two values [u]v[/u] and [u]f[/u].

Every quantity of this algebra
has either the value, [u]v[/u], or the value, [u]f[/u].

Every true proposition which is a
quantity of this algebra has the value [u]v[/u].

Every false proposition which is
a quantity of this algebra has the value, [u]f[/u].

[u]Definitions of the Vinculum and Obelus.[/u]

Every quantity written upon a sheet of assertion, is either written
with a vinculum extending under the [del]whole expression[/del] [ins]and[/ins] is thereby asserted to
have the value [u]v[/u].

with an obelus extending over the
whole of it, [ins]and[/ins] is thereby asserted to
have the value [u]f[/u].
