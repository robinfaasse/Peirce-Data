(75) $x \prec y = x \curlywedge (y \curlywedge y) = (x \curlywedge x) \curlywedge y$,*
(76) $x \infty y = (x \curlywedge x) \curlywedge y = x \curlywedge (y \curlywedge y)$.

We further have

(77) $\bar{x} = x \curlywedge x$
(78) Necessarily, $x \curlywedge (x \curlywedge x)$
(79) Necessarily, $(x \curlywedge x) \curlywedge x$
[del](80) $(x \curlywedge y) \curlywedge z = (x \curlywedge z) \curlywedge (y \curlywedge z)$[/del]

[del](80) $(x \curlywedge y) \curlywedge z = (x \curlywedge z) \curlywedge (y \curlywedge z)$[/del]
(81) $x \curlywedge (y \curlywedge z) = (x \infty y) \cdot (x \infty z)$
(82) $(x \curlywedge y) \curlywedge z = (x \curlywedge z) \cdot (y \curlywedge z)$
(83) $x \curlywedge (y \curlywedge z) = (x \curlywedge y) \cdot (x \curlywedge z)$
(84) $(x \cdot y) \curlywedge z = x \curlywedge (y \cdot z)$

It is probable that some of these formulae are contained in Mrs. Franklin's paper in [u]Studies in Logic[/u] (Boston: Little, Brown & Co. 1883), although the point of view is so different that it would require attentive reperusal to pick them out.
