There is another little group of algebraic words which must now be defined in the imperfect way in which they can be defined for dichotomic mathematics. In the first place there are the pair of terms, [u]constant[/u], or [u]constant quantity[/u], and [u]variable[/u], or [u]variable quantity[/u]. These words were introduced by the Marquis d'Hôpital in [date]1696[/date]. Suppose two couriers to set out, at the same instant, from two points 12 miles apart and to travel toward one another, the one at the rate of 7 miles an hour, the other at the rate of 8 miles an hour; when will they meet? They evident approach one another at the rate of 7 plus 8, or 15 miles an hour; and therefore they will [del]to[/del] reduce the distance of 12 miles to nothing in 12/15 of an hour, [ins]12 times 4, or[/ins] [del]or[/del] 48, minutes.
