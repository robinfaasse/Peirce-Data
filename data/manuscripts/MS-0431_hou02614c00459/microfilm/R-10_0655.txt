We then read off the conclusion below, noting well, that
each [del]compartment[/del] [ins]quadrant[/ins] consists of two compartments, since the
line that does not pass through the sign concerned is disregarded,
and unless [u]both[/u] are shaded, the quadrant is not closed. For
example, given that $y \ \bar{\infty} \ z$ and $x \ \bar{\infty} \ y$, our diagram becomes
as here shown.

[fig]Venn-like diagram with shaded regions and variables x, y, z[/fig]

It will be seen that although three quadrants appear, at first glance,
to be cut off from the lower sign, yet two of them are not really
cut off, since the more remote parts of them are unshaded. The
conclusion, therefore, is, that $x \ \bar{\infty} \ z$.

Such diagrams go by the name of Euler's diagrams,
although they are far older than Euler, and are very likely an-
cient. But the plan of shading them is due to Mr. Venn.*
Further on, I shall show how they may be rendered [del]more[/del] [ins]even[/ins] more efficient.
