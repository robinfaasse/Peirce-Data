Mathematics is the study of [ins](what is true)[/ins] hypothetical states of things. That is its essence and definition. Everything in it, therefore, beyond the [del]description of[/del] [ins]first[/ins] precepts for the construction of the hypotheses, has to be of the nature of [del]an[/del] apodictic [del]conclusion[/del] inference. No doubt, we may reason imperfectly and jump at [ins]a[/ins] conclusion; still, the conclusion so guessed at is, after all, that in a certain supposed state of things something would necessarily be true. Conversely, too, every apodictic inference is, strictly speaking, mathematics. But mathematics, as a serious science, has, over and above its essential character of being hypothetical, an accidental characteristic peculiarity, — a [u]proprium[/u], as the Aristotelians used to say, — which is of the greatest logical interest.
