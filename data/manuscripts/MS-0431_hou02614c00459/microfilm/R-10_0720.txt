If we identify $v$ with $\dot{\Delta}$, and $f$ with $\dot{\nabla}$, the distinction
between relative multiplication and composition will disap-
pear in regard to the ordinary quantities of dichotomic
algebra. For just as

$$\begin{array}{c}
v \cdot v = v \\
v \cdot f = f \\
f \cdot v = f \\
f \cdot f = f
\end{array}$$

so

$$\begin{array}{c}
\dot{\Delta} \cdot \dot{\Delta} = \dot{\Delta} \\
\dot{\Delta} \cdot \dot{\nabla} = \dot{\nabla} \\
[del]\dot{\nabla} \cdot \dot{\Delta}[/del] \ [ins]\dot{\nabla} \cdot \dot{\Delta}[/ins] = \dot{\nabla} \\
\dot{\nabla} \cdot \dot{\nabla} = \dot{\nabla}
\end{array}$$

But in regard to these new quantities, the distinction will
be maintained. For example,

$$\dot{\infty} \cdot \dot{\infty} = \dot{i} \qquad \dot{\infty} \cdot \dot{\infty} = \dot{\infty} \qquad \dot{\infty} \cdot \dot{\infty} = \dot{v}$$

I shall not further enlarge upon this matter at this
point, although [del]it ope[/del] the conception mentioned opens
a wide field; because it cannot be set in its proper
light without overstepping the limits of dichotomic ma-
thematics.
