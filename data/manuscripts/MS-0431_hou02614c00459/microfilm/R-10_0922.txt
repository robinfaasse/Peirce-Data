It needs to be remarked that, in all kinds of algebra, quantities are included in parentheses, square brackets, or braces, to signify that, in ulterior operations, they are to be regarded as forming a whole operand. The ordinary treatises have no occasion to dwell upon this sign, which almost explains itself. Yet it is one of the very most important, perhaps the very most indispensible, of all the signs of algebra; and it is efficient in the discovery of truth to the highest degree. It is, in fact, a sort of abstraction, although an abstraction from which the mind can with difficulty abstain; so that it requires some exercise of thought to see that there is any abstraction. It is so, however; for, identifying the sign of the operation being performed with a sign for its result, it proceeds to treat this as a subject of operation.
