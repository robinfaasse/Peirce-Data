[u]XV[/u]. Every expression of a quantity has the contrary value to the expression which results from the substitution in it of the internal negative of each quantity and operation.

Demonstration. For, in the first place, this is true regarding single letters; [del]for[/del] [ins]let[/ins] [u]x[/u] represent any single letter. Then, since [u]x[/u] operates upon nothing, [u]x[/u] gives the contrary value to [u]x[/u] when it operates upon whatever [u]x[/u] operates upon. In the second place, it is true of expressions operated upon by the vinculum and obelus. For since [u]x[/u] has the opposite contrary value to [u]x[/u], the vinculum is its own internal negative; and since [u]x[/u] has the contrary value to [u]x[/u], it is its own internal negative. In the next place, the proposition is true of any operator upon two operands; for by the definition [u]x[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] [u]y[/u] has the contrary value to that of [u]x[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] [u]y[/u]. Hence, if [u]y[/u] is [u]u[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] [u]v[/u], [u]x[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] ([u]u[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] [u]v[/u]) has the contrary value to [u]x[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] ([u]u[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] [u]v[/u]). And so if [u]y[/u] = [u]w[/u], [u]x[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] [u]y[/u] has the contrary value to [u]x[/u] [fig]obelus[/fig] [u]w[/u]. And by formation reasoning, it is evident that this will be the so in every case.
