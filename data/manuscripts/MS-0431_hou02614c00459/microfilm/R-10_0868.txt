A rhema is described as monadic, dyadic, polyadic, etc.
according as the number of [del]its[/del] blanks is one, two, more than
two, etc. A monadic rhema (from [greek]ρημα[/greek]) is a rhema
with no blank, consequently, a proposition. Such an ex-
pression as

[u]— is neither the same as nor other than —[/u]

is not, properly speaking, a rhema, since

[ins]Paul[/ins] [del]John[/del] is neither the same as nor other than Barnabas

conveys no idea, and consequently is not a sign, and a fortiori
not a proposition. But it is a [del]somewhat[/del] formal rhema. In like
like manner

[u]— either is or is not —[/u]

is a formal rhema, only. This one is merely empty of all
signification; the other signifies [del]too much,[/del] [ins]more than[/ins] and is absurd.
The negative of an absurd rhema is empty, and [del]vice versa.[/del]
A [del]monadic[/del] proper rhema, that is [del]one[/del] a rhema that is neither absurd
nor empty, may be said to confer logically possible being
upon an indefinite individual that might fill its blank.
