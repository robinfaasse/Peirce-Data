[u]III[/u]. A quantity, written on a sheet of assertion, may, [del]without loss of truth,[/del] be [del]trans[/del] formed by the insertion of two obeli over it.

Demonstration. Let [u]x[/u] represent what is written. Being written upon the sheet of assertions, it is an assertion. It, therefore, [del]affirms that something is true[/del] [ins]virtually asserts[/ins], [del]or at least consists in the affirmation that some proposition[/del] [ins]virtually at least, a proposition[/ins] [del]represented by the algebra[/del] is true, that is, has the value [u]v[/u]. Then, since every such proposition is [u]v[/u] or [u]f[/u], but not both, it virtually implies that [u]x[/u] [del]is false[/del] [ins]has the value [u]f[/u][/ins]. But [u]x̄[/u] merely asserts that [u]x[/u] has the value [u]f[/u]. Hence [u]x̄[/u] asserts no more than is virtually implied in [u]x[/u], and there will be no loss of truth in inserting the two obeli.
