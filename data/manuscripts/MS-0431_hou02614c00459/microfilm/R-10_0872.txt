A function is an unknown whose value depends upon that of another unknown or unknowns, called the argument or arguments. The relation of a function to its argument or arguments is called the operation.

In considering the possible relations between two [del]quantities[/del] [ins]objects[/ins], $x$ and $y$, there will be the following [del]four[/del] [ins]definite[/ins] cases

$$\begin{matrix}
x = v & y = v \\
x = v & y = f \\
x = f & y = v \\
x = f & y = f
\end{matrix}$$

That none of these [del]alternatives[/del] should be possible is absurd. If only one is possible, both [del]quantities[/del] [ins]objects[/ins] are known. If only two are possible, in both of which either $x$ or $y$ preserves the same value, that object is known. If all four are possible, the objects are independent, that is, not functions of one another.
