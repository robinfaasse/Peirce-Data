With this lexical preface, we come down to our
dichotomic mathematics, which [del]at first[/del] [ins]it[/ins] shall
treat algebraically. The first thing to be done is to fix
upon a sign to show that any quantity, say [u]x[/u], has the
value [u]v[/u], and upon another to signify that it has the
value [u]f[/u]. The simplest suggestion is that universally
used since man began to keep accounts; namely,
to appropriate a place in which we are to write
whatever is [u]v[/u], say the upper of two lines, and the
lower of which is appropriated to quantities whose
value is [u]f[/u]. That is, we open one account for [u]v[/u], and
another for [u]f[/u]. [del]We shall properly begin the first line
with v itself, for v is the very first thing whose value
is v, and the second line we begin with f[/del]
