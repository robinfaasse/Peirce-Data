One assertion will admit every possible set of values; it is the
empty form of assertion $x \underline{\text{X}} y$. One will violate the hypotheses,
by excluding every set of values; it is the absurd assertion $x \underline{\text{Y}} y$.
Six assertions will admit two sets of values and exclude two. Of
these, four may be called [u]degenerate propositions[/u], they deter-
mine the value of one of the two quantities, and entirely leave
the other entirely indeterminate. They are $x \underline{\text{D}} y$, $x \underline{\text{R}} y$, $x \underline{\text{S}} y$,
$x \underline{\text{A}} y$; which may [ins]perhaps[/ins] be more cursively written $x \underline{\nabla} y$, $x \underline{\nabla} y$, $x \underline{\Delta} y$,
