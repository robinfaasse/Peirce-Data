If $y$, then if $z$, necessarily $y \cdot z$.
Consequently, by the last three propositions,
If $x \cdot \overline{y \cdot z}$, then of $x \cdot \overline{y}$, necessarily $y \cdot \overline{z}$.
But in (27) substituting $\overline{y}$ for $y$ we find
If $x$ [del]then[/del] [ins]is[/ins] if $\overline{y}$, necessarily $x \cdot \overline{y}$;
so that
If $x \cdot \overline{y \cdot z}$, then if $z$, necessarily, if $\overline{y}$, then $y \cdot \overline{z}$
But by in (2) substituting a false proposition for $z$ and $y$ for $x$,
Either $y$ or $\overline{y}$.
Hence,
If $x \cdot \overline{y \cdot z}$, then if $z$, either $y$ or $y \cdot \overline{z}$
