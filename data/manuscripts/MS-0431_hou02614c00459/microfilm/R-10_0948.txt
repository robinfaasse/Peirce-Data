The relation of inclusion signified by [u]⤙[/u] may be defined
in three clauses, as follows:

[pn](49)[/pn] Necessarily, either [u]x[/u] or [u]x[/u] ⤙ [u]y[/u] ;
[pn](50)[/pn] If [u]y[/u], then necessarily [u]x[/u] ⤙ [u]y[/u] ;
[pn](51)[/pn] If [u]x[/u] ⤙ [u]y[/u], then if [u]x[/u], necessarily [u]y[/u].

If we substitute [u]x[/u] for [u]y[/u] in (49) and (50), they become
Necessarily, either [u]x[/u] or [u]x[/u] ⤙ [u]x[/u]
If [u]x[/u], then necessarily [u]x[/u] ⤙ [u]x[/u]

Syllogizing these, we find
[pn](52)[/pn] Necessarily [u]x[/u] ⤙ [u]x[/u],
which is the [u]Formula of Identity[/u].

I will now prove that
[pn](53)[/pn] If [u]x[/u] ⤙ ([u]y[/u] ⤙ [u]z[/u]), then necessarily [u]y[/u] ⤙ ([u]x[/u] ⤙ [u]z[/u]).

[del]Assume that [u]x[/u] ⤙ ([u]y[/u] ⤙ [u]z[/u]).[/del]

Now in (51), substitute [u]y[/u] ⤙ [u]z[/u] for [u]y[/u]. Then we have,
If [u]x[/u] ⤙ ([u]y[/u] ⤙ [u]z[/u]), then [ins]if [u]x[/u],[/ins] necessarily [u]y[/u] ⤙ [u]z[/u].
