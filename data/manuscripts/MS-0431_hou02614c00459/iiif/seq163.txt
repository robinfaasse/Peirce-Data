sequence. It is sufficient that either the antecedent is false
or the consequent true. But the "would have to" imports
that the proposition in which it occurs would hold good
whatever [del]expressions[/del] [ins]quantities[/ins] were substituted for [u]x[/u]
and [u]y[/u]. The demonstrations are corollarial. If from [u]x[/u]

true that either [u]x[/u] is not [u]v[/u] or [u]y[/u] is [u]v[/u], [del]it[/del] [ins]then[/ins] it follows, by (1),
that either [u]x[/u] is not [u]v[/u] or [u]y[/u] is not [u]v[/u], and by (2), that
either [u]x[/u] is [u]v[/u] or [u]y[/u] is not [u]v[/u], which is what is meant by
saying that from y being [u]v[/u] it would follow that [u]x[/u] were [u]v[/u].
