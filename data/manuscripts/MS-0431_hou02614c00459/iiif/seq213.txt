[del]Either[/del] Necessarily either [u]x[/u] or [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u].
Hence by (51)
If [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]y[/u], then necessarily either [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u] or [u]y[/u].
By this with (55) we have (54).
[del]I[/del] will now prove that
(56) If [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]y[/u], then necessarily ([u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u]) $\prec$ ([u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u]), which
is the Rule of Contraposition.
By (54), if [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]y[/u], then necessarily from [u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u] would
follow [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u]. But in (50) substitute [u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u] for [u]x[/u] and
[u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u] for [u]y[/u]. Then we have
If [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u], then necessarily ([u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u]) $\prec$ ([u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u]).
From this and the last,
If [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]y[/u], then necessarily if [u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u] we have ([u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u]) $\prec$ ([u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u])
In (49), substitute [u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u] for [u]x[/u] and [u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u] for [u]y[/u]. Then we have
Necessarily either [u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u] or ([u]y[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u]) $\prec$ ([u]x[/u] $\prec$ [u]z[/u]).
Syllogizing the two last, we have (56).
