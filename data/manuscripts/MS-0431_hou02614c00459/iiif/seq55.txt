A [u]function[/u] is an unknown whose value depends upon that of another unknown or unknowns, called the [u]argument[/u] or [u]arguments[/u]. The relation of a function to its argument or arguments is called the [u]operation[/u].

In considering the possible relations between two [del]quantities[/del] [ins]objects[/ins], x and y, there will be the following [ins]four[/ins] definite cases

x = v  y = v
x = v  y = f
x = f  y = v
x = f  y = f

[ins]pairs of values[/ins] That none of [del]these[/del] [ins]alternatives[/ins] should be possible is absurd. If only one is possible, both [del]quantities[/del] [ins]objects[/ins] are known. If only two are possible, in both of which either x or y preserves the same value, that object is known. If all four are possible, the objects are independent, that is, not functions of one another.
