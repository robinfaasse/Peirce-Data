A rhema is described as monadic, dyadic, polyadic, etc.
according as the number of its blanks is one, two, more than
two, etc. A [u]medadic[/u] rhema (from μηδέν) is a rhema
with no blank, consequently, a proposition. Such an ex-
pression as
--- is neither the same as nor other than ---
is not, properly speaking, a rhema, since
[del]John[/del] [ins]Paul[/ins] is neither the same as nor other than Barnabas
conveys no idea, and consequently is not a sign, and [u]a fortiori[/u]
not a proposition. But it is a formal rhema. In [del]like in[/del] [ins]somewhat[/ins]
like manner
--- [del]either[/del] is or is not ---
is a [u]formal[/u] rhema, only. This one is merely empty of all
signification; the other signifies [del]more than[/del] [ins]too much,[/ins] and is [u]absurd[/u].
The negative of an absurd rhema is empty, and [del]conver[/del] [ins]vice versa.[/ins]
A [u]proper[/u] [ins]monadic[/ins] rhema, that is [del]one[/del] a rhema that is neither absurd
nor empty, may be said to confer logically possible being
upon an indefinite individual that might fill its blank.
