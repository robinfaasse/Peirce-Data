and $R$ its radius. Also let $2\omega'$ be the angle of
the required sector and $R'$ its radius. We have
first from the fundamental property of triangles
$R : R' :: \sin(\omega+\omega') : \sin\omega$
and the sector sought $\frac{2 \times 3.1416 \times R'^2 \omega'}{360^\circ}$
In order that this be a maximum $R'^2 \omega'$ need
only become so. But $R' = \frac{R \sin\omega}{\sin(\omega+\omega')}$
Hence $R'^2 \omega' = \frac{R^2 \sin^2\omega \cdot \omega'}{\sin^2(\omega+\omega')}$ and it is only
necessary that $d. \frac{\omega'}{\sin^2(\omega+\omega')} = 0$.
or $\frac{1}{\sin^2(\omega+\omega')} - \frac{2\omega'\cos(\omega+\omega')}{\sin^3(\omega+\omega')} = 0$.
Hence $2\omega' = \tan(\omega+\omega')$.
Cor. If $\omega$ is very small we have $\omega = \omega'$.

<u>Question 11.</u> In a given right-angled Triangle
it is required to determine the greatest inscribed
Parabola, whose abscissa is parallel to the perpen-
dicular of the triangle.

[fig]Right-angled triangle with inscribed parabola[/fig]

Solution.

Let $BD = CP = y''$; $CD = a$; $A'D = y'$;
$AB = a$ $BAC = \omega$, $DP' = x'$.
and $y^2 = 2px$ the equation of the parabola
We have because the curve passes through $C$ and $C$ the
equations. $y'^2 = 2pa\tan\omega$
$y''^2 = 2px'$
The triangle $AB'E$ gives $x' = AB' \tan\omega$
But $AB' = a - y' - y''$.
So that $x' = (a - y' - y'') \tan\omega$
therefore $y''^2 = (a - y' - y'') 2p\tan\omega$
Multiplying by $a$ $a y''^2 = (a - y' - y'') 2ap\tan\omega$
$= (a - y' - y'') y'^2$
hence $y''^2(y'+y'') = a(y'^2 - y''^2)$
Dividing by $y'+y''$ $y''^2 = a(y' - y'')$
$y'' = y' - \frac{y''^2}{a}$
But the area of the parabola $= \frac{2}{3} DPBC + \frac{2}{3} D'P'C + \frac{2}{3} P'C'F - \text{etc}$
$= \frac{2}{3} a y' \tan\omega + \frac{2}{3} y'' (a - y' - y'') \tan\omega + \frac{2}{3} y'' \tan\omega - \frac{2}{3} y''^2 \tan\omega$
$= \frac{2}{3} \tan\omega (a - y'') (y' + y'') + \frac{2}{3} \tan\omega (y''^2 - y''^2)$
So that we have to find when
$(4a - y'' - 3y') (y' + y'') = (4a - 4y' + \frac{y''^2}{a}) (2y' + \frac{y''^2}{a})$ is
a maximum.
that is $4y'^3 - 6ay'^2 - 8a^2y' + 8a^3 = 0$
this is the irreducible case of Cubic Equations.
