7. In the drawing of a lottery, according to the
old system, there remained 6,300 tickets in the
wheel, amongst which were two capital
prizes, and were to be drawn on nine successive
days, 700 tickets each day; what was the probability
of drawing the two prizes on each of the nine days,
and what on drawing them at all on the same
day?

Solution. Let $n$ be the number of days still
to be drawn, and $m$ the number of tickets drawn on
each day. Then,
1. If one had been already drawn, the chance of
drawing the other on any particular day is, of course,
$\frac{1}{n}$.
2. If one had not yet been drawn, the chance that
one would not be drawn on a certain day would
be $\frac{n-1}{n}$, that the other would not would also be
$\frac{n-1}{n}$. So that the chance that both would not
would be $(\frac{n-1}{n})^2$. the chance that one of the two
would be drawn is then $1 - (\frac{n-1}{n})^2 = \frac{n^2 - (n-1)^2}{n^2} = \frac{2n-1}{n^2}$.
3. The chance that one would be drawn on a certain
day is $\frac{2}{n}$. the chance that the other would also is
$\frac{m-1}{mn-1}$ or the number of tickets to be drawn on that
day divided by the whole number, excluding in both
cases the ticket already drawn. The chance, then, that
both will be drawn on a given day is $\frac{2}{n} \cdot \frac{m-1}{mn-1} = \frac{2(m-1)}{n(mn-1)}$.
4. the chance of both being drawn at all on the
same day, is the product of the number of days by the chance
of their being drawn on the a given day or $n \cdot \frac{2(m-1)}{n(mn-1)} = \frac{2(m-1)}{mn-1}$.
