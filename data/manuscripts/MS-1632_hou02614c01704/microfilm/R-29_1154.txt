Question 17. Suppose a semicubical and a common
parabola to have the same abscissa and semi-
ordinate; it is required to find the difference of
their areas; the length of the arc of the semi-
cubical being given, and that of the common
a maximum.

Solution. Let $y^3 = a x^2$ be the
semicubical. By differentiation $3 y^2 dy = 2 a x dx$
Squaring $9 y^4 dy^2 = 4 a^2 x^2 dx^2 = 4 a y^3 dx^2$
$dx^2 = \frac{9 y}{4 a} dy^2$
$ds = \sqrt{(dx^2 + dy^2)} = \sqrt{(1 + \frac{9 y}{4 a})} dy$
$s = \frac{8 a}{27} (1 + \frac{9 y}{4 a})^{\frac{3}{2}} + const.$
As $s$ and $y$ vanish together, $const = - \frac{8 a}{27}$
so that $s = \frac{8 a}{27} (1 + \frac{9 y}{4 a})^{\frac{3}{2}} - \frac{8 a}{27}$.
$= \frac{8 a}{27} (\frac{27 y}{4 a} + \frac{243 y^2}{16 a^2} + \frac{729 y^3}{64 a^3}) - \frac{8 a}{27}$
$(\frac{27 s}{8 a} + 1)^2 = \frac{729 s^2}{64 a^2} + \frac{27 s}{4 a} + 1 = 1 + \frac{27 y}{4 a} + \frac{243 y^2}{16 a^2} + \frac{729 y^3}{64 a^3}$
Dividing by $\frac{27}{4 a}$ $\frac{27 s^2}{16 a} + s = y + \frac{9 y^2}{4 a} + \frac{27 y^3}{16 a^2}$
Substituting for $a$ its value $\frac{y^3}{x^2}$ and we have
$\frac{27 x^2 s^2}{16 y^3} + s = y + \frac{9 x^2}{4 y} + \frac{27 x^4}{16 y^3}$
Transposing and multiplying by $\frac{16 y^3}{27}$
$x^4 + (\frac{4 y^2}{3} - s^2) x^2 = \frac{16 s}{27} y^3 - \frac{16 y^4}{27}$

222.
Given $\left\{ \begin{aligned} x - y + \sqrt{(\frac{x - y}{x + y})} &= \frac{20}{x + y} \\ x^2 + y^2 &= 34 \end{aligned} \right\}$ to determine
the values of $x$
and $y$ by a quadratic.

Solution. The first equation, multiplied by $x + y$
becomes. $(x^2 - y^2) + \sqrt{(x^2 - y^2)} = 20$.
Hence, $\sqrt{(x^2 - y^2)} = 4$, $x^2 - y^2 = 16$.
To and from $x^2 + y^2$ add and subtract $x^2 - y^2$.
$2 x^2 = 50$. $x = 5$
$2 y^2 = 18$. $y = 3$.
