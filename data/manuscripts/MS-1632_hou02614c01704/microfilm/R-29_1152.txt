16. Through a given point to draw a straight line cutting an ellipse, given in magnitude and position, so that the intercepted part may be of a given length.

Solution. Let $A$ and $B$ be the axes of the ellipse, $x$ and $y$ the coordinates of the given point referred to these axes, $\rho$ the radius vector of the ellipse referred to the given point, and $\varphi$ the angle which $\rho$ makes with $A$. The polar equation of the ellipse referred to the point given is.
$\left. \begin{aligned} A^2 \rho^2 \sin^2 \varphi + 2 A^2 y \rho \sin \varphi + A^2 y^2 \\ + B^2 \rho^2 \cos^2 \varphi + 2 B^2 x \rho \cos \varphi + B^2 x^2 \end{aligned} \right\} = A^2 B^2$

To find the lengths of $\rho$ corresponding to any value of $\varphi$, we must solve this as a quadratic and obtain.
$\rho = P \pm \sqrt{R}$
$P$ and $R$ being functions of $\varphi$ and known quantities.
The difference between the two values of $\rho$ or the intercepted distance is $2 \sqrt{R}$. So that we have
[del]$2 \sqrt{R} = 2d$ the given dist[/del]
$R = d^2$
But $R = \frac{- A^2 B^2 + A^2 y^2 + B^2 x^2}{A^2 \sin^2 \varphi + B^2 \cos^2 \varphi} + \left( \frac{A^2 y \sin \varphi + B^2 x \cos \varphi}{A^2 \sin^2 \varphi + B^2 \cos^2 \varphi} \right)^2$
