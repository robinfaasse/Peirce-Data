8. Given the base and vertical angle of a
plane triangle, and either the radius of the
inscribed circle, or the ratio of the two lines
drawn from the extremities of the base to the
centre of the inscribed circle, to
construct the triangle.

[fig]Triangle with inscribed circle center and lines to base[/fig]

<u>First Solution</u>. When the radius of the
inscribed circle is given $= r$, the vertical angle $= C$.
and the base $= AB$. On $AB$, describe a segment $ADB$
to contain an angle $= (180^{\circ} + C) = 90^{\circ} + C$. At the distance
$PD = r$ draw $DD'$ parallel to $AB$. Join $AD$, $DB$. Make
$BAC = 2 BAD$ and $ABC = 2 ABD$, $ABC$ is, then, the
required triangle.

For since $ADB = 90^{\circ} + C$. $DAB + DBA = 180^{\circ} - ADB = 90^{\circ} +$
$ACB = 180^{\circ} - (CAB + ABC) = 180^{\circ} - (2 DAB + 2 DBA) = C$ as required.
Moreover. Since $AD$ and $BD$ bisect the angles $CAB$ and $CBA$.
$D$ must be the centre of the inscribed circle and $DP = r$ the
radius as required.

[fig]Triangle with inscribed circle center and lines to base[/fig]

<u>Second Solution</u>. When the ratio of
the two lines drawn from the centre
of the inscribed circle to the
extremities of the radius is
given equal to that of the lines $p$ and $q$, the other denom-
inations as before.

In the indefinite line $GD$ set off $DP = p$. Draw $PE = q$
and making the $APE = 90^{\circ} + C$. Draw $ACB = $ the Base.
and $BD$ parallel to $PE$. make $BAC = 2 BAD$ and $ABC = 2 ABD$.

It may be shown as before that $C$ is the required angle
and $D$ the centre of the inscribed circle. It is evident
also that $AD : DB = AP : PE = p : q$.

9. Given two integral cubes to determine two
other integral cubes having the same difference.

Solution. Let the cube roots of cubes sought
be.
$ma + nb$
$m'a + n'b$
$m''a + n''b$
$m'''a + n'''b$

and let $m$ $m'$ $m''$ $m'''$ be such that
$m^{3} - m'^{3} = m''^{3} - m'''^{3}$

Let also $n'''$ be determined by the equation.
$n''' = \frac{m'^{2}n' + m''^{2}n'' - m^{2}n}{[del]m'''^{2}[/del]}$

It is first to be shown that this supposition is
perfectly and general, and that such values can be
given to the five indeterminates $a, b, n, n', n''$
that the above cube roots may assume any values
whatever as $p, p', p'', p'''$
