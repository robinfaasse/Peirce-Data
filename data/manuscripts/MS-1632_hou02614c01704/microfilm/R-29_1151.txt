14. It is required to find three numbers
such that their sum shall make a square,
and the sum of their squares a cube.

Solution. Let $x + y + z = s$
and $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = s'$
and let the numbers sought be $x s^p s'^{p'}$, $y s^p s'^{p'}$, and $z s^p s'^{p'}$.
Their sum is $(x+y+z) s^p s'^{p'} = s^{p+1} s'^{p'}$,
and the sum of their squares is $(x^2+y^2+z^2) s^{2p} s'^{2p'} = s^{2p} s'^{2p'+1}$.
As the first of these is to be a square,
$p$ must be an odd number
and $p'$ an even one.
As the second is to be a cube,
$p$ must be divisible by $3$
as well as $2p'+1$.
$p$ must be of the form $3(n+1)$
and $p'$ of the form [del]$2(3n'-1)$[/del] [ins]what is the same[/ins] $2(3n'+2)$.
The quantities are then $x s^{3(n+1)} s'^{2(3n'+2)}$, $y s^{3(n+1)} s'^{2(3n'+2)}$ &c.
[marginalia]
$\frac{4p'+2}{3}$
[/marginalia]
Making $x=1$, $y=2$, $z=3$ gives and $m=0$ $n=0$.
Scholium. the same method may be easily extended
to more numbers in which the sum of the $n\text{th}$ powers
is to make an $m\text{th}$ power &c.
