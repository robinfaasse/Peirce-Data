[u]Chapter IV. Addition.[/u]

Suppose we have counted several heaps of beans, separately; and find that they are, for instance, 35, 127, 491, etc. If now we ask what will be the result of counting all together as one lot, we ask a question in addition. In the same way, if we have weighed several portions of flour, and ask what they would weigh if put into the scales together, this is a question in addition. In point of fact, if the flour has been weighed to milligrammes, the weight is a number of milligrammes; and in like manner, any [del]measure[/del] [ins]measure[/ins] is a number.

It will make no difference, what kinds of things the units are. There can be no difference, for we might have counted the stars and beans together by double counting.
