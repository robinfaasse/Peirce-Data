[u]Bath[/u]

Hebrew liquid measure = 72 logs = 6 hins = [del]1/10[/del] [ins]1/10[/ins] cor.
It corresponds to the dry measure the epha = 72 logs
= 18 cabs = 10 gomers = 3 seahs = 1/10 cor.

A. There seems to have been a bath of about 36 litres.
For,
1st Josephus says the hin was equal to two Attic [u]χόες[/u].
Auries makes the [u]χους[/u] 3.03 litres. The measures found
at Naxos make it 2.96 litres. Call it 3 litres. It follows
that the bath was 36 litres.

2nd Josephus says the Hebrew cor contains 10 Attic
medimni. Now the medimnus is 16 [u]χόες[/u] or 48 litres.
If we suppose he meant metretae, [del]the bath would[/del] [ins]this statement[/ins]
agree with the other.

3rd. Solomon's molten sea is said by Josephus to have
been strictly hemispherical, and according to 1 Kings vii
it was 10 cubits in diameter and contained 2000 baths.
Taking the sacred cubit at 0.525 metres (according to
the Egyptian cubits which are preserved) the total contents
of the sphere would be
4/3 π R³ = 4/3 x 3 1/7 x [del]0.145[/del] [ins]18.09[/ins] = 75.8 metre cubes
and 1/2000 part of this would be 37.9 litres to the bath.

4th. Josephus also says the gomer was 7 Attic [u]κοτύλαι[/u].
This would make the bath only 17.5 litres, which doubled
would be 35 litres.

5th. The sath was a heaped measure, so that all state-
ments about it are approximate and doubtful. Various
writers, Josephus, Epiphanius, etc say that it was 1 1/2
modius or 24 sextarii. This would make the epha
(and presumably the bath) 72 sextarii. The Farnese
congius of 6 sextarii contains according to Huse
3.387 litres. Hence the sath would be 13.547 litres.
But according to Epiphanius the heaping increases
it by 1/4 part. This would be 2.71 litres of heaping
a very large allowance. Making this allowance and
multiplying, the epha would be 32 1/2 litres. With
a little smaller allowance for heaping it would be
36.
