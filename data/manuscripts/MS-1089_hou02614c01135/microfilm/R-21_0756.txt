[u]Determinations of the Lengths of Decimetre
Scales Nos. 3, 4, and 5.[/u]

Starting with Metre No 49, we have the comparisons of this
with the Mètre des Archives, and the Discussion by Förster.
The coefficient of expansion has been determined
by me with a high degree of precision. See Gravity or Initial
Stations.

Glass metre scale No 1, was compared on the Blair com-
parator with No. 49. Namely, [date]1884 Dec 20-22[/date] ten times
at temperatures about 8°.5 C, and [date]1884 Aug 1-27[/date] ten times
at temperatures about 23°.5 C, and by way of check twice [date]1884
Dec 29[/date] at about 29°.5. The intervals between the different lines
of this Glass metre scale No 1 were measured by Micro-
meter A of Blair comparator [date]1884 Dec 30[/date]. Besides this,
Glass metre scale No 1 was compared with Metre [del]No[/del] 49
on the Saxton step comparator [date]1881 June 20-27[/date], at temps.
from 73° F to 76° F. [del]([/del]

Glass decimetre No 3 was compared [ins]on the Blair com-[/ins]
[del]parator with all the decimetres of glass metre scale[/del]
No 1, [date]1884 Oct 21 - Dec 9[/date], six times at temperatures ranging
from 12°.5 C to 20° C. Besides this, Glass decimetre No 3 was
compared on my micrometric comparator [date]1879 May and June[/date]
with [del]the[/del] all the decimetres of glass metre scale No 1 at tem-
peratures from 68° to 75°. But as my comparator was not
adapted to supporting so long a bar, I attribute little weight
to these comparisons. Further Decimetre No 3 was compared
[date]1878 May 28[/date] with Schott's brass Decimetre at 54°.7 C. For
the coefficient of expansion see below.

Glass decimetre No 4 was compared with extraordi-
nary precision on my micrometric comparator with
glass decimetre No 3, first [del]1879[/del] [ins][date]1879[/date][/ins] July 14-31 at tempera-
tures from 70° F to 84° F, and then [date]1880 Jan 30-Feb 9[/date] at tempe-
ratures from 40° F to 49° F. The relative coefficient of expan-
sion between the two is also thus determined.

Glass decimetre No 5 was compared on the Blair compa-
rator with all the decimetres of glass metre scale No 1, [date]1884
Sep 12 - Oct 9[/date] at about 22° C, six times. Coefficient of
expansion unknown.
