§ 2. The word "science" has three principal acceptions.

Men who have been educated in Jesuit colleges often use the word in the sense of the Greek [del]ἐπιστήμη[/del] [ins]ἐπιστήμη[/ins], the Latin scientia.

One can learn exactly what this meaning is by going through the articles in the [u]Index Aristotelicus[/u] of Bonitz. Briefly, it is certainty, or certain knowledge.

Near the beginning of the XIXth century, Coleridge, in his introductory dissertation to the Encyclopaedia Metropolitana, brought into vogue another meaning of science, as organized knowledge.
