A mark of the second order determines the subject in two
ways, viz: 1[ins]st[/ins] to relate to a certain object and 2[ins]nd[/ins] to relate to it
in a certain respect. Hence it [del]is separable[/del] consists of two marks
of the [del]first[/del] [ins]the subject[/ins] order which are essential to each other, viz: 1[ins]st[/ins], a
mark which it has in common with the correlate & 2[ins]nd[/ins] the distinction
which the subject [del]has from the correlate[/del]. For example; "on [del]my[/del] [ins]the[/ins] right hand"
consists 1[ins]st[/ins] of [ins]relatively[/ins] being [del]next to me[/del] 2[ins]nd[/ins] of being at the right hand.

A mark of the third order implies two things beside the subject;
it therefore 1[ins]st[/ins] determines the subject to stand in a certain relation to
the second thing 2[ins]nd[/ins] to stand in a certain relation to the second thing 3[ins]rd[/ins] to stand
in such relations to the two that these relations shall involve each other, for
otherwise we should have merely two marks of the second order & not one
of a different order. Hence, a mark of the third order consists of [del]three[/del]
mutually involved marks of the 2[ins]nd[/ins] order[del], namely[/del]. Each of these
marks of the second order must consist of two marks of
the 1[ins]st[/ins] order. Accordingly the mark of the third order consists
