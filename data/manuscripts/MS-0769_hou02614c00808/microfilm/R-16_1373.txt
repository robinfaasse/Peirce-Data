§ 1 Definition of Logic

Against all previous definitions of logic (except, perhaps, that given by Spalding in 1857 that it is the "theory of [del]thought[/del] [ins]reasoning[/ins]"), I shall urge a very sweeping and strange objection. All these definitions regard logic as referring exclusively to thought or cognition. Yet thoughts and cognitions are events which happen in a particular subject at particular times; whereas [del]the[/del] relevance has no such limits and remains whether it is thought or not. Two replies will be made to this objection; first, that by thought is meant thought in general and that in which the [del]limi-[/del] tations of the particular are eliminated; and second that logical relations only exist by being thought and that therefore it is not true that they remain whether thought or not.
