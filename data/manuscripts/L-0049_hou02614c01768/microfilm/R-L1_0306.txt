that he has acted and
intends to act in your

and I think you will make
a grave mistake if you
assume a hostile position
toward him.

In your original letter
you speak of him as
"a man of the highest
standing"; and I find
your judgment is confirmed
by my legal friends in
Boston one of whom
remarked that he should
class Stickney with Choate
and Carter if he were
speaking of the New York
Bar.

It is only fair
for me to add that my
disinclination to involve
myself in your affairs
is based much more upon
a consideration of your
own statements than it is
upon the contents of Mr.
Stickney's letter.

Your affectionate cousin
Francis Blake
[date]15 January, 1892[/date]
