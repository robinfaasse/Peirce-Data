5 W. 50th St. New York, [date]1896 Apr. 27[/date].

My dear Frank:

I have invented, and applied for a patent, for a piece of a machine absolutely indispensable to a certain machine which is just being patented, and which I believe is destined to be used (it will not be sold) by the million. It seems to be on a good business basis; but there is another company with very large capital already existing which is likely to take an interest, one way or the other, in the new machine. In my opinion, it is demonstrable that this new machine cannot get along without my invention; and as far as I have been able to collect information, the company owning the new machine is strong and confident of vast profits. So that if the other company wished to fight them, there would be no way so good or cheap as through my invention. But I should prefer a royalty of $1 or so from the others.

These two companies are thus the only parties with whom I can think of dealing. I feel that the first thing for me to do is to sell 49 per cent of my patent to a strong, honest, and interested business man, who will undertake, or quietly direct, the negotiation. He must pay me at least $5000 cash, and something more contingently. If you, or anybody, can find me such a man, you would have to get your bonus out of the 49 per cent and the contingent payment.

Now, if you would like to hear more about it, I will tell you all you want to know. I believe my patent, if it passes, ought to be worth a million, more or less. It has nothing to do with telephones nor telegraphs.

Very faithfully
C. S. Peirce
