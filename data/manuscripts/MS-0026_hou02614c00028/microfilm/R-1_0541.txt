$4^{\underline{\text{th}}}$, If an expression in the dyadic algebra shows one relative written
after another with a [del]long[/del] [ins]long[/ins]-tailed cross between them, as $l \dagger b$, this is
termed a [u]relative sum[/u], the operation being termed relative addition, the pre-
ceding relative the [u]adder[/u], and the succeeding the [u]addend[/u]; and in order
to translate the expression into the general algebra, it is necessary to write
the same index as correlate of the adder and as relate of the addend, the
quantifier of this index being $\Pi$ and being written in the Peircean after the
quantifiers of the relate of the multiplier and of the correlate of the multipli-
cand, but not after any other $\Sigma$ except those which are entitled to precedence
by the last rule, and a sign of aggregation is to be substituted for the long-
tailed cross, which is the sign of relative addition. For example, $l \dagger b$ will
mean $\Pi_{x} \Pi_{z} \Pi_{y} l_{xy} + b_{yz}$. So, $l b (l \dagger b)$ will mean
$\Pi_{x} \Pi_{y} \Sigma_{u} \Pi_{v} l_{xu} \cdot b_{uy} \cdot (l_{xv} + b_{vy})$
