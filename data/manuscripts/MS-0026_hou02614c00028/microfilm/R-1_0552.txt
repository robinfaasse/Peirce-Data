The sign of equality, [u]=[/u], is written between two propositions in order to assert that they are alike under the circumstances supposed in being both true or both false. That is [u]x=y[/u] means [u]x⤙y[/u] and [u]y⤙x[/u]. If the circumstances are not precisely defined, the meaning is that under all admissible circumstances the [del]two[/del] propositions are alike; that is, while they may be now true and now false, they are always true or false together. We do not, in logic, recognize any difference between propositions which under all possible circumstances agree in respect to truth or falsehood.

The terms [u]x[/u] [ins]and [u]y[/u] are called the aggregants and [u]x✛y[/u] the aggregate.[/ins]
