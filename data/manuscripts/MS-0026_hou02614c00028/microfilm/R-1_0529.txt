<u>Theorem 7</u>. Taking any collection whatever, there is a relation which
every unit of that collection bears to everything, and which nothing else bears
to anything. That is,
(14) $\Pi_{\alpha}\Sigma_{\beta}\Pi_{\underline{x}}\Pi_{\underline{y}} (\bar{q}_{\alpha\underline{x}} + r_{\beta\underline{x}\underline{y}}) \cdot (q_{\alpha\underline{x}} + \bar{r}_{\beta\underline{x}\underline{y}})$.

This is a consequence of the first order in (2), (3), and (8); so that (2) $\cdot$ (3) $\cdot$ (8) $\prec$ (14).
For multiplying (8) by (5) in which for $\alpha$ and $\underline{x}$ we substitute $\delta$ and $\underline{z}$, we get
$\Sigma_{\delta}\Pi_{\alpha}\Pi_{\beta}\Sigma_{\gamma}\Pi_{\underline{x}}\Pi_{\underline{y}}\Pi_{\underline{z}} q_{\delta\underline{z}} \cdot (\bar{q}_{\alpha\underline{x}} + \bar{q}_{\beta\underline{y}} + r_{\gamma\underline{x}\underline{y}}) \cdot (q_{\alpha\underline{z}} + \bar{r}_{\gamma\underline{z}\underline{y}}) \cdot (q_{\beta\underline{y}} + \bar{r}_{\gamma\underline{z}\underline{y}})$.
Identifying $\beta$ with $\delta$ and $\underline{z}$ with $\underline{y}$, we get this becomes
$\Sigma_{\delta}\Pi_{\alpha}\Sigma_{\gamma}\Pi_{\underline{x}}\Pi_{\underline{y}} q_{\delta\underline{y}} \cdot (\bar{q}_{\alpha\underline{x}} + r_{\gamma\underline{x}\underline{y}}) \cdot (q_{\alpha\underline{y}} + \bar{r}_{\gamma\underline{x}\underline{y}})$
Omitting the first factor, dropping $\delta$, which then no longer appears in the Boolean,
and substituting $\beta$ for $\gamma$, we get (14). Q.E.D.
