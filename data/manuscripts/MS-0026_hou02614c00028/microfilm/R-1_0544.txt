[u]Art. 7[/u]. I now return to the proper subject of this paper. There are
three axioms relating to collections.

The first axiom relates to a single collection. It is as follows:

[u]Axiom 1[/u]. Every possible object belongs to a collection from which all
other objects are excluded.

If we write [math]q_{\alpha b}[/math] to mean "[math]b[/math] is a unit of the collection [math]\alpha[/math]," then this axiom
is expressed in the general algebra as follows:
(42) [math]\Pi_x \Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_y \ q_{\alpha x} \cdot (1_{xy} \dagger \bar{q}_{\alpha y})[/math],
and in the dyadic algebra as follows:
(42') [del][math]\breve{q} \bar{\breve{q}} (\bar{q} \bar{\breve{q}}) \dagger[/math][/del] [math]1 \prec \breve{q} (\bar{q} \dagger 1)[/math].

The second axiom relates to a pairing of collections, and is substantially
that every collection has its complementary collection so related to it that
every possible object is included in one of this pair of collections and is
excluded from the other. But it is desirable to modify this expression of
this; because there is a logical inelegancy in [del]in[/del] implicitly asserting the same
thing in two different axioms. Now there [ins]are[/ins] propositions deducible from the
first [del]axiom[/del] which are also deducible from the second, as it has just been
stated. Such, for example is the proposition that everything belongs to
a collection. This evidently follows from (42); for by simply dropping the
second factor of the Boolean, — a proceeding justified by (7), — and then drop-
ping from the Peirceian the quantifier of [math]y[/math], which no longer appears in the
Boolean, we have at once
(43) [math]\Pi_x \Sigma_{\alpha} \ q_{\alpha x}[/math].
