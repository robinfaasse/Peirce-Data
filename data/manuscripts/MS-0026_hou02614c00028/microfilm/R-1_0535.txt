[u]Arithmetical Relations[/u]. A number with indices attached to it not
fewer than that number, means that the objects denoted by those indices
form a collection of that [del]num[/del]ber of different things existing in the
universe of discourse. But the only relations required are the following:

$0$ means [del]a[/del] [ins]the[/ins] [del]proposition[/del] essentially (i.e. by definition) false, of
which one expression is "[del]whatever[/del] [ins]something[/ins] is false is true."

$0_{x}$ [del]means[/del] "$x$ does not exist."

$1_{x}$ means "$x$ is an existing thing" but it will be best to write this $1_{xx}$;

$1_{xy}$ means "$x$ is identical with $y$."

$2_{xy}$ means "$x$ is not $y$"

Instead of $\overline{0}$, the sign $\succ$ Taurus, or any of the signs of the zodiac may
be used. For for certain purposes it is advantageous to have a number
of [del]signs[/del] equivalent signs for $\overline{0}$.

It will be observed that $\overline{0}_{x} = 1_{x}$, $\overline{1}_{xy} = 2_{xy}$, $\overline{2}_{xyz} = 3_{xyz} + 1_{xyz}$, etc.

For all numbers higher than $1$, we have equations like the following:

$3_{abcd} = (3_{abc} + 3_{abd} + 3_{acd} + 3_{bcd}) \cdot (\overline{3}_{abc} + \overline{3}_{abd}) \cdot (\overline{3}_{abc} + \overline{3}_{acd}) \cdot (\overline{3}_{abc} + \overline{3}_{bcd}) \cdot (\overline{3}_{abd} + \overline{3}_{acd}) \cdot$ [del]etc[/del]

But it is not true that $1_{xy} = (1_{x} + 1_{y}) \cdot (\overline{1}_{x} + \overline{1}_{y})$; because $1_{x}$ is absurd, and
nothing involves an absurdity if there is any possible interpretation not absurd;
while for all higher numbers than $1$, there is no relation [ins]implied in the counting[/ins] that does not consist
of relations of pairs.
