the two relations of "being more than" and of "being less than" are relations
of precisely the same form, and consequently do not differ in any intelli-
gible way, but only as two drops of water differ, by their haecceities, or the
brute force of existence. This remark is confirmed by the consideration
of their limits. The maximum collection is that which embraces all
possible units, while of all possible subjects nothing can be predicated
except that of any pair of contradictory predicates one or other is
true, which is the absolute minimum of predication. The minimum
collection is that which excludes all possible units; and [del]of the[/del]
all predicates are true if they are not asserted of any possible subject.
Thus, the absolute limits of collections and of predicates are the same;
namely, the absolute total of collections corresponds to the absence of
all distinguishing predicate (and it is the essence of a predicate to
be distinguishing), while the absolute absence of all collectional content
corresponds to the absolute total of all predicates. [ins]It is true that[/ins] [del]Now between[/del] these
limits are interchanged; the absolute total in either case corresponding
to the absolute zero in the other. But there is no intelligible differ-
ence between an absolute total and an absolute zero. The intelligible
element is the absoluteness. The distinction merely concerns the
brute existence.
