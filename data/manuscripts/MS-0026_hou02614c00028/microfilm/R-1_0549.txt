[u]Art. 4.[/u] The terms which distinguish the different kinds of mathematical propositions will here be [del]under[/del] used in the following senses:

A [u]postulate[/u] of a given branch of mathematics is a proposition embodying a part of the fundamental hypothesis which is adhered to throughout that branch.

An [u]axiom[/u] is a propositional formula embodying either a principle of logic or a principle borrowed from another branch of mathematics without further inquiry.

A [u]corollary[/u] is a proposition which is an immediate consequence of some proposition already admitted, or which can, at least, be deduced from such proposition without the aid of any other proposition of the same branch of mathematics.

A [u]theorem[/u] is a proposition, deducible from two or more propositions of the same branch of mathematics, and which amounts to denying the possibility of some hypothesis.

A [u]problem[/u] is an interrogative proposition, followed by a solution which shows [u]how[/u] a given hypothesis is possible, this being demonstrated by deduction from two or more accepted propositions of the same branch of mathematics.
