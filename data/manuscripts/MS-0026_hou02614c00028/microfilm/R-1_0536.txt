The general method of drawing conclusions by means of this algebra. The process of reasoning consists, first, in [del]multiplying[/del] [ins]compredicating[/ins] the premises together, and secondly, in transforming the [del]compredicate[/del] so resulting. Each premise may be introduced as a compredicant any number of times; and the conclusions will always differ as this number [del]differs[/del]. If a premise is introduced $N$ times, the conclusion is said to be of the $N^{\underline{th}}$ order in that premise. The interest of a conclusion increases with $N$, when $N$ is small, until a maximum interest is reached when it diminishes with the increase of $N$.
