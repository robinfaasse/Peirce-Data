On Multitude

The quantifiers of different aggregants and compredicants are commu-
table; that is,

(21) $\Sigma_a \Pi_b \Pi_u \Sigma_v\ m_{ab} \cdot n_{uv} = $ [del]$\Sigma$[/del] $\Pi_u \Sigma_a \Sigma_v \Pi_b\ m_{ab} \cdot n_{uv}$ etc.

Any universally quantified, or distributed index, that is, any
index attached to a $\Pi$ can be identified with any index that in the
Peircian is to the left of it; that is

(22) $\Sigma_i \Pi_j\ l_{ij} \prec \Sigma_i\ l_{ii}$     $\Pi_i \Sigma_j \Pi_k\ g_{ijk} \prec \Pi_i \Sigma_j\ g_{iji}$, etc.

Any index can be diversified by changing it, in part of its occur-
rences to a new index, which new index must be particularly quantified,
[ins]i.e.[/ins] [del]as[/del] be undistributed, or attached to a $\Sigma$ and be placed in the Peircian
to the right of the old one; that is

(23) $\Pi_i\ l_{ii} \prec \Pi_i \Sigma_j\ l_{ij}$     $\Sigma_i \Pi_j\ g_{iij} \prec \Sigma_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k\ g_{ijk}$, etc.

When the Boolean consists of [del]two[/del] [ins]or more[/ins] compredicants, we may diversify
all the indices [ins]which are[/ins] [del]in[/del] [ins]of different[/ins] compredicants [del]and which[/del] at the same time
follow in the Peircian after a given index, and that without altering
their modes of quantification. But from the point where any index
occurring in [del]two[/del] compredicants and quantified by $\Pi$ is diversified
to the end of the Peircian all the indices must be diversified. Thus,
if $s$ signifies "— slanders — to —" and $p$ signifies "— praises — to —,"

(24) $\Sigma_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k\ s_{ijk} \cdot p_{ijk} \prec \Sigma_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k \Pi_u \Sigma_v\ s_{ijk} \cdot p_{iuv}$

But it is not true that

$\Sigma_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k\ s_{ijk} \cdot p_{ijk} \prec \Sigma_i \Pi_j \Pi_u \Sigma_k\ s_{ijk} \cdot p_{iuk}$
