[u]Theorem 2[/u]. There is a collection which includes all possible objects.
That is
(5) $$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{x} q_{\alpha x}.$$

This is a consequence of the first order from axioms (2) and (3); so that $(2) \cdot (3) \prec$ (5). For the product of the last two factors of (3) is
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{\beta} \Sigma_{\gamma} \Pi_{x} \bar{q}_{\alpha x} \cdot \bar{q}_{\beta x} + q_{\gamma x}.$$

Multiplying this by the first factor of (2), whose indices we accent, we get
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{\alpha'} \Sigma_{\beta'} \Pi_{\beta} \Sigma_{\gamma} \Pi_{x} \Pi_{x'} (q_{\alpha' x'} + q_{\beta' x'}) \cdot (\bar{q}_{\alpha x} \cdot \bar{q}_{\beta x} + q_{\gamma x}).$$

Identifying $\alpha'$ with $\alpha$, $\beta$ with $\beta'$, and $x'$ with $x$, we have by the application of the same general formulae that were used in Theorem 1, and are almost always applied,
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{\beta'} \Sigma_{\gamma} \Pi_{x} q_{\gamma x}$$

Dropping from the quantifying part the indices $\alpha$ and $\beta'$, which do not occur in the Boolean, and changing $\gamma$ to $\alpha$, we get (5). Q.E.D.

[u]Corollary 1[/u]. From (5), by carrying the $\Sigma$ to the right we get immediately
[del](6)[/del] (5 bis) $$\Pi_{x} \Sigma_{\alpha} q_{\alpha x}.$$
That is, every object is [del]con[/del] a unit of some collection.

[u]Scholium 1[/u]. This corollary can also be deduced immediately from (1); so that there is an inelegant pleonasm in the statement of the axioms.
