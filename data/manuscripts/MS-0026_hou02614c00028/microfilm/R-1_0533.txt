[u]On Multipliers[/u]

(25) If two sets of indices occur in the Boolian only in different aggregants or in different compredicants, their quantifiers are commutable in the Peircian, so long as the [ins]order of the[/ins] [del]order of the qu[/del] succession of [ins]the[/ins] different indices of each of these sets is preserved. Thus,

[del](24)[/del] $\Pi_i \Sigma_j \Sigma_k l_{ij} \cdot b_{kj} = \Sigma_k \Pi_i \Sigma_j l_{ij} \cdot b_{kj}$ , etc.
$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k l_{ij} + b_{ik} = \Pi_i \Sigma_k \Pi_j l_{ij} + b_{ik}$ , etc.

(26) Any index [del]that is[/del] distributed, or universally quantified, i.e. is attached to a $\Pi$ can be identified with (that is [ins]omitted from the Peircian and[/ins] changed throughout [ins]the Boolian[/ins] so as to become identical with) any index which in the Peircian is to the left of it. Thus,

[del](25)[/del] $\Sigma_i \Pi_j l_{ij} \prec \Sigma_i l_{ii}$ $\Pi_i \Sigma_j \Pi_k g_{ijk} \prec \Pi_i \Sigma_j g_{iji}$ , etc.

(27) It follows, by contraposition, that any index can be diversified, that is, changed in any part of its occurrences in the Boolian to a new index, which must be undistributed, or particularly quantified, i.e. must be attached to a $\Sigma$, and its quantifier [del]must be placed somewhere and[/del] can be placed anywhere to the right of that of the old index in the Peircian, but must not be placed to the left of it.

(28) When the Boolian consists of two compredicants, we may diversify all the indices whose quantifiers are in the Peircian to the right of any one point, [del]and this without[/del] giving to each new index the same mode of quantification (by $\Pi$ or by $\Sigma$) that the old one that gave rise to it had, and [del]of so[/del] arranging the new [ins]indices with[/ins] [del]quantifiers[/del] their quantifiers in the same order in the Peircian as those which gave rise to them, provided that the new indices replace [del]each[/del] the one that gives rise to it throughout one compredicant of the Boolian but nowhere in the other.
