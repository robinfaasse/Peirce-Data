The sign [ins]$\Psi$[/ins], which is a cursive modification of Jevons's $\cdot|\cdot$, is [del]called the sign of aggregation and is[/del] so used that, letting $x$ be the proposition written before it, and $y$ that written after it, $x \mathbin{\Psi} y$ [del]asserts[/del] is that proposition which is [del]necessarily[/del] [ins]true by logical necessity[/ins] true if $x$ is true, and also if $y$ is true, but is false if it be not so necessitated to be true.

The sign [ins]$\cdot$[/ins] [ins]heavy[/ins], [del]a[/del] dot above the line, being a form of the algebraic sign of multiplication is so used [del]that[/del] letting $x$ be whatever proposition is written before it, and $y$ whatever proposition is written after it, $x \cdot y$ is that proposition which is true by logical necessity if $x$ and $y$ are true, and is false if not so necessitated to be true. This completes the description of the notation of the Boolian.
