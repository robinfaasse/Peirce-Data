• Religion must be subject to good sense. It is always
in danger of being carried to excess. "Religious excesses"
is the very just phrase of men of the world.
Morality cannot be carried to excess. Logic can-
not be carried to excess; and it is not subject to
good sense, but on the contrary gives good sense
its law. But religion if not taken in moderation
leads to insanity & that not as is sometimes said, because
it is [del]perverted[/del] [ins]adulterated[/ins], but because of the element of it that
is most essential, — the mystical element. It not
only leads to insanity, but also directly to impurity.
It always has from the earliest phallic worship to
modern camp-meetings

¶ I say that mystery is the most essential part
of religion. A religion may exist without miracles,
that is, I do not see why it might not, though per-
haps none ever has. But a religion without
mystery, a religion without anything different
from ordinary nature, — like Confucianism, —
ceases to be religion. I don't say religion could
exist without a God, but [del]it[/del] certainly a theosophic
doctrine does not in itself constitute a religion
