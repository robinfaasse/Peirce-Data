Now, it is obvious, that whether I conclude, as in the first figure,
from one things being in or out of another which is itself in
or out of a third, that the first is in or out of a third; or, as
in the [del]second[/del] [ins]third[/ins] figure, from the same things being in or out of
two things that one of these is in or out of the other; or as
in the [del]third[/del] [ins]2nd[/ins] figure from two things being in or out of a third
that one is in or out of the other; [del]the[/del] either case, the only [del]bound[/del]
determining boundary established between the subject and predi-
cate of the conclusion is the boundary of the conception of
the middle term. Therefore, to adhere to the illustration,
1[sup]st[/sup] [fig]three small circles side by side[/fig] if the circle of the middle term is removed
from the other terms so that they are out of it and it is
out of them, it is impossible to say whether they are in or
out of each other. In other words, [u]no conclusion can be
drawn from negative premises[/u].
2[sup]nd[/sup] If it is desired in the conclusion to establish a boundary
between the major & minor terms, since that boundary must be
the circumference of the middle term one of them must
be outside that circumference and the other inside; that
is to say [u]from two affirmative premises, an affirmative
conclusion must be drawn[/u].
