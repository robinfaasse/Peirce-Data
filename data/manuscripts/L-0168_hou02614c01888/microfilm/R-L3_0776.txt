President D. C. Gilman
Johns Hopkins University

New York [date]1896 May 21[/date].

My Dear Sir:

I write you on a personal matter small to you but not so to me. I cannot believe you really intend to leave the Johns Hopkins to come to New York; for to my mind to send out one such class as I used to have there is a greater thing than to fit out all New York with a good education & good principles, — though that is certainly great, too. Besides, the Johns Hopkins is your creation; and surely you would not abandon it, unless it were either hopeless to carry out your idea, or it were so perfectly carried out that it would run as well without you. Still, as the Newspapers talk as if you might come, I bethink me that if you did so, I might have a difficulty afterwards in regaining possession of the books I left there. I haven't just now the money to pay for them. My too ardent devotion to the study has prevented me from having it. I have a picture by Albert Bierstadt, about 2 1/2 x 3 1/2 feet is the canvas, — the sort of thing he gets $2500 for. I will try to sell it for $1500 or borrow $1250 on it. Then, out of that money, if you will aid me, I shall be able to recover the books. I do not suppose there is anybody in Baltimore who would want the picture. Bierstadt's works sell mostly abroad. They are too American for Americans. But it is not impossible that I may sell it here, where there are so many foreigners, a few of whom understand pictures. Supposing I got the money in a short time, I wonder what the University would ask me for my books. I got $500 for them; and of course should expect to pay more to get them back. yours very truly C. S. Peirce
5 W 50th St. New York City
