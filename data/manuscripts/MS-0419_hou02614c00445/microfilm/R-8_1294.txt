<u>Chapter XIII. Simplification for Dual Relatives.</u>

193. The whole apparatus of $\Sigma$, $\Pi$, and indices may be dispensed with in all ordinary cases, at least, by means of a device published by me in 1883*. This depends upon taking advantage of the fact that in a formula one letter follows after another in a line. It is assumed that the [del]last[/del] [ins]first[/ins] index of a letter is the same as the last of the one that went before it, except where certain symbols are introduced, in which case the first index of a letter is the same as the first of the [del]group of letters[/del] [ins]letter or group[/ins] under parenthesis before it. This convention dispenses with indices of dual relatives, but all other terms can be reduced to dual relatives. Non-relative terms are easily conceived as relatives by adding "that is coëxistent with" [del]to each[/del]; as

$w$ coexistent with
$m$ man coexistent with, etc.

[del]Plural[/del] [ins]Triple[/ins] relatives are decomposed into dual relatives [del]thus[/del] [ins]as follows[/ins]:
Like $A$ gives $B$ to $C$. Call this individual act of giving $D$. Then the triples relation is compounded of these three dual relations, $A$ is the agent of $D$, $B$ is the direct object to $D$,
