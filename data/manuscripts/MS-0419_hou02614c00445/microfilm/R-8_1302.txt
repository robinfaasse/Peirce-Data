[u]5th. Species[/u]. Premises: $\breve{p} \dagger \bar{h} \dagger \mu$ and $\breve{\mu} \dagger \breve{h} \dagger \bar{s}$.
Multiplying relatively
$(\breve{p} \dagger \bar{h} \dagger \mu) (\breve{\mu} \dagger \breve{h} \dagger \bar{s})$.
This gives by the All-important
$(\breve{p} \dagger \bar{h} \dagger \mu) \breve{\mu} \dagger \breve{h} \dagger \bar{s}$,
And thence $(\breve{p} \dagger \bar{h} \dagger \mu \breve{\mu}) \breve{h} \dagger \bar{s}$,
or $(\breve{p} \dagger \bar{h}) \breve{h} \dagger \bar{s}$,
or by conversion,
$\breve{s} \dagger h (\breve{h} \dagger \bar{p})$.
This is the general conclusion. But in the present case
we further conclude
$\breve{s} \dagger h \breve{h} \dagger \bar{p}$,
$\breve{s} \dagger \bar{p}$.

The premises will yield another conclusion, thus:
$\breve{p} \dagger \bar{h} \dagger \mu (\breve{\mu} \dagger \breve{h} \dagger \bar{s})$,
$\breve{p} \dagger \bar{h} \dagger T \breve{h} \dagger \bar{s}$.
[del]But this is deducible from the first conclusion.[/del]
[del]that is Every P wants every quality that is the only one[/del]
[del]possessed by any S. Our syllogism was not subtle enough for this.[/del]
