But each conclusion when obtained should be
expressed in the system we have just been considering.

The problem will be this. We have two premises, each with
two indices. We wish to infer a proposition in two indices from
which these premises can be reciprocally deduced; or
if that is not [del]possible[/del] [ins]there is no single one possible[/ins], then we wish to get the best
set of two inferences from the premises from which they
may be recovered.

For example: Given $0 \dagger a \dagger 0$ and $0 \dagger b \dagger 0$, or
$\Pi_i \Pi_j a_{ij} \cdot \Pi_k \Pi_l b_{kl}$.

We infer
$\Pi_i \Pi_j a_{ij} \cdot b_{ij}$.

This is the same as
$(0 \dagger a \dagger 0) \cdot (0 \dagger b \dagger 0) = 0 \dagger ab \dagger 0$

2. Given [del]as[/del] $(a \dagger 0)$ and $0 \dagger b \dagger 0$, or
$\Sigma_i \Pi_j a_{ij} \cdot \Pi_k \Pi_l b_{kl}$.

We easily infer
$\Sigma_i \Pi_j \Pi_k a_{ij} \cdot b_{kj}$

And this may be written
$\infty [a \cdot (0 \dagger \breve{b}) \dagger 0]$.
