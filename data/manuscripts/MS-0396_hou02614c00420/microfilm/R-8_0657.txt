Suppose that we use the form $A:B$ to express an elementary
relative term or one that relates to a single pair of
individuals so that $A$ is the same as $(A:B)\text{ to }B$ Or if we
write this last $(A:B)B$ then we have $A = (A:B)B$. Just as a
general absolute term is indeterminate between the individual
$A\ B\ C\ D\ \dots$ so a general relative term is indeterminate
between some of the elementary relatives of a block like this
$$\begin{matrix}
A:A & A:B & A:C & A:D \\
B:A & B:B & B:C & B:D \\
C:A & C:B & C:C & C:D \\
D:A & D:B & D:C & D:D
\end{matrix}$$

A relation may be combined with an absolute term
in several ways. The $1^{\text{st}}$ of these ways may be called the
simple application and may be defined as follows.
If $a$ & $b$ are absolute terms & $m$ & $n$ relative terms then
$$\begin{matrix}
\text{if } m \prec n \\
\text{and } a \prec b \\
\text{then } ma \prec nb
\end{matrix}$$
