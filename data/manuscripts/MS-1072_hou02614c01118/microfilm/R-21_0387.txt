The probable error of a single division calcu-
lated from these numbers is $\pm 0.^{\mu}0145$. But
this involves the error of observation. Now each
of the above numbers is the mean of 6 point-
ings[ins]*[/ins], and from the discrepancies of the single
pointings it appears that the probable error of
the mean of 6 is $\pm 0.^{\mu}0122$. Hence, the proba-
ble error of the division is $\pm \sqrt{(0.^{\mu}0145)^{2} - (0.^{\mu}0122)^{2}}$
$= 0.^{\mu}01$. Thus, the purely accidental errors of
ruling amount to only one two millionth
of the breadth of a two centimetre grating. This
supposes the [del]two[/del] extremities of a division to be
marked by single lines. Since they are actually
marked by three lines the error to be feared from
this cause is only one three millionth of the breadth
of the grating.
