CHAPTER I.

[marginalia][ins]Indeed,[/ins][/marginalia] [del]I[/del]NMOST of friends! Formerly having been questioned by thee concerning the occult nature of the lodestone, I will in a rude narration make it public. [del]For,[/del] among philosophers nothing is delightful without participation of knowledge;[sup]1[/sup] and the nature of good things wanders in darkness and is dimmed, until it is set up into the light of mutual surrender.[sup]2[/sup] For the love of thee I will set down in plain speech things that to the mass of students are utterly unknown. Still, it is only of properties [del]of[/del] this stone that are quite manifest[sup]3[/sup] that we shall treat in this epistle; which[sup]4[/sup] is merely a part of a treatise in which we are to teach how to make physical instruments.[sup]5[/sup] To treat of the occult properties [del]of[/del] this stone depends upon the art of stone-cutting.
