_Epistle of Petrus Peregrinus._

Although the matters, concerning which you have
inquired, are, as I say, manifest, yet they will be
of priceless value. Moreover, for the vulgar they
are as illusions and fancies;[del]’[/del] so that in respect to the
vulgar they are secrets. Yet to astrologers and to [u]
students of nature[/u] they will be manifest enough.
To them these things will be a solace, and for sailors
no slight aid. Thus is seen the intention of this
work.
