ROGER BACON'S OPINION OF
PETRUS PEREGRINUS.

Roger Bacon, in his Opus Tertium, written two years
before this treatise on the magnet, in speaking of his pro-
ject of setting on foot an extensive investigation into
nature says (cap. xi.):

Oporteret habere mathematicos
optimos, qui non solum scirent
ea quæ translata sunt et facta,
sed addere ad opera, [u]quod est facile[/u]
[u]bonis mathematicis.[/u] Non sunt enim nisi
duo perfecti: scilicet Magister Jo:
London. et Magister Petrus de
Maharicuria Picardus.

It would be requisite
to gain the best mathe-
maticians, who should
not only know the things
that have been trans-
mitted to us and done,
but should be capable of
adding to [del]the[/del] [ins]those[/ins] perform-
ances [del]of them[/del] [ins]of[/ins]. This
would be difficult; for
there are not but two
that are thoroughly ac-
complished, to wit, Mas-
ter John of London, and
Master Peter of Mari-
court, the Picard.
