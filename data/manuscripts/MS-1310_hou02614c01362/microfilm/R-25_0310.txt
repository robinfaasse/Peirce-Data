Time was when educated people could imagine
that the idea of inductive science could spring
full grown from the brain of Francis Bacon; but
in our days we have learned better the natural
course of development of ideas. Thoughts of
that comprehensive kind do not spring up in the
mushroom-beds of individual brains. They re-
quire the broader fields of societies; and genera-
tions must pass by before they can acquire any
maturity of strength. There is plenty of testi-
mony both of Peregrinus and of his pupil, Roger
Bacon, from which we may securely infer that
they were acquainted with older physicists, al-
though those elders may perhaps not have com-
mitted their experiments to writing. But whether
it will be possible ever to make out with any
plausibility the earlier history of experimental
science, previous and prelusive to the work of
