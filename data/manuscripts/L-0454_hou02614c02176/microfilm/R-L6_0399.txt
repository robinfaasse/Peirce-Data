LAKE SUPERIOR DIVISION.

Madison, Wis. [date]January 24,1897[/date].

To the Director,
U. S. Geological Survey,
Washington, D.C.

Sir:

In refe ence to the matter of slaty cleavage referred to
Prof. Peirce, I would not have stated my view exactly as given in
your letter, although there is nothing in that statement which is
incorrect.

My position is:- I hold the view that a structure, which
I call [u]cleavage[/u], under proper conditions is developing at any
given moment normal to the maximum pressure. It does not follow
that its final position is normal to the final direction of maximum
pressure. Indeed, as a result of the rotational character of the
forces, or of the rotation of the material upon which the forces
are acting, the final direction will often be inclined to the
maximum pressure. I freely admit that a structure does develop
along shearing planes, inclined to the pressure, as held by Mr.
Becker. By me this structure is called [u]fissility[/u]. This structure
I think to be different from cleavage, although oftentimes, perhaps
more frequently than any other way, fractures along cleavage planes
result in fissility.
