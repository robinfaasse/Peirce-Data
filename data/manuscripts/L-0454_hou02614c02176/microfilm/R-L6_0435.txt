This is the most incontestible point in the whole inquiry; so that it will not be necessary to consider it in detail.

There are many causes, that have certainly been operative toward producing this result. Besides the pressure, there has been, as Herschel well remarked, friction, and there has been dessication. The conduction of heat has been suggested as a possible cause. A "creeping motion" of [ins]men-[/ins]tioned September 1843 by Prof Phillips (Rep. B.A. Cork Meeting p. 60) would have this result.

In addition to that Daniel Sharpe, [date]1846 Dec 2[/date], in a paper "On Slaty Cleavage" (Q. J. Geol. Soc. Lond. III. part i, p. 74) demonstrated with precision that the distortion of shells contained in such rocks proves flattening; and Haughton made calculations of its amount, and showed it to be very great. There are figures of such distorted deformed shells found in slaty rocks in a classical memoir by Sedgwick and Murchison (Geol. Trans [2] V. Plate LV), with a description by James Sowerby. De Koninck found such [del]for[/del] deformed fossils in Belgium, Henry D. Rogers in the Alleghanies (Edin. New Philos. J. XXXIII. 144), and other observers in Russia and elsewhere.

The position of many cleavable rocks with reference to intrusive masses affords further evidence of the same thing.
