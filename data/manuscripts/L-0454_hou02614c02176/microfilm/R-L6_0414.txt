[del]Especially,[/del] [ins]almost[/ins] in regard to mica, everybody knows that it sticks [ins]as if[/ins] pasted together; and [del]without[/del] without pretending to having much acquaintance with it, I cannot help asking whether it is conceivable that it should be separated by a rap, as slate is separated. If small crystals of mica are imbedded in slate, the action of cleaving the slate would tear them apart, if their cleavage coincided with the plane of the cleft; but this would be no argument to show that the crystalline cleavage was in any important measure a cause of the slaty cleavage. If there is much mica in [del]small[/del] particles, having their cleavage planes parallel to the cleavage planes of the slate, as Prof. Van Hise assures us is the case, this would of itself be sufficient to account for its "very frequently" happening that the mica particles were torn apart in the cleaving of the slate. It does not seem to me, therefore, that Prof. Van Hise comes near to proving that the crystalline cleavage of such crystals as are found in slate could contribute anything considerable toward the slaty cleavage.
