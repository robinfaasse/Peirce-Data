Even Thomson and Tait's definitions will give no countenance to Dr.
Becker, when he calls scission a "rotational strain". A rotational
strain is a strain in which, [del]all[/del] [ins]some[/ins] parts of the body being rotated
relatively to other parts, give rise (like every strain) [del]to stress[/del].
But in scission, the whole body having first been subjected to
a non-rotational strain, is turned round as a whole. This rota-
tion is no strain; [del]for it gives rise[/del] to no stress. [del]For[/del] For remember
that stress is not an external force; it is simply the effort

the rotation of the whole body considered is not so. A scission is a

Considered as a strain it does not differ from a pure shear.
