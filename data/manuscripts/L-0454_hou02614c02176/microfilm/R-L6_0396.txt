DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR

UNITED STATES GEOLOGICAL SURVEY

WASHINGTON, D. C., January 5, 1897.

Prof. Charles Saunders Peirce,
84 Broad Street, Room, 6,
New York, N.Y.

Sir:-

There is a difference of opinion between Professor Van Hise and Mr. Becker on the origin of slaty cleavage. The professor adheres to the old view that cleavage is due to pressure acting normally to the cleavage surfaces. Mr. Becker has propounded a theory according to which slaty cleavage is induced in viscous masses only, and in a direction at an acute angle to the pressure. Mr. Becker's papers, though said to be simple from a mathematical point of view, are still too mathematical for the average geologist, who is thus at a loss to decide which opinion to adopt.

The question is one of manifest importance in the study of orogeny and in mining geology, and I therefore desire to obtain a deliberate expert opinion from a physicist who, like yourself, is familiar with terrestrial questions. I should feel justified in paying $150 for such an opinion from you, and, if you signify your acceptance of the offer, I will forward you copies of the papers (one by Van Hise and three by Becker) covering the ground of dispute.

Very respectfully,

Director.
