What evidence is there that the crystalline cleavage planes are parallel to the rock-cleavage?

Prof. Van Hise seems to say (p. 635) that he has observed this.

Sorby denies it emphatically, in regard to Penrhyn slate and the Devonshire limestones. In regard to the former, ([u]Edin. New Philos. J.[/u] IV. pp. 140, 143), he says only the decidedly "unequiaxed" grains of mica show parallelism with the rock-cleavage. In his investigation of the cleavage of Devon limestones ([u]Phil. Mag.[/u] [4]. XI. p. 35), after a particular examination of the question, he says:

"In many [observed specimens of cleaved limestones], small perfect crystals have been formed, either of calcareous spar, dolomite, or brown-spar [ferriferous dolomite]... we should expect that their axes would have some relation to the direction of the [rock-]cleavage; but, instead of this being the case, they are inclined promiscuously in all positions... I never saw a cleaved limestone of which it might be said that the crystals were developed at the same time as the cleavage, so as to give rise to it."
