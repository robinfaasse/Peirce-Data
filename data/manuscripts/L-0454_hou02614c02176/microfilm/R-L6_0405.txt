[u]Cleavage[/u]

very unlike that of slate; nor are there many crystals which
cleave as easily as slate seems to do in the hands of a skillful
Welsh slater. When in place of one crystal there are very many
in a magma, the dissimilarity seems greater. I can only point
to the doubt, without attempting positively to say that slaty cleavage
could not be so produced.

As to the flattening of the particles producing slaty cleavage,
it depends upon how much they are individualized. That any
flattening in a homogeneous mass would produce any tendency
to cleavage at all, I should think antecedently rather unlikely.
I should certainly ask for positive evidence of it, and such evidence
is not forthcoming. Instances are common in the chemical labora-
tory of flattening [del]by[/del] [ins]solidification and otherwise[/ins] without flaw;
and it never was brought to my attention that anything like slaty
cleavage was so produced. Still, different materials act differently;
and I can only say that, as far as I can see, Prof. Van Hise brings
no evidence upon that point. It is true that among the [del]remarks[/del]
[ins]many[/ins] well-arranged facts which he brings forward, there are some
which may contain proof of this. But if they do, he has not
make it evident, because when he has a fact against Dr. Becker's
hypothesis, he at once jumps to the conclusion that it supports
his own, and thus [del]fails to observe, whether[/del] [ins]fails[/ins] or at least to make
