[u]I 1 $\beta$ and I 2 $\beta$[/u].

What evidence is brought that the crystalline cleavage planes are parallel to the rock-cleavage, and what that the broad tabular faces are so parallel?

Prof. Van Hise seems to establish this by Sorby's microscopical work and by his own. But Sorby says ([del]Edin.[/del] [ins]Edin.[/ins] New Philos. J. IV. pp. 140, 143) that the rounded grains of mica lie in all positions; only the decidedly "unequaxed" being placed as supposed.

[u]I 1 $\gamma$ and I 2 $\gamma$[/u].

What miscellaneous consequences of the two hypotheses can be compared with observation?

Where the mica-flakes are few, the slate ought to present imperfect cleavage.
