The general supposition ought to be
that 1 < T T T is true. But Schröder
has not got the conception of substantive
possibility.

He notices that the universe may
become a continuum, but does not
see that the individuals are then
fictitious. On the contrary he calls
the points of a line individuals, which
they are not; for they do not satisfy his
definition Vol II. §47.

He says $i, j, k,$ etc denote individua
[u]non separata[/u] (he does not use this phrase)
He explains the colon but does not
consider it as a sign of division.

He explains the general matrix of
dual relatives
$x^{2} = \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{j} (i:j)$
