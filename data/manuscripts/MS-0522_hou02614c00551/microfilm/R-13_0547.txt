The $1^{\text{st}}$ problem of p. 239 is very
easy.
Given $x = (\bar{u} + 1) w + u$
That is $w \prec x + (\bar{u} + 1) w + u$
from which obviously $x$ cannot be elimi-
nated
And $2^{\text{nd}}$ [del]$(u + 1) w$[/del]
$w \prec (u + 1) \cdot \bar{u} + x$

This has two factors $u \prec x$
and $w \prec 1 + x$
whence at once $w \prec x + 1 + x$
or $w \prec (x \cdot x + 1) w$
which is the result he writes dividing
$\bar{x} + 1 + (\bar{x} + 1) x = 0$
which is [del]$\bar{x} + 1$[/del]
$w \prec x + w \cdot (x + \bar{x})$
whence $w \prec (x \cdot x + 1) w$
