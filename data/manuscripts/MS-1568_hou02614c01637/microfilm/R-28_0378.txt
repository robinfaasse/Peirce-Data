Both parties alike assumed that an innate idea would be something absolutely fixed and unquestionable, something not subject to the test of experience. This feeling was unshaken by Kant's question of whether a universal and necessary conception need be accepted. Nor could Whewell persuade the world that self-evident truth had been subject to an historical development.
