Dear Professor Peirce

You may imagine with
what keen interest I am following
your vindication of Spontaneity in
Nature.

I think I am beginning to catch
a glimpse of your general theory of
"evolution", and I find that your
thought pierces much farther into
the mystery of becoming than my
attempts at probing have been able
to reach. You seem to look upon
primordial all-being as mind in
perfect fusion or utmost freedom of
activity. The things of this world arise
therein by a process analogous to that
by which crystals form; namely by
fixation of activities that have fallen
