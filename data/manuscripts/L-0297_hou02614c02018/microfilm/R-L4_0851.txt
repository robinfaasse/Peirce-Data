[date]May 9th 92[/date]

Professor C S Peirce
Dear Sir

Allow me to express my delight at finding that so eminent and clear-sighted a mathematician and logician has come to the conclusion, that the theory of Necessity — however serviceable as a working-hypothesis — affords no adequate interpretation of the actual state of things.

It may perhaps interest you to see how through researches in a different field a fellow-worker has arrived at similar results.

Protoplasmic studies have long ago convinced me that specific modes of reaction on the part of bodies are of far greater consequence in nature than extrinsic mechanical causation.

To break through the rigid bonds of necessity, which means fatalism in all natural occurrences, I hold to be the most important task devolving on scientific interpretation. May your serious endeavor in this direction bring about this wholesome deliverance.

With kind regards
Yours very truly
Edmund Montgomery

I take the liberty of sending you some articles, in which in an all too feeble way I have sought to find a [del]solution[/del] [ins]way out[/ins] of the dilemma of freedom & necessity.
