[u]Acetylene Gas.[/u]

Some years ago, acetylene was much talked of as an illuminating gas, and several companies were heavily capitalized and their [del]stocks and bonds floated[/del] [ins]capital subscribed[/ins] to introduce it. Fortunes were certainly made, and perhaps fortunes were lost. Then came several explosions, by one of which a café was wrecked. Chemists shook their heads. Insurance companies forbade acetylene. Since then, the general public has heard nothing more about this gas, and the prevalent idea is that it has failed and gone.

But it is not so. [del]The[/del] [ins]Different[/ins] companies have been active in a quiet way. They know now how to use acetylene so that it shall be [del]perfectly[/del] safe. They have not moved because their investigations were not complete. No one felt sure of its ground either against the others or against the Welsbach burner, which, when burning water-gas, gives the cheapest light yet introduced. Within another year acetylene will again come prominently [del]before the public[/del] as one of the best of illuminants, especially for domestic purposes and in the country.
