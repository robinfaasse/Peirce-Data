When I was young I felt the
different sensations of the air
about me as others feel
shapes and colours. I
perceived changes of atmosphere
as if I were touching objects.
Even now I can trace my 
mental state by the remembrance 
of some particular quality
in the air. I know exactly 
what the air felt like when I 
was a little girl and used to
hide in the laurels because 
I was miserable: I remember
it on the long drives to 
Cornwall, and on the morning
when my mother died.
This afternoon I came 
over a field in which 
I had often played 
as a child. The air 
suddenly felt to me 
as it used then. 
I could not describe
it, but I should 
know it any where. 
It came upon me 
like a distinct memory, 
and yet it had nothing 
to do with my memory 
of any particular events.
It was just that the 
air was of the same
kind, and so it produced 
in me a repetition 
of the sensation which 
I used to experience.
