We need not inquire into the divisions
of the last two Kingdoms.
Theoretical Science divides itself, at
once, into
1 [u]Mathematics[/u], which merely traces out
the necessary consequents of hypotheses, for
the truth of which this science assumes no
responsibility; nay sometimes (almost always)
acknowledges the unreality or at least the
inexactitude.
2. [u]Philosophy[/u], [ins](or Cenoscopy)[/ins] which aims only at so much
truth as can be inferred with likelihood or
plausibility from the knowledge common to
all grown persons.
3 [u]Idioscopy[/u], [ins]*[/ins] or [u]Special Science[/u], which oc-
cupies its energies mostly in acquiring strange
experiences, and then in inferring Truths from
them mostly only plausible.
Special Science divides itself into
two parallel streams, the one studying
the Products of Finite Minds, especially of Men,
[u]Psychic Science[/u]; the other studying the Material
Universe, [u]Physical Science[/u]. This latter kind
of science is far the more developed. Both
streams [del]do[/del] have three [del]kinds[/del] [ins]orders[/ins] of Science, these
divisions being thoroughly analogous in
