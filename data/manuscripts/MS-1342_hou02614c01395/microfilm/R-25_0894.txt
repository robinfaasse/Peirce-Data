[u]CHAPTER II.[/u]

[u]Of the Place of Logic among the Sciences.[/u]

This chapter, like the last, is merely preliminary. Its object is to afford the reader a further preliminary insight into the nature of the question that this book seeks to answer. In doing so much will be provisionally asserted that cannot [del]now[/del] [ins]yet[/ins] be scientifically proved.

Logic, as the author conceives it, is a science.

By a science is not meant "systematized knowledge," but serious, [ins]sane,[/ins] intelligent inquiry which must be pursued by generation after generation. What is meant by serious inquiry? In order to answer that question, I must call attention to some well-known facts which give the word [u]experience[/u] the meaning that it carries.
