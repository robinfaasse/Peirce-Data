The fourth fact is a man can choose a line of conduct that shall tend to change any given belief of his or shall tend to maintain it unchanged as he pleases. That is, he can [ins]in reference to any belief bring on[/ins] [del]increase[/del] his experience [del]in[/del] [ins]on[/ins] the above broad sense of that word, although he cannot bring about any change he may desire. He can sometimes so act that his belief may [del]be[/del] get changed in one way but is protected from an opposite change. Thus, he may put an ounce weight in one pan of a balance and then put a letter into the other pan. If then his belief has been that the letter weighs a little less than an ounce, he may by this gain of experience be convinced that it weighs more than he thought but he cannot [del]be convinced[/del] [ins]become convinced[/ins] that it weighs less than he thought.
