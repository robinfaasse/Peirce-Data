[u]Chapter II. On the Place of Logic
among the Sciences.[/u]

This chapter is merely introductory. Its purpose is
not to prove anything, but merely to give the reader
a further preliminary insight into the nature of the
inquiry [ins]to[/ins] which the author proposes in this book to
address himself.

Logic, as he conceives it, is a science; and by a
science he does not mean "systematic knowledge,"
but he means serious inquiry; that is to say, a mode
of life in which the energies of those who pursue it are
directed toward the determination of that way of
thinking about a given subject to which experience
