The fourth question is one which has exercised me considerably.
But I have now obtained a satisfactory solution of it. It is true that
there is great exercise of intellect in framing a mathematical
hypothesis like that of the theory of functions. But as a purely arbi-
trary hypothesis, there is no element of cognition in it, and conse-
quently it has nothing to do with the [del]place of[/del] nature of mathema-
tics [u]as a science[/u].

The fifth question is raised by a remark of [u]Sylvester[/u]. But the
reply is that all these other kinds of reasoning are in mathematics
merely ancillary and provisional. Neither Sylvester nor any mathe-
matician is satisfied until they have been swept away and replaced
by demonstrations.
