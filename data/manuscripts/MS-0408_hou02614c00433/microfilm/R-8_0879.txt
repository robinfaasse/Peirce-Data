Petrus Hispanus was a noble Portugese who, having taken
degrees in all the faculties in Paris, returned to Lisbon and was
appointed head of that school which ultimately developed into
the University of Coimbra. Subsequently, he was head physician
to Pope Gregory X, who created him Cardinal; and he was
crowned Pope [date]1276 Sep 20[/date]. He began his pontificate with
promise of grandeur; but a part of his palace fell
upon him and he died in consequence of his injuries [date]1277[/date]
May 16. This man, [ins]who had he survived would surely have been reckoned among the world's great men,[/ins] according to the tradition, [del]was[/del] the
author of the Summulae Logicales, [del]which was[/del] the regular
text-book in logic almost to the very end of scholasticism.
There are, it is true not very many printed editions subsequent
to 1520; but [del]so many had been[/del] [ins]over 50 editions having by that time [ins]procured[/ins][/ins] printed upon substantial
linen paper, [del]the[/del] copies could always be [del]obtained[/del] [ins]printed[/ins] in plenty.
