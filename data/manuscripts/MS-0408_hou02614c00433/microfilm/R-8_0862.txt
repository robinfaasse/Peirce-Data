DIVISION III. SUBSTANTIAL STUDY OF LOGIC

Chapter VI. The Essence of Reasoning

§ 60. Before we can discuss any of the questions of logic to advantage, it is necessary to explain the excellent and well-established terminology, — excellent because of its clearness and freedom from the least ambiguity, though representing an undeveloped stage of thought. To explain that terminology, it is necessary to set forth the outlines of the traditional logic. This seems roundabout, yet after all there is no waste of time, for since there are traditional ideas [ins]about[/ins] [del]of[/del] reasoning, it behooves every educated man to know what they are. They are filtered through and through our law, our language, our common sense. The words which express those ideas, and which are many of them used in common speech, particularly in English speech are also a real heritage. They add to our wealth in enabling us to understand one another. Accordingly, I propose to [del]to[/del] open each chapter of this division by explaining the appropriate part of the traditional logic with special reference to its terminology.
