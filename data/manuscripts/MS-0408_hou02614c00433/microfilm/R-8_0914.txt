§ 63. [ins][[/ins]We now come to the proper subject of this chapter. What
is the nature of inference? What says the traditional syllogism? That
an inference consists of a colligation of propositions which
[ins]if true[/ins] render certain or probable another collected proposition. If,
[del]to ask what, then[/del] to get to the bottom of the matter, we ask
what is the nature of a proposition, traditional logic tells us,
that it consists of terms, — two terms, usually — connected together
by another kind of sign, a copula.

This is tolerably explicit, and, so far, good.
