[pn]Ms 408[/pn] [u]DIVISION III. SUBSTANTIAL STUDY OF LOGIC.[/u]

[u]Chapter VI. The Essence of Reasoning.[/u]

§ 60. Before we can discuss any of the questions of logic to advantage, it is necessary to explain the established terminology so far as it relates to that question. In this division, then, I shall begin each chapter with remarks about the words and about the traditional logical doctrine [del]to[/del] which these words belong. Though this has no value in itself, it is highly important as acquainting the student with what men approve among ways of thinking, and with what are meant by current phrases.

As the literature of logic is immense, I must select a few books which I will consult in these summary statements. Here is the list of them:

1. The logical books of Aristotle, called the Organon. They are, the book of Predicaments, or Categories; the Perihermeneias or De interpretatione, a discussion of the forms of judgment, in which a somewhat different theory is adopted from that of the other books, Two books of Prior Analytics, treating of syllogistic and allied matters, Two books of Posterior Analytics, a more philosophical and general study of reasoning, Eight books of Topics, looking at the subject from a more practical point of view, and one book of Elenchi Sophistici or Refutations of fallacies.
