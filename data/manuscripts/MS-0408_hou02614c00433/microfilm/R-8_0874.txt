As to the theological writings, it is best to conform our
views to the recorded facts rather than to conjectures. The
Nestorian paper may have been written by Boetius at
a very early age, and the works on the trinity later.
On the other hand, it is quite possible that the Nestorian
[del]piece[/del] [del]works[/del] may have been written by some older member of
the distinguished family of Boetius, one of whom was suf-
ficiently [del]distinguished[/del] [ins]prominent[/ins] to be slain by Valentinian III in
A.D. 454; but it seems to me the writings upon the
Trinity are marked by the style of thought of the great
Boetius. On the whole, then, I think the balance of
evidence is the Boetius was born or early became a
Christian, more probably the former.
