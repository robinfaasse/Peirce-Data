The name of the scribe of the mathematical
papyrus, Ahmes, is a common one in ancient
Egypt, frequently written in Greek [u]Ἀμασις[/u], like the
name of the Hebrew prophet, which was possibly
the same. It occurs in the papyrus only in the following
ungrammatical sentence: "Composed was this book [del]....[/del]
by the scribe 'Ehmsw composed this book."

The name 'Ehmsw, if it is a name, seems to mean,
"I the child of the moon". It has two determinatives, the
last meaning "the person in question", the other meaning "birth".
Of the [del]most[/del] phonetic part, w is affix of the first person,
ms means child, [ins](or msw might mean "birth")[/ins] and 'Eh moon. If 'Ehms were to
