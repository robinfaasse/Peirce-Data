[u]Basic rules[/u].
I. We always have a right to a blank sheet.
II. If we can write two graphs separately we can write them side by side.
III. Every unenclosed part of a graph can be transformed [del]in whatever way it could be transformed if it stood alone.[/del] [ins]in any way in which it could be transformed if it stood alone.[/ins]
IV. Two individual indices transformable the one into the other can be joined by a heavy line of connection.

[u]Commentary[/u].
I. But the only thing we are always entitled to assert is an empty form of proposition, such as, "What I aver is true." Hence the blank sheet means that.
II and III. Hence it appears that writing two assertions together asserts both.
IV. That is, individual identity involves that which the continuity of the line implies.
