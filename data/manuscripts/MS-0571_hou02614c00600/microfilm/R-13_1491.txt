[u]§3 Logical Addition and Multiplication.[/u]

[[Note. The sign [del]≼[/del] [ins]⪯[/ins] as explained above, is, we may
trust, free from every trace of ambiguity. But
[del]though[/del] [ins]while[/ins] it does not [del]hover betwixt[/del] [ins]hesitate between[/ins] two meanings,
it does [del]convey[/del] [ins]carry[/ins] two meanings at one and the
same time. The expression [u]x⪯y[/u] means
that either x is false or y true; but it also
means that x is at least as low as y upon
a scale. In short, [u]x⪯y[/u] not only states
something, but states it under a particular
aspect; and though it is anything but a
poetical or rhetorical expression, it conveys
its purport by means of an arithmetical
simile. Now, elegance requires that this
simile, once adopted, should be adhered to;
and elegance, as we shall find, is every whit
as important a consideration in the art of
reasoning as it is in the more sensuous
modes to which the name of Art is commonly
appropriated. Following out this analogy, then,
we proceed to inquire what [ins]are to be the[/ins] logical
significations of addition, subtraction, multiplication,
and division.
