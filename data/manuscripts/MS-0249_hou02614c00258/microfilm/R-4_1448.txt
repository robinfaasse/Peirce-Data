[u]Plane ordinal geometry[/u].

A line, [u]a[/u], drawn in the plane separates it into two regions, $\alpha$ and $\bar{\alpha}$. A second line, [ins]B,[/ins] [del]must[/del] penetrate successively the regions $\alpha$, $\bar{\alpha}$, separating them into $\alpha\beta$, $\alpha\bar{\beta}$, [del]$\bar{\alpha}\beta$[/del], $\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}$. The following rule is self-evident:

[u]Rule 3[/u]. In passing along infinity in a plane which is separated by unlimited curves, we pass from the region $\alpha\beta\gamma\delta$ etc. (say the wholly positive region) by successive requalifications of single letters to $\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}\bar{\gamma}\bar{\delta}$ etc., the wholly negative region, and then by requalifications of the letters in the same order back to $\alpha\beta\gamma\delta$ etc.

Hence, the order of succession of regions formed by 2 lines [del]is[/del] [ins]may is either[/ins] $\alpha\beta$, $\alpha\bar{\beta}$, $\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}$, $\bar{\alpha}\beta$. If there are three lines, the order in passing round infinity will be $\alpha\beta\gamma$, $\alpha\beta\bar{\gamma}$, $\alpha\bar{\beta}\bar{\gamma}$, $\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}\bar{\gamma}$, $\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}\gamma$, $\bar{\alpha}\beta\gamma$
