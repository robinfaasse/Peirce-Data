[u]Ordinal Geometry[/u].

By ordinal geometry, I mean the doctrine of the arrangement of segments.

This subject is naturally treated by means of the Boolean algebra. A whole being cut into two parts, these are denoted [ins]respectively[/ins] by a letter, as [u]a[/u], and by the same letter overscored, as [u]ā[/u]. [ins]The being or not being overscored may be called the qualification of the letter.[/ins] The product of two letters denotes the common part of the two spaces they [ins]severally[/ins] denote. A fraction denotes the space denoted by its numerator with the implication that this is wholly included in the space denoted by the denominator.

A single point, [del]as[/del] [ins]A[/ins], upon a line does not divide the latter into segments; for the line returns into itself through infinity. A second point, B, gives two segments, which may be denoted by [u]b[/u] and [u]b̄[/u].
