duo decimal measures, and fractional quantities, are infinitely
preferable to the metric system in the mechanic arts, but how to
measure these advantages as compared with the metric system passes
my comprehension; as for example, the loss that would result to
our Railroads from simply changing the dimensions of our bolts and
nuts to the metric system, to say nothing of the more extensive
use of these indispensable articles made by the country at large.
In a paper that I have just read before the Committee of Coinage,
Weights & Measures, I simply stated the loss to the Railroads as
incalculable. It is perfectly evident that a bolt with metrical
threads and nuts would be neither better nor worse than those made
to our one inch basis. We should go through all the agony of
change for no result whatever, except that our metrology would
then conform to that of the French metric system. We add nothing
to our productive ability, nor would we improve the quality.
When I contemplate the enormous destruction that would result from
such legislation that is contemplated by the bill now before
Congress, I cannot conceive the degree of folly which must possess
the men who favor it. It seems to me that if you could write a
paper of the character of that report you prepared for the
National Academy of Sciences you would better reach the intelli-
