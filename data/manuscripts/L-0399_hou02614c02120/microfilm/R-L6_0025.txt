Milford Pa [date]1904 Feb. 22[/date]

My dear Mr. Sellers:

I have carefully considered what you say about the "incalculable" disadvantage of the Metric System. But I do not like that word. The whole question is one of "how much," and I believe that estimates can be made which cannot be impugned to the extent to which it would be requisite to do so in order to show that the Metric System would be advantageous. In the first place, I hold it to be an axiom that a conscientious man will not exert influence to bring about a public change without inquiring into the advantages and the disadvantages and that the burden of proof is upon him to show that the former decidedly and unquestionably overbalance the latter.

Now the advantages of the metric system are, first, that it is decimal and second that it is a universal commercial language. In order to estimate the value of the first, I will begin by asking what is the value annually to the people of the U.S. of decimal money of accounts. The number of book-keepers and accountants is about 1 percent of the number of persons in gainful occupations. These being all expert arithmeticians [del]the English system of money[/del] would be appreciably just as convenient for them as a decimal system
