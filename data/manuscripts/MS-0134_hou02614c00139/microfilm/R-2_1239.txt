[u]Scholium[/u]. Space-measurements have two primitive modes from which all others are derived. They are [del]the measures of[/del] [ins]intervals[/ins] [del]distances[/del] between two points along the ray through them, and measures of intervals between two planes [ins]around[/ins] [del]along[/del] the ray through the whole of which each passes. The one mode of measurement supposes a metrical image to so move or jump by perversion that all its [del]points[/del] [ins]particles in one ray[/ins] occupy [del]one[/del] [ins]points in that[/ins] ray, and also to be carried from ray to ray; the other supposes a metrical image to so move or jump by perversion that all the flat films through one ray continue to contain that ray, and also to be carried from ray to ray.

[u]Definitions[/u]. The measure of the interval between one point to another along the ray through them is called their [u]distance[/u], or the [u]length[/u] of the stretch between them.

The measure of the interval [ins]from[/ins] [del]between[/del] one plane to plane around the ray they both contain is called their [u]inclination[/u], or the [u]angle[/u] of the stretch between them.

[u]Definitions[/u]. The quantity of the stretch from a point through the length of a ray [ins]round[/ins] back to the same point again is called [ins]the[/ins] [u]circuit[/u] [ins]along[/ins] [del]of[/del] the ray. Half the circuit [ins]along[/ins] [del]of[/del] a ray is called a [u]quadrant[/u]. The adjective is [u]quadrantal[/u].

The quantity of the stretch from a plane round a ray back to the same plane again is called the [u]circuit[/u] round the ray. Half the circuit round a ray is called a [del]right[/del] [u]angle[/u]. The adjective is [u]rectangular[/u] or [u]orthogonal[/u].

The quantity of motion round or along a ray required to restore every particle and flat film of the metrical image to its place is called a [u]revolution[/u].
