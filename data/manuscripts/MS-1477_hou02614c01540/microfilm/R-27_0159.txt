Prof. Frederick J. E. Woodbridge's 'The Philosophy of Hobbes in Extracts
and Notes collated from his Writings' (Minneapolis: The H. W. Wilson Co.)
is, we presume, well adapted to the use of hearers of some course of
lectures. It contains some 370 pages from the [del]Leviathan and from the[/del]
[u]Elements of Philosophy concerning Body[/u] (the [u]De Corpore[/u]). We are not,
for the most part, very favorable to such volumes of extracts from
philosophical writings [ins]which deserve to be[/ins] [del]easily[/del] obtained in full, and which would
seem to be worth reading in their entirety by anybody interested in
philosophy. This may not apply to Spinoza, [del]for[/del] [ins]in regard to[/ins] whom a considerable
class of persons have a burning curiosity that [del]will[/del] can quickly be
appeased; but for such writers as Locke, Reid, and particularly
Hobbes, with his strong style and [ins]generally[/ins] interesting matter, we really do
think it [del]is[/del] a pity to encourage fragmentary reading. There are
several very beautiful old editions of his works, [del]and the Leviathan[/del]
while the press-work of this one is hardly better than indifferent;
and the [u]Leviathan[/u] may be had well-printed for less than the
cost of this volume. There is, however, a portrait of Hobbes; and the
notes which are extracted from other parts of Hobbes's writings
add to the value of this volume.
