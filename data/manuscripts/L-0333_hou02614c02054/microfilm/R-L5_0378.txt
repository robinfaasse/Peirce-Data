Dearest Father

I have several letters of yours to reply to. You keep your counsel about the Johns Hopkins and the Math Journal. I write Sylvester in reply to a second letter that if after all the aid & comfort you had given Gilmore if he then had made you a "foolish" offer it was not only foolish but indecent & that I was not surprised if in that case you had chosen to withdraw from all support of the Johns Hopkins & that I was sorry that his influence there had not been strong enough to prevent such an occurrence. I told him you had guarded complete silence to me about it.

Your advice about deferring my further move is of course quite correct. I shall be highly gratified if I get $3500.

better provided with spring clothes than with winter ones.

I am projecting two lectures on Science & Scientists to deliver round. The first is to be as entertaining as possible and is intended to draw an audience for the second. My theory is that the second might be [del]made to pay in[/del] [ins]this way.[/ins] The second to consist of choice extracts from Hagen Hisch's integral tables. Don't you think that would go down if illustrated by photographs projected on a screen? In case of this, I lecture was called for Beerens de Haan might be drawn upon.

My idea is in the first lecture to speak of the moral dispositions which scientific men ought to have in order to be successful in their studies & also to glance at the faults which don't seem to have prevented great eminence in science. In the second lecture I should propose to speak of the intellectual discipline & regimen of science.

With best of love to mother & aunt Lizzie & all

Your loving

C S P
