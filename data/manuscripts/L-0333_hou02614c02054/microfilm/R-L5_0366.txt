[u]The external product of two vec-[/u]
tors, is the parallelogram they
form, account being taken of
its plane and the direction of
moving round it, or as Hamilton
says, its aspect. We therefore
have
$[v_1 v_2] = v_1 v_2 \sin \angle^{v_1}_{v_2} \cdot I$
where $I$ is [del]a unit[/del] [ins]unit[/ins].
This reminds one strongly of
what is written in quaternions
as $-V(v_1 v_2)$ but it is not
the same thing in fact, because
$v_1 v_2 v_3$ is a solid and therefore
a new kind of quantity. In
fact, Grassmann has got hold
(though he doesn't say so) of an

Eight-fold algebra, which may
be written in my system as follows.

[u]Three rectangular vectors[/u]
$i = M:A - B:Z + C:Y + X:N$
$j = M:B - C:X + A:Z + Y:N$
$k = M:C - A:Y + B:X + Z:N$

[u]Three rectangular planes[/u]
$I = M:X + A:N$
$J = M:Y + B:N$
$K = M:Z + C:N$

[u]One solid[/u]
$V = M:N$

[u]Unity[/u]
$1 = M:M + A:A + B:B + C:C$
$+ X:X + Y:Y + Z:Z + N:N$
(The unity might be omitted)

[del]For[/del] [ins]In[/ins] this multiplication we have
$[v_1 v_2] = - [v_2 v_1] \qquad v^2 = 0$
The relation of these two multipli-
cations is exceedingly interesting.
