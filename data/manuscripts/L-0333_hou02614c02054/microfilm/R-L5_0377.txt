I enclose a letter from Sylvester. I hope your name won't be taken off the Journal. They cannot have intended any slight & I suppose you approve of the conduct of it.

I don't know whether the transfer will be made or not but I think it about time to give those engineers a few knocks in the newspapers.

I hope Clarence King will get the Geology. He is a practicable man & will know how to get money from Congress without loss of self respect (as Hayden does). Newberry supports him, and the two surveys will sustain one another. They will be to some degree in the same boat.

Patterson decides to send me to Paris next fall. It would be better I think to go sooner. Still my private convenience suits the delay. I must try every way to raise a little money so as to have hair, gloves, coat, & shoes, to conduct my negotiations there. [pn]30[/pn]
