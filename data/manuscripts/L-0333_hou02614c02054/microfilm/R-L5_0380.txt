Cambridge [date]July 30 79[/date]

My Dearest-Charice -

I have just stepped into the shoes of [del]Alvord[/del] [ins]Peirce[/ins] Brothers and Co., as regards yourself. Hereafter instead of owing them [del]12[/del] 6 pounds sterling 11 shillings and nine pence, you owe me $\frac{1105}{100}$ [ins]95[/ins] dollars, you may pay at your leisure, and will [del]not be liable to any charge for[/del] interest. Fanny Wright is again with Charlie Longfellow has starved us all [del]up[/del] - and we do not see how it is likely to end. My own individual opinion is - that they will before long be settled in Rome or in Italy. When there are Englishmen and Americans sufficient to keep them in countenance and the sooner this form of settlement comes, the better for us all - A Bostonian and child withal [del]red[/del] - I have been enjoying the cool of Old Orchard for a few days - and among my pleasures, there was talking about [del]Father[/del] [ins]heartily[/ins] of my dear Charlie "a stronger man than his Papa."

Give my kindest regards to Madame [del]Patterson[/del] [ins]Patterson[/ins]; when you see her and write her, your mother is writing, so a word -

your ever loving Papa
Benjamin Peirce

C. S. Peirce Esq.
