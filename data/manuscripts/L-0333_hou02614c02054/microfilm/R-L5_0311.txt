[pn]Sept. 18. 72. VII[/pn]

U. S. Coast Survey Office.
Washington, [date]Sep 18 1872[/date]

My dear Father

Newcomb told me very privately, that Harkness was seriously moving to get up an attack on the Survey in the winter & have it transferred to the Hydrographic Office. He asked that every professor at the observatory, hoped he would go on & get squelched. For my part, I don't think Harkness is such a fool. Certainly if the grand attack is coming I don't see why this is not a very favorable moment to have it come. I think Harkness is far too active & energetic a man to take any undecided course with. One must either be his friend or enemy, otherwise

in a very aggressive way, do you mean to say that there is no way in which a person in this country can learn mathematics. In Germany all this is very different. M.H. said G. & I have no concern in defending American Colleges & I don't take the least interest in you. I will inform Professor Peirce of your application & that is all I feel bound to do.

I have given a letter to you to Dr. Weiss asst at the Vienna Obsy, probably future director. He was very polite to me there. They are to have a new obsy & there & he is here finding out about American intentions in the way of astronomy. Sent by his gov't. His wife is pleasanter than he. I wish you were in Cambridge to do something for them as he did much for me in Vienna & introduced me at Lisbon's where I was treated in the kindest manner.

The tooth powder arrived safe.

Very best love to mother, aunt Lizzie & all.

Your loving C. S. Peirce
