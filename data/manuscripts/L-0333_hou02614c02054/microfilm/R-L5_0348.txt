The demonstration is excessively simple. The question is whether it is correct. Tell me what you say. Here it is

$a =$ equatorial radius
$b =$ polar radius
$l =$ astronomical latitude
$v =$ geocentric "
$q =$ equatorial gravity
$p =$ polar "
$g =$ gravity at latitude $l$.
$r =$ radius " "

By geometry,
$$r = \frac{ab}{\sqrt{a^2 \sin^2 v + b^2 \cos^2 v}}$$
$$dr = \frac{a^3 \sin^2 v . db + b^3 \cos^2 v . da}{(a^2 \sin^2 v + b^2 \cos^2 v)^{\frac{3}{2}}} = \frac{\left(\frac{b}{a} \tan^2 l \, db + da\right) \left(\frac{b^2}{a^2} \tan^2 l\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\left(1 + \frac{b^2}{a^2} \tan^2 l\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$$
$$\cos(l-v) = \frac{\cos l (1 + \frac{b^2}{a^2} \tan^2 l)}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{b^4}{a^4} \tan^2 l}}$$

Now, if one level surface (surface of equilibrium) is an ellipsoid, every exterior level surface is an ellipsoid. Hence, going from one surface to another
$$g \cos(l-v) dr = p \, da = q \, db$$
$$g = \frac{p \sqrt{1 + \frac{b^4}{a^4} \tan^2 l}}{\cos l \left(1 + \frac{bp}{aq} \tan^2 l\right)}$$

If we make $\frac{b}{a}$ and $\frac{p}{q}$ very near unity, this reduces to Clairaut's formula. But the difference between the two in the actual case is important.
