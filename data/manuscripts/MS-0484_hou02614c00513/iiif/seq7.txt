Logicians have always recognized three forms of symbols,
to wit, terms, propositions, and argumentations. A term
is a symbol in which the representative and reactive [ins]aspects[/ins] functions
of the object are left entirely vague except so far as they
may be determined by the qualitative element, [del]or[/del] [ins]that is, the[/ins] generalized

nouns. But these are really mere accidental parts of speech
peculiar to certain classes of languages. They have no general
logical significance. They are mere fragments of symbols.
That is, “-is a man” is a symbol; but “man” is more
nearly an icon. A proposition is a symbol in which the
representative element, or reason, is left vague and unexpressed,
but in which the reactive element is distinctly indicated.
