§1. Of Graphs in general.
A [u]graph[/u], in the most general sense, would be a
diagram composed, first, of [u]spots[/u], or parts treated as
points having simple visible qualities, or colors, and
also different [u]sides[/u]; second, of [u]nexes[/u], or parts treated
as lines, though they too may have colors and sides;
third, of [u]faces[/u], or parts treated as surfaces; and so
on to higher dimensions.
An [u]infimal[/u] graph is one which has no faces nor
elements of higher dimensionality, nor [del]any[/del] [ins]distinguishable[/ins] sides to its nexes.
A [del]bond-nexed[/del] [ins](called a bond)[/ins] graph is an infimal graph in which
every nex [del]has[/del] two extremities and no more.
A graph has its Listing numbers. The census-theorem, as applied
to infimal graphs, is: The chorisis of the graph less its cyclosis
equals the chorisis of the spots [del]less[/del] plus the singularity of the nexes.
A [u]valental[/u] graph is [del]an infimal[/del] [ins]a bond-nexed[/ins] graph in which each
color[del]s[/del] of a spot determines the number of distinguishable sides,
called [u]hands[/u], that spot possesses; and the end of a [del]nex[/del] [ins]bond[/ins] is
attached to each hand. The unattached ends of the bonds are
called the [u]loose ends[/u] of the graph.
