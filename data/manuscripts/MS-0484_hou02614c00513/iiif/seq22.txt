[u]Rule XXV[/u]. ([u]Rule of Protraction and Retraction[/u]). An [u]evenly enclosed[/u]
heavy line (i.e. so enclosed as to its outermost part) may be [del]be[/del] retracted
into [del]within[/del] an [u]even number[/u] of ovals while an [u]oddly enclosed[/u] heavy
line may be protracted out from [u]any number[/u] of ovals.
1[ins]st[/ins]. This holds of unenclosed heavy lines. For by [u]VII[/u] an individual
index may be attached to any such line; and thereupon by [u]XVIII[/u] it can
be retracted into any even number of enclosures, and finally by [u]XXIII[/u] the
index can be erased.
2[ins]nd[/ins]. If the rule, so far as it relates to retractions and protractions
through even numbers of ovals, holds good so long as the retrahend
or protrahend is not enclosed in more than N ovals, then it
holds good so long as it is not enclosed in more than N+1 ovals.
