This syllogism in Darii is thus analyzed into a long series of inferential steps. Of these inferences 7 [del]each[/del] depend on each of II and IV, 6 on V, two on each of III, VII, X, 1 on each of IX, XI, XII. Rules I, VI, VIII, XIII are not explicitly involved. Rule XIII is certainly not involved; but then this is of a different character from the other basic rules, & probably is only one of a number of logical propositions not included among those rules. VIII is a derived rule. For either an oval with some contents is scriptible or not. If not, then if an individual index occurs at all, it occurs attached to an unenclosed heavy line. By V this line may be broken; and by II and IV [ins]the indivi-[/ins] [del]it can[/del] dual index can be written unenclosed and attached only to that line. But if an oval can be written, [del]and an individual index at all[/del] may be written within [ins]it[/ins] by XI it follows that if the contents of the oval were scriptible anything would be scriptible, [del]including any individual index unenclosed with an otherwise free line attached to it. But if the contents of the oval is not scriptible, there is no falsity in saying that if it were that index could be so written. Hence, if the contents of the oval were scriptible, as well as any individual index joined to an otherwise free heavy line, then anything would be scriptible. But if a graph consisting of the contents of the oval together with such index so attached were scriptible, so by II and IV, would be each of those parts. Hence, by XII, the contents of the oval may be written in an oval with the individual index attached[/del]
