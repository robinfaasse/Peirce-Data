[u]Rule XIV[/u]. Every unenclosed partial graph, whether attached to the rest
or not, can be erased.

For, by [del]rule V[/del], any attached unenclosed partial graph can be detached
by severing the heavy lines attaching it to the rest. It is, therefore, only
necessary to show that [del]every[/del] unattached unenclosed partial graph
can be erased. That is, if the whole graph consists of two partial
graphs, P and Q, not attached to one another, it is to be shown that
Q can be erased. For, by II, it could be erased if it stood alone. [triple dot symbol] by IV,
it can be erased when it is an unattached unenclosed part of PQ. Q.E.D.
