[u]§4. Remarks on the equivalence between existential graphs
and familiar language.[/u]
It is obvious that the [del]above[/del] conventions require us to
interpret [fig]line-loves-line[/fig] as, "somebody loves somebody", and
[fig]line-loves-line line-benefits-line[/fig]
as "somebody loves somebody and somebody benefits somebody." On
the other hand
[fig]line branching to loves-line and benefits-line[/fig]
means that the same person both loves somebody and benefits somebody.
So
[fig]line looping into loves[/fig]
means, somebody loves himself.

The last graph but one might be read, "somebody is loved by
a benefactor of something coexistent with him." The last graph
might be read, "something is loved by something identical with it."

The last graph but one might also be read, "there are three things,
A, B, and C, [ins]whether[/ins] identical or not, such that A loves B and A benefits C."
