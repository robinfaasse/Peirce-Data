§4. [u]Basic Formal Rules of Existential Graphs[/u].
I now proceed to formulate the formal properties of graphs from
which all legitimate illative transformations of them can be
deduced without reference to the constitutive conventions,
of meanings of the graphs.

[u]Rule I[/u]. Any graph once written may always be written.
[u]Rule II[/u]. The whole graph may be erased.
[u]Rule III[/u]. Graphs that [del]may[/del] [ins]might[/ins] be written separately may be written
side by side.

[u]Rule IV[/u]. Any separate unenclosed part of a graph may be
transformed as if it stood alone.

[u]Rule V[/u]. Any unenclosed [del]connecting[/del] [ins]heavy[/ins] line may be broken.

[u]Rule VI[/u]. No change in the shape of a heavy line is important
so long as it is joined to the same hands, and no others, and so
long as its least enclosed part remains in the same ovals and
no others.

[u]Rule VII[/u]. Every heavy line might be joined to some individual
index if we knew what index to choose.
[ins]Rule VII'. It makes no difference to what part of a heavy line an individual index is joined.[/ins]

[u]Rule VIII[/u]. Any index of an individual of the universe may
be written down [ins]unenclosed[/ins] with a heavy line, otherwise free, attached
to it.
