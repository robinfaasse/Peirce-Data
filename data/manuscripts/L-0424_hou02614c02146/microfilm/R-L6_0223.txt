My analysis of the topical properties of space gave me a great deal of puzzling, and this effect of my stupidity causes me to be, rather vain of it. It is as follows: [u]Postulate 1[/u]. Space is continuous. [u]Postulate 2[/u]. Space has three dimensions. [u]Postulate 3[/u]. Space has no topical singularities. [u]Postulate 4[/u]. The Listing numbers for space, are each 1.

By a topical singularity, B, of a locus, A, I mean that B is a locus contained [ins](and of less dimensionality, than)[/ins] in A, and that from B, particles, filaments, or films can [del]can[/del] move in A along more or fewer tracks than from the majority of loci of like dimensionality with B in the neighborhood of [del]it[/del] in A. If the tracks are singularly many, I call it a singularity by excess; if few, by defect. For example, from an ordinary point [pn]5[/pn] on a line a particle can move two ways. This is true even of a prickly line, by which I mean a line all along which the points of furcation are infinitely close together. At an extremity of a line, the index of [ins]topical[/ins] singularity is minus 1; at a furcation, the same index is positive. A line may be punctually interrupted at a point from which it nevertheless proceeds two ways. That is, there are two coincident extremities. The index of such a singularity is -2. There is a census-theorem about these singularities which makes this necessary. A surface or solid may have singularities of different dimensionality [ins]but by the same index.[/ins]
