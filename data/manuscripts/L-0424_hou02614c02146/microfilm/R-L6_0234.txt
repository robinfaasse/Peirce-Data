Milford Pa [date]1900 Jan 1[/date].

My dear Story:

May the nineteen hundreds be happy for you Mrs. Story, and your son!

I have a new way with the map question that I think will do. To explain it, I begin with generalities.

By the denumeral multitude, I mean, that of the collection of finite whole numbers. By the first abnumeral multitude, I mean that of all possible collections of whole numbers. By the [ins]$(N+1)$th[/ins] [del]Nth[/del] abnumeral multitude, that of all possible collections of objects belonging to a collection of the Nth abnumeral multitude. There is no greatest multitude.

One lapse of time may contain another. Every lapse of time contains itself. If one contains a second that contains a third, the first contains the third.

Of two lapses of time that contain no lapse in common, one is subsequent to the other. If one is subsequent to a second that is subsequent to a third, the first is subsequent to the third.

Whatever is true of subsequence is also true of antecedence, as far as the properties of time go.

If any lapse of time, T, contains a lapse of time A, and also a lapse of time, B, [del]having[/del] [ins]simply[/ins] no lapse in common with A, and subsequent to it, then there is a lapse subsequent to A and contained in T which contains every lapse contained in T and subsequent to A.
