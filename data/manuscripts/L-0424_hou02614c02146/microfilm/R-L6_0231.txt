Milford Pa [date]1900 Jan 1[/date] [pn]8[/pn]

My dear Story:

May the nineteen hundreds be happy to you and Mrs. Story and the young man.

I have a new way with the map-proposition that I think leads to the desired result. I begin with some generalities about topical geometry.

By a [u]point-figure[/u] I mean a place within which no motion can take place, (because it has no continuity.)

By a [del]line-fi[/del] [ins]number of particles[/ins] I mean anything [ins]movable[/ins] that at each instant occupies a [u]point-figure[/u].

By a [u]line-figure[/u], I mean a place that cannot be filled at one instant by a number of particles, but can be so filled in the course of time, the particles moving.

By a [u]number of filaments[/u], I mean anything [ins]movable[/ins] that at each instant [del]occupies[/del] [ins]fills[/ins] a [u]line-figure[/u].

By a [u]surface-figure[/u], I mean a place that cannot be filled at any instant by a number of filaments, but can be so filled in the course of time.

By a [del]film[/del] [ins]number of films[/ins], I mean anything [ins]movable[/ins] that at each instant fills a [u]surface-figure[/u].

By a [u]solid-figure[/u], I mean a place that cannot be filled at any instant by a number of films, but can be so filled in course of time.

etc.

By a [u]point[/u] I mean an indivisible point-figure. A [u]particle[/u] is a movable [ins]that can occupy a point[/ins].

By the [u]chorisis[/u] of a place I mean the number of points that can be taken in it so that a particle cannot move from one to another. If P is any place, I denote its chorisis by X_P.

By a [u]simple line[/u], [ins]or oval,[/ins] I mean a line-figure of chorisis one such that taking any point whatever upon it, there are just two ways in which a particle placed at that point can leave it while remaining in the line-figure.
