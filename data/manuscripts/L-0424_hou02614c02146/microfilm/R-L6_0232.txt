My dear Story:

Happy nineteen hundred to you and Mrs Story & your Son.

I have a new method with the map-proposition which leads (if I mistake not) to the desired result. For this purpose, I have to consider some generalities about topical geometry.

By a [u]sublocus[/u] I mean a place (point-figure, line-figure, surface-figure, solid-figure) within a place.

By a [u]singularity[/u] of a place I mean a [ins]one-piece[/ins] [del]of[/del] sublocus of that place from which the directions in which an object filling it can instantly and entirely leave it without breaking or welding [ins]are[/ins] different in number from those of ordinary subloci of the same dimensionality.

The value of a singularity is [del]the number of ways of leaving it divided by the number of ways of leaving an ordinary sublocus of the same dimensionality.[/del] [ins]one less than the quotient of[/ins]

Thus, a particle can leave an ordinary point on a line in two ways. Therefore, the singularities of a line are as follows:

[u]1st[/u] N-way nodes, or points of branching, or say corners, having the value [del]1/2[/del] [ins]1/2[/ins] N

[u]2nd[/u] Extremities, having the value -1/2.

[u]3rd[/u] Detached points, having the value -1.

The total singularity of every line is a whole number. If X is the chorisis, or number of pieces, [del]and[/del] K the cyclosis, and S the total singularity of any line,
X - K + S = 0.

A filament can leave an ordinary [del]point[/del] [ins]line[/ins] on a surface in two ways. Hence a surface has the following line-singularities:

[u]1st[/u] N-way splits having the value 1/2 N

[u]2nd[/u] Edges, having the value -1/2.

[u]3rd[/u] Detached [del]or partly[/del] (wholly or except at points) lines, having the value -1.
