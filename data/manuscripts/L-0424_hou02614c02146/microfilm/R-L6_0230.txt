My dear Story,

Here is more about map-coloring. I omitted to mention (because I make no use of the proposition) that it may be assumed that the map has no region of less than five boundaries. For if there is a region of 4 boundaries, let two opposite ones be erased. Then the map has two regions less, and supposing every map of fewer regions than the original can be colored with four colors, this can be so colored & then the [del]quadri[/del] tetragonal region can be restored.

Listing's theorem introduces the periphraxis of a surface. Hence it follows that if a certain number of regions (re-member, [del]a[/del] [ins]a[/ins] region has no cyclosis), boundaries, and trihedral [del]summits[/del] [ins]coigns are[/ins] possible in a map covering the whole spheroidal surface, it is impossible in a map [del]covering[/del] [ins]covering[/ins] any portion of that surface without cyclosis. This seems good reasoning. But it is not; for 2 hexagons and 5 pentagons cover the surface in Fig. 1 and do not in Fig. 2. I suppose the explanation is that boundaries and [del]summits[/del] [ins]coigns[/ins] on the edge of the map surface are only to be counted [del]as[/del] [del]half[/del].
