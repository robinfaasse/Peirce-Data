My dear Story:

I come across an old proof of mine that only four colors are required to color a map on a spheroidal surface, regions bordering one another (touching along a line) having different colors.

I define a spheroidal surface as one on which every line returning into itself without branching or crossing cuts the surface into two regions. I omit the easy proof from this that not more than four regions can all border on one another.

Now if more than four colors are required to color a given map on such a surface, then there is a region which in every way of coloring is [del]either[/del] bordered by regions of four colors.
