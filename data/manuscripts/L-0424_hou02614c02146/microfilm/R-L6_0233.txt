My dear Story:

May the nineteen hundreds be happy for you, Mrs. Story, and the young man!

I have a new way with the map-proposition that, if I mistake not, will lead to the desired result. To explain it, I begin with some generalities.

By the denumeral multitude I mean that of [ins]all[/ins] [del]the[/del] finite whole numbers. By the first abnumeral multitude I mean the multitude of different collections of whole numbers. By the (N+1)th abnumeral multitude, I mean the multitude of collections of objects each drawn from [del]an the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] collection of Nth abnumeral multitude. There is no greatest multitude.

Of any two lapses of time [del]having[/del] [ins]containing[/ins] no lapse in common one is subsequent to the other. Whatever is subsequent to anything is subsequent [ins]to anything[/ins] itself subsequent to the same thing.

[del]For[/del] If one lapse of time is subsequent to another, every lapse of time contained in the former (including itself) is subsequent to every lapse of time contained in the latter.
