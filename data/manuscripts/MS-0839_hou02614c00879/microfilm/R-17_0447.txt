To the doctrine of all and some, as treated in
the ordinary text-books (Watts, Logick, II. ii. 1), some-
thing has to be added. The distinction is between
[del]taking[/del] [ins]taking any[/ins] object in the universe, no matter what,
and the selection of a suitable. If [del]both[/del] [ins]two[/ins] selections
are of the same kind their order is indifferent; not
so, if they are different kinds. Thus, to say, "Take
any person, A, then a person, B, can be found
such that A loves B," is very different from
saying, "A person, B, can be found, such that
whatever person A be taken, A loves B." [del]The[/del]
[del]latter asserts all the former asserts, and more.[/del]
The denial of the [del]former[/del] [ins]first[/ins] proposition is, "A person
A can be found such that taking no matter
what person, B, A does not love B," and the
denial of the second proposition is, "Take any
person, B, then a person, A can be found
such that A does not love B." The rule
is too obvious to require formal statement.
