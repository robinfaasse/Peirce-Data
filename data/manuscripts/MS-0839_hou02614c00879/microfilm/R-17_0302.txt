The subject of a statement, or what it is all about,
cannot be indicated by any description thereof, however
specific. So we saw in the last paper. A truth it is
of philosophical [del]significance[/del] [ins]importance[/ins]. Herein, for instance, lies,
as Kant distinctly showed, the fallacy of Anselm's
argument that God necessarily exists because in
the idea of God existence is implied; for the logical
proposition yields the corollary that existence
cannot be described.

It will be best, in view of such applications
of it, to look into this logical proposition more
narrowly.
