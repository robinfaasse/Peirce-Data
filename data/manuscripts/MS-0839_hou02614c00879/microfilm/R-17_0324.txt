That the converse of a transitive relation is itself
[del]a transitive relation[/del] is evident, by taking the converse of
$$t t \prec t$$
when we get
$$\breve{t} \breve{t} \prec \breve{t}.$$

But this is not true of the negative. For example, let
$\Omega$ (the zodiacal sign of Leo) signify "at least twice as
great as". Then the inference holds
$$A \Omega B \quad B \Omega C$$
$$\therefore A \Omega C;$$
But
$$5 \bar{\Omega} 3 \quad 3 \bar{\Omega} 2$$
yet it is not true that $5 \bar{\Omega} 2$. What is true of the negative
is that
$$\bar{t} \prec \bar{t} \dagger \bar{t}.$$
and also
$$\breve{\bar{t}} \bar{t} \prec \bar{t}$$
$$\bar{t} \breve{\bar{t}} \prec \bar{t}$$
$$\bar{t} \prec \bar{t} \dagger \breve{\bar{t}}$$
$$\bar{t} \prec \breve{\bar{t}} \dagger \bar{t}$$

Every negative of a transitive has the form
$$\bar{t} \breve{\bar{t}}$$
