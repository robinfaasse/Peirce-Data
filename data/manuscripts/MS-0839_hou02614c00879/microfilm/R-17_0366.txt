Besides the principal motive [ins]for[/ins] the study of logic, namely to acquire the art of reasoning, there is another secondary motive, namely to understand how other people reason, and what they mean when they talk about reasonings. This makes it desirable to mention a good many things, which, in themselves, are hardly worth notice; and, in particular, to explain all the phrases of logic which are in men's mouths, although they [ins]often[/ins] express quite superficial conceptions.

The traditional [del]work[/del] logic divides the operations of the understanding into Simple Apprehension, Judgment, and Reasoning, which are the operations of thinking names (or terms), [del]judgments[/del] [ins]propositions[/ins], and [del]inferences[/del] [ins]argumentations[/ins]. All signs are divided by the logicians into names, propositions, and argumentations, [del]and[/del] A Mental name, or what one thinks when one thinks of the meaning of a name, [del]is a[/del] is called a [u]Concept[/u]. A mental proposition is called a [u]judgment[/u]. An argumentation within ones own mind, by which the thinker is more or less inclined to believe something, or by which he sees that something must be true, is called an [u]inference[/u].
