1 Inference is [del]the[/del] [ins]substitution[/ins] of one inference for another. We
write
$$\begin{array}{c} A \\ \therefore B \end{array} \quad \begin{array}{l} \text{premise} \\ \text{conclusion} \end{array}$$

A substitution in general may be written
$$\begin{array}{c} m(A) \\ \therefore B \end{array}$$
where $m$ indicates what sort of substitution is made. We may
write $\therefore \frac{A}{B}$ as $1 \therefore \frac{A}{B}$ so as to reduce it to the general form.

If we substitute $B$ for $A$ [del]on principle[/del] by substitution $m$, then
[del]then we may[/del] and substitute $C$ for $B$ by substitution $n$, then
we have substituted $C$ for $A$ by a combined substitution which
we may write $nm$. So that,
If $\begin{array}{c} A \\ m \therefore B \end{array}$ and $\begin{array}{c} B \\ n \therefore C \end{array}$ then $\begin{array}{c} A \\ nm \therefore C \end{array}$

For $mm$ we may write $m^2$. Then we have $1^2 = 1$ In general $m^2$ is
different from $m$. And $mn$ from $nm$.
We have generally $\begin{array}{c} A \\ \therefore A \end{array}$ but not generally $m \therefore \begin{array}{c} A \\ A \end{array}$
