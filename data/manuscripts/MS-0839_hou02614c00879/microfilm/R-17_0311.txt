[u]Diagrams for Lecture 5.[/u] Gamma-graphs

$X\text{---}A$ means "$X$ is the sheet of assertion"

$X\text{---}\epsilon\text{---}Y$ means "$X$ is the area of the enclosure $Y$."

$X\text{---}\phi$ " "$X$ is a permission."

$X\text{---}\chi$ " "$X$ is a fact."

$X\text{---}\mu$ " "$X$ is a blank."

$X\text{---}\kappa$ " "$X$ is an enclosure."

$X\text{---}\sigma\begin{matrix}\text{---}Y\\ \text{---}Z\end{matrix}$ " "[del]$X$ carries $Y$ as its entire graph in so far as it is of[/del] [ins]so far as $X$ makes it to be so[/ins]"
"$Y$ is on $X$ [del]by virtue of $Y$[/del]
the nature of $Z$ to make it do so."

That is to say, for example,

[fig]Diagram with letters A, K, X, epsilon, sigma[/fig] means "An enclosure is [ins]the entire graph[/ins] on the sheet of assertion [del]by[/del] as a fact"

[fig]Diagram with letters A, K, X, epsilon, sigma, phi, J[/fig] means "It is permitted to place on the sheet of assertion [ins]as the entire graph,[/ins] an enclosure on whose area an enclosure is placed as a fact"

$X\text{---}\delta\text{---}Y$ means "The graph-replica denoted by $X$ contains as a part of it the replica $Y$"

$X\text{---}\upsilon\begin{matrix}\text{---}Y\\ \text{---}Z\end{matrix}$ means "$X$ is a line of identity having its terminals at $Y$ and $Z$."

$X\text{---}\xi\text{---}Y$ means "$X$ is a replica of the same graph of which $Y$ is a replica, or is [del]equivalent[/del] [ins]equivalent[/ins] to $Y$."

$X\text{---}\gamma$ means "$X$ is a graph-replica."
