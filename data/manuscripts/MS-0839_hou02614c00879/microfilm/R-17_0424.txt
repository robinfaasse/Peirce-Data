Logicians talk of [u]the conclusion[/u] from given
premises; and logical machines have been
made to grind out this one conclusion. But
in reasoning about relations, almost any premise
yields an infinite multitude of different conclusions.
Thus, from "Everybody loves somebody," we [ins]can[/ins] infer,
Everybody loves a lover of somebody;
Everybody loves a lover of a lover of somebody;
Everybody loves a lover of a lover of a lover
of somebody; etc.
All these are included in the one statement that
Everybody loves a lover of [ins]an[/ins] infinite succession
of lovers (some or all of whom may be identical)
each loving the next. But in very many cases,
