The French and German
Socialists had at their command
the same weapons as the Russians.
They were perfectly acquainted
with the same theory. They too had
behind them the great traditions of
the revolutionary past. Why did
they fail to act? For the simple
reason that they did not want to
act. The difference between them and
the Bolsheviks was a difference not
of knowledge, but of will. To
the Bolsheviks, the world was
not a mechanism which followed
an inevitable course, but a
living process which they could
themselves influence and direct.
Hence the dynamic character
of the Bolshevist revolution, as
distinguished from the static
orthodoxy of the French and German
Socialists. To the Bolsheviks,
the Marxist theory was not
a dogma, but a guide to action.
They did not wait for conditions
to become “ripe” for revolution;
they set to work to create the
conditions. They did not wait for
the masses to “awaken”; they
set to work to awaken them.
They were the masters of history,
not its slaves. Such was the spirit
which gave birth to the October
Revolution, and which has ever
since remained the characteristic
of the Soviet regime. It is the
spirit of creative energy, of
revolutionary audacity, of un-
shakable faith in the power of
human will to overcome all
obstacles and to fashion a new
world. In the words of Lenin:
“We must build a new society,
not with the hands of others,
but with our own hands.” This
is the secret of the Bolshevist
success.
