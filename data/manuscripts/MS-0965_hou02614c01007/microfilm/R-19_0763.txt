[u]Creation[/u]
Stationary motion. For according to the law of
energy if the mean [u]vis viva[/u] is less than the
sum of the mean virials, the bodies will be drawn
together, and the [u]vis viva[/u] will [del]be[/del] increase twice
as fast as the virial, so that it will soon be
sufficiently great to fulfill the condition of stationary
motion. If, however, the attraction varies as
rapidly as the inverse cube of the distance,
by the law of energy the [u]vis viva[/u] will increase
no faster than the virial, and the consequence
will be that stationary motion may never
be attained [del]but[/del] [ins]but[/ins] the bodies (supposed to be
points that are centres of force) will continually
draw nearer and nearer in narrowing spirals.
Or if the [u]vis viva[/u] is too great for stationary
motion they will move further and further
away. Thus, [del]in[/del] [ins]an[/ins] attraction inversely as the
cube would seem [del]to be[/del] [ins]to reach[/ins] the limit of rapidity
of change which the tendency to generalization
would produce, and hence [del]three dimensions[/del] is
the largest number of dimensions which
is consistent with the ultimate effect of that
tendency.
