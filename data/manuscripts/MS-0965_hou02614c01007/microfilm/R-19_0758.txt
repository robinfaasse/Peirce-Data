<u>Creation</u>

The tendency to generalization works in favor
a habitual association of bodies and of sta-
tionary motion, it would work against an
attraction inversely as the distance. For it
must be remembered that the tendency to genera-
lization, like the principle of natural selection,
and unlike mechanical law, is teleological in
its action; that is, it works to effect an end, and
[del]to bring about[/del] [ins]gradually[/ins] [del]by[/del] [ins]finds[/ins] all means to that end.

On the other hand, attraction inversely to any
power as high or higher than the cube is un-
favorable to stationary motion. For suppose
the mean vis viva is not great enough for the
stationary, compared to the mean distance, be-
tween pair of bodies to give stationary motion.
Then by the law of energy, the vis viva will
increase while the mean distance diminishes.
But the mean vis viva will increase in the case of
attraction inversely as the cube of the distance just
as fast as the vis viva, and in the case of higher powers
faster, so that stationary motion will never be
brought about.

But in the case of attraction inversely as
the square of the distance, stationary motion
will always be brought about unless the mean
vis viva is too great relatively to the mean distance
of the particles.
