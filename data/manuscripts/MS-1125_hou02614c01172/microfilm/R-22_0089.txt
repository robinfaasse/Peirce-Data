The idea of Weierstrass, that of the arithmetization of mathematics, now
dominates the big half of the science. It constitutes an admirable per-
fectionment. Yet I would be willing to wager that it does not hold the
lead as long as the idea of the geometrization of algebra did, so surely does
one great step give occasion for another, the opportunity of a still greater
mind. In the eighteenth century, a theorem could still confer fame. Taylor's
Theorem, if I remember rightly, was published in [date]1713[/date]. Now it must be a method,
