I have dwelt chiefly of science because this has been the peculiar
glory of the nineteenth century; and science and the scientific spirit have
been a great factor in whatever else of greatness the century has displayed.
[del]S[/del] Practical achievements are before the eyes of the public; and
I am inclined to defer to popular judgments in this matter, as
being, though fallible enough, less so than an individual
reasoned opinion might probably be. According to general
belief, the great mass of practical ability in our days is
turned to the pursuit of wealth, making a dearth of great
men. I throw Napoleon, the greatest figure in history since Mahomet,
and the most marvellous man in the practical way since
Alexander, into the eighteenth century, because his greatness
was fully developed before 1801, and it is the productiveness of
the nineteenth century in great men that is being considered. This
at once necessarily makes the nineteenth century inferior to
the eighteenth in the production of great practical men.
