We use the mark $ to mean dollars. Thus
$1 is one dollar, $2 is two dollars.

We write ct for cents. So
$1 and 100 cts
are the same thing

Or, we can put a point, called the DECIMAL
POINT after the 1 and instead of writing
$1 we can write $1.00. Then

$$\begin{array}{lll}
\$1.01 & \text{will be} & 101\text{ cents} \\
\$1.02 & & 102 \\
\$1.03 & & 103 \\
\$1.45 & & 145\text{ cents} \\
\$7.50 & & 750 \\
\$0.01 & & 1\text{ cent}
\end{array}$$

We write cm. for centimetres and mm. for
millimetres. Then

$$\begin{array}{lll}
1.0\text{ cm} & \text{is} & 10\text{ mm} \\
1.1\text{ cm} & & 11\text{ mm} \\
0.1\text{ cm} & & 1\text{ mm} \\
4.7\text{ cm} & & 47\text{ mm}
\end{array}$$
