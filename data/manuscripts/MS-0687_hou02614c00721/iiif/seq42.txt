of "good sense". When, in reading a scientific memoir, I come
across such an expression as "it is natural to think,"
I wish to tell the writer that the scientific man has no
concern with what it is natural to think, — except as
an object of study. Reasoning in a given way is not ren-
dered sound by it being natural to think in that way, but
by the fact that on every occasion in the future (as well
as the past) in which such premisses shall be found true,
as these have been found true, the proper conclusion will
as a matter of [u]fact[/u], and not a mere matter of thinking,
be found true, too. It is natural to man to err. Nothing is
more natural than bad reasoning. Indeed, it is impos-
sible to reason otherwise than naturally; and if good
reasoning is nothing but natural reasoning, there is
no [del]procedure[/del] [ins]procedure[/ins] of reason that is not good.
