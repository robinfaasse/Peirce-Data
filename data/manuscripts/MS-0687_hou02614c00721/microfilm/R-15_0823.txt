The most marked characteristic of human reason-
ing, as compared, for example, with the conduct of
birds and other animals, is [del]the[/del] [ins]the[/ins] extreme frequency
with which it blunders. Animals may of course meet
with unexpected events, and may otherwise be deceived;
but they rarely blunder, while reason is blundering
all the time. My observation of successful practice,
both in men remarkable for their success, and in myself,
has been that, while it thinks things out carefully,
yet at every step it is guided by instinct. I once was
on the night boat from Newport to New York, and my
stateroom being on the lee side and the boat crowded, the
air was bad, and I got up [ins]in the morning[/ins] in a lethargic condition.
As soon as I arrived at the Brevoort House, I found
I had left an extremely valuable watch in my berth.
