[pn]23[/pn] question arises whether two collections
might not be so great that each would
be "greater" than the other. It would seem
that the fact that my terminology leads
at once to this question must have been
the principal reason why other logicians
have avoided a definition that suggested
so strange a question. But for my part,
seeing that the question is a real and
fundamental one that has to be answered,
in whatever form the nomenclature re-
quires it to be put, it seems to me an
additional recommendation of my
simpler definition that it at once points
the way to this question. I have no doubt
[ins](in which I disagree with some others)[/ins] that
the answer is that no two collections can
be each greater than the other. But I con-
fess that so far I have not been led to put
my name to any demonstration of the
proposition; though another logician
has put forth a professed demonstration
of the answer I regard as the true one; and
I have not said that his demonstration
is fallacious. It is such a very subtle ques-
tion that I am in no hurry to commit
myself.
