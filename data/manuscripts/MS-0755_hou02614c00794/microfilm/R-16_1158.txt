Of the same scientific character, though its errors are apt to be larger (but no Inductive reasoning is justified unless the reasoner acknowledges that his conclusion is [ins]probably[/ins] [del]is[/del] only an approximation to the truth, and holds it as subject to future revision) is the reasoning by which, having found a certain proportion of a carefully formed sample to have a certain character, we [ins]provisionally[/ins] conclude that [del]about the same proportion of[/del] the whole class from which the sample has been drawn has that same character.

Much weaker, yet justifiable reasoning is where the sample drawn does not consist of numerable nor measurable units, but is, for example, either a sample of the characters of some object, or is a sample of the consequences of a given general state of things.
