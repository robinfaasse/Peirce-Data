Note on Recorde's Ground of Artes.
[ins]Written without consulting an Bibliography, and therefore only preliminary.[/ins]

There is internal evidence that part of it was composed in 1542.

But I do not think it likely that [del]an[/del] [ins]the[/ins] old book would have been dedicated to Edward VI who came to the throne [del]who came to the[/del] [del]throne[/del] Jan 28. 1547. From the tone of the dedication and the precocity of Edward, I think it likely that he was about 14 at the time. So that the book was probably published in 1551 or 1552, notwithstanding a slip in one of the copies says there was an edition in 1540.

The edition of 1558 declares itself to be a revised edition. There is nothing to indicate that this revision was by anybody but the author.

Some verses by Dr. Dee in Willsford's edition state that he twice revised the book. These revisions must have been before that of John Mellis, for various reasons. One is that Mellis borrows the first two lines of these verses, and also that he imitates Dee's style in signing his preface. One of Dee's revisions, [del]according to some other verses by him at the end of[/del] [del]Mellis's edition was published shortly after Billingsley's Euclid, that is,[/del] [del]shortly after 1570.[/del] I suppose this was Dee's second revision.

Mellis in his dedication says that he had before (in the course of eight years) published two previous revisions. An example in multiplication speaks of "this present year 1590" Hence, Mellis's first revision was dated 1582.

The edition [del]containing[/del] revised by Robert Hartwell seems to have been based upon a former one by R.C. According to Willsford's address [del]to the reader[/del] at the end of his edition, it would seem that that was a faithful reprint (perhaps with some corrections) of Hartwell's first edition; and the example in multiplication above referred to speaks of "this present year 1630." This therefore, was probably the date of Hartwell's revision. Again on p 103 "this present year 1645" is mentioned.

The edition of 1646 speaks of "this present year 1645."

Thus, there were the following recensions:
I. Recorde's 1[ins]st[/ins]. 1551 circa.
II Recorde's 2[ins]nd[/ins] 1558 or earlier
III Dee's 1[ins]st[/ins] 1561?
IV Dee's 2[ins]nd[/ins] 1573?
V Mellis's 1[ins]st[/ins] 1582
VI Mellis's 2[ins]nd[/ins] About 1587.
VII Mellis's 3[ins]rd[/ins] 1590
VIII Edition by R.C. (Perhaps 1623)
IX Hartwell's 1630.
X Willsford's ([del]Mellis[/del]) 1654? 1662?
XI Edward Hatton's 1699.
