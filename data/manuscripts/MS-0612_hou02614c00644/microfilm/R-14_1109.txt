I go on to explain what I mean by one thing being determined to
[del]Accord with[/del] another. Let there be three Subjects $\lambda$, $\mu$, and $\nu$ and three
characters P, Q, and R. If P and Q both possess $\mu$, [ins]which is the same as to say[/ins] and P is not more
definite in its nature than Q, or P possesses $\mu$ and Q possesses $\nu$ but
would not have possessed $\nu$ if P had not possessed $\mu$, or if P possesses
$\mu$, Q possesses $\nu$, R possesses $\lambda$, and if R had not possessed $\lambda$, Q would not have
possessed $\nu$, and if it had not been true that if R had not possessed $\lambda$, Q would
not have possessed $\nu$ then it would not be true that if P had not possessed $\mu$,
R would not have possessed $\lambda$; in either of these three cases Q is said
to be determined to Accord with P.
