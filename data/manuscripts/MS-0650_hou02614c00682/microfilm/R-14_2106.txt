Search me out the very most ruinously
thoughtless of boys, who as he approaches the age
of sixty will lost the last spark of his boyhood,
without having gained the first fibre of manhood,
— you will most probably find him among those lads whose
native powers of thought had been the most vigorous, — and
even he must be able to see that the faculty [del]which was write[/del] [ins]which[/ins] [del]who[/del] elevates
man above all the rest of the fauna of our globe is the power
of self-control. Let us try then, — as if we were [del]men[/del] chemists
of the mind, — to analyze that power and isolate its basic
element. Max Müller's theory, hasty and crude though it
certainly is, — the theory that we first speak, and then clothe
[del]in speech[/del] the meaning of what we had at first spoken
without thought, will perhaps help our gropings.
