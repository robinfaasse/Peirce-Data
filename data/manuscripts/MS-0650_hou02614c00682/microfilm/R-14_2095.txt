Its purpose is to aid one in talking to himself. Have you ever read either of two little books by Max Müller published by the Open Court Co. at a quarter each. That is to say, that is the price in [ins]thick[/ins] paper covers; but they are worth binding. What [del]most[/del] struck me in reading them, — their principal point is stated in each; but it is the one on the nature of language which is the best of the two, though [del]both[/del] [ins]each[/ins] are richly worth reading, though the [del]doctrine[/del] [ins]truth[/ins] is little more than half true, — what chiefly struck me, I say, was the singular ignorance they betray. Müller, like a good many men who begin by swallowing whole the philosophy of their teachers without sticking to it afterwards as if they were glued to it, was pretty well along in life before he found out that the medieval scholastics went deeper into the nature of thought and things than he had supposed.
