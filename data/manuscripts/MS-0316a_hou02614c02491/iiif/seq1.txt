Communication from Murray Murphey to Richard
Robin: July 1967

Dear Dick,

I assume that the Multitude and Continuity referred to is that which I
cited in note 3 on 240 of my book. I do not have a film, but I do have some
notes in hand on it, and it was in the material in Houghton Library when
I worked there. That material was very ill classified -- in fact, it was just
a file cabinet draw into which papers and notebooks had been jammed. What I
have on paper is the following:

May 15, 1903. Multitude and Continuity. A Lecture at Harvard.
p.5. Peirce made his first contribution to the subject of multitude in 1867.
p.7. He made his second contribution in 1881. He also first learned of Cantor's
work in 1884.
