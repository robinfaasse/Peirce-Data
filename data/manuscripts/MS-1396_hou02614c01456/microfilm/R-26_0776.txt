Babylon, by Pythagoras, himself. For the most likely

Cambyses in Egypt (which is almost impossible owing
to the late date of the invasion of Cambyses) is that he
was taken prisoner in Asia Minor by Cyrus, before
the capture of Babylon, and so was brought into
contact with Brahmans. But on the next page, one
catches Dr. Bascom saying of the early Eleatics that
"being — one of the mind's explanatory notions — was
regarded as the [del]the[/del] substantial, the substratum of all things.
Being is, not-being is not." Now, this interpretation
is a gross anachronism. Reflective thought was
not sufficiently developed to render such a doctrine
possible. On the other hand, all we know of the
Eleatics makes it quite manifest that the doctrine
was a sort of rude anticipation of the principle
of excluded middle, conceived in so hard a fashion
as to exclude continuity.
