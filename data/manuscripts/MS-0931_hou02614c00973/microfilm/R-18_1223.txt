On the other hand the proposition which does not imply that its predicate is not essentially contained in its subject – (which I shall term an [u]attributive proposition[/u]) says nothing which is not said by the corresponding proposition which does [ins]imply[/ins] [del]say[/del] this (which I shall call a [u]subsumptive proposition[/u]). And thus these are related to one another as universals & particulars although they arise from [del]a[/del] determinations of the predicate like affirmatives & negatives. Thus instead of two respects in which propositions differ, there will be four; and instead of four forms of propositions, there will be sixteen. It is plain that contradictory propositions will differ in all four respects – Let us next see what modifications this theory requires in the doctrine of syllogism –

In the first place then it is plain that in the third figure both premises cannot be necessary, and also that in the first figure [del]if the case is necessary, the conclusion is so likewise[/del] the conclusion must agree with the case in being necessary or contingent. On the other hand both premises may be contingent in the third figure.
