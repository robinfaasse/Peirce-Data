Dear Sir:

As you ask me to criticise your Book VI Chap. VI, I proceed to do so minutely.
In the title, I should translate "conversione" rotation, not revolution; first, because it is
more accurate, and second because it serves to give the reader a juster idea of Gilbert's hazy
state of mind to show him the variety of words that he uses for the same glimmer of an idea.
Why you should translate "vigore" by energy, unless you deliberately mean to deceive the reader,
I cannot see. Energy is quite a modern word. Certainly it did not exist in Elizabethan English
in which the Greek word would have been translated "act", as in Latin by "actus." I think the hon
honest way is to translate "vigore" vigor. I don't see the slightest pertinency in your note
about the clypsedra. On the other hand, I think it might be well to remind readers that the
pendulum clock was not yet invented, and that he states the different means of measuring time
in the order of their precision. "Vertigo" I should translate: whirling. I don't see why you
translate "major" less; certainly, if you are going to do so, a footnote is called for to explain
why you make Gilbert commit such a blunder. Why do you omit to translate "annis solaribus tropi-
cis"? "Sed meliore intelligentia aequaliter et admirabili constantia" : but by a better intelli-
gence equally and with an admirable constancy. You seem to overlook the moral approval in this.
"Convertabunturque suis temporibus": rotate in their own times. ertipiae: turning. Et ad melius
bonum intelligentia": an intelligence to a better good. You do not reproduce the grotesque
awkwardness of Gilbert's expression. Circumfertur: is carried round. "(copernici nyctonesibus":
the hypotheses of Copernicus. Gilbert uses the proper technical expression; if you think your
readers wont understand it, you can explain it in a note. "Ad solem": to the sun. The translation
"sunward" seems to me to convey no meaning at all. he means, of course from noon to noon. You mig
might say in reference to the sun. "Medium orbis concentricii Lunae": the middle of an orb
concentric to the moon. Motum periodicum: periodic motion. I think you should allow Gilbert the
technically correct language he chooses to use. You can explain in a note that he means one
complete revolution. or as we should now say the sidereal motion. We take the motion relative
to the sun, not the period(or sidereal) just as a day is a complete revolution of the earth
