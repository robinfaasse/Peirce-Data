New York, January 26th, 1894.
2337 Seventh Avenue.

Mr. C. S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.

My Dear Mr. Peirce:-
Your letter of the 19th inst., has been trans-
mitted to me, and I have to thank you again for sending me such
very interesting and always instructive communications. You always
give me a great deal to think over. I scarce dare enter upon a
continued revision of my Gilbert after the many shortcomings you
kindly have pointed out, [ins]S[/ins]o much for having hurried it, rushed it,
in response to ill advised people who feared the early appearance
of the long promised English edition. I am, of course, anxiously
awaiting the latter, but, from what I have gathered through my
English friends, the wonderful work for which the Gilbert Club was
originally formed is not likely soon to materialize.

I wish I could see the elaborate notice you have written for
the "Nation", and trust you have not dealt severely with me. You
are displaying good judgment with the Peregrinus, and will save
yourself many annoyances and regrets.

Since you are not ready for the publication of your Peregri-
nus, I will do my best to make up something that might be used as
a Biographical Notice should you think same worthy of insertion
