Suppose the molecular proportion of
$\text{NaCl}$ in the solution is $p$, that of the
water $1-p$. Suppose these act by [del]double[/del]
[del]decomposition[/del] at some rate of [ins]double[/ins] change
decomposition to produce $\text{HCl}$ and $\text{NaOH}$,
proportionally to the amounts present. So that
$$u = c(p-x)(1-p-x),$$
where $x$ is the molecular amount of
double decomposition. Then the rate of the
inverse double decomposition is
$$v = C x^2$$
$C$ is evidently far greater than $c$
When equilibrium is reached
$$C x^2 = c(p-x)(1-p-x)$$
$$= c p(1-p) - c x + c x^2$$

$$(C-c)x^2 + cx = cp(1-p)$$
$$x^2 + \frac{c}{C-c} x = \frac{c}{C-c} p(1-p)$$
$$+ \frac{1}{4} \frac{c^2}{(C-c)^2} \qquad \frac{1}{4} \frac{c^2}{(C-c)^2}$$
