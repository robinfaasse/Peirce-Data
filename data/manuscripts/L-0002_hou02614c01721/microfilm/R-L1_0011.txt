Boston Jan 3[ins]d[/ins] 1892.

Dear Mr Peirce.

I have read your article with much interest, and I admire your argument extremely though I must admit that I do not [del]shun[/del] [ins]understand[/ins] what you mean by "chance", since my mind is so limited I do not think I grasp the notion of an effect without a cause. Of course there are many effects whose causes escape us. In the same way however I should have to admit that I can't grasp the notion of infinity, at which you will, of course, smile.

But if I apprehend your reasoning there is not after all so great a gap between us, for you are clear that order prevails, and that it prevails by reason of certain tendencies which we call laws, not knowing in the least what they are.
