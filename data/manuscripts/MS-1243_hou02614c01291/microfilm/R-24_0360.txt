فائدة fâ-idat 'advantage'.
The mark ّ  called [del]tashdîd[/del] [ins]تشديد[/ins], tashdîd,
[del]'severe[/del] 'severe strengthening' (شدّ shadda 'he
strapped') is placed over a consonant as a
sign of its being doubled. Thus, the last word
is pointed شَدَّ.
The mark ْ  called, as is its effect, سكون, sukûn,
'pacification' (from سكن sakana 'he had got
quiet') placed over a consonant, shows that the
letter is not followed by a vowel.
The mark ٱ  placed over an initial ا or over
initial ال and called وَصْلَة waṣlat 'union'
(from وصل waṣala 'he joined') shows that the
letters over which it is placed are not pronounced.
The mark ~ called, like its effect, مَدَّة maddat
'extension' (from مدّ madda 'he stretched') is
placed over an initial ا to show that it is
to be pronounced like fatḥat followed by ا.
