The three vowel points:
فتحة fathat, a line above the preceding consonant as فَتَحَةَ;
ضمة dammat, a looped line [fig]damma symbol[/fig] above the preceding consonant;
كسرة kasrat, a line below the preceding consonant.

فتحة means 'opening', fem. of noun of action of فَتَحَ fataha, he opened. Shows organs of speech are to be wide open.
ضمة means 'bringing together' shows rounding & pursing of lips, from ضَمَّ damma 'he brought together'.
كسرة means 'fracture' shows thin opening, from كَسَرَ kasara 'he broke'.

The vowel points may be doubled [fig]double vowel points[/fig] being put for [fig]vowel points[/fig], thus: [fig]tanwin symbols[/fig]. This is to indicate the تَنْوِين tanwîn, or pronouncing with an n-sound at the end; from نُون the name of the letter ن.

The mark [fig]hamza symbol[/fig], called هَمْزَة hamzat, is attached to a vowel-point to show that no consonant precedes. It is attached to one of the weak [del]prolongative[/del] [ins]consonants[/ins] [fig]weak consonants[/fig]. There are two cases. In the first place, at the beginning of words it is always attached to ا and may [del]be me[/del] carry any vowel. As, أَلِف alif, the letter ا, أَمَلَ amala 'he hoped', أُمِلَ umila 'it was hoped', إِيمَالٌ îmâlun 'the giving of hope'. In these cases the hamzat is not marked in unpointed writing. But in the middles of words, the hamzat really indicates a hiatus, that is, a consonant, as its name 'a pushing back with a blow' from هَمَزَ hamaza 'he pressed', indicates.
