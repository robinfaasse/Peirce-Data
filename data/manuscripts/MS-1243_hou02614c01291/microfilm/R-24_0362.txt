The enormous majority of the words
of this wonderfully rich language, are
regarded as "parts", or regular formations
from verbs, whether the latter exist or
not. And it is the remarkable regu-
larity of these formations, combined
with a sort of analogy between sense
and sound, that makes the amazing
multitude of words possible.

It is best to begin with the syntax,
which is extremely simple; because
it throws a light on the accidence,
which is surpassingly complex.

A categorical proposition does
not need any assertory verb in
Arabic. The reason no doubt is
that every part of the verb involves
an indefinite predication. Thus,
take the following:
