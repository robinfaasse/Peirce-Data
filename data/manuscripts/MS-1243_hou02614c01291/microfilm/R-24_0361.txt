[u]Pronunciation[/u].

The general characteristic of the pronunciation is the tremendous energy of it. [del]The Arabs themselves only produce the sounds with great effort, to which we are unaccustomed.[/del]

The Latin rule for accent is generally right, except that the imperative is accented on the last syllable.

The vowels are not clear.
Fathah as a in hat
Dammah as u in bull
Kasrah as i in hit.
They are short. But Fathah followed by ا
Dammah " " و
Kasrah " " ي
represent the long vowels
Fathah followed by و gives ow in cow.
Fathah followed by ي gives i in fine.

The labials ف ب م و are our w, m, b, f.
The dentals ن ت د are the French n, t, d.
The sigmals ز س are our z, s
I do not know what variety of r is represented by ر.

ر is formed further back than our l
ث is our th in thin ذ our th in this.
ط is a t pronounced with the tongue full against the front part of the palate. Say between tin and chin. I represent it by ty
ظ is pronounced with the organs in the same position but is a vocal continuant, apparently with a slight sibilation. I represent it by zy, but it has not so much buzz as that suggests.
ص is a sort of s, very forcibly pronounced; but as well as I can make out, the hiss is made against the lower teeth. I mark ṣ
ض The sound of this is still more enigmatic. Some say it is a very forcibly pronounced d. Others that it is the sonant of ص and yet not a z but a dh sound. I mark ḍ
ش is simply our sh
ي is our y
ك is our k.
ق is a very gutteral k. I mark it q.
غ is the sonant trill of this. It is the sound heard in gargling [ins]without the voice[/ins] [del]like clearing throat[/del]
خ is nearly the same [del]without the voice[/del]
ع is the sound of a sheep's bleat. It affects the following vowel.
ح is produced by closure of glottis. [del]A very rough[/del] [ins]forcible[/ins] h.
