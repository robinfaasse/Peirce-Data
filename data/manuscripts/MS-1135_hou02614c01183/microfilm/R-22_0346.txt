The game of chess goes back into the night of time. That is, it has been played nearly as today for 14 centuries; a similar game with the same name was played in the middle ages [ins]in Europe[/ins] [del]in Europe[/del] and in India in the tenth century A.D.; and allied games were played in ancient Egypt [del]2000 B.C.[/del] [ins]1600 B.C.[/ins], that is back to the Hyksos Kings. No other important game can boast such antiquity. It is played by 2 persons. [u]Implements[/u]: a 64-squared board checked in black and white, [ins]placed between the players,[/ins] a white square being at the corner nearest each players right hand; and 32 chess men, 16 white and 16 black (there is great latitude as to the actual colors, but they are called white and black), each set belonging to one of the players. The chess-men of each color consist of 8 "pawns" [ins](or common soldiers)[/ins] all alike, and 8 pieces, namely 1 King, 1 Queen, [del]originally a vizier,[/del] [ins](in most languages a "Lady")[/ins] (calculated to be worth over 10 pawns), 2 Rooks (or castles, as in most languages), originally elephants (each calculated to be worth nearly 5 pawns), 2 bishops (in French fools, in German, runners), originally archers (each calculated to be worth about 3 1/2 pawns), and 2 knights, or horsemen (each calculated to be worth over 3 pawns). There are two usual systems of designating the 64 squares of the board. The system most usual in continental Europe assigns a single designation for each square. Namely, the [del]rows[/del] [ins]files[/ins], or rows of 8 squares extending across the board from player to player, are lettered, a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, beginning at White's left hand (Black's right hand); while the ranks, or rows of 8 squares extending across the board, at right angles to the files, are numbered, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, beginning at the rank nearest the player of the white men.
