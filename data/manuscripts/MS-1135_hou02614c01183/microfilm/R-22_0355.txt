There are two [del]various[/del] types of opening, the [u]open[/u] and the
[u]close[/u]. The former are the lively games, in which some
pieces are early brought out and an active attack is made,
[ins]ordinarily by "White", or the first player.[/ins]
If the attack does not succeed, it will usually leave the
attacking party in a position of danger. The defensive player
will, therefore, desire that his opponent should make a
premature attack without having previously made some
move which will be necessary in order that later the
attack may be kept up. In the close games, each party
devotes many moves at the beginning in maneuvers of the
pieces behind the pawns, so that when the action finally
comes, they may be thoroughly prepared. These games are
[del]very[/del] abstruse and dull.

A position is said to be winning, powerful, fine, well-opened,
well-secured, etc. or cramped, crowded, incommoded, un-
comfortable, exposed, scattered, hazardous, untenable.
