When Newton's diagram is constructed as above described, no measure-
ments are necessary, either of the proportions of light in mixtures or
of the lengths of lines on the diagram. But after laying down three
points to represent three colors, we can take the point equidistant from
these three to represent to color composed of equal parts of the three colors;
and then if any color is matched by a mixture of two others in given
proportions, the distances of the point representing it from those representing
the others [del]are[/del] [ins]will[/ins] always be in the reciprocal proportions. Thus, if color C results
from 3 parts of A and 2 of B, then the distances of point C from points
A and B will be in the ratio of 2 to 3. By means of this rule, and by
measuring the proportions, we can lay down [ins]the point for[/ins] a color without waiting for
the completion of the network.
