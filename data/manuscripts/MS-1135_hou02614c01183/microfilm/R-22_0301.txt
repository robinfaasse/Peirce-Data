[ins]Light[/ins] is a more natural method of defining colors.

[del]Under any[/del] If the intensities of different lights are reduced until they are hardly visible, they seem to differ in no other respect than in intensity. When the intensity is greater, their color-qualities may appear very different; and besides differing in the total amount of light, either the lighter or the darker may be the more decidedly possessed of distinctive color-quality, [del]that is[/del] [ins]or in other words,[/ins] may have more chroma. When the intensity is very great each approaches toward yellow and their distinctive [u]hues[/u] are less marked. A color may be defined, 1[ins]st[/ins] by its luminosity, which is the photometric intensity of the sensation (in the case of the color of a body, it is supposed to be seen [ins]in[/ins] under ordinary daylight), 2[ins]nd[/ins], by its [u]chroma[/u] or departure from a grey, and 3[ins]rd[/ins] by its [u]hue[/u], as red, pure red, scarlet, orange, yellow, green, cyan blue, pure blue, violet blue, violet, purple, crimson, [del]and[/del] the series thus returning to pure red again.
