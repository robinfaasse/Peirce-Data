My dear Professor Crafts:

As you are unable to do the work with the electrical furnace, I have arranged to do the whole myself. I note your opinion about the expense of heating dolomitic limestone; but I am in possession of a number of facts which indicate a not much higher temperature would be required. However, I won't go into these now, because I shall soon know more about it.

I regard the success of the St. Lawrence water power as certain as any such enterprise can be. There may be some error in the calculations; for I have not the data to make them for myself [del]but[/del] [ins]I shall receive them tomorrow.[/ins] But from what I have seen with my own eyes, I am certain that there is a great water power there which can be developed at a low cost. It is bound to be very profitable if it is once started, even if acetylene comes to naught. That it will be started, barring some very extraordinary event, such as the conquest of the United States, or something of that order of improbability, and that in a few years at latest, I am very confident. It maybe I shall not be in it; but I think I shall. I find no difficulty in getting capital, as much as I need to go in. The amalgamation of the acetylene companies is now all but assured.
