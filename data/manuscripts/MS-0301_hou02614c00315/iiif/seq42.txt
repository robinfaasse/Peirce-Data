[del]his [ins]those[/ins] [del]those[/del] results are given here as illustrating the
general form of the titration curves for proteins and
some of the effects of temperature, salt, etc.
More complete and accurate data are now available
for the titration of purified crystalline egg albumin.
These results, which include the determination of the
acid and alkali binding over the whole range of
stability of this protein, and also an investigation
of the acid binding in the presence of various
salts, will be given in detail later (cf. Chapter III).
The maximum acid-binding capacity of the
native egg albumin is found to be 86 to 90
equivalents per mole (45000 grams) depending
somewhat on the salt concentration and the nature
of the acid used.
The alkaline titration of native egg albumin
as determined by the [del]electrometric method is
probably slightly in error due to the instability of the
protein [ins]at alkaline pH’s [/ins] especially at temperatures above 15°.[/del]
