10 Church St.
Schenectady
[date]April 16th 92[/date]

My Dear Peirce -

I wrote you
some time ago to Milford,
but receiving no answer
concluded that you must
be in New York, and in a
recent letter Parkin tells me
that such is the case.

My book is at length com-
pleted, the first volume
printed, the second volume
nearly done, so that the
Harpers say it will be out
in a few days.

Since writing to you about
it last I have added a con-
cluding chapter entitled "The
Scotch-Irish, the Puritans of
the South" containing facts

[page break]

Democrats, because, as I imag-
ine, they hated England and every
thing English with the intensest
bitterness. This was true of
them in New Hampshire and
Maine as well as in Virginia
and the South generally. Putting
all the facts together it has
occurred to me that Peirce may
have had some Scotch-Irish
blood.

The health I am sorry to say
does not improve. I am very
thankful to have it hold out
for the completion of my book.
Have been confined to the house
for several weeks, but hope to
get out again with the warmer
weather.

Yours Sincerely
Douglas Campbell.
per H. W. C.
