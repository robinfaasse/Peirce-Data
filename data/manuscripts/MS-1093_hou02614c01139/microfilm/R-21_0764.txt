[u]Note on the Theory of the Economy of Research[/u]. By [del]Asst.[/del] [ins]C. S. Peirce[/ins]

[pn]I C 2 a # C[/pn]

When a research is of a quantitative nature, the progress of it is marked by the diminution of the probable error. The results of non-quantitative researches also have an inexactitude or indeterminacy which is analogous to the probable error of quantitative determination. To this inexactitude, although it be not numerically expressed, the term probable error may be conveniently extended.

The doctrine of economy, in general, treats of the relations between utility and cost. That branch of it which relates to research considers the relations between the utility and the cost of diminutions of the probable error of our knowledge. Its main problem is how with a given expenditure of money, time, and energy, to obtain the most valuable [del]addition[/del] [ins]addition[/ins] to our knowledge.
