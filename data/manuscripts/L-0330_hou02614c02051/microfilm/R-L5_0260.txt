To the Commissioner of Patents:

[u]Be it known[/u] that I, Charles Sanders Peirce, a citizen of the United States, residing on my wife's farm of Arisbe, in Westfall township, Pike County, Pennsylvania, have invented a new and useful improvement in [u]Map Projections[/u], and desiring further to mature the same, file this my Caveat therefor and pray protection of my right until I shall have matured my invention.

The following is a description of my newly invented Map Projection, which is as full, clear, and exact as I am able at this time to give.

1. The object of this invention is to represent one surface, such as the surface of a part of the earth, upon another, such as a sheet of paper or of glass, in a systematic manner so that certain practical questions, such as how a given ship ought to steer at a given time, may be commodiously solved thereby.

2. This particular system of representation which constitutes my new invention I term the [u]Skew Mercator[/u], and would extend the same designation to any chart made according to this system.

3. [del]While applicable to any two surfaces whatever,[/del] I have specially developed [del]the system for the case of the representation of a part of an ellipsoid of revolution, with a compression of about [frac]1/300[/frac] upon a plane surface.[/del]
