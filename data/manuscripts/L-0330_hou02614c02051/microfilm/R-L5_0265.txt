In order to make the description more clear, it will be well briefly to state some facts of chemistry. When the solution of a chloride is subjected to electrolysis, chlorine and the metal are supposed to be first liberated; but what we observe is the formation of basic products at the [del]anode[/del] [ins]cathode[/ins], and [del]at[/del] [ins]about[/ins] the anode chlorine, hypochlorous acid, chloric acid, etc. in varying proportions. The less soluble the oxide of the metal the [del]more[/del] greater will be the proportion of chlorine formed. Thus, magnesia being insoluble, the chloride of magnesium subjected in solution to electrolysis yields chlorine hardly mixed with [del]any other[/del] chlorous compound; and for this reason is best chloride for electrolysis when we wish to obtain chlorine water.
