For the sake of clearness it is well to remark that[/del] geodetic lines (which are [del]the shortest lines that can be drawn upon the surfaces on which they are drawn between points upon taken not too far apart[/del]) do not generally return into themselves; and that in particular of the surface of an oblate ellipsoid of revolution, like the earth, no geodetic line returns into itself after passing over 360° of longitude, except a line lying in a plane perpendicular to, or including, the axis of revolution. Upon a sphere the geodetic lines are great circles and, as such, return into themselves; but a geodetic line on an ellipsoid, passes back and forth between the same latitude north and south, in [u]not precisely[/u] 360°, so that if that line be indefinitely prolonged it will cover the whole surface between the extreme latitudes with a grating of lines, its successive cycles.
