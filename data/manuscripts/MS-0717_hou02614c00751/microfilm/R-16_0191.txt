[del]Art.[/del] [ins]Art.[/ins] 5. [u]Division 4.[/u] The philosophical categories are
1. First.
2. Second.
3. Third.

[u]Definition 5.[/u] The category of [u]first[/u] is the conception of an object in its being, [del]as[/del] [ins]as[/ins] not having anything behind it.

[u]Definition 6.[/u] The category of [u]second[/u] is the conception of an object in its relation to another, in reaction with which its character lies.

[u]Definition 7.[/u] The category of [u]third[/u], is the conception of an object as medium between two others which it brings into connection with each other.

[u]Art. 6.[/u] [u]Illustration 1.[/u] Philosophy is a science of facts: its data are the general phenomena of experience. One of these data is that the ideas of [u]first[/u], [u]second[/u], [u]third[/u], are perpetually occurring together and so as to form a complete set. [del]A fond-[/del] A high esteem for triads among many communities. Every such triad is an express recognition of a special worth in the ideas of [u]first[/u], [u]second[/u], [u]third[/u]. Thus, as an anthropological fact, these ideas naturally tend to take a high place in the human mind. This, by itself, gives them importance. But further, those ideas to which the human mind naturally assigns a high place have, with few exceptions, turned out to be of real importance.
