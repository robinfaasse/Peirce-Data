[u]Art. 4[/u]. A triad is something more than a cogegeries of pairs. For example, A gives B to C. Here are three pairs: A parts with B, C receives B, A enriches C. But these three dual facts taken together do not make up the triple fact, which consist in this that A parts with B, C receives B, A enriches C, [u]all in one act[/u]. Take another illustration. But if there is a furcation of the line, so that it leads from A to B and C and from B to A and C, there is an essentially different feature. Thus, in triads we must expect to find peculiarities of which pairs give no hint.
