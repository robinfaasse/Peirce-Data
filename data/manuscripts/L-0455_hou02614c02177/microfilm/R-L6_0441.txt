embraces the theses of the 132
members of the graduating class of
1892, & describes their examination
by "visitors". One afternoon, while I
was in our "Court", a College
friend from the West, looked over
certain of the theses, &, as a result,
telegraphed an offer of a position
to one of the graduates whose thesis
struck him as very good.

While we do not print all our
theses, some of them are made
up into papers for the Proceedings
of the Am. Academy of Arts & Sciences,
for the Chemical Journal, for the

Institute of Mining Engineers, of Civil
Engineers, of Mechanical Engrs, etc,
& for our own Technology Quarterly.
Secty Nofman has promised to bring
me a few of the papers published
by the Chemical Dept., & when he
does, I will mail them to you.

Faithfully yours
F. A. Walker
