[u]Properties of Space[/u].

[u]First Property[/u]. Space has parts.
These are termed places.
[u]Logical Comment[/u].* "Space, says Dr. F. E. Abbot, is the Receptacle of
Things." Objects are in a certain relation to it, [del]that we need not analyze[/del] called being
contained in it. To say that it has parts is to say that there is a multitude of somethings
so called [del]which[/del] in any of which objects of some kind, — I do not confine myself to bodies, —
may also be contained. These parts are [u]in[/u] space; and some of them are [u]in[/u] [ins]each is in itself[/ins] others. Some
are not in some others. If A is [u]in[/u] B, and B [u]in[/u] C, then A is [u]in[/u] C.

[u]Second Property[/u]. Every place, except points, may be
cut up into places of the same kind.
Thus, parts of Space of the same kind as Space are called
spaces, or [u]solids[/u]: now every space may be cut up into spaces.
No matter how many subdivisions are made, places of the
same kind remain to be cut up. The dissection instead of
exhausting their number, only multiplies them; there is
