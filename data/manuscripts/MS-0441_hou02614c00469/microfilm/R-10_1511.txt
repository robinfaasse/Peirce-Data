I next proceed to form the [ins]3[u]rd[/u][/ins] Figure of probable reasoning. This
is derived from the first by interchanging the major premise and
conclusion, while denying both. You will remember that $r$ merely
means that the true ratio is in one or other of two parcels. Let $q$ denote
any ratio contained in the [del]other[/del] parcel to which $r$ does not belong.
Then the denial of the proposition
The proportion $r$ of [del]the[/del] [ins]the $S$s[/ins] [ins]{$M$s?}[/ins] have the character $\Pi$
will be
The proportion $q$ of the [ins]{$M$s?}[/ins] [del]the $S$s[/del] have the character $\Pi$.
The form of the [del]for[/del] denial is the same as that of the assertion. Hence,
the third figure will be

These $S$s are drawn at random from the $M$s;
Of these $S$s the proportion $q$ have the haphazard character $\Pi$;
$\therefore$ Probably (and approximately), the proportion $q$ of [del]the $M$s[/del] have the character $\Pi$.
