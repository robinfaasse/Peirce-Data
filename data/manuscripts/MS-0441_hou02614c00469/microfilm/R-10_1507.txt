Demonstrative inference is the limiting case of probable
inference. Certainty [u]pro[/u] is probability 1. Certainty [u]con[/u] is pro-
bability 0. Let us start, then, with probable syllogism in the
first figure. This runs as follows:

The proportion [u]r[/u] of the Ms possess [u]$\Pi$[/u] as a [del]haphazard[/del] character;
These Ss are [u]drawn at random[/u] from the Ms;
$\therefore$ [u]Probably and approximately, the proportion [u]r[/u] of the Ss possess [u]$\Pi$[/u].[/u]

Four explanatory remarks are here called for:

1[ins]st[/ins], the phrase "probably and approximately" in the conclusion,
means, loosely speaking, that the probability of the proportion ap-
proximating to the value [u]r[/u] is greater the wider the limits of approxima-
tion are chosen. Were the conditions of the premises [u]exactly fulfilled[/u], the
phrase "probably and approximately" would imply agreement with the law
of the probability curve. These conditions need not, however, be [u]exactly[/u]
fulfilled and that law has to be modified accordingly. I might occupy
a whole lecture on this point.

2[ins]nd[/ins], The proportion, [u]r[/u], has not necessarily any precise numerical
value. It may stand for "more than half," or for "nearly all or none"
or for "near any simple ratio," and so forth. In short, [del]we are[/del] [ins]one is[/ins] to
conceive that all possible values from 0 to 1 are distributed in any
way whatever into two parcels, and that the statement is that the value
of the ratio is contained in a specified parcel.
