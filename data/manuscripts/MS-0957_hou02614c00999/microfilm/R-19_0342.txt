[u]The second order.[/u]

It produced three profound and original systems of philosophy, those of Thomas Aquinas, Duns Scotus, and Ockham; as well as several others that are very interesting. These, in my judgment, [del]are[/del] were produced by agapastic action of the third kind.

But little by little scholasticism lost its earnestness, and slipped, by a tychastic process, into [del]a[/del] pedantry truly contemptible.

Meantime came printing, the renaissance, the discovery of America, the reformation. Here was a very complex movement of many elements acting in many ways. A little later came Copernicus. Then, Galileo and [del]Kepler[/del] Kepler. Then, Descartes. Then, Newton. [del]To discuss[/del] I intend to discuss the nature of the influences at work in all these movements in a separate volume. I may, however, say, in [del]general[/del] [ins]brief[/ins], that their great characteristic was originality.
