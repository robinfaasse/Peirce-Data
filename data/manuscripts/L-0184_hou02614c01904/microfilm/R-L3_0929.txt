Dear Sir,

Your note of the 11th in reply
to my criticism of the Pamphlet duly re-
ceived. As to their intrinsic value, of
course, there is no question and my write
to you was merely the expression of an o-
pinion, which may be a, mistaken one,
of an Assistant thoroughly acquainted
with the people living in the country
covered by the maps, and prompted solely
by a desire to have the reputation of our
work here, which stands very high, sus-
tained, and perhaps I did [ins]not[/ins] express my-
self as I intended, - curiosity and spring
into a pseudo captiously critical. I
thought the opinion of one so thoroughly
identified with the field work might per-
haps be acceptable. Surely had no in-
tention of dictating to the "office."
