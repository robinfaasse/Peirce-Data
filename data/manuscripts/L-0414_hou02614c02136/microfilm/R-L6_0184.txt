Mr. Herbert Spencer, regrets that he must take
measures for diminishing the amount of his correspondence.

Being prevented by his state of health from writing more
than a short time daily, he progresses but slowly with the work he
has undertaken, and his progress is made slower by absorption of
his time in answering those who write to him. Letters inviting him to
join Committees, to attend Meetings, or otherwise to further some public
object, letters requesting interviews and autographs, letters asking
opinions and explanations, these, together with acknowledgements of
presentation copies of books, entail hindrances which, though trivial
individually, are collectively serious - serious, at least, to one whose
hours of work are so narrowly limited.

As these hindrances increase, Mr. Spencer is compelled
to do something to prevent them. After long hesitation he has
decided to cut himself off from every engagement that is likely to
occupy attention, however slight, and to decline all correspondence
not involved by his immediate work.
