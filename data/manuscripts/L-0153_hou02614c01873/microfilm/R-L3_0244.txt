EDITORIAL ROOMS OF THE STANDARD DICTIONARY.

New York, [del]March 27, '93.[/del] 1892.

Professor C. S. Peirce,
Milford, Pa.

Dear Sir;

Your favor of March 23rd at hand. There are a number of terms in your list that are left blank, to which our Dr. Funk called your attention at the time you left the MS., e.g., [del]ash,[/del] azure, Bismarck, bronze, chestnut, china blue, citrine, claret, coral red, navy black, oil yellow, old gold, parrot green, phlox purple, etc. His understanding was that you were to fill out the formulas for these colors. He has no remembrance of your ever saying anything about the minus sign in any previous talk that he had with you. The first he observed this was when he saw the signs in the [del]list.[/del] [ins]sample of list. He called attention to it at once, and you replied on May 8th, '92,[/ins] The suggestion of a standard color table, however, was his own, not yours. He suggested it to you when you came into the office. It was in his mind to have such a table long before you called at the office. The question in his mind was as to how it should be made, and it was in reference to that he consulted with you.

It is not worth while to have any further discussion of this matter. We have paid you in full for what you have done. For whatever portion of your work we are able to use, if any, we shall be glad to give you credit. It is our rule not to hold anyone responsible for any work that he did not do. Hence, we shall drop your name from all future editions of our prospectus as the responsible editor of the color department.

As to criticism of the Dictionary, we would be glad to have you make the most critical examination of the pages of the Dictionary and return them to us with your criticisms. If you are willing to do this, we shall send you plate proofs for this purpose and pay you at the rate of $1.50 per hour up to the limit of 30 hours, after which time we may deem it to our advantage to extend the arrangement. Be so kind as to write us whether you will be willing to do this. If so, we shall send on the proofs for your general criticism. Do not do any work upon the proofs already sent you, for they were not the revised proofs, having to pass through the hands of some 200 specialists and our expert proofreaders here before ready for casting.

Yours respectfully, Funk & Wagnalls Co
