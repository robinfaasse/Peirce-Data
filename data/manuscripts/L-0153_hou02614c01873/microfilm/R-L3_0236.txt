PUBLICATION HOUSE OF
FUNK & WAGNALLS COMPANY,
18 AND 20 ASTOR PLACE.

EDITORIAL ROOMS OF THE STANDARD DICTIONARY.

NEW YORK, [date]June 3rd,[/date] 1892.

Professor Charles S. Peirce,
12 West 39th St., N. Y. City.

Dear Sir;

Our Dr. Funk has reported to our Executive Committee your proposition for a steady engagement upon our Dictionary. There is a point about our arrangement with you for a table of colors that we do not quite understand. It was understood that the table was to be a practical one for the Dictionary, that is, one that would be of service to those who consult [ins]a dictionary;[/ins] but we find that in the condition in which it now is [ins]it would be of no service.[/ins] We have submitted it to a number of persons, some of them experts, and not one, without additional verbal explanation by ourselves, is able to understand it. Published in the shape it now is, it will be of no service. It should be perfected, that is, made practicable. One fatal mistake in the table, for practical purposes, is the minus terms.

We have been lead to believe by an examination of this work that you are so technical and scientific as to be out of the reach of the average consulter of a Dictionary. Are we not right in asking that you put this table in a shape that is available for us? We will then be able to intelligently consider propositions for further services. We note a number of important terms [ins]in[/ins] [del]for[/del] your table that are not treated at all, as ash, azure, old gold, etc. Such a table, to be valuable, must be a very plain one, and must be absolutely accurate and sufficiently comprehensive.

Yours very respectfully,

Funk & Wagnalls Co.
H.
