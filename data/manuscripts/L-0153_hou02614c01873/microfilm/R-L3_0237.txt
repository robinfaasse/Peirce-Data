EDITORIAL ROOMS OF THE STANDARD DICTIONARY.

NEW YORK, [date]July 6th,[/date] 1892.

My Dear Professor Peirce:

Upon my return from the West, I find your letter of June 4th to Messrs. Funk & Wagnalls. Speaking of the elimination of the minus signs in the table, you say, "At the same time, your experts who cannot understand a mode of expression, etc., . . . . prove their disgraceful ignorance." The point was not whether our experts understood the minus signs, but whether a table prepared that way would be serviceable to a sufficiently large number of persons who consult a Dictionary. I am striving to make a Dictionary that the average man can readily understand. My test of a definition or of a table is, [u]Is it comprehended by average intelligence?[/u]

You say that the form of the table used "was expressly agreed to by Dr. Funk, though you called attention to the fact that there would be minus signs." You will remember that I asked you whether it was possible to make a table that would be exact and serviceable. I told you the result I wished to secure. I did not dictate as to the method of securing that result---I make no claim to be a specialist in colors. I paid you for the table without examining it, having supreme confidence in your expert knowledge as well as in you. It may be that the table is serviceable to average intelligence. I am asking you to make this clear. What I wish to know is, suppose there was a case in Court which would turn upon the reproduction of a certain color. A standard would be necessary in order to determine this question. Now, would your table furnish the standard? If so, how would the Court discover the shade you design? Are there color disks in the market that can be readily used in a way that [u]will interpret your table?[/u] If there are not such disks in the market, can they be placed in the market? If they are in the market, where can they be obtained? Of course, I know that there are some kinds of color disks in the market.

We shall be very glad to arrange with you for further work, provided your work can be made to conform to our fundamental rules, one of which is that all definitions must be within the grasp of average intelligence.
