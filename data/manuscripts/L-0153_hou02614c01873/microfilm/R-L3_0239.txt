12 W 38th St. [date]1892 July 12[/date]

My dear Dr. Funk:

It strikes me that you must have lost the enclosures of my letter, as your answer is not at all to the point. Have you lost them?

You say my remark about your charlatan "experts" [ins]on color[/ins] is not to the point. But it was [u]you[/u], not I, who spoke of their opinions as of any account! (Your other experts may be all right.)

Your rule that every definition must be intelligible to the average capacity, — by which you evidently mean the average man in his average state of uneducatedness, — is a miserable one. To see how it works in practice, I turn to your sample pages. I find the [u]Absolute[/u], in the mathematical sense, defined thus:

"A geometric conception proposed by Cayley to show the relation of projective and metric geometry."

Now this conveys no definite idea to the educated nor to the uneducated. True, a man without a mathematical education cannot grasp the idea, and therefore no correct definition can be made intelligible to him.
