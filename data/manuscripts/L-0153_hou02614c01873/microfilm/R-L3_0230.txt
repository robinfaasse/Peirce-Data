18 AND 20 ASTOR PLACE.

EDITORIAL ROOMS OF THE STANDARD DICTIONARY.

NEW YORK, [date]May 11th, 1891.[/date]

Professor C. S. Peirce,
12 West 39th St., New York City.

Dear Sir;

There is still a point about your table which we do not understand. We of course understand your equation. You say 100 Amaranthine plus 20 V.B. is equal to 50 R plus 4 G plus 66 U. But how is one from this formula to make an amaranthine color? If he combines 50 R, 4 G and 66 U, he will have 100 Amaranthine plus 20 V. B., but how is he to get rid of his 20 V.B.? The explanation should be fuller. Is there no way by which the mixture can be effected except by disks or by a color box? That is, cannot a formula be given which a painter can follow. If you were to tell a painter or a chemist to mix so many parts of R., so many parts of G. and so many parts of U., and he would thereby have such a color, he would understand that. That is, is it not possible to give a method by which this can be done independent of disks and color boxes? If it must be done by disks or color boxes, the explanation should be fuller. Please let us have what is necessary to make the table fully intelligible and practicable.

We have under consideration your suggestion for further work along colors and in other departments of the Dictionary. It will be well for you to do nothing further along these lines until we have a definite contract.

Yours very respectfully,

Funk & Wagnalls Co.,
H.
