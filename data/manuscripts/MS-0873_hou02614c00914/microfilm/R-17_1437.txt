makes us think that it is perhaps a fact.

It need not be a fact. Until it has been systematically tested,
it remains a mere guess. Experience shows, however, that skillful
guesses have a tolerably fair chance of being true.

Should it be a matter of any importance to explain a given
phenomenon, and should we be able to think of but one possible
way of explaining that phenomenon, we adopt the hypothesis on
probation. Our proper course then will be to endeavor to
base upon the hypothesis a [ins]considerable[/ins] number of positive predictions
of such a nature that they can be tested by experiment.
If the predictions are verified, we ought to go on to make fresh
ones; for there is no such thing as a final, definitive, and
