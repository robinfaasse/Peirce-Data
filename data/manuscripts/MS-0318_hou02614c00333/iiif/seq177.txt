Note. The Census Theorem Enunciated.

The demonstration is very easy, and may be omitted.

A [u]point[/u] is an indivisible place[ins]. Its dimensionality is zero.[/ins]. A movable thing that at each and every instant [del]occupies[/del] [ins]occupies[/ins] a point only may be termed a [u]particle[/u]. If it moves, it will in the course of a lapse of time have occupied a place called a [u]line[/u], whose dimensionality is 1. A movable thing that at each and every instant occupies a line and nothing more may be termed a [u]filament[/u]. If it moves, it will in the course of a lapse of time occupy a place called a [u]surface[/u], whose dimensionality is 2. [fig]ink blot[/fig] A movable thing that at each and every instant precisely fills a surface, [del]may[/del] be termed a [u]film[/u]. If it moves it will in the course of a lapse of time occupy a place called a [u]solid[/u], whose dimensionality is 3. Ordinary space has a dimensionality of 3. Higher places and movables are best named by their dimensionalities.
