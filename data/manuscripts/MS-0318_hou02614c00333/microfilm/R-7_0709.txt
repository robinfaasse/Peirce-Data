[pn](Prag. 20)[/pn] Let us, then, examine into the mental circumstances
under which a sign first introduces its significant character
to our notice, as well as into those under which it, at length,
ceases to live as one of our signs. Beginning with the former
question, if, before it had ever suggested itself to my mind,
someone had asked me to guess in how many unrelated
ways a sign could first come to be recognized as such, I
should hardly have hit [ins]at once,[/ins] upon the right answer, which
is, By one only. The truth of the answer, however, becomes
obvious as soon as that way is specified; namely, By in-
ference. For [del]a[/del] decidedly significance can neither be
