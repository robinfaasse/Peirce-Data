53[/pn] The only way of attaining any satisfactory general knowledge of [del]positive fact[/del] [ins]experiential truth[/ins] is by the inductive testing of theories. This is, therefore, the only way to ascertain the meaning of [del]an[/del] [ins]a[/ins] current concept; and there are few fields of inquiry to which that method adapts itself so readily. [del]One[/del] [ins]Remembering that a concept has meaning only as it is predicated of objects to which would be applicable always seem desirable, one[/ins] need only create in the imagination as great a variety of [del]examples[/del] [ins]of the supposed meaning[/ins] and then [del]as[/del] try whether or not the concept would be applicable to each of them. One thus pursues a strict experimental method. It may turn out that the meaning lies in a feeling, or in some single thing or event.
