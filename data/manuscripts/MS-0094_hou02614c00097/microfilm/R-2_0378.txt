[u]Equal Angles of the Isosceles Triangle.[/u]

Let the line [u]BD[/u] be drawn so as to bisect the angle [u]ABC[/u].
Then the two triangles [u]ADB[/u] and [u]DBC[/u] will be equal, since they have two sides [u]AB[/u], [u]BD[/u], and the included angle [u]ABD[/u], respectively equal to the two sides [u]BC[/u], [u]BD[/u], and the included angle [u]DBC[/u]; and the angle [u]A[/u] will be equal to [u]C[/u].

[del]56.[/del] Corollary [ins]57.[/ins] An equilateral triangle is also equiangular.

[del]57.[/del] Theorem [ins]74.[/ins] The line [u]BD[/u] (fig. 32), which bisects the angle [u]B[/u], at the vertex of an isosceles triangle, is perpendicular to the base, and bisects the base.

[u]Proof[/u]. a. For, on account of the equality of the triangles [u]ABD[/u] and [u]BCD[/u], [u]AD[/u] must be equal to [u]DC[/u].
b. Moreover, the angles [u]BDA[/u] and [u]BDC[/u] are equal, and are therefore right angles by the very definition of the right angle [del]page 2[/del].

[del]58.[/del] Theorem. If, in a triangle, two angles are equal, the opposite sides are also equal, and the triangle is isosceles.

[u]Proof[/u]. In the triangle [u]ABC[/u] (fig. 32), let the angle [u]A[/u] be equal to the angle [u]C[/u].
Invert the triangle, and place it in the position [u]BCA[/u]; and, as the two triangles [u]ABC[/u] and [u]CBA[/u] have the side [u]AC[/u] and the adjacent angles [u]A[/u] and [u]C[/u] of the one respectively equal to [u]CA[/u] and the adjacent angles [u]C[/u] and [u]A[/u] of the other, their other sides must be equal, or [u]BC[/u] must be equal to [u]BA[/u].

[del]59.[/del] Corollary [ins]58.[/ins] An equiangular triangle is also equilateral.
