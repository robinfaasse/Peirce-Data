<u>Scholium</u>. The motion just described may be, for the
present, called quasi-translation. Like rotation it is either
elliptic, hyperbolic, or parabolic.

Elliptic quasi-translation returns into itself, the whole axis
being described in a finite number of displacements.

In hyperbolic [ins]quasi-[/ins]translation, the axis has two parts, and
the particles move toward one point on it and away from
another, or the motion being reversed [del]for[/del] toward the former
and away from the latter.

In parabolic translation, one [del]point[/del] [ins]particle[/ins] on the axis remains fixed,
[del]this is the[/del] one point at an infinite distance. This is ordinary trans-
lation, and the great peculiarity is that all the particles of the
body describe straight lines. This is impossible for any kind
of motion except one which has the characteristic properties of
the ideal bodies we term "rigid."
