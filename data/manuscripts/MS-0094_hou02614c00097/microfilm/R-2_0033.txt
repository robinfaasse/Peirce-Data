[u]Definition 28[/u] (Of the connection of the parts of time)

The following statements give a logical analysis of the mathematical properties of time:

1. Of any two instants, one is later, the other earlier.

2. No instant is later than itself.

3. If one instant, A, is later than another, B, itself later than a third, C, then A is later than C.

4. Given any two instants, I the earlier, J the later, there are always instants later than I [del]while[/del] [ins]but[/ins] earlier than J.

5. In each moment while anything is changing a rate of change exists.

6. Observation leads us to suppose that changing things tend toward a state in the immeasurably distant future different from the state of things in the immeasurably distant past.

The above defines the geometer's idea of the connection of the parts of time. No 5. implies that [del]the time is a[/del] multitude of instants, or [del]an instant is the limit of[/del] of an endless series of times is an instant.
