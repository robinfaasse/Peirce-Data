Motion through any singly continuous multitude of places may be performed in two orders, [ins]the[/ins] one the reverse of the other. For such a multitude has a serial order, and every serial order has a reverse order. If, therefore, motion could take place in one order and not in the other, it would be owing to the relation of one [del]point[/del] [ins]place[/ins], A, to another, B, being intrinsically different from the relation of B to A. But by the constitution of space no such difference exists in the case of points. Nor, if the places are lines or surfaces, is there anything in the mode of connection of the points which directly generate the lines and indirectly the surfaces which establishes such a difference. In fact, such a difference could not be [del]ge[/del] [ins]a[/ins] geometrical [ins]one; for it would consist in a peculiarity in the relation of the places to the course of time. Now geometry has nothing to do with time, except as a convenient aid to reasoning.[/ins] [del]one; for in that case it would have to depend upon some difference between the points.[/del]
