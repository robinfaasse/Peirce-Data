[u]Subchapter 3. Polygons and Polyhedra.[/u]

§40.
[u]Definition 110.[/u] A [u]polygon[/u][ins], in the topological sense,[/ins] is a surface bounded by a succession of limited lines [del]with with marked points[/del] called its [u]sides[/u], each side confine to the next with points called [u]vertices[/u] between every two successive sides, and bounding them. In Graphics and in Metrics, [u]polygon[/u] has [del]a[/del] modified meanings.

[del][u]Polygons.[/u][/del]

[marginalia]without furcations, not furcated.[/marginalia]

[del]Definition 28.[/del]
[del]A[/del] [u]polygon[/u] is a surface bounded by [ins]limited[/ins] [del]simple[/del] lines.

[del]Definition 29.[/del]
The bounding lines are called the [u]sides[/u] of the polygon.

[del]Definition 30.[/del]
The bounding points [del]between[/del] [ins]of[/ins] the sides, between every pair of successive sides, are called the [u]vertices[/u]* of the polygon.
[[ins]*[/ins]Singular: [u]vertex[/u].]

[u]Scholium.[/u] The word [u]polygon[/u] is shortened from the Greek word of the same meaning polygonon (πολύγωνον), an adjective used for a noun in the usual Greek way, and compounded in Greek style from polys (πολύς), [u]many[/u], and gonia (γωνία), [u]angle[/u], so that it means having many angles, or vertices. The first two syllables may (as with other words in [u]poly[/u]) be replaced by a Greek numeral to show just how many vertices the polygon spoken of possesses.
