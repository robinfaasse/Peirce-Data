To find the Ratio of two Lines.

the segment sought would be a semicircle described upon the diameter [u]AB[/u].

156. [u]Problem.[/u] To find a common measure of two given straight lines, [u]AB[/u], [u]CD[/u] (fig. 87), in order to express their ratio in numbers.

[u]Solution.[/u] a. The method of finding the common divisor is the same as that given in arithmetic for two numbers. Apply the smaller [u]CD[/u] to the greater [u]AB[/u], as many times as it will admit of; for example, twice with a remainder [u]BE[/u].

Apply the remainder [u]BE[/u] to the line [u]CD[/u], as many times as it will admit of; twice, for example, with a remainder [u]DF[/u].

Apply the second remainder [u]DF[/u] to the first [u]BE[/u], as many times as it will admit of; once, for example, with a remainder [u]BG[/u].

Apply the third remainder [u]BG[/u] to the second [u]DF[/u], as many times as it will admit of.

Proceed thus till a remainder arises, which is exactly contained a certain number of times in the preceding.

This last remainder is a common measure of the two proposed lines; and, by regarding it as unity, the values of the preceding remainders are easily found, and, at length, those of the proposed lines from which their ratio in numbers is deduced.

If, for example, we find that [u]GB[/u] is contained exactly three times in [u]FD[/u], [u]GB[/u] is a common measure of the two proposed lines.

b. Let [u]GB[/u] = 1;

and we have

[u]FD[/u] = 3 [u]GB[/u] = 3,
[u]EB[/u] = 1. [u]FD[/u] + [u]GB[/u] = 3 + 1 = 4,
[u]CD[/u] = 2. [u]EB[/u] + [u]FD[/u] = 8 + 3 = 11,
[u]AB[/u] = 2. [u]CD[/u] + [u]EB[/u] = 22 + 4 = 26;
