Now these two are equal. Summing the two

$$\left\{ \begin{aligned}
&\text{Number of reversals} \times \text{O} \\
&+ \text{Number reentrant angles} \times \text{O} \\
&- \text{Sum reentrant angles}
\end{aligned} \right. \quad \left\{ \begin{aligned}
&\text{Number interior angles} \times \text{O} \\
&- \text{Sum interior angles}
\end{aligned} \right.$$

That is the [del]sum of[/del] [ins]excess of[/ins] the sum of interior angles over
the sum of reentrant angles is as many times
a circuit as the number of interior angles exceeds
the [del]numbers[/del] [ins]total of the[/ins] [ins]numbers[/ins] of reentrant angles and reversals.

<u>Corollary</u> Since the number of sides equals the
number of angles, this may be expressed as follows,
(calling two reversals a revolution)
The sum of the interior angles increased by two
circuits for every revolution and for every reentrant
angle equals the sum of the reentrant angles
increased by a circuit for every side.
