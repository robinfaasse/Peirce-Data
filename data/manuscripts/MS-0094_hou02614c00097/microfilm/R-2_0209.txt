§ 109. What is the freedom of a place, A, which is restricted to containing, in common with a fixed place, B, a sort of place, C? Begin with the freedom of A. This is equal to the freedom of the number of points required to fix A. But suppose [del]it[/del] [ins]A[/ins] is required to contain a fixed point; then, its freedom is plainly that of one less than that number of points; and so if it is required to contain any number of fixed points. Suppose, however, that the [del]number of[/del] points A is restricted to containing are not absolutely fixed; then, their freedom must be added to get that of A. If in place of being restricted to containing points, A is restricted to containing some other sort of place whose freedom is equivalent to that of a number of points, it is all the same. Hence the answer to the question is [del]that it[/del] as follows:

<u>Theorem 14</u>. The freedom of a place, A, restricted to containing in common with a fixed place, B, a sort of place C, is equal to the freedom [del]in B[/del] of the number of points requisite to fix the position of C [ins]in B[/ins], added to the freedom in space of the remainder of the points required to fix A, less their freedom in A.
