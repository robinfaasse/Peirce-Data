§12 Scholium. When an [del]observer[/del] spectator stands upon the sea-shore with his eye about 12 feet above the sea-level, the line between sea and sky, owing to the combined effects of the earth's curvature and the refraction of light, appears lower than his eye by the breadth of a line [fraction]1/100[/fraction] inch thick viewed at a distance of ten inches. And this [del]will be[/del] which is called the [u]dip of the horizon[/u] will be more, [ins]if the[/ins] [del]in pro[/del] spectator's eye is higher, in the proportion of the square root of that height.

If there were no refraction and the earth's [del]is[/del] surface, instead of being curved, were plane, there would [ins]practically[/ins] be a similar line between sea and sky, only a trifle higher. Whether if vision were perfect, and no objects intervened, such a line would exist, we will not here inquire. Suffice it that for pictorial purposes it may be assumed that there would be such a line at the height of the eye.
