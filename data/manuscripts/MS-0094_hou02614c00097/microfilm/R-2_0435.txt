CH. XI. § 196.] SIMILAR POLYGONS

[u]Equilateral similar Polygons are equal.[/u]

[u]Solution.[/u] Join [u]AC[/u], [u]AD[/u], &c. Draw [u]A'C'[/u], [u]A'D'[/u], &c.,
making the angles [u]B'A'C'[/u] = [u]BAC[/u], [u]C'A'D'[/u] = [u]CAD[/u], &c.

Draw [u]B'C'[/u], making the angle [u]A B'C'[/u] = [u]ABC[/u], and
meeting [u]A'C'[/u] at [u]C'[/u]. Draw [u]C'D'[/u], making the angle [u]A'C'D'[/u]
= [u]ACD[/u], and meeting [u]A'D'[/u] at [u]D'[/u]; and so on.

The polygon [u]A'B'C'D'[/u] &c. thus constructed, is the re-
quired polygon.

[u]Proof.[/u] For, by § 173, the successive triangles [u]A'B'C'[/u],
[u]A'C'D'[/u], &c. are similar to [u]ABC[/u], [u]ACD[/u], &c. each to each,
and therefore, by the preceding theorem, the polygons
are similar.

195. [u]Theorem.[/u] If the similar polygons [u]ABCD[/u] &c.
[u]A'B'C'D'[/u] &c. (fig. 109) have a side [u]AB[/u] of the one
equal to the homologous side [u]A'B'[/u] of the other, the
polygons are equal.

[u]Proof.[/u] The polygons are, by § 170, equiangular;
they are also equilateral, for, by § 170, the ratio of [u]BC[/u]
to [u]B'C'[/u] is the same as that of [u]AB[/u] to [u]A'B'[/u], or the ratio of
equality; that is, [u]BC[/u] = [u]B'C'[/u], and, in the same way
[u]CD[/u] = [u]C'D'[/u], &c.

If, then, [u]A'B'[/u] is placed upon [u]AB[/u], [u]B'C'[/u] will take the di-
rection of [u]BC[/u], because the angle [u]B[/u] = [u]B'[/u]; and [u]C'[/u] will
fall upon [u]C[/u], because [u]B'C'[/u] = [u]BC[/u]; and in the same way
it may be shown, that [u]D'[/u] falls upon [u]D[/u], [u]E'[/u] upon [u]E[/u], &c;
so that the polygons coincide, and are equal
