[u]Theorem 30[/u]. An optical metron has six degrees of freedom.

[u]Demonstration[/u]. If two [del]points in[/del] particles of the metron [ins]remain at[/ins] [del]are[/del] fixed points, it can still turn round the ray through them. But if a third particle out of that ray be fixed it can no longer move, because then the flat lamina through those particles must remain in [del]that[/del] [ins]the[/ins] plane of the three points. Now, each particle if free can move in three ways, because space has three dimensions. But the fixing of the first particle prevents the second from moving in one way, namely along the ray through the two. Thus the second particle is only free to move two ways. The fixing of the second prevents the third from moving on [del]either of[/del] the rays through it and [ins]one of[/ins] the two others. Thus, it only has one way to move. Thus, the metron can move in 3, and 2, and 1 ways.
