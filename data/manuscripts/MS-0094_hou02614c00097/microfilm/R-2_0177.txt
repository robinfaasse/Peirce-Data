These innumerable multitudes of systems of surfaces have their surfaces differently shaped, but all their properties are exactly analogous. It is, therefore, impossible to find any [ins]intrinsic[/ins] property which distinguishes one from another. Some shapes appear to us simpler than others; but that is only a peculiarity of our minds, not of the objects; for [del]them[/del] [ins]there[/ins] is no geometrical property which distinguishes one from another. Indeed, they have no properties except such as are deducible from the eight just enumerated together with the general properties of space. Nevertheless, they are geometrically distinguishable by their relations to one another. It is, therefore, desirable to select one system to which the others may be referred and in terms of which they can be described.
