§ 14. [del]Mathematicians[/del] [ins]Geometers[/ins] find them forced by considering space as continuous, to regard it as returning into itself. That is to say a particle travelling along any path which goes off toward the north pole of the heavens would come back from the south pole, — we do not say in any finite lapse of time, we do not say it would do so without the velocity were more than infinite. We are not saying anything about the measures. By one system of measures that is finite which by another system is infinite. We simply say continuity forces us to consider every regular line [del]and [ins]which[/ins] [del]returns[/del] [ins]runs[/ins] [del]into itself[/del][/del] [ins]off[/ins] to opposite points of the heavens as returning into itself.

There are various ways of showing this. They will none of them appear convincing at first; for one has to acquire the faculty of thinking rightly about matters so unfamiliar.
