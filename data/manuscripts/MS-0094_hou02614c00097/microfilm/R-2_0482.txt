[u]Sum of the Sides of a Spherical Polygon.[/u]

[u]Proof.[/u] From the centre [u]O[/u] (fig. 180) of the sphere
draw the radii [u]OA[/u], [u]OB[/u], [u]OC[/u], &c. to the vertices [u]A[/u], [u]B[/u], [u]C[/u],
&c. of the spherical polygon [u]ABC[/u] &c. The plane an-
gles [u]AOB[/u], [u]BOC[/u], &c. form a solid angle at [u]O[/u]; and the
sum of these angles is, by § 339, less than four right
angles. The sum of the arcs [u]AB[/u], [u]BC[/u], [u]CD[/u], &c. is, con-
sequently, less than a circumference of a great circle.

440. Corollary. If then, we denote the sides of a
spherical triangle by [u]a[/u], [u]b[/u], [u]c[/u], we have
[u]a[/u] + [u]b[/u] + [u]c[/u] < 360°.

441. Theorem. The angle formed by two arcs of
great circles is measured by the arc described from its
vertex as a pole, and included between its sides.

[u]Proof.[/u] The arc [u]AM[/u] (fig. 177) measures the angle
[u]ACM[/u], which, by § 315, measures the angle of the planes
[u]DCA[/u] and [u]DCM[/u]; and therefore, by § 430, it measures
the angle [u]ADM[/u].

442. Corollary. The value of the arc [u]AM[/u] expressed
in degrees, minutes, &c., is the same as that of [u]ADM[/u].

443. Theorem. If from the vertices of a given
spherical triangle as poles, arcs of great circles are
described, another triangle is formed, the vertices of
which are the poles of the sides of the given triangle.

[u]Proof.[/u] Let [u]ABC[/u] (fig. 181) be the given triangle; let
[u]EF[/u], [u]DF[/u], and [u]DE[/u] be described, respectively, with [u]A[/u], [u]B[/u], [u]C[/u]
as poles.

Then, since [u]E[/u] is in the arc [u]EF[/u], the distance from [u]E[/u] to
[u]A[/u] is, by § 425, a quadrant; and since [u]E[/u] is in the arc
[u]DE[/u], the distance from [u]E[/u] to [u]C[/u] is also a quadrant; and,
therefore, by § 427, [u]E[/u] is a pole of [u]AC[/u].

In the same way it may be shown, that [u]D[/u] is a pole of
[u]BC[/u], and [u]F[/u] a pole of [u]AB[/u].
