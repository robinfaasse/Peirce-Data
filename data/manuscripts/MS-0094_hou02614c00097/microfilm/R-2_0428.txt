[pn]50[/pn] PLANE GEOMETRY. [CH. XI. § 175.

Similar Polygons and Arcs. Equiangular triangles are similar.

171. Definitions. The altitude of a parallelogram is the perpendicular, which measures the distance between its opposite sides considered as bases.

The altitude of a triangle is the perpendicular, as [u]AD[/u] (fig. 96), which measures the distance of any one of its vertices, as [u]A[/u], from the opposite side [u]BC[/u] taken as a base.

The altitude of a trapezoid is the perpendicular, as [u]EF[/u] (fig. 97), drawn between its two parallel sides.

172. Theorem. Two triangles [u]ABC[/u], [u]DEF[/u] (fig. 98), which are equiangular with respect to each other, are similar.

Proof. Place the angle [u]D[/u] upon its equal [u]A[/u]; [u]E[/u] must fall upon [u]E'[/u], and [u]F[/u] upon [u]F'[/u]; and [u]F'E'[/u] is parallel to [u]BC[/u], because the angles [u]AE'F'[/u] and [u]ACB[/u] are equal. Hence, by § 160,

[u]AE'[/u] : [u]AC[/u] = [u]AF'[/u] : [u]AB[/u],

that is,

[u]DE[/u] : [u]AC[/u] = [u]DF[/u] : [u]AB[/u].

In the same way, it may be proved that

[u]DE[/u] : [u]AC[/u] = [u]EF[/u] : [u]BC[/u] = [u]DF[/u] : [u]AB[/u].

173. Corollary. Hence, and from § 67, it follows that two triangles are similar, when they have two angles of the one respectively equal to two angles of the other.

174. Corollary. Two right triangles are similar, when they have an acute angle of the one equal to an acute angle of the other.
