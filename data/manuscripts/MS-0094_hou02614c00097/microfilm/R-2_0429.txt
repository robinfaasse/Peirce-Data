176. [u]Corollary.[/u] The parallel sides are homologous.

177. [u]Theorem.[/u] Two triangles are similar, when the sides of the one are equally inclined to those of the other, each to each, as [u]ABC[/u], [u]DEF[/u] (fig. 99).

[u]Proof.[/u] For if one of the triangles is turned around, by a quantity equal to the angle made by the sides of the one with those of the other, the sides of the two triangles become respectively parallel, and they are, therefore, by § 175, equiangular and similar.

178. [u]Corollary.[/u] Two triangles are similar, when the sides of the one are respectively perpendicular to those of the other, and the perpendicular sides are homologous.

179. [u]Theorem.[/u] Two triangles [u]ABC[/u], [u]DEF[/u] (fig. 98) are similar, if they have an angle [u]A[/u] of the one equal to an angle [u]D[/u] of the other, and the sides including these angles proportional, that is,
[u]AB[/u] : [u]DF[/u] = [u]AC[/u] : [u]DE[/u].

[u]Proof.[/u] Place the angle [u]D[/u] upon [u]A[/u]; [u]E[/u] falls upon [u]E'[/u], and [u]F[/u] upon [u]F'[/u]; and [u]E'F'[/u] is parallel to BC, by § 162, because
[u]AB[/u] : [u]AF'[/u] = [u]AC[/u] : [u]AE'[/u].
Hence, by § 30, the angle [u]C[/u] = [u]AE'F'[/u] = [u]E[/u],
and [u]B[/u] = [u]AF'E'[/u] = [u]F[/u];
that is, the triangles [u]ABC[/u] and [u]DEF[/u] are equiangular, and, by § 172, similar.
