[u]Chapter III. Degrees of Freedom.[/u]

§105. [u]Scholium[/u]. A remark is called for respecting a form of language. It is usual with geometers to speak of places as moving, — a [u]line[/u] is said move so as to generate a [u]surface[/u], which surface may again be considered to move, so as to generate a [u]solid[/u]. Such expressions are unobjectionable. Nevertheless, in this treatise, they are, for the sake of those who have little aptitude for mathematics rather avoided. Instead of saying a [u]line[/u] moves, it is considered a little clearer to say that a [u]fibre[/u], which at any one instant occupies a line, moves. The idea is exactly the same; but a moving line is called a [u]fibre[/u], and a moving surface a [u]lamina[/u], and a moving point a [u]particle[/u], in cases where the association of ideas seems to render these forms of expression more suggestive. This departure from the usage of geometers [del]is[/del] [ins]is[/ins] only worth mention for the sake of remarking that [del]in the present[/del] [ins]in the present[/ins] chapter it is deemed best to put it aside, and to speak, as geometers usually do, of moving points, lines, and surfaces.
