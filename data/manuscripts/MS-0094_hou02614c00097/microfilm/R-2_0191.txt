[u]The Leading Classes of Figures.[/u]

§ 99. Other figures result from compounding points, planes,
and rays. The principal kinds will here be sufficiently
described to give an idea of their general nature.

Configurations.

Definition 140.
A figure formed of a finite [ins]collection[/ins] [del]number[/del] of elements of
different kinds is called a [ins]tame, or[/ins] configuration.
[del]A regular figure is one every part of which is governed by the same law, so that
from any fraction of it the whole could be constructed by carrying out the same law.[/del]

A plane configuration is a configuration of points
and rays all lying in one plane.

A spacious configuration is a configuration of points,
planes, and rays [del]all[/del].

A [del]regular[/del] [ins]homogeneous[/ins] plane configuration is a plane configuration in
which [del]the number of rays is equal to the number of points,
and[/del] all the [del]rays[/del] [ins]points[/ins] have the same number of rays through
them, and all the rays have the same number of points on
them. The rays may happen to intersect at points that
are not counted as points of the configuration.
