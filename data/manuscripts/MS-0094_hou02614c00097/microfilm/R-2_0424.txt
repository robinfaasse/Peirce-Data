[pn]46[/pn] PLANE GEOMETRY. [del][CH. IX. § 162.[/del]

[u]A line drawn Parallel to a Side of a Triangle.[/u]

[del]Solution. Suppose the number of parts is, for example,
six. Draw the indefinite line AC, take AC of any con-
venient length, apply it six times to AD. Join and the
last point of division D by the line BD, draw parallel
to DB, AE, being applied six times B, divides
into six equal parts.[/del]

[del]Proof. For if, through points of division of AD, lines
are drawn parallel to DB, they must, by the preceding
theorem, divide AB into six equal parts, of which AE is
one.[/del]

[del]160.[/del] Theorem. [del]If a line DE (fig. 90) is drawn[/del] [ins]A line[/ins] through
two sides [del]AC[/del] [ins]AB[/ins] of a triangle [del]ABC[/del] parallel to the
third side [del]BC[/del], it divides those two sides proportionally,
[del]so that we have[/del]

[del]AD : DB = AE : AC.[/del]

[u]Proof.[/u] Suppose, for example, the ratio of [del]AD : AB[/del] [ins]AD to DB[/ins]
to be as 4 to 7. [del]AB[/del] [ins]DB[/ins] may then be divided into 7 equal
parts [del]Aa, ab, bc, &c.[/del] [ins]of which AD contains 4;[/ins] and if
lines [del]aa', bb', cc', &c.[/del] are drawn parallel to BC, [del]AC[/del] [ins]EC[/ins] is
divided into 7 equal parts [del]Aa', a'b', b'c', &c., of which
AE contains 4. The ratio of AE to AC is, therefore, 4
to 7, the same as that of AD : AB.[/del]

161. Scholium. b. The case in which AD and AB are
incommensurable, is included in this demonstration by
the reasoning of § 98.

162. Corollary. In the same way
[del]AD : BD = AE : EC.[/del]
and
[del]BD : AB = EC : AC.[/del]
