[pn]110[/pn] SOLID GEOMETRY. [CH. XVI. § 356

[u]Convex Surface of right Prism or Cylinder.[/u]

a right prism or cylinder is the perimeter or circumference of its base multiplied by its altitude.

[u]Proof.[/u] [u]a.[/u] The area of each of the parallelograms [u]ABFG[/u], [u]BCGH[/u], &c., which compose the convex surface of the right prism (fig. 164) is, by § 247, the product of its base [u]AB[/u], [u]BC[/u] &c., by the common altitude [u]AF[/u]; and the sum of their areas, or the convex surface of the prism, is the sum of these bases, or the perimeter [u]ABCD[/u] &c., by the altitude [u]AF[/u].

[u]b.[/u] This demonstration is extended to the right cylinder by increasing the number of sides to infinity.

354. [u]Theorem.[/u] The section of a prism or cylinder made by a plane parallel to the bases is equal to either base.

[u]Proof.[/u] [u]a.[/u] Let [u]LMNO[/u], &c. (fig. 163) be a section of the prism made by a plane parallel to the bases. It follows, from § 334, that [u]LM[/u] is parallel to [u]AB[/u], [u]MN[/u] to [u]BC[/u], &c.; and, consequently, the angle [u]LMN[/u] is equal to [u]ABC[/u], by § 29, the angle [u]NMO[/u] to [u]BCD[/u], &c. Moreover, in the parallelograms [u]ABLM[/u], [u]BCMN[/u], &c., [u]AB[/u] is equal to [u]LM[/u], [u]BC[/u] to [u]MN[/u], &c., and the polygons [u]ABCD[/u] &c., [u]LMNO[/u], &c. are equiangular and equilateral with respect to each other, and are, therefore equal, by § 195.

[u]b.[/u] The demonstration is extended to the cylinder by increasing the number of sides to infinity.

355. [u]Corollary.[/u] Hence, from § 342, two prisms or cylinders of equal bases and altitudes are equivalent.

356. [u]Corollary.[/u] Any prism or cylinder is equivalent to a right prism or cylinder of the same base and altitude.
