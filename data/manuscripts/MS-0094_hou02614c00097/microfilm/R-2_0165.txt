§80 But now suppose $C$ is not equal to $B^2$ but
differs from $B^2$ by $D$, where $D$ may be a positive
or a negative number. Then the equation is
$$x^2 + 2Bx + B^2 + D = 0.$$
Then $(x+B)^2 = -D.$
Now [del]a[/del] [ins]no[/ins] positive number multiplied into itself gives
a positive number; and [del]a[/del] [ins]its[/ins] negative multiplied into
it gives the negative of that number; but the negative
multiplied into itself gives the negative of that negative
or the positive square again. So no positive or negative
number multiplied into itself gives a negative number. Hence
$-D$ must be positive or the equation has no root. We have
$$C = B^2 + D$$
Hence $-D = B^2 - C$;
[del]If this is positive[/del] and we have
$$(x+B)^2 = B^2 - C$$
Take the square root of the two quantities. Then
$$x+B$$
may be $\sqrt{B^2-C}$ or it may be the [ins]corresponding[/ins] negative number.
[del]Either then[/del] [ins]Thus, the two roots are[/ins]
$$X_1 = -B + \sqrt{B^2-C}$$
$$X_2 = -B - \sqrt{B^2-C}.$$
And we see that agreeably to what we found above
$$X_1 + X_2 = -2B.$$
