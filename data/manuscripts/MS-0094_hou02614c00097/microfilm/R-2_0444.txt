[pn]66[/pn] PLANE GEOMETRY. [CH. XII. § 227.

[u]To circumscribe a Circle about a Regular Polygon.[/u]

[u]Solution[/u] Find, by § 149, the circumference of a circle
which passes through the three vertices [u]A[/u], [u]B[/u], [u]C[/u]; and
this circle is circumscribed about the given polygon.

[u]Proof[/u]. Suppose the circumference divided into the
same number of equal arcs [u]AB'[/u], [u]BC'[/u], &c. as that of the
sides of the given polygon. The chords [u]AB'[/u], [u]B'C'[/u], &c.
form, by § 202, a regular polygon, which, by § 206, is
similar to [u]ABCD[/u] &c.

Hence,
the angle [u]ABC[/u] = the angle [u]AB'C'[/u];
and, consequently, by § 108, the arc [u]ABC[/u], which is twice
the arc [u]AB[/u], is equal to the arc [u]AB'C'[/u], which is twice the
arc [u]AB'[/u]. We have then,
the arc [u]AB[/u] = the arc [u]AB'[/u],
and the chord [u]AB[/u] is equal to the chord [u]AB'[/u], and coincides
with it. The polygons [u]AB'C'D'[/u] &c., [u]ABCD[/u] &c., must,
therefore, by § 195, coincide; and the circle is circum-
scribed about the given polygon.

223. [u]Corollary[/u]. There is a point [u]O[/u] in every regular
polygon equally distant from all its vertices, and which
is called the [u]centre[/u] of the polygon.

224. [u]Corollary[/u]. If we join [u]AO[/u], [u]BO[/u], [u]CO[/u], &c., the
angles [u]AOB[/u], [u]BOC[/u], [u]COD[/u], &c. are all equal, and each
has the same ratio to four right angles, which the arc [u]AB[/u]
has to the circumference.

225. [u]Corollary[/u]. The isosceles triangles [u]AOB[/u], [u]BOC[/u],
[u]COD[/u], &c. are all equal.

226. [u]Corollary[/u]. The angles [u]OAB[/u], [u]OBA[/u], [u]OBC[/u], [u]OCB[/u],
&c. are all equal, and each is half of the angle [u]ABC[/u].
