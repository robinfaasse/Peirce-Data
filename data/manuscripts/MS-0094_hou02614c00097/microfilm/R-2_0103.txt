[u]Subchapter 4. Polyhedra[/u].

§ [del]4[/del] [ins]113[/ins]. [u]Definition [del]119[/del][/u]. A polyhedron [ins]in the topological sense,[/ins] is a solid the surface of which is composed of confine polygons called the [u]faces[/u] of the polyhedron, and the sides of these, each common to two faces are called the [u]edges[/u] of the polyhedron, and the edges taken together form [del]at[/del] with the points uniting them a tree called the [u]polyhedral net[/u], and the points which are polyhammata of the tree are called the [u]summits[/u] of the polyhedron.
