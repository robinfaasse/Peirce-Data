[marginalia]Demonstration[/marginalia] 123. _Theorem._ The angle _BAC_ (fig. 63), formed by two chords, and which has its vertex between the centre and the circumference, [del]has for its measure[/del] [ins]centrally subtends[/ins] half the arc _BC_ contained between its sides plus half the arc _DE_ contained between its sides produced.

[marginalia]central[/marginalia] [del]Proof.[/del] Join _BE_; and, as _BAC_ is an exterior angle of the triangle _ABE_, we have, by § 71,
_BAC_ = _BEA_ + _ABE_.
But the [del]measure of[/del] [ins]central[/ins] _BEA_ is, by § 107, half of _BC_; and that of _ABE_ is half of _DE_; therefore the [del]measure of[/del] [ins]central[/ins] _BAC_ is
1/2 _BC_ + 1/2 _DE_.

[marginalia]Demonstration[/marginalia] 124. _Theorem._ Two parallels _AB_ and _DC_ (figs. 64, 65, 66), intercept upon the circumference equal arcs _AD_, _BC_.

[marginalia]central[/marginalia] [del]Proof.[/del] Join _BD_. The alternate-internal angles _ABD_ and _BDC_ are equal, by § 30; and therefore, the arcs _AD_ and _BC_, the double of their [del]measures[/del] [ins]central[/ins], are equal.

_Scholium._ In applying this demonstration to figs. (65 and 66), the letters _A_ and _B_ must denote the same point; and in (fig. 66) the letters _D_ and _C_ must also denote the same point.

125. _Corollary._ The arcs _AD_ and _BC_ (fig. 66) being equal must be semicircumferences, and the chord _BC_ must be a diameter.
