§ 92 [u]Definition 137[/u]. To say that one place is [u]contained in[/u] another is [del]to[/del] say that every particle which moves or rests in the former place likewise does so in the latter.

[u]Corollary 13[/u]. From this definition we infer,
1, that every place is contained in itself, and
2, that if one place is contained in another [del]itself[/del] contained in a third, then the first is contained in the third.

[u]Corollary 14[/u]. [del]From[/del] The definitions of the plane and ray imply the following propositions:
1. Every two Planes contain a Ray in common.
2. Two different Planes contain nothing not contained [del]in[/del] [ins]by[/ins] one Ray.
3. Every Ray and Plane contain a Point in common.
4. No Ray and Plane contain two Points in common, unless the Ray is contained in the Plane.
5. Every two Points are contained in one Ray.
6. No two different Points [del]both[/del] [ins]are both[/ins] contained in two different Rays.
7. Every Ray and Point are contained in one Plane.
8. No Ray and Point not contained in it are contained in two different Planes.
