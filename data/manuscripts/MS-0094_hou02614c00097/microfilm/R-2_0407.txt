112. Theorem. In the same circle, or in equal circles, equal arcs are subtended by equal chords.

[u]Demonstration.[/u] Let [u]AB[/u] and [u]CD[/u] be equal arcs of equal circles. Let [O] and [Q] be the respective centres of those circles. Then
$$\angle AOB = \angle CQD$$
But because the circles are equal
dist OA = dist QD
dist OB = dist QC.
Hence in the triangles AOB and DQC two sides of [ins]each[/ins] and [del]them[/del] [ins]their[/ins] included angles are equal. Hence the opposite sides [u]AB[/u] and [u]CD[/u] are equal. Q.E.D.

114. Theorem. In the same circle, or in equal circles, if the sum of two arcs be less than a circumference, the greater arc is subtended by the greater chord; and, conversely, the greater chord is subtended by the greater arc.
