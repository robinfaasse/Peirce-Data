393. [u]Definitions.[/u] If a pyramid or cone is cut by a plane parallel to its base, the portion which remains after taking away the smaller pyramid or cone, is called the [u]frustum of a pyramid or cone[/u], as [u]ABCD[/u] &c. [u]MNOP[/u] &c. (fig. 171.)
The [u]convex surface[/u] of the frustum of a pyramid is the sum of the trapezoids which compose its lateral faces.
The polygons [u]ABCD[/u] &c., [u]MNOP[/u] &c. are the [u]bases of the frustum[/u], and the distance between its bases is its altitude.

394. [u]Corollary.[/u] The frustum of the right cone (fig. 174) may be considered as generated by the revolution of the trapezoid [u]OO'A'A[/u] about the side [u]OO'[/u].
The side [u]AA'[/u], which is called the [u]side of the frustum[/u], in this case, generates the [u]convex surface[/u].

395. [u]Theorem.[/u] The area of the convex surface of the frustum of a regular pyramid is half the product of the sum of the perimeters of its bases by the altitude of either of its trapezoids.
