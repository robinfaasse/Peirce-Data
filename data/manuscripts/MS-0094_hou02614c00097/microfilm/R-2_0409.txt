[u]Tangent to a Circle.[/u]

**117. Corollary.** The perpendicular erected upon the middle of a chord passes through the centre, and also through the middle of the arc subtended by the chord.

**118. Definitions.** A secant is a [del]line[/del] [ins]ray[/ins] which [del]meets[/del] [ins]cuts[/ins] a [del]the circumference of a circle[/del] [ins]curve[/ins] in two points, as *AB* (fig. 55).

A tangent is a [del]line, which has only one point in common with the circumference,[/del] [ins]ray, the limit of an endless series of secants which cut a curve at points at distances from one another diminishing indefinitely. Or, the tangent may be conceived as intermediate between [del]the secants and the[/del] all that series of secants and the ray in which the two points of intersection [del]have coalesced[/del], so that it cuts the curve in two points immeasurably near to one another.[/ins] as *CD*.

**119. Theorem.** The direction of the tangent is the same as that of the circumference at the point of contact.

[del]Proof.[/del] [ins][u]Demonstration[/u][/ins] Draw through the point *M* (fig. 55) the secant *ME* and the tangent *MD*.

If the secant *ME* is turned around the point *M* so as to diminish the angle *EMD*; the secant *ME* will approach the tangent *MD*, and the point *E* will approach the point *M*. When *ME* is turned so far as to pass through the point *P* next to *M*, the angle *DME* will be infinitely small, since *P* is at an infinitely small distance from *M*; and the line *ME* will approach infinitely near the tangent *MD*, that is, it will, by § 99, coincide with this tangent, which has therefore, by § 11, the same direction with the circumference at *M*.
