§155 Theorem 31. The motion can move so that all the [del]particles[/del] [ins]flat laminae[/ins] [del]containing[/del] [ins]containing[/ins] any one ray remain fixed, while all the particles in that ray move along the ray.

<u>Demonstration</u>. The principle of duality not having yet been [del]violated[/del] [ins]falsified[/ins], this follows from the theorem in regard to the possibility of rotation. An independent demonstration may also be given.

By the definition of the motion, any particle of the metron may be moved to any other, and therefore successively to the whole continuous multitude of points in a ray; and any straight fibre may be turned into any ray through that point. It may, therefore, be held in the ray it is already in, as the particle moves along the ray [ins](for this only amounts to a continuous series of adjustments or holdings).[/ins] Any flat lamina may be moved into any plane through that ray. It may, therefore, be continually held in the same plane all the while. Any one such lamina through the axis remaining in its plane, all the rest are fixed by the last clause of the definition of the metron.
