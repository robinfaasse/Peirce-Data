68. Corollary. In [ins]an ordinary[/ins] triangle, there can only be one right angle, or one obtuse angle.

69. Corollary. In [ins]an ordinary[/ins] right triangle, the sum of the acute angles is equal to a right angle.

70. Corollary. An [ins]ordinary[/ins] equilateral triangle, being also equiangular, has each of its angles equal to a third of two right angles, or $\frac{2}{3}$ of one right angle.

71. Corollary. In any [ins]ordinary[/ins] triangle $ABC$, if we produce the side $AC$ toward [del]$D$[/del] [ins]$D$[/ins], the exterior angle $BCD$ is equal to the sum of the two opposite interior angles $A$ and $B$.

72. Theorem. The sum of all the interior angles of [ins]an ordinary[/ins] polygon is equal to [del]as many times[/del] [ins]twice[/ins] two right angles as it has sides minus [del]two[/del] [ins]four[/ins].

[u]Proof[/u]. Let $ABCDE$, &c. (fig. 37), be the given polygon.

Draw from either of the vertices, as $A$, the diagonals $AC$, $AD$, $AE$, &c.

The polygon will obviously be divided into as many triangles as it has sides minus two, and the sum of the angles of these triangles is the same as that of the angles of the polygon. But the sum of the angles of each triangle is, by § 65, equal to two right angles; and, consequently, the sum of all their angles is equal to as many times two right angles as there are triangles, that is, as there are sides to the polygon minus two.
