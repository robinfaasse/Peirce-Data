§ 82. We see that in certain cases, namely when $C > B^2$, the expressions for the roots of [ins]the[/ins] quadratic
$X_1 = -B + \sqrt{B^2 - C}$
$X_2 = -[del]B[/del] [ins]B[/ins] - \sqrt{B^2 - C}$
do not represent any calculable numbers.

Nevertheless, according to the rules of algebra, these expressions satisfy the equation. Thus
$X_1^2 = 2B^2 - 2B\sqrt{B^2 - C} [del]+ B^2[/del] - C$.
$X_2^2 = 2B^2 + 2B\sqrt{B^2 - C} - C$
$2BX_1 = -2B^2 + 2B\sqrt{B^2 - C}$.
$2BX_2 = -2B^2 - 2B\sqrt{B^2 - C}$.
So that $X_1^2 + 2BX_1 + C = 0$
$X_2^2 + 2BX_2 + C = 0$.

As "literal" ([ins]i.e.[/ins] formed of letters) expressions, they present nothing extraordinary. But there are no corresponding numbers.

Here we have an instance of a phenomenon frequent in mathematics, namely, that our diagrams, or our formulae, or whatever it may be we are thinking with, insist upon being generalized*.
