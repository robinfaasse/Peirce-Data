Chapter II [del]Regularity[/del] of Space.

Subchapter I Homogeneity.

§13. Any collection of points is intrinsecally[ins]<sup>*</sup>[/ins] precisely like every equal and equally distinguishable collection.

Thus, any single point is just like any other point in itself and in all its intrinsec relations.

It is true that some points are at an infinite distance from our neighborhood; and such points are in metrical relations very different from all other points. But a system of measurement does not belong to space in itself. One system, that in common use, is preferable to all others because it simplifies the expression of dynamical laws, and because it is practicable. But these are extra-geometrical considerations.
