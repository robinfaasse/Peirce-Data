[u]Envelopes.[/u]

§55. If the student draws two circles one lying nearly quite within the other and with a pair of compasses draws [del]several[/del] [ins]a hundred or more[/ins] circles having their centres at nearly equal intervals on the large circle and cutting the small one at right angles, he will see that these circles have for their outline, or envelope, as it is called, a pretty curve. Everyone of the multitude of circles just grazes this curve.

[fig]Two intersecting circles, one smaller and mostly inside the larger one.[/fig]

Definition [ins]131[/ins]. An envelope of a [del]gradually[/del] moving place [ins]P₁[/ins] is the place generated by the mobile place which is the limit of the intersection figures of situations of P indefinitely nearer and nearer to one another. The envelope is [del]also[/del] the boundary of the place generated by the moving place, P, [ins]if P is unlimited[/ins].

The envelope of an [ins]unbounded[/ins] moving surface is generally a surface; [del]it[/del] [del]moving line for the moving surface generates a solid and the[/del] [del]boundary of a solid is a sole surface.[/del]

A moving line generally has no envelope; for if the line is unbounded, so generally is the surface [ins]it generates[/ins]. That it should be otherwise, it is necessary that some part of the line should move along a line in contact with itself, and therefore lying in the same surface. [del]So[/del] [ins]Hence, if[/ins] a moving line has an envelope, this envelope is a cuspidal edge of the surface generated by the line.

It may happen that the envelope of a moving place has a dimensionality lower than that place, because the intersection-figure may be stationary. It may also happen that successive positions of the moving place [del]touch one another instead of intersecting[/del]. [ins]In the case of a moving surface the envelope may have a cuspidal edge, the envelope of [del]intersection of successive[/del] positions of the surface.[/ins]
