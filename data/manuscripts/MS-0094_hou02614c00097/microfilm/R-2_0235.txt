[u]Scholium[/u]. We here use the traditional phraseology
which [del]one[/del] speaks as if lines and surfaces were
something we make. This is not strictly correct. The
lines and surfaces are places which are there, whether
we think of them or not. They are quite ideal, it is true.
But [del]there[/del] they are there, in this sense, that we can think
of them as being there without being drawn into any
absurdity. [del]But[/del] The ordinary [del]phraseology[/del] diction
however which [del]makes[/del] represents us as drawing, — or
[del]in[/del] the technical language "describing", — lines and surfaces,
and which talks of a moving [ins]line[/ins] [del]particle[/del] as generating
a surface, with other such expressions, is rather
convenient, and can hardly mislead.

§119 [u]Definition 164[/u]. [del]The station point, or eye[/del] In perspective the eye
of projection is generally called the [u]station point[/u]. The plane of the
section is called the [u]plane of delineation[/u]. The original figures are called
[u]natural[/u], or [u]original[/u].
