[u]To make a Square in a given Ratio to a given Square.[/u]

Join [u]AD[/u], and draw [u]DE[/u], perpendicular to [u]AD[/u], and equal to the side of the fourth given square; and so on. The line which joins [u]A[/u] to the extremity of the last side is the side of the required square.

[u]Proof.[/u] For, by § 256,
[u]AC[/u]² = [u]AB[/u]² + [u]BC[/u]²,
[u]AD[/u]² = [u]AC[/u]² + [u]CD[/u]² = [u]AB[/u]² + [u]BC[/u]² + [u]CD[/u]²,
[u]AE[/u]² = [u]AD[/u]² + [u]DE[/u]² = [u]AB[/u]² + [u]BC[/u]² + [u]CD[/u]² + [u]DE[/u]²; &c.

264. [u]Scholium[/u] If either of the squares [u]BC[/u]², [u]CD[/u]², &c. were to have been subtracted instead of being added, the problem might still have been solved by means of § 262.

265. [u]Problem.[/u] To make a square which is to a given square in a given ratio.

[u]Solution.[/u] Divide any line, as [u]EG[/u] (fig. 133), by § 163, into two parts, at the point [u]F[/u], which are to each other in the given ratio of the given square to the required square.
Upon [u]EG[/u] describe the semicircle [u]EMG[/u]; draw [u]FM[/u] perpendicular to [u]EG[/u].
Join [u]ME[/u] and [u]MG[/u]; take, on [u]ME[/u] produced if necessary, [u]MH[/u] = [u]AB[/u] the side of the given square.
Draw [u]HI[/u] parallel to [u]EG[/u], meeting [u]MG[/u] in [u]I[/u], and [u]MI[/u] is the side of the required square.

[u]Proof.[/u] Produce [u]MF[/u] to [u]P[/u]; and, as the triangle [u]HMI[/u] is, by § 109, right-angled at [u]M[/u], we have, by § 260,
[u]MH[/u]² : [u]MI[/u]² = [u]HP[/u] : [u]PI[/u].
But by § 181,
[u]HP[/u] : [u]PI[/u] = [u]EF[/u] : [u]FG[/u];
whence, on account of the common ratio [u]HP[/u] : [u]PI[/u],
[u]MH[/u]² : [u]MI[/u]² = [u]EF[/u] : [u]FG[/u].
