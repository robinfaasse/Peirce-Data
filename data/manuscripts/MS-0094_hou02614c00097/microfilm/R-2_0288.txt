Intersections of Rays.

[u]Theorem 22. Space contains a quadruply continuous multitude of rays[/u]. Demonstration.

§ 138. [del]Every[/del] [ins]Every[/ins] ray cuts every plane, except the book of planes hinged about it. Take two planes and a point in each; [del]then, are[/del] [ins]then[/ins] just one ray exists through each such pair of points, [del]except when both points are in both planes[/del] [del]and these are all the rays in space[/del] (except that there is only one ray for all the pairs of points which are both in both planes, and only one ray for all the pairs of points of which one is any one point in both planes,) and these are all the rays of space. Hence, since there is a doubly continuous multitude of points in each plane, there is a quadruply continuous multitude of rays in space.

§ 139 [u]Theorem 23. Cutting every ray there is a triply continuous multitude of rays[/u].

Demonstration. Through every ray there is a singly continuous multitude of planes. In each [ins]of those[/ins] planes there is a doubly continuous multitude of rays, all of which [del]cut[/del] are different and all cut the hinge ray, except one in each plane which coincides with it. The double continuity of the rays in one plane, together with the single continuity of planes, makes up a triple continuity.
