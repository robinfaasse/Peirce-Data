The three curves which are plane sections of the regulus are called [del]conic[/del] [u]the conic sections[/u], because they are plane sections of an ordinary cone. They are the most important, by far, of all curves. They are also called plane curves of the second order, because each is cut in two points by a plane.

Taking one of these sections of the hyperboloid (so called because some of its [ins]plane[/ins] sections are hyperbolas) we see how if a ray be drawn in the plane of this section so as to cut the curve, and be then moved the two points in which it cuts the curve may run together, and for an instant coalesce, and how [del]the next[/del] in a moment the two points disappear.
