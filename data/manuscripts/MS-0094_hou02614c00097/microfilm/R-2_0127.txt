[u]Definition [del]32[/del] [ins]33[/ins][/u]: The natural and perverted sense of the parts of a continuum of an odd number of dimensions, are two arrangements in which all the parts have the same general boundary-relations and connections, but which cannot be converted, the one into the other, without breach of continuity and without the aid of another dimension.

In the case of a line [del]the points occupied by[/del] a continuous series of particles, these may be conceived to [del]be reversed[/del] [ins]have their order[/ins] of sequence reversed, though such reversal cannot be effected by any continuous change of place. There are two senses, or ways, of motion along a line, each the reverse of the other.

In like manner, it may be conceived that, in place of all the objects which fill three dimensional space, their looking-glass images are substituted. But by no motion or gradual change can this substitution be effected. The looking-glass image of a shape is called the [u]perverse[/u] of that shape.
