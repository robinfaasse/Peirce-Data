§ 106. The [u]degrees of freedom[/u] of any thing [ins]are[/ins] the number of independent singly continuous motions of which it is susceptible. For example, a child's jumping jack, if it has [del]a[/del] joints at the shoulders, hips, elbows, knees, and neck, and and all the joints can be moved separately, has 9 degrees of freedom; but if it is only to be moved by pulling the string, though all the joints move, they do not move independently, and there is but one degree of freedom. A piece attached [del]to a[/del] fixed piece by a ball and socket joint has [del]two[/del] [ins]three[/ins] degrees of freedom. [del]It[/del] [ins]A point[/ins] can be displaced in any plane, and in any second plane; but all other displacements can be produced compounding more or less of these first two.
