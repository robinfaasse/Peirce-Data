[u]Definition 55[/u]. A complex [del]respect to[/del] [ins]quality in[/ins] which objects may differ by imperceptible degrees is a [u]continuum[/u].

Thus, color is a continuum; for objects may in respect to color differ not only in hue, but also in chroma[ins]*[/ins], and in luminosity. So also is place; for "quality" in the above definition must not be understood in the sense in which place is not a quality[ins]†[/ins].

[u]Definition 56[/u]. A continuum whose variability is [del]entirely[/del] [ins]perfectly[/ins] uniform and returns into itself is called an [del]entire[/del] [ins]perfect[/ins] continuum.

Thus, place, as conceived by the geometer is entire; but color is not.

[u]Definition 57[/u]. A character of a continuum exactly definite, so as not to allow the slightest variation is called a character individual in respect to the continuum. A character every possible variety of which may in the course of time be possessed by an object individual at every instant is called a linear character.
