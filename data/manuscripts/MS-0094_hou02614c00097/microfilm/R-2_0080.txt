§30. A surface is boundary between solids;
A line is boundary between surfaces;
A point, or point-pair, is boundary between lines.
A [ins]simple[/ins] surface not bounded by lines is generally a complete closed boundary between solids, though solid space may be so connected that [del]of[/del] [ins]it is possible to go round from one side to the other of[/ins] special unbounded surfaces. [Thus, the solid space of ordinary geometry is so connected that we can pass through infinity from one side to the other of a plane, which is unbounded by any line.]
A [ins]simple[/ins] line not bounded by points, that is, a self-returning line, is generally a complete boundary between surfaces, though there are surfaces on which it is possible to pass round from one side to the other of special unbounded lines. [Thus, on a ring surface there are [ins]obviously two[/ins] [del]such[/del] lines.] A point-pair is generally a complete boundary between lines though there are lines on which it is possible to pass round from one side to the other of special point pairs.
