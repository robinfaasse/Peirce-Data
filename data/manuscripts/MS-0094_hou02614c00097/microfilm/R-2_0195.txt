A continuous series of places, whether [del]of[/del] onedimensional or
higher, [del]usually has[/del] generates another place which usually has
a boundary; and this boundary is termed the [u]envelope of that
series of places[/u]. A plane curve is the envelope of its tangents.

There are thus two distinct ways of conceiving a plane curve:
either as the aggregate of its points [ins](which are called its ineunts)[/ins]
or as the envelope of its tangents [ins](which are called its exeunts.)[/ins]. These conceptions may be
combined by conceiving a ray to have a point upon it, and
to perpetually [del]perpetually[/del] turn round that point while the
point perpetually slides along the ray. Thus the [ins][u]sliding[/u][/ins] point [del]at
once[/del] describes the curve, which at the same time the
turning ray envelopes.
