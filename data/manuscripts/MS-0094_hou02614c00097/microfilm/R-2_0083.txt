[u]Definition 89[/u]. The number of figures [ins]in a place[/ins] [del]for[/del] which it is necessary to obliterate that every bag it can contain may be able to shrink to nothing is called its [u]periphraxis[/u].

As an [del]example[/del] [ins]illustration[/ins] of [del]these[/del] the above conceptions, imagine [del]us for[/del] all space to contain nothing but two [ins]metallic[/ins] rings linked together.

[fig]Two linked rings with cross-hatching[/fig]

The insides of the two rings, [ins]i.e.[/ins] the space occupied by the [del]material[/del] [ins]metal[/ins] of which they are composed, has [ins]its[/ins] separation-number equal to 1. The vacant space has a separation-number equal to 0. The metal-air surface has a separation-number equal to 1. Each ring-surface has a cyclosis equal to 2; for one self-returning fibre may go round the bar of the ring, and the other along the length of it. [del]If we cut round the bar so as to break the latter way in another[/del] We shall examine this more closely [del]for other[/del] [ins]each[/ins] [del]sections[/del]. [del]The solid space occupied by the metal of the ring has a cyclosis equal to one.[/del] If we make an incision round the bar (breaking the surface, but not making a new surface at the break) so that the latter fibre is cut, the former remains, for it cannot slip over the interruption of the surface at the cut. This will be further explained later. The solid space occupied by the metal of each ring has a cyclosis equal to one. There is no fibre round the bar which cannot shrink to nothing in that space. The vacant space has a cyclosis equal to 2. Each [del]surface[/del] [ins]unbounded metal space[/ins] has a periphraxis equal to 1. The periphraxis of [del]a[/del] [ins]the[/ins] [del]unbounded[/del] is zero. That of the outer space is 1. The unboundedness of the spaces is zero.
