[u]Subchapter 2. Connectivity.[/u]

§ 38. [u]Definition [del]104[/del] [ins]104[/ins].[/u] To break the continuity of a surface at a point so that a fibre moving over the surface cannot pass over that point is called [u]puncturing[/u] the surface at that point.

The effect of puncturing is to convert the point of puncture into an infinitely small rim. This definition is to be understood in this sense. [marginalia]The first puncture of a surface does not affect the cyclosis but diminishes the periphraxis.
Subsequent punctures increase the cyclosis, but do not affect the periphraxis.[/marginalia]

[pn]105[/pn]
[u]Definition [del]105[/del] [ins]105[/ins].[/u] To break the continuity of a surface along a line upon it from rim to rim, so that a particle moving upon the surface cannot cross that line is called [u]making a cross-cut[/u], or [u]cutting it across[/u] along that line.

The effect of cutting across a surface is to convert the line of the cross-cut into two partial rims. The definition is to be understood in this sense.
