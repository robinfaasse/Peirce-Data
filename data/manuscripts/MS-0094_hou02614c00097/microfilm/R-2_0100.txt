Connectivity of Solids.

§ 39. If we make an interruption in unbounded space, say by
placing in it a disk, or rimmed perissad, we may call this an
increase by 1 of the connectivity of space; and we may conceive
the same effect to be produced by every [ins]separate[/ins] rupture of space.

If the edges of two such disks coalesce in part, the effect is
undone. But how [del]much[/del] great a length is joined makes
no difference so long as all is not joined. If a portion
of one rim joins another, the effect is the same, provided
the joined portions do not run together. If 3 disks join in
three places, or 2 in 2 places, or 1 in one place, the
effect is always to make one quoit-shaped, or two rimmed,
surface. Hence, we must regard the connectivity of the space
interrupted by such a surface as unchanged. If we allow a
rim to shrink into itself and close up, the connectivity is increased
by one. Hence, the connectivity of space separated by a glo-
bular surface into two parts must be regarded as increased
by two. But if two rims of two disks are joined together
throughout their length, there is no change in the connec-
tivity; for the two disks increase the connectivity by
2, and so does the globular surface which they form. Hence
