spherical triangle by [u]A[/u], [u]B[/u], [u]C[/u]; and the sides respectively

447. Theorem. The sum of the angles of a spheri-
cal triangle is greater than two right angles.

Proof. Let [u]A[/u], [u]B[/u], [u]C[/u] be the angles of the spherical
triangle. The sides of its polar triangle are 180° — [u]A[/u],
180° — [u]B[/u], and 180° — [u]C[/u]. Now the sum of these sides,
is, by § 440, less than 360°, that is,
360° > (180° — [u]A[/u]) + (180° — [u]B[/u]) + (180° — [u]C[/u]), [ins][fig]handwritten mathematical symbols[/fig][/ins]
or,
360° > 540° — [u]A[/u] — [u]B[/u] — [u]C[/u],
or, by transposition,
[u]A[/u] + [u]B[/u] + [u]C[/u] > 540° — 360°,
or,
[u]A[/u] + [u]B[/u] + [u]C[/u] > 180°;
that is, the sum of the angles [u]A[/u], [u]B[/u], [u]C[/u] is greater than
180°.
