of three points which are expressed each by a syzygy in
A, B, C, D whose multipliers add up to 0. Thus, take the
syzygy
$$aA + bB + cC - (a+b+c)D = F$$
Now put
$$aA - aD = \frac{1}{3} P$$
$$bB - bD = \frac{1}{3} Q$$
$$cC - cD = \frac{1}{3} R$$
and we have
$$\frac{1}{3}P + \frac{1}{3}Q + \frac{1}{3}P = F.$$
Take any second syzygy
$$lA + mB + nC - (l+m+n)D = G$$
This is equal to
$$\frac{l}{3a} P + \frac{m}{3b} Q + \frac{n}{3c} R.$$
This is the same as if divided by the sum of the multipliers.
We shall find that it follows from this that all the
points expressed by syzygies in A, B, C, D whose mul-
pliers add up to 0 lie in one plane.
