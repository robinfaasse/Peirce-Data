The connectivity of a closed (i.e. bounded) triply confinitive surface with a number of punctures is equal to the number of punctures.

Any such puncture may be stretched out so as to be a boundary to the surface; and therefore the connectivity of a bounded triply confinitive surface with a number of punctures is one more than the number of punctures.

To cut such a surface into a number of parts requires one less than that number of cross-cuts [del]and hence the connectivity of a number of triply confinitive surfaces is equal to the sum[/del] [ins]but this operation increases the number of boundaries[/ins]. [del]But this operation[/del] Each cross-cut increases the number of boundaries by 1. Consequently, the connectivity of a number of triply confinitive [ins]bifacial[/ins] surfaces is 2 more than the holes and [ins]outer[/ins] boundaries diminished by twice the number of pieces, or [del]distinct[/del] [ins]separate[/ins] surfaces.

The connectivity of [del]a non-facial[/del] [ins]the surface of a single strip of paper with ends pasted together after half a twist[/ins] surface is 2. For if the strip be cut across, two cross-cuts are made in the surface, one on each side of the [del]strip[/del]. The result is 2 simply connected surfaces, one on each side of the strip. The connectivity of these is, by the rule, 2 added to 2, the number of boundaries, and diminished by 4, or twice the number of separate surfaces.
