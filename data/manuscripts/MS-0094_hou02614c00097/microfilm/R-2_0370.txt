§194 [del][u]Theorem[/u][/del] [ins]67[/ins] [u]The perpendicular distance from a point to a ray is shorter than any other distance between that point and a point of the ray.[/u]

[Demonstration. For in passing along the ray {BC} from its vanishing point [ins]a particle[/ins] [del]one[/del] passes to points nearer and nearer to [A] any other point not in the ray. [fig]Diagram showing point A, perpendicular to line BC, and concentric circles centered at A intersecting BC[/fig] When it first crosses any given circle having its centre at [A] it passes from the outside to the inside. For it comes from the vanishing-point of the ray which is outside every circle, and it cannot get into the inside of any circle without crossing the circle; for the circle divides [del]severs[/del] the plane into an outside and an inside region. Now the ray only cuts each circle twice; and [del]one[/del] [del]the[/del] twice it cuts each circle on opposite sides of the foot of the perpendicular, until it reaches the [ins]foot of the[/ins] perpendicular. Therefore, the circle through the foot of the perpendicular is inside all the other circles [del]having their centres at [A][/del] which any [del]point[/del] [ins]particle[/ins] travelling along the ray passes through. Hence, the foot of the [del]perpendicular[/del] is [del]the[/del] point of the ray nearest the centre.]
