[pn]34[/pn] PLANE GEOMETRY. [CH. VIII. § 122.

Angles formed by Secants and Tangents.

120. _Theorem._ The tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of contact.

_Proof._ The radius [u]OM = ON[/u] (fig. 58) is shorter than any other line, as OP, which can be drawn from the point O to the tangent MP; it is therefore, by § 39, perpendicular to this tangent.

[marginalia]Demonstration[/marginalia] 121. The angle [u]BAC[/u] (fig. 59), formed by a tangent and a chord, has for its measure half the arc [u]BMA[/u] comprehended between its sides.

[marginalia]Demonstration[/marginalia] _Proof_ _a._ Draw the diameter [u]AD[/u], and we have
[ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] BAC = [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] DAC - [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] DAB.
But [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] DAC, being a right angle, has for its measure half of a semicircumference, as [u]ABD[/u]; also [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] BAD has, by § 107, for its measure half of the arc [u]BD[/u]. The measure of [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] BAC is therefore
[u]1[/u]/2 (ABD - BD) = [u]1[/u]/2 AMB.

_b._ In the same way, it may be shown that BAE has for its measure half the arc [u]BDA[/u].

[marginalia]centrally subtends[/marginalia] 122. _Theorem._ The angle [u]BAC[/u], formed by two secants (fig. 60), two tangents (fig. 62), or a tangent and a secant (fig. 61), and [del]which[/del] [ins]having[/ins] [del]has[/del] its vertex without the circumference of [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] circle, has for its measure half the concave arc [u]BMC[/u] intercepted between its sides, minus half the convex arc [u]DNE[/u].

[marginalia]Demonstration[/marginalia] _Proof._ Join BE; and as [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] BEC is an exterior angle of the triangle ABE, we have, by § 71
[ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] BEC = [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] ABE + [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] BAC,
whence [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] BAC = [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] BEC - [ins]∠[/ins] [del]∠[/del] ABE.
