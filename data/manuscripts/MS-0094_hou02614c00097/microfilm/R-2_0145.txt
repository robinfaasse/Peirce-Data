§ 70. Just as the values of two unknown quantities can be
deduced from two equations of this kind, so the values of three
can be deduced from three. For example, suppose
$$x + y - z = 1$$
$$x - y + z = 2$$
$$x + y + z = 3$$
From the first we have
$$x = 1 - y + z,$$
Substituting this in the others, one gets
$$1 - y + z - y + z = 2,$$
$$1 - y + z + y + z = 3,$$
or what is the same
$$-2y + 2z = 1,$$
$$0y + 2z = 2,$$
these give, by the formulae above
[del]The first of these giv[/del]
$$y = \frac{2 \cdot 2 - 2 \cdot 1}{2 \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot 2} = \frac{4 - 2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$
$$z = \frac{-2 \cdot 2 - 0 \cdot 1}{-2 \cdot 2 - 2 \cdot 0} = \frac{-4}{-4} = 1$$
And substituting these, one gets
$$x = 1 - y + z = 1 - \frac{1}{2} + 1 = 1\frac{1}{2}.$$
