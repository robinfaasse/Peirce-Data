[u]Qualitative Barycentric Notation[/u].

§133. There is another notation of great utility. Namely, A and B being any two points, we write
[7A + 3B], [5A - 2B],
and in short any expressions formed of sums of multiples of A and B, [del]to denote[/del] or as we may say "syzygies"* of A and B, to denote [del]our[/del] points [del]here[/del] on the ray through A and B. It is to be understood that [7A + 3B] is the same point as [14A + 6B], and [5A - 2B] the same as [2B - 5A]. Strictly speaking the sum of the multipliers should be made 1, by dividing every [ins]multiplier[/ins] [del]one[/del] by their sum. Thus, [7A + 3B] means
[ [frac]7/7+3[/frac] A + [frac]3/7+3[/frac] B ] ;
and [5A - 2B] means
[ [frac]5/5-2[/frac] A - [frac]2/5-2[/frac] B ].
There is a slight difficulty in the case of expressions like [A - B] where the sum of the multipliers is zero. We will examine that presently.

*A "syzygy" is more properly a linear equation between a number of quantities; but no confusion arises from using it in the sense of the text.
