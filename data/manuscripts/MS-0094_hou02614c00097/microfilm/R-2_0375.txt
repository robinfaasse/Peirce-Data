[del]43. Definitions. A plane figure is a plane terminated on all sides by lines.
If the lines are straight, the space which they contain is called a rectilineal figure, or polygon (fig. 21), and the sum of the bounding lines is the perimeter of the polygon.[/del]

[ins][u]Definition 20A[/u] A polygon is [del]strictly[/del] a figure composed of the vertices and legs of angles; but the word is very commonly used to mean [del]a figure[/del] a plane figure composed of a number of points each joined to two of the others by straight lines.
For the names of special polygons, see topology.
A trigon is almost invariably called a triangle and a [del]quadr[/del] tetragon is very often called a quadrilateral.[/ins]

44. Definitions. The polygon of three sides is the most simple of these figures, and is called a triangle; that of four sides is called a quadrilateral; that of five sides, a pentagon; that of six, a hexagon, &c.

[del]45.[/del] Definition [ins]20B[/ins] A triangle is denominated equilateral (fig. 22), when the three sides are equal, isosceles (fig. 23), when two only of its sides are equal, and scalene (fig. 24), when no two of its sides are equal.

[del]46.[/del] Definition [ins]20C[/ins] A right-triangle is that which has a right angle. The side opposite to the right angle is called the hypothenuse. Thus ABC (fig. 25) is a triangle right-angled at C and the side AB is the hypothenuse.
