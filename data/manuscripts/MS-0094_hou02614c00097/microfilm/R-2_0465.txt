[u]367.[/u] Corollary. Prisms or cylinders of equivalent
bases and equal altitudes are equivalent.

[u]368.[/u] Corollary. Prisms or cylinders of equivalent
bases are to each other as their altitudes; and those of
the same altitude are to each other as their bases.

[u]369.[/u] Corollary. Denoting by $R$ the radius, and by $A$
the area of the base of a cylinder; and using $\pi$ as in
§ 237, we have, by § 280,
$$A = \pi \times R^2.$$
Denoting, also, by $H$ the altitude, $V$ the solidity of the
cylinder, we have, by § 366,
$$V = A \times H = \pi \times R^2 \times H.$$

[u]370.[/u] Definitions. A pyramid is a solid formed by
several triangular planes proceeding from the same point,
and terminating in the sides of a polygon, as $S\text{-}ABCD$
&c. (fig. 171).

The point $S$ is the [u]vertex of the pyramid[/u].

The polygon $ABCD$ &c is the [u]base of the pyra-[/u]
[u]mid[/u].

The [u]convex surface of the pyramid[/u] is the sum of the
triangles $SAB + SAC$, &c.

The [u]altitude of the pyramid[/u] is the distance of its ver-
tex from its base.

[u]371.[/u] Definitions. A pyramid is [u]triangular[/u], [u]quad-[/u]
[u]rangular[/u], &c., when the base is a triangle, a quad-
rilateral, &c.
