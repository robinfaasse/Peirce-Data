[u]Definition 83[/u]. That continuum of points through which a particle moves in being quasi-translated from one point to another along their common ray is called a [u]stretch[/u], or limited straight line. The part of the metron occupying a stretch may be called a [u]stick[/u]. The number of times a stick has to be placed in partial stretches in order successively to fill up all the parts of a longer stretch, is said to be the number of times the [u]length[/u] of the stick has to be multiplied to give the [u]length[/u] of the longer stretch. The [del]quantity[/del] [ins]magnitude[/ins] of the stretch between two points is called the [u]distance[/u] between those points. There are two different distances between two points according as [del]we[/del] [ins]our[/ins] measure moves the short way or the long way round the ray, which, be it remembered, must be conceived as self-returning. The sum of them is a [u]circuit[/u]. We denote it by [u]𝛳[/u], though, of course, it is a different sort of quantity from an angular circuit. The two distances are [u]supplementary[/u]. The smaller is the principal value of the distance. A length has but one value.

A length may be written [u]AB[/u], its supplement being [u]BA[/u].
