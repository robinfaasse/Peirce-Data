183. [u]Theorem.[/u] The perpendicular [u]AD[/u] (fig. 101) upon the hypothenuse [u]BC[/u] of the right triangle [u]BAC[/u] from the vertex [u]A[/u] of the right angle, divides the triangle into two triangles [u]BAD[/u], [u]CAD[/u], which are similar to each other and to the whole triangle [u]BAC[/u].

[u]Proof.[/u] a. The right triangles [u]BAC[/u] and [u]BAD[/u] are similar, by § 174, for the acute angle [u]B[/u] is common to them both.

b. In the same way it may be shown, that [u]DAC[/u] is similar to [u]BAC[/u], and, therefore, to [u]BAD[/u].

184. [u]Corollary.[/u] From the similar triangles [u]BAD[/u], [u]BAC[/u], we have
[u]BD : BA = BA : BC[/u],
that is, the leg [u]BA[/u] is a mean proportional between the hypothenuse [u]BC[/u] and the adjacent segment [u]BD[/u].

a. In the same way [u]AC[/u] is a mean proportional between [u]BC[/u] and [u]DC[/u].

185. [u]Corollary.[/u] From the similar triangles [u]BAD[/u], [u]CAD[/u], we have
[u]BD : DA = DA : DC[/u],
or, the perpendicular [u]DA[/u] is a mean proportional between the segments [u]BD[/u], [u]DC[/u] of the hypothenuse.
