[pn]138[/pn] SOLID GEOMETRY. [CH. XVIII. § 461.

Sides compared with opposite Angles.

[u]a.[/u] If, then, [u]AC[/u] is acute, we have
[u]B'C'[/u] = [u]AC[/u], that is [u]AC[/u] < [u]B'A[/u].
Hence, by § 459, in the triangle [u]AB'C[/u],
the angle [u]B'[/u] < the angle [u]ACB'[/u].
But since [u]B'[/u] and [u]B[/u] are each equal to the angle of the
planes [u]BAB'[/u], [u]BCB'[/u], they are equal; and, therefore,
the angle [u]B[/u] < the angle [u]ACB'[/u].
Again, since [u]AC[/u] is acute and [u]AB[/u] obtuse,
[u]AC[/u] < [u]AB[/u];
and, in the triangle [u]ABC[/u], by § 459,
the angle [u]B[/u] < the angle [u]ACB[/u].
That is, the angle [u]B[/u] is less than either the angle [u]ACB[/u] or
its supplement [u]ACB'[/u]; but one of these angles must be
acute, and therefore the angle [u]B[/u] is acute.

[u]b.[/u] If [u]AC[/u] is obtuse, we have
[u]BC'[/u] = [u]AC[/u],
that is, [u]AC[/u] > [u]BA[/u];
and, therefore, by § 459,
the angle [u]B[/u] > the angle [u]BCA[/u].
Also, as [u]B'A[/u] is acute,
[u]AC[/u] > [u]B'A[/u],
and, therefore, by § 459,
the angle [u]B'[/u] > the angle [u]B'CA[/u];
that is, the angle [u]B[/u] is greater than either the angle [u]ACB[/u]
or its supplement [u]ACB'[/u]; but one of these angles must be
obtuse, and therefore the angle [u]B[/u] is obtuse.
