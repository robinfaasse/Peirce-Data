[pn]130[/pn] SOLID GEOMETRY. [CH. XVIII. § 439.

[u]Spherical Segment, Sector.[/u]

[u]436. Definitions.[/u] When two parallel planes cut a sphere, the portion of the sphere comprehended between them is called a [u]spherical segment[/u], and the portion of the surface of the sphere comprehended between them is called a [u]zone[/u].

The [u]bases[/u] of the segment are the sections of the sphere, and the [u]bases of the zone[/u] are the circumferences of the sections.

The [u]altitude[/u] of the segment or zone is the distance between the sections.

One of the cutting planes may be tangent to the sphere, in which case the zone or segment has but one base.

[u]437. Definition.[/u] While the semicircle [u]DAE[/u] (fig. 177) turning about the diameter [u]DE[/u] describes a sphere, every circular sector, as [u]DCF[/u] or [u]FCH[/u], describes a solid, which is called a [u]spherical sector[/u]. The [u]base[/u] of the sector is the zone generated by the arc [u]DF[/u], or [u]FH[/u].

[u]438. Theorem.[/u] Either side of a spherical triangle is less than the sum of the other two.

[u]Proof.[/u] From the centre [u]O[/u] (fig. 179) of the sphere draw the radii [u]OA[/u], [u]OB[/u], [u]OC[/u] to the vertices [u]A[/u], [u]B[/u], [u]C[/u] of the spherical triangle [u]ABC[/u]. The three plane angles [u]AOB[/u], [u]AOC[/u], [u]BOC[/u] form a solid angle at [u]O[/u]; and each of these angles is, by § 338, less than the sum of the other two. But they are measured by the arcs [u]AB[/u], [u]AC[/u], [u]BC[/u]; and, therefore, each of these arcs is less than the sum of the other two.
