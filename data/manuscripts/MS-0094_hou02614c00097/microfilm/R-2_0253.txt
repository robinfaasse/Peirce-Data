[u]Chapter V. Notations for Graphics.[/u]

§132. We shall use square brackets, [ ], to denote the [u]point[/u] anyway
determined by the places denoted by the letters written within
the brackets. Thus
\[ [\alpha\beta\gamma] \]
[del]will denote[/del]
is the point contained at once in the planes $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$.
\[ [\beta\underline{c}] \]
will denote the point where the ray, $\underline{c}$, cuts the plane $\beta$.

We shall use parentheses, ( ), to denote a [u]plane[/u]. Thus
\[ (B\underline{c}) \]
will denote the plane containing the point $B$ and the ray
$\underline{c}$.

We shall use braces, { }, to denote rays. Thus,
\[ \{\alpha\beta\} \]
will be the ray contained in the two planes $\alpha$ and $\beta$. So
\[ \{AB\} \]
will be the ray through the two points $A$ and $B$.

[u]Theorem 2.1[/u]. If two rays $\underline{a}$ and $\underline{b}$ are concurrent with
two others, $\underline{c}$ and $\underline{d}$, then
\[ \{[\underline{a}\underline{c}][\underline{b}\underline{d}]\} = \{[\underline{a}\underline{d}][\underline{b}\underline{c}]\}. \]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. For $[\underline{a}\underline{c}]$ write $P$ for $[\underline{b}\underline{d}]$ write $Q$;
for $[\underline{a}\underline{d}]$ write $R$ for $[\underline{b}\underline{c}]$ write $S$.
Then $\{[\underline{a}\underline{c}][\underline{b}\underline{d}]\} = \{PQ\}$. Since $\underline{a}$ contains $P$ and also $R$,
$\underline{a} = \{PR\}$;
