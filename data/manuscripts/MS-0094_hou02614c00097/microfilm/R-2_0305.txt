§151 [u]Theorem 29[/u]. [u]The metron has six degrees of freedom[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. For any particle of it can [ins]be[/ins] carried to any point of space. Now there is a triply-continuous multitude of points in space; so that there are 3 degrees of freedom in that possibility. Next any straight fibre of the metron [del]through[/del] that particle can be carried into any ray through that point. Now there is a doubly-continuous multitude of rays through every point; so that there are 2 more degrees of freedom in that possibility. Finally any flat lamina of the metron containing that [del]fibre[/del] can be carried into any plane through that ray. Now there is a singly-continuous multitude of planes containing any [del]one[/del] [ins]one[/ins] ray; so that there is an additional degree of freedom in that possibility. But that lamina, fibre, and particle being fixed respectively in a plane, a ray, and a point no further motion is possible; so that in all there are just 3 plus 2 plus 1, or 6 degrees of freedom. [u]Q.E.D.[/u] This demonstration mutatis mutandis applies even to a non-optical metron.
