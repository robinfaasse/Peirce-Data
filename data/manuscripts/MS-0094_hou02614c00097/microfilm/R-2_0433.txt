CH. XI. § 192.] SIMILAR POLYGONS.

[u]To divide a line in extreme and mean ratio.[/u]

[u]Proof[/u]. Join [u]AC[/u] and [u]BD[/u]. In the triangles [u]AOC[/u] and
[u]BOD[/u], the angle [u]O[/u] is common, and the angles [u]BAC[/u] and
[u]BDC[/u] are equal, by § 108; these triangles are, there-
fore, similar, by § 173, and give the proportion
[u]AO[/u] : [u]DO[/u] = [u]CO[/u] : [u]BO[/u].

191. [u]Theorem[/u]. If, from a point [u]O[/u] (fig. 106), taken
without a circle, a tangent [u]OC[/u] and a secant [u]OA[/u] be
drawn, the tangent is a mean proportional between the
entire secant and the part without the circle, that is,
[u]AO[/u] : [u]CO[/u] = [u]CO[/u] : [u]BO[/u].

[u]Proof[/u]. When, in (fig. 105), the secant [u]OC[/u] is turned
about the point [u]O[/u] until it becomes a tangent, as in (fig.
106), the points [u]C[/u] and [u]D[/u] must coincide, [u]CO[/u] must be
equal to [u]DO[/u], and the proportion (fig. 105)
[u]AO[/u] : [u]DO[/u] = [u]CO[/u] : [u]BO[/u],
becomes (fig. 106) [u]AO[/u] : [u]CO[/u] = [u]CO[/u] : [u]BO[/u].

192. [u]Problem[/u]. To divide a given line [u]AB[/u] (fig. 107)
at the point [u]C[/u] in extreme and mean ratio, that is, so that
we may have the proportion
[u]AB[/u] : [u]AC[/u] = [u]AC[/u] : [u]CB[/u].

[u]Solution[/u]. At [u]B[/u] erect the perpendicular [u]BD[/u] equal to
half of [u]AB[/u]. Join [u]AD[/u], take [u]DE[/u] equal to [u]BD[/u], and [u]AC[/u]
equal to [u]AE[/u], and [u]C[/u] is the required point of division.

[u]Proof[/u]. Describe the semicircumference [u]EBF[/u] with the
radius [u]DB[/u] to meet [u]AD[/u] produced in [u]F[/u]; and, by the pre-
ceding proposition,
[u]AF[/u] : [u]AB[/u] = [u]AB[/u] : [u]AE[/u];
and, by the theory of proportions,
[u]AF[/u] : [u]AF[/u] - [u]AB[/u] = [u]AE[/u] : [u]AB[/u] - [u]AE[/u].
