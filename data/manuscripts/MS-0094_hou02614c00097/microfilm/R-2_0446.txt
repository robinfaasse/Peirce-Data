[pn]68[/pn] PLANE GEOMETRY. [CH. XII. § 232.

[u]Homologous Sides of Regular Polygons.[/u]

[u]Solution.[/u] Through the points [u]A[/u], [u]B[/u], [u]C[/u], [u]D[/u], &c. draw the tangents [u]A'B'[/u], [u]B'C'[/u], [u]C'D'[/u], &c. and [u]A'B'C'D'[/u] is the required polygon.

[u]Proof.[/u] The triangles [u]AB'B[/u], [u]BC'C[/u], &c. are by § 151, isosceles; they are also equal, for the sides [u]AB[/u], [u]BC[/u], &c. are equal, and the angles [u]BAB'[/u], [u]ABB'[/u], [u]CBC'[/u], [u]BCC'[/u], &c. are equal because they are measured by the halves of the equal arcs [u]AB[/u], [u]BC[/u], &c. Hence the angles [u]A'[/u], [u]B'[/u], &c. are equal, and the sides [u]A'B'[/u], [u]B'C'[/u], &c. are equal, and [u]A'B'C'[/u] &c. is a regular polygon of the same number of sides with [u]ABC[/u] &c.

231. [u]Corollary.[/u] A regular polygon of 4, 8, 16, &c.; 3, 6, 12, &c.; 5, 10, 20, &c.; 15, 30, 60, &c. sides; or, one similar to any given regular polygon may, therefore, be circumscribed about a circle by means of §§ 212-221, and 228.

232. [u]Theorem.[/u] The homologous sides of regular polygons of the same number of sides are to each other as the radii of their circumscribed circles, and also as the radii of their inscribed circles.

[u]Proof.[/u] Let [u]ABCD[/u], &c., [u]A'B'C'D'[/u], &c. (fig. 126) be regular polygons of the same number of sides, and let [u]O[/u], [u]O'[/u] be their centres; [u]OA[/u], [u]O'A'[/u] are the radii of their circumscribed circles, and the perpendiculars [u]OP[/u], [u]O'P'[/u] are the radii of their inscribed circles.

Join [u]OB[/u], [u]O'B'[/u]. The triangles [u]OAB[/u], [u]O'A'B'[/u] are similar, by § 173, for the angle [u]OAB[/u] = [u]OBA[/u] = [u]O'B'A'[/u] = [u]O'A'B'[/u] for each is half the angle [u]ABC[/u] = [u]A'B'C'[/u]. Hence, by § 198,

[u]OP[/u] : [u]O'P'[/u] = [u]AB[/u] : [u]A'B'[/u] = [u]OA[/u] : [u]O'A'[/u].
