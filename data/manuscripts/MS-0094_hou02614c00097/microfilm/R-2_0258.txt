The [ins]four[/ins] points $A, B, C,$ and $D$ may themselves be conceived
to be expressed [del]in terms[/del] [ins]as syzygies[/ins] of four other points, as $P, Q, R, S$.
Then, if in the expression of a point in terms of $A, B, C, D$,
we replace these four letters by their equivalents in terms of
$P, Q, R, S$, we have the point expressed directly by a syzygy
of $P, Q, R, S$; and if the multipliers of $A, B, C, D$ add up to $1$,
and the multipliers of $P, Q, R, S$ in [ins]each of[/ins] the expressions for
$A, B, C, D$ add up to $1$, so also will the multipliers of
$P, Q, R, S$ in every expression resulting from the substitution.
Thus if in the expression
$$a A + b B + c C + \overline{1-a-b-c} D$$
we substitute for $A$
$$A = p P + q Q + r R + \overline{1-p-q-r} S$$
we get
$$ap P + aq Q + ar R + \overline{a-ap-aq-ar} S + b B + c C + \overline{1-a-b-c} D$$
and the multipliers all add up to $1$. For all are cancelled in
such a sum except only $1$.

As for those syzygies whose multipliers add up to $0$, it is
very evident that if for $A, B, C, D$ be substituted syzygies
whose multipliers add up to $1$, the result will be a syzygy
whose multipliers add up to $0$.
