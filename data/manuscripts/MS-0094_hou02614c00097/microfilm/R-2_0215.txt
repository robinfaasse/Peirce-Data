Numbers of Places of No Freedom.

[u]§112 Definition 150.[/u] A regular condition is one which prescribes that one omaloid is to contain or be contained in another, or which is compounded of such prescriptions.

[u]Theorem 15.[/u] The only [del]possible regular conditions which[/del] [ins]restrictions upon the freedom of[/ins] a point [del]can fulfil[/del] [ins]are[/ins] equivalent to requiring it to lie in one, two, or three planes.

[u]Demonstration.[/u] Regular conditions for a point must require it to be contained in a certain number of planes, rays, and points. It cannot fulfil the condition of lying in four planes unless these are so situated that in lying on three of them it thereby lies in the fourth, so that that part of the condition is of no effect. The condition that a point should lie in a [del]plane[/del] [ins]ray[/ins] is equivalent to requiring it to lie in two planes having that ray in common; for every ray is the whole of what two planes contain in common. The condition that a point should coincide with a fixed point is equivalent to requiring it to lie at once in a ray and in a plane; for every point is the whole common contents of a [del]plane[/del] ray and a plane. Thus, every regular condition is equivalent to requiring the point to lie in a plane or planes, and these can be equivalent to three at most. Q.E.D.
