§161. [u]Theorem 38[/u]. If the motion of the metron is such that there is any point of space infinitely quasi-distant from [del]any[/del] [ins]some[/ins] other, then there is a surface such that in no possible motion [ins]of the metron[/ins] is any particle carried to or away from that surface, nor is any plane carried into or out of contact with it.

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let [u][O][/u] be any point, ordinary in its metrical relations, and let [u][P][/u] be a point infinitely quasi-distant from the former. If the quasi-translation along {OP} as axis is hyperbolic there are only two points infinitely distant from [O]; if that quasi-translation is parabolic, there is only one. But all points on the axis, [ins]except P[/ins] are infinitely distant from P, because they are at finite distances from [O]. Thus let [u][Q][/u] be any point, then
[del]{OQ}[/del]
|PQ = |OP [ins]±[/ins] |OQ.
In the case of hyperbolic translation if |OP = ∞ and |OQ = ∞, then |PQ = 2∞ = ∞.

Thus, no translation [ins]of the metron[/ins] along {OP} can move the particle at [u][P][/u]. If the metron is moved so as to bring the fibre in {OP} into any other through [O], there are still only at most two points on that ray infinitely quasi-distant [del][O][/del]; and to one of these the particle at [u][P][/u] must be carried.
