[u]Definition 192[/u]. A right triangle is a triangle having a
right angle. The side opposite the right angle is called the
hypothenuse, the others the legs.

Theorem 50. The square on the hypothenuse of a right
triangle is equal to the sum of the squares on the legs.

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let ABC be a [del]right[/del] triangle right angled at
[fig]Right triangle ABC with altitude BP[/fig] B. [del]For[/del] Let {PB} be a ray through [B]
perpendicular to {AC} and let [P] be
the intersection of the two rays.
Then the triangles APB and ABC are similar
For they have the angle A in common, and [del]it[/del] $\angle$APB = $\angle$ABC for
both are right angles. The third angle being two right angles less the sum of
the others is equal in the two triangles. But triangles whose angles are [ins]all[/ins] equal
[del]are[/del] similar. For the similar reasons the triangles ABC and BPC are similar.
We have then
|AP : |AB = |AB : |AC
|PC : |BC = |BC : |AC

Multiplying means and extremes
|AP x |AC = (|AB)$^{2}$
|PC x |AC = (|BC)$^{2}$

Adding
(|AC)$^{2}$ = (|AP)$^{2}$ + (|BC)$^{2}$

Now the square [del]on a[/del] line is proportional to the square
of the line. Hence, etc.
