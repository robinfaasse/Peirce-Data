[u]§ 9 Preliminary explanation of the nature of Geometry.[/u]

[u]Definition 32.[/u]
Mathematics is the art which traces out the consequences of hypotheses. The mathematician may frame his hypothesis so as to conform, as closely as convenient, to [del]his[/del] [ins]something[/ins] he believes to exist in nature, But the framing of the hypothesis is not, strictly speaking, mathematician's business. It is generally a matter of consultation between the mathematician and his [del]employer[/del] [ins]client[/ins], say, for example, the physicist. The mathematical work, proper, begins with a hypothesis already assumed. The mathematician is thus merely reasoning about his own [ins]mental[/ins] creations; and for that reason, although mistakes are quite possible in [del]mathematics[/del], and have in fact been committed by the greatest adepts, even by generations of acute minds, yet practical certainty is attainable in mathematics alone of all branches of [ins]purely[/ins] human knowledge.

[u]Definition 33.[/u]
Since all the subjects of the mathematician's discourse [del]are figments of the brain[/del], when we speak of the existence of anything in mathematics, we mean its existence as a part of the hypothesis.
