[u]Scholium[/u]. The above are [ins]called[/ins] the five fundamental numbers
of intersectional geometry, since all others can be calculated

[u]Scholium[/u] The student may be puzzled by [del]the fact that[/del]
two rays generally have no point in common, [del]but[/del] [ins]while[/ins] if they
are coplanar they have one point. But this is because a
point, whose freedom is 3, when required to [del]lie[/del] [ins]be[/ins] in two
rays has its freedom reduced to -1. While when required
to lie in a plane and in the intersections of this plane
with two other planes its freedom is zero. Of course, degrees
of freedom may be changed by peculiar relations being
imposed upon the elements of the figure.
