[u]CH. VII. § 63.][/u] POLYGONS. [u]19[/u]

The greatest Side of a Triangle opposite the greater Angle.

[del]Proof. 1.[/del] Suppose the angle C > B (fig. 34). Draw CD so as to make the angle BCD = B.
Then will BD = CD
and AB = AD + DB = AD + DC.
But AD + DC > AC
Hence AB > AC.

[del]2. Conversely. Suppose AB > AC, the angle C must be greater than B; for if C were equal to or less than B, AB would by § 61 and the preceding demonstration, be equal to or less than AC.[/del]

[u]Corollaries[/u] Since two angles of a triangle cannot be equal unless the sides opposite them are equal, it follows that if two sides of a triangle are unequal the angle opposite the greater is greater than the angle opposite the less.
