[u]Intersection-figures, and their disappearance in pairs.[/u] [pn]110[/pn]

§55. A boundary, being a place where one region suddenly ends and another begins, is a place of discontinuity; and we naturally expect some particular manifestation of this discontinuity. This we find in the phenomenon of the coalescence and disappearance of points of intersection. When a body with sharp outlines moves across a fixed place with sharp boundaries, we see points of intersection running together. We do not mean that the particles of the body run together, nor that the points of the fixed place run together. But the congress of the sharp outline of the body with the [del]sharp[/del] [ins]abrupt[/ins] boundary of the place gives birth to a new form, the intersection-figure, consisting either of a row of points [ins]in a fixed line[/ins] (or of a curve on a fixed surface. This intersection-figure is particularly mobile. While the body [del]moves[/del] [ins]is moving[/ins] [del]a[/del] [ins]but[/ins] little, the change of the intersection-figure is often vast. And in [ins]almost[/ins] every case, we soon see two of the mobile points of the intersection-figure running together. Their speed of approach increases as they [del]approach[/del] [ins]draw near to one another[/ins], until at the instant when they meet it becomes infinite, and this infinite speed carries the two points into "Imaginary Space,"—a part of the [del]secret &[/del] ideal space [ins]to[/ins] which Elementary Geometry is a stranger. What meets our [del]mind's[/del] gaze is the two points running together [del]faster &[/del] faster without limit, becoming one for a single instant only, and then disappearing [del]permanently[/del] [ins]for good[/ins].
