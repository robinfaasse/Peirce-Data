§ 45. Let us take a few examples. Consider a pentagonal do-
decahedron. [ins]Suppose the question is how many summits it has.[/ins] Here the connective valency of the whole surface
is 2. The valency of each of the 12 faces is 1, making 12
in all. Each face is a pentagon, and has 5 edges. That would
make 5 times 12, or 60, edges; [del]But[/del] [ins]but[/ins] each edge belongs to two
faces; so that there are only half as many, in all, or 30. So
the formula is
2 = 12 - 30 + Number of Summits
There are therefore 20 summits.

Suppose the question is, how many faces can all touch one
another upon the surface of an anchor ring. The valency of
the whole surface is zero. The valency of each face is 1. The
number of edges, [ins]since each face touches all the rest, and each edge belongs to two faces,[/ins] is one half of the number of faces multiplied
by one less than the number of faces. If three faces come together
at each summit, the number of summits is one third of the product
of the number of faces into one less than that number. The edges will
thus exceed the summits by [del]1/6 of the[/del] [ins]1/2 less 1/3, or 1/6, of the[/ins]
product of the number of faces into one less than that number.
Hence this product is [del]6 times the number of faces; for[/del]
[del]the number of faces diminished by 1/6 of the product equals zero,[/del] [ins]as is formula[/ins]
the valency of the whole surface. Hence, 6 is one less than the
number of faces, or 7 faces can each touch all the others
on the surface of a ring. If the student tries to mark them on a
ring he will see the utility of our formula for answering such a
question.
