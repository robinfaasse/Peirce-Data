Chapter I. Geometrical Elements.

§ 173 [Scholium. A geometrical [u]surface[/u] is a [ins]Place[/ins]
thinner than any [u]Gold leaf[/u]. It is a mere cleft
as between a submerged [u]stone[/u] and the water about
it. A geometrical [u]line[/u] is [ins]a Place[/ins] finer than any spider-
line. It is the mere crack [ins]at[/ins] between a partially submerged
stone the air and the water. A geometrical [u]point[/u] is a
place smaller than any needle point, as between
four bodies which fit perfectly together.]

§ 174. [Scholium. A line which viewed from the end
is foreshortened so as to cover but one point in
the sky is a right line or ray. [u]Direction[/u] is pointing
to a particular fixed point on the sky. A right line
has one direction throughout. The direction of a
curved line changes continually along its length.
A line has two opposite directions, but we suppose one
of them to be marked upon it as positive, and its proper
direction. A [del]s[/del] surface which fits upon itself
however much it be slid or turned about, [ins]planes[/ins] and even
when reversed, is called a plane. The being exposed
squarely to a particular point of the sky is called its aspect
