[u]BOOK V METRICS[/u]
[u]PART I THE PHILOSOPHY OF METRICS.[/u]
[u]Chapter I. Principles of Measurement[/u]

§448.
[u]Definition 176.[/u]
[u]Metrics[/u] is that part of geometry which treats of measurements in space.

[u]Definition 177[/u] [u]Measurement[/u] is the application of a scale of numbers. A [u]scale[/u] is a continuous series of numbers which can be shifted continuously so that the continuity and sequence are preserved. This shifting has but one degree of freedom so that, in general, if one point of the [del]scale[/del] [ins]scale[/ins] is fixed it cannot be shifted along itself.

[u]Scholium[/u]. There are two fundamental kinds of space-measurement, namely, the measurement of [del]points[/del] [ins]the positions of points[/ins] along a line [del]of generation[/del] and the measurement of the [del]positions[/del] [ins]positions[/ins] of surfaces round a line [del]of intersection[/del]. But we shall limit ourselves to optical measurement.

[u]Definition 178[/u] [u]Optical measurement[/u] is space measurement in which the line of measurement is a straight line, or ray, and in which the surfaces whose positions are measured are planes.
