If therefore the rule holds for any number of lengths on a side,
that the number of little triangles in the composite triangle
is the square of that number, it will also hold for one number
greater, and thus for all numbers. But it does hold for $N=1$.
Hence it holds in all cases. Now all these triangles are in all
respects equal. They are therefore of equal area; and consequently
the area of the composite triangle is proportionate to the number
of lengths of the small one. Now these small ones may be made
indefinitely small and thus measure the length of large triangles with
indefinite closeness of approximation. Consequently the areas of
similar triangles are always proportionate to the squares of the lengths
of corresponding sides.
