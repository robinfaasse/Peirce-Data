[del]65.[/del] [ins]33.[/ins] In Book II. Chap. iii, where we studied the bucket of water with the lump of ice, we remarked that points naturally occur in pairs. Nevertheless, we saw in Chap iv that planes are considered by geometers to be surfaces on which two self-returning lines may intersect in one point only. We are thus led to remark that surfaces belong to different categories upon which different sorts of lines with different sorts of intersections are possible.

[del]Definition [del]37.[/del][/del] [ins]Definition 34.[/ins] A perissad surface is a closed surface on which any number of lines may be drawn, every pair of these lines cutting one another in a odd number of points, all such points being distinct.

Definition [del]38[/del] [ins]35[/ins] An artiad, or globular surface is a closed surface upon which every pair of self-returning lines intersect an even number of times, zero being considered as an even number. A sphere is an example of an artiad surface.
