[u]Chapter III[/u]. Knots.

[u]Definition 115[/u].
50. A Knot is flexible unbreakable thing [ins]of a certain number of dimensions in itself, but occupying two more, and in those[/ins] [del]extended chiefly[/del] not reducible to a simple form. Or, a knot may be called a fibre which cannot [del]be bent[/del] by any bending be made to lie on a globular surface without crossing itself; but which can be so spread out on a surface of higher connectivity. The following diagrams illustrate this. The dotted parts are supposed to be on the further side of the surface

[fig]Trefoil knot on a sphere-like surface[/fig] [fig]Knot on a torus-like surface[/fig]

It is [del]im[/del]possible to draw an evanescible circuit on a surface on which a knot is drawn without cutting the knot; but such a circuit will itself make a knot. A circuit not making a knot will cut the knot at least twice.

[u]Definition 116[/u]. A linkage is a knot composed of several distinct lines. The single lines are not necessarily knotted.

[u]Definition 117[/u]. An interlacing is a linkage in which no pair of lines is linked, but only triplets.
