If, now, the triangle $ACF$ is subtracted from the whole figure $ABCE$, the remainder is $ABFE$; and if $BDE$ is subtracted from the whole figure, the remainder is $ABCD$. Hence, as $ABCD$ and $ABFE$ are the remainders, after taking equal triangles from the same figure, they must be equivalent.

246. [u]Corollary.[/u] A parallelogram is equivalent to a rectangle of the same base and altitude.

247. [u]Corollary.[/u] The area of a parallelogram is the product of its base by its altitude.

248. [u]Corollary.[/u] Parallelograms of the same base are to each other as their altitudes; and those of the same altitude are to each other as their bases.

249. [u]Problem.[/u] Every triangle is half of a parallelogram of the same base and altitude.

[u]Proof.[/u] For the triangle $ABC$ (fig. 39) is, by § 77, half of the parallelogram $ABCD$ of the same base and altitude, and it is, therefore, by § 245, half of any parallelogram of the same base and altitude.

250. [u]Corollary.[/u] All triangles of the same base and altitude are equivalent.

251. [u]Corollary.[/u] The area of a triangle is half the product of its base by its altitude.

252. [u]Corollary.[/u] Triangles of the same base are to each other as their altitudes, and triangles of the same altitude are to each other as their bases.
