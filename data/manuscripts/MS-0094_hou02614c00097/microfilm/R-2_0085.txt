Thus, suppose the line ABC is horizontal from A to B and vertical from B to C. Then a particle moving from A to B would have an endless series of positions in which its motion was horizontal. Hence as B is the limit, it would be moving horizontally at B. The same thing is true from B to C except that here the motion is vertical. B is the limit of the inverse series, therefore [ins]the particle[/ins] is moving vertically at B. Thus, the particle must be capable of moving two ways at once [del]or it must remain[/del] [ins]or[/ins] a lapse of time at B (which it cannot do if it has any velocity when it gets there) or its velocity must be diminishing and [ins]must[/ins] vanish at B. [del]The latter case is what must always happen. No force however great can make a particle move two ways at once.
