[pn]132[/pn] SOLID GEOMETRY. [CH. XVIII. § 445.

[u]Sides and Angles of polar Triangle.[/u]

[u]444. Definition.[/u] The triangle $DEF$ is called the [u]polar triangle[/u] of $ABC$, and in the same way $ABC$ is the [u]polar triangle[/u] of $DEF$.

As several different triangles might be formed by producing the sides $DE$, $EF$, and $DF$, we shall limit ourselves to the one $DEF$, such that the pole $D$ of $BC$ is on the same side of $BC$ with the vertex $A$; $E$ is on the same side of $AC$ with the vertex $B$; and $F$ is on the same side of $AB$ with the vertex $C$.

[u]445. Theorem.[/u] If the sides and angles of a spherical triangle and of its polar triangle are expressed in degrees, minutes, &c., the sides of either triangle thus expressed are respectively supplements of the angles of the other triangle.

[u]Proof.[/u] Produce the sides $AB$, $AC$ (fig. 181), if necessary, to $G$ and $H$.

Since $F$ is the pole of $AB$, and $E$ the pole of $AC$, we have, by § 425,
$$EH = FG = 90^{\circ}.$$

Hence
$$EF = EH + HF = 90^{\circ} + HF$$
$$GH = GF - HF = 90^{\circ} - HF,$$
and, therefore,
$$EF + GH = 180^{\circ}.$$

But, by § 441 and 442,
$$GH = \text{the angle } BAC,$$
hence
$$EF + \text{the angle } BAC = 180^{\circ};$$
that is, the side $EF$ and the angle $BAC$ are supplements of each other.
