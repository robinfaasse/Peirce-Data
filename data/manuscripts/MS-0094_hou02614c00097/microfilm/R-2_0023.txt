The systematic way of doing that is to call the occurrence next before one, [u]1[/u], [u]zero[/u], [u]0[/u], the [ins]occurrence[/ins] next before that [u]minus one[/u], [u]-1[/u], the occurrence next before that [u]minus two[/u], [u]-2[/u], and so back endlessly.

[marginalia]Definition 3.[/marginalia]
[u]Minus[/u] is the latin for "less", and means "less than zero." Thus, [u]-2[/u] is two less than zero.

[marginalia]Definition 4[/marginalia]
The numbers less than zero are called [u]negative[/u] numbers; those greater than zero are called [u]positive[/u] numbers.

[marginalia]Definition 5.[/marginalia]
The positive numbers may for distinction, when desired, have [u]+[/u] written before each, to be read [u]plus[/u], "more", meaning "more than zero." Thus, 4 may be written +4, [del]and read[/del] [ins]and read[/ins] "plus four", i.e. four [del]more than zero[/del].

[marginalia]Definition 6.[/marginalia]
The character + or - written, or understood, before a number is called the [u]sign[/u] of the number. [del]Plus[/del] is called the [u]positive sign[/u]; [u]minus[/u], the [u]negative sign[/u]. Every number [ins]of this system[/ins] is understood to have a sign; though the [ins]sign of a[/ins] positive number is not usually written.

[marginalia]Definition 7[/marginalia]
The figure which remains after the sign is stricken off is said to express the [u]absolute value[/u], or [u]modulus[/u], of the number. Some mathematicians say the modulus is [u]signless[/u]; others that it is positive. This is a mere question of phraseology.

[marginalia]Definition 8.[/marginalia]
The signs of two numbers are either [u]like[/u] or [u]contrary[/u].

[marginalia]Definition 9[/marginalia]
This system of numbers is called the system of [u]real integers[/u].
