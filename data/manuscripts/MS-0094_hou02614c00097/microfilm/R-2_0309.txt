[u]Definition 102[/u]. That continuum of planes through which a flat lamina moves in being rotated from one plane to another about their common ray is called an [u]angular space[/u]. The part of the motion occupying an angular space is a [u]wedge[/u]. The number of times a wedge has to be placed in partial angular spaces in order to successively [del]to fill[/del] [ins]occupy[/ins] all the parts of a larger angular space, is said to be the number of times the [u]angular quantity[/u] of the wedge has to be multiplied to [del]give[/del] [ins]measure[/ins] the [u]angular quantity[/u] of the large angular space. [del]The angular quantity of[/del] [ins]The magnitude of[/ins] the angular space between two planes is also called the [u]inclination[/u] of those planes. There are two different angular spaces between any two planes; for a lamina in being rotated from one to the other can be rotated either way, that is, in either "sense". [del]These[/del] [ins]The[/ins] quantities of these angular spaces evidently amount, summed, to a circuit. They are said to be [u]supplements[/u] of one another. The smaller of them is the principal [del]value of the[/del] inclination, or the inclination simply. An angular quantity has but one value. An angular quantity is often called an [u]angle[/u].
