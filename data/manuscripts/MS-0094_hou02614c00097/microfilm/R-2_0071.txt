[u]BOOK III TOPOLOGY[/u].

Chapter I [del]Elementary[/del] [ins]Fundamentals[/ins]. [u]Generation, Intersection, Enclosure[/u].

§24. Topology studies points, lines, surfaces, and spaces as occupied by particles, fibres, laminae, and spacious bodies which are quite plastic, and can be distorted and compressed or expanded to any extent, so long as their continuity and mutual abutments are not destroyed.

[u]Generation and Intersection[/u].

§25. We have seen [del]that to be in[/del] [ins]at?[/ins] a point is an individual spatial character; [del]it is[/del] a complete geometrical determination with [del]no[/del] [ins]every particle of[/ins] [del]room[/del] [ins]definite[/ins] [del]for[/del] [ins]requiring[/ins] [del]geometrical[/del] [ins]anything to occupy[/ins] [del]variation[/del] [ins]a point[/ins] [del]and it cannot move or nor squirm even squirm.[/del]

A particle, which at any one instant occupies a point merely, [del]in[/del] the course of time occupies a line. Thus, the character of being in a line is more general than that of being [ins]at[/ins] a point. So a fibre [ins]or filament[/ins], which at each instant occupies a line merely, [del]in[/del] the course of time occupies a surface; so that the character of being in a surface is more general still. And a lamina, which at each instant occupies a surface, [del]in[/del] the course of time occupies a solid space, which is thus another generalization.

The spacious body is the real thing, and generalization can be carried no further.
