[pn]CH. XVI. § 335.][/pn] SOLIDS. [pn]123[/pn]

<u>Solids of a Frustum of a Pyramid or Cone.</u>

$\frac{1}{3} V \times H - \frac{1}{3} V' \times H'$,

and, for the sum of three pyramids, which have $H''$ for
their altitude and for their bases $V$, $V'$ and the mean pro-
portional $\sqrt{VV'}$ between $V$ and $V'$,

$\frac{1}{3} H'' \times (V + V' + \sqrt{VV'})$
$= \frac{1}{3} H'' \times V + \frac{1}{3} H'' \times V' + \frac{1}{3} H'' \times \sqrt{VV'}$,

and we are to prove that these solidities are equal, or that

$V \times H - V' \times H' = V \times H'' + V' \times H'' + \sqrt{VV'} \times H''$.

Now $H'' = H - H'$,

and, by § 379,

$V : V' = H^2 : H'^2$,

whence $\sqrt{V} : \sqrt{V'} = H : H'$,

and, multiplying extremes and means

$\sqrt{V} \times H' = \sqrt{V'} \times H$.

If we multiply this equation successively by $\sqrt{V}$ and $\sqrt{V'}$
we obtain, by transposing the members of the first pro-
duct,

$\sqrt{VV'} \times H = V \times H'$,
$\sqrt{VV'} \times H' = V' \times H$;

the difference between which is

$\sqrt{VV'} \times (H - H') = V \times H' - V' \times H$, or
$\sqrt{VV'} \times H'' = V \times H' - V' \times H$.

And if we add to this last equation the equations

$V \times H'' = V \times H - V \times H'$
$V' \times H'' = V' \times H - V' \times H'$,

we get, by cancelling the terms which destroy each other,

$\sqrt{VV'} \times H'' + V \times H'' + V' \times H'' = V \times H - V' \times H'$,

which is the equation to be proved, and the solidity of the
frustum is therefore equal to

$\frac{1}{3} H'' \times (V + V' + \sqrt{VV'})$.
