Difference of aspect is a quantity of the same nature
as difference of direction. A plane has one aspect
all over one side; but a curved surface has a gradu-
ally varying aspect. A plane has two opposite aspects;
but we [del]shall[/del] assume that one of these is marked in
some way, so as to distinguish it as positive.

An [u]angle[/u] is the distance, or difference of
direction, of two points in the sky. Such distances
can be added and subtracted under the same
condition as distances in long measure, namely
that they are measured along the directest
way.

[fig]Line segment with points A, B, and C[/fig]

The distance [del]between two points[/del] from [A] to [C], or
|AC equals the distance from [A] to [B], or |AB, plus
the distance from [B] to [C] or |BC, on condition
that [B] is in a straight path from [A] to [C]. It
is the same in the sky.

The angle of one right line to another is the
difference of their directions. That is, each being prolonged
in the positive sense until it pierces the sky
the distance between the two vanishing points
in the sky is the angle between the lines. The point
of intersection of the right lines (if they do intersect) is the
vertex of the angle, the two directed rays its legs.
