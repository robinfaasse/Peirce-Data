[u]Enclosure[/u]

§ 29. Definition 85. A [u]place[/u] is said to be [u]topologically enclosed[/u] by another place when [del]anything which occupies it[/del] [ins]there is [del]no way[/del] [ins]no path[/ins] by which a particle situated in [del]it[/del] [ins]the former place[/ins][/ins] cannot move [del]to[/del] somewhere without passing through the place which is said to enclose [del]it[/del] [ins]the former[/ins].

In ordinary parlance we distinguish the [u]inside[/u] and [u]outside[/u]. The outside is that side of a boundary on which lies the firmament, or infinitely distant part of space. But the study of the firmaments belongs to Metrics. Neither topology nor graphics recognize it as different from any other surface. Consequently, for topology there is no distinction of inside and outside, except that they are different sides of a boundary; and in topological thought the boundary encloses the outside just as much as it does the inside.

If [ins]the surface of[/ins] a planet is part land and part water, then disregarding quantitative considerations we may equally say that there is a continent [del]with[/del] [ins]surrounding[/ins] a lake, or an ocean [del]with[/del] [ins]surrounding[/ins] an island. [ins]Indeed,[/ins] In like manner, [del]space[/del] since space returns into itself, if it contains a number of hollow shells, any one may be regarded as containing all the others.

Definition 86. An unbounded surface is called a [u]closed surface[/u], a not strictly accurate expression, since the word ought to mean that the inside is entirely separate from the outside, which depends on the constitution of space. [del]But[/del] Thus, a plane in ordinary space though unbounded ought not in strict accuracy to be called closed. Nevertheless, we shall so denominate it.

Definition 87. A self-returning line which is a boundary, or one of the boundaries of a surface, is called a [u]rim[/u] of that surface; and the surface is called a [u]rimmed[/u], that is, a bounded surface.
