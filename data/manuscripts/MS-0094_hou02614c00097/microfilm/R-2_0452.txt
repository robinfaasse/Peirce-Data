256. [u]Theorem.[/u] The square described upon the hypothenuse of a right triangle is equivalent to the sum of the squares described upon the other two sides.

[u]Proof.[/u] Let squares be constructed upon the three sides of the right triangle [u]ABC[/u] (fig. 130), right-angled at [u]B[/u]. From [u]B[/u] let fall upon [u]AC[/u] the perpendicular [u]BDE[/u], and the square [u]ACSR[/u] is divided into the two rectangles [u]ADER[/u] and [u]DCES[/u].

Now the area of [u]ADER[/u] is, by § 242, [u]AD[/u] [del]X[/del] [ins]x[/ins] [u]AR[/u] = [u]AD[/u] [del]X[/del] [ins]x[/ins] [u]AC[/u]; and the area of the square [u]ABNM[/u] is, by § 243, [u]AB[/u]².

But, by § 184,
[u]AD[/u] : [u]AB[/u] = [u]AB[/u] : [u]AC[/u];
or multiplying extremes and means,
[u]AB[/u]² = [u]AD[/u] [del]X[/del] [ins]x[/ins] [u]AC[/u];
that is, the square [u]ABNM[/u] is equivalent to the rectangle [u]ADER[/u].
