**363. Corollary.** Any two parallelopipeds of equivalent bases and the same altitude are equivalent.

**364. Corollary.** Parallelopipeds of the same base are to each other as their altitudes, and parallelopipeds of the same altitude are to each other as their bases.

**365. Theorem.** _The solidity of a triangular prism is the product of its base by its altitude._

_Proof._ Let $ABC\ DEF$ (fig. 170) be a triangular prism.

Draw $BG$ parallel to $AC$, $CG$ parallel to $AB$, $GH$ parallel to $AD$, meeting the plane $EDF$ in $H$. Join $EH$, $FH$; $AH$ is, evidently, a parallelopiped; and $BCG\ EFH$ is a triangular prism.

The triangular prisms $ABC\ DEF$ and $BCG\ EFH$ are equivalent, by § 355; since their altitude is the same and their bases $ABC$ and $BCG$ are equal, by § 77. Hence each of the prisms is half of the parallelopiped $AH$, and has half its measure, or the product of $\frac{1}{2}\ BCG$ by the altitude, that is, the product of its own base by its altitude.
