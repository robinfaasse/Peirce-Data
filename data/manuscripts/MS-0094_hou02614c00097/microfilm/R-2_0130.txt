§ 58. The branch of mathematics called [u]Algebra[/u] is of [del]an[/del] [ins]a degree of[/ins] importance in life not easily exaggerated. The great thing is to know enough of it to be able to think algebraically. This is to be acquired by exercise; and the best occasion for such exercise is when some subject presents itself [del]which[/del] the understanding of which is facilitated by algebra. [del]Of such subjects geometry is the very first.[/del] There is no subject more facilitated, perhaps none as much, as geometry. Therefore, whether the student is to [del]make[/del] [ins]take[/ins] a separate course in algebra, or not, — or even if he already knows something about it, — he cannot do better than to pay some attention to it [del]in the course of his study[/del] while he is studying geometry.

By algebra is usually meant that method of mathematical procedure in which quantities are denoted by letters.
