Second, suppose the concurrent rays are coplanar. Then
the three concurrent rays are a perspective delineation of three
concurrent rays not coplanar. Namely, we can assume the
plane $(OCI)$ as the plane of delineation, taking for the
station-point, which we may denote by $[O']$, any point not
in that plane. Then, in the plane $(O'OU)$, we may assume
any line [ins]through $[O']$[/ins] [del]of[/del] of which $\{OU\}$ becomes the delineation. We
draw the ray [ins]through $[O']$[/ins] $\{O'U\}$ and denote the point where it cuts the
new ray [del]by[/del] [ins]by[/ins] $[U']$. We draw the line $\{O'V\}$ and denote the point
where it cuts the new ray through $[O']$ by $[V']$. Then $[U]$ is the
delineation of $[U']$ and $[V]$ of $[V']$; while $[C],[G],[I],[J]$ are delineations
from the same centre of themselves. But we have just seen
that the intersections of $\{CI\}$ and $\{GJ\}$, of $\{CU'\}$ and $\{GV'\}$, of $\{IU'\}$ and
$\{JV'\}$ will lie on one ray. The delineation of this ray will
be a ray. Hence, the intersections of $\{CI\}$ and $\{GJ\}$, of $\{CU\}$ and $\{GV\}$,
of $\{IU\}$ and $\{JV\}$ will also lie on one ray, since these are the
delineations of the other set of intersections.
