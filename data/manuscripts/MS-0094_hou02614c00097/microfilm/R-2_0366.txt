§190. [u]Theorem 64[/u]. Three points in one ray cannot be equally distant from one point not in the [del]firmament[/del] [ins]firmament[/ins] [del]absolute[/del].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let [A], [B], [C], be three points in one ray {ABC}. And let [D] be a point [ins]neither[/ins] in the ray nor in the [del]absolute[/del] firmament.

[fig]Triangle with vertices D, A, B, C on a line[/fig]

Then it cannot be that [del]for[/del]
dist. DA = dist. DB = dist. DC.

For suppose this the case. Then by the Pons Asinorum
in the triangle ADC, ∠DAC = ∠DCA,
In the triangle ADB, ∠DAB = [del]∠DBA[/del] [ins]∠DBA[/ins], and
In the triangle BDC, ∠DBC = ∠DCB.

But if [B] be between [A] and [C],
∠DAB = ∠DAC
∠DCB = ∠DCA
∴ ∠DBA = ∠DBC = [fraction]1/2[/fraction] ◯

Hence all the rays {DA}, {DB}, {DC}, would be perpendicular to {AC} and yet contain a common point not in the [del]abs[/del] firmament, which is absurd.
