there came to him the Image of the colors arranged on a surface or in
space. Indeed, this arrangement may have been strictly simultaneous
with the reception of the First Impression. For whence did he get
the idea of spatial arrangement? Undoubtedly from a Habit, or
constitution, of the brain. So far, Kant was right in pronouncing
space to be the form of our intuitus. His principal error, in this matter,
lay in his never having been able to free himself from the Nomi-
nalistic notion that therefore space is not thoroughly Real;
whereas he ought to have said "and thereby and therein it is Real."

which is no man's Figment, nor that of any body of men; that
