[pn]2[/pn] supposed that the reader has much acquaintance with these other subjects, — at any rate, the kind of acquaintance that is wanted, and almost needed in beginning Logic, — I shall [ins]in this introduction[/ins] [del]here[/del] name these other subjects, (of which there are four,) and shall make such remarks upon each, as seem likely to be of service to a young person in approaching the proper subject of this Book.

The first of these four subjects is [ins]called[/ins] Mathematics, which includes at present Arithmetic, Geometry, and Algebra, about all three of which the reader very likely knows something. A more satisfactory explanation of what mathematics is shall, however, be given. But first before doing that something shall be said about the word "Mathematics."
