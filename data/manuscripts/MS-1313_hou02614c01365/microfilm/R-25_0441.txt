But he was [ins]at the same time,[/ins] by philosophical instinct, an idealistic evolutionist. Like the other Rosicrucians in both these respects, he was confronted with the problem, how are the manifold kinds of matter which chemistry reveals to us to be accounted for upon the principles of idealistic evolutionism? Whoever will ponder upon this problem, even at this day, will inevitably be led to a cosmology which will seem to dualistic and nominalistic minds to be most extravagant mysticism. This judgment may be in part just. Our first efforts to solve such difficult problems must be open, in some measure, to such criticism.
