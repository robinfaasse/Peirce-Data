When we once come to regard the [u]Triumph-Wagen[/u], no longer as antedating Coppernicus, but as a production of the age of Galileo, Harvey, Gilbert, and Keppler, it does not appear as a marvellous performance. The only circumstance at which one hesitates is that a solid scientific chemist, who mind moved in the world of reality and veracity, should have mixed the description of his experiments with so much degraded [del]nonsense[/del] [ins]bombast[/ins]. We can only surmise that the wealthy Thölde, or the master-spirit behind him, purchased those secrets of antimony from some indigent chemist and worked them into the otherwise nonsensical book in which they appear.
