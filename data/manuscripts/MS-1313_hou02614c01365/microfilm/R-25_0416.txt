The treatise [u]Von den natürlichen und über-natürlichen Dingen[/u]
is written in a somewhat plainer style. After repeating the doctrine
of the [u]prima tria principia[/u], it enters upon discussions concerning
biblical miracles, the doctrine of signatures, spirits, sirens,
succubae, etc. Chapter the second treats of the first "Tinctur-Wurzel"
of the metals, which, we are told, "is a supernatural, flying, fiery
Spirit, which keeps itself in the air, and naturally seeks its habitation
in the ground and in water." The remaining seven chapters treat in
cryptical style of the methods of dissolving the seven metals.

The [u]Triumph-Wagen des Antimonii[/u] contains, embedded in
vast masses of speechifying, about 30 plainly described
chemical preparations. Of these 17 are genuine descriptions
of experiments by a skillful chemist, and are distinguished from
the few perspicuous chemical directions that are to be found in
Raymond Lully, in Arnold de Villanova, and other medieval
