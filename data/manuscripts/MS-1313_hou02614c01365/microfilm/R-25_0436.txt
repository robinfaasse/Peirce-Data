It is impossible to suppose that the writer was not a [ins]highly[/ins]
accomplished chemist; no chemist would admit such a sup-
position. If there be any truth in him, he accomplished cures which
astounded physicians. Of course, he would exaggerate his cures;
but then his patients would do so, too. He could not but have
become famous as a healer. His medicines would be sought
after. He certainly did not wish to bury his chemical discoveries;
else he would not have written them down in the glowing enthu-
siasm he betrays. He must have been willing to communicate
them, as [ins]indeed,[/ins] he tells us he was. How, then, could it be that he
and his discoveries should pass completely out of memory and
escape all record for a century and a quarter? How is [del]it[/del] that
