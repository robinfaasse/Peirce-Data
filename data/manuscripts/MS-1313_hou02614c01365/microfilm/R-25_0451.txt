Who then was Thölde? He was a man of property, part owner of the salt
works at Frankenhausen; and he was [ins]chemical industry[/ins] for many years secretary of the
Rosicrucian Brotherhood,— a society founded on literary fraud and
soaked in it.

But if this was the case, we ought to find in the books Rosicrucian
doctrines. Some do. Besides a certain sensuous-mystical tone more
easily felt that illustrated in short citations, there is a warm passage
about the holiness of the passion of love which is hardly in place
in the mouth of a celibate. True, Valentine excepts love which is
"entzündet" by being "irregular"; but a Benedictine would have
sternly answered him that even the most legitimate passion contains
an element of sin. The constant talk about pagan gods and goddesses
sounds more Rosicrucian than monastic. Then, Valentine holds not
only that all animals, vegetables, and minerals have souls (which is
hardly orthodox) but also that such a species, as antimony has
a soul;— not this or that piece of antimony, understand, but anti-
mony in general, has a soul which feels and reasons. This peculiarly
and extremely Rosicrucian. The doctrine of the correspondence between
the Macrocosm and Microcosm is also Rosicrucian and heterodox.
In all the religious gush of which the Triumph-Wagen especially is full,
there is not the slightest reference to any church dogma, and nothing that
