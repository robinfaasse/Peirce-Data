[u]The Skew Mercator.[/u]

There is a [del]Central Great Circle[/del], represented on the map chart by the axis of abscissas.

The origin of coordinates is the point of this axis nearest the North Pole.

The axis of ordinates is the meridian through this point, and we reckon Longitudes from this [ins]same[/ins] meridian.

Let $\lambda$ be the Longitude so reckoned
$\varphi$ the Latitude.
$e$ the eccentricity of a meridian

Calling the compression $e = \frac{1}{294.98} = .0033900$ [ins]$(2 - \frac{1}{294.98}) \cdot .0033900$[/ins]
$= .0067686$
$\epsilon = \sqrt{[del].0067686[/del]} = .08227$

Denote by $r$ the radius vector on the polar stereographic projection.

$$r = \left(\frac{1 - \epsilon \sin \varphi}{1 + \epsilon \sin \varphi}\right)^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}} \tan (45^\circ + \frac{\varphi}{2})$$

For (Clarke's Geodesy, p. 104) The radius of curvature of a meridian is $\frac{a(1-\epsilon^2)}{(1-\epsilon^2\sin^2\varphi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ and that of a normal section perpendicular to the meridian is $\frac{a}{(1-\epsilon^2\sin^2\varphi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}$
