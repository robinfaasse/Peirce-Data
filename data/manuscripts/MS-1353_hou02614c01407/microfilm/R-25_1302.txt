[u]Varying Scale of Mercator[/u]

$\alpha$ Azimuth of geodesic
$u$ Reduced lat.
$U$ ditto of northernmost points of geodesic
$e$ Eccentricity of meridian

$\sin \alpha = \frac{\cos U}{\cos u}$

$\alpha' = 90^{\circ} - \alpha$
$\sin \alpha' = \sqrt{1 - \frac{\cos^{2} U}{\cos^{2} u}}$

$\cos U' = \sqrt{\cos^{2} u - \cos^{2} U}$

$\sin \alpha'_{2} = \frac{\sqrt{\cos^{2} u - \cos^{2} U}}{\cos u_{2}}$

$\alpha_{2} = 90^{\circ} - \alpha'_{2}$
$\sin \alpha_{2} = \frac{\sqrt{\cos^{2} u_{2} - \cos^{2} u + \cos^{2} U}}{\cos u_{2}}$

$\cos U'' = \sqrt{\cos^{2} u_{2} - \cos^{2} u + \cos^{2} U}$
$\therefore \sin^{2} U'' = \sin^{2} U - \sin^{2} u + \sin^{2} u_{2}$

Hence if [del]rectangular[/del] [ins]geodesics[/ins] [del]great circles[/del] are
drawn between [del]great circles[/del] whose northern-
most points are constant, the difference
of the squares of the sines of the reduced latitudes
of their extremities is constant, or
$\sin u \cdot d \sin u = \sin u_{2} d \sin u_{2}$

Let two such be drawn infinitely near, and
compare their infinitesimal segments
$ds = a \sqrt{1 - e^{2} \cos^{2} u} \cdot d\varphi = a \sqrt{1 - e^{2} \cos^{2} u} \frac{du}{\cos \alpha}$
$= a \frac{\sqrt{1 - e^{2} \cos^{2} u}}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{\cos^{2} U}{\cos^{2} u}}} du = [del]\text{f}[/del] a \frac{\sqrt{1 - e^{2} \cos^{2} u}}{\sqrt{\sin^{2} U - \sin^{2} u}} d \sin u$
