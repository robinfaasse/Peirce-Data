[u]The Skew Mercator.[/u]

Chapter I. The Mathematical Theory.

§0. It is a [del]great[/del] comfort to me, who am reading mathematics constantly, to [ins]get[/ins] [del]see[/del] a subject treated ab ovo, provided the elementary part is abridged, even if I am quite familiar with it. Hence, I hope the navigators for whom this exposition is intended will not object to my going into elementary explanations.

§1. A map is a representation of a round surface upon a plane, by which we mean that,
1st. In a general way, [u]points[/u] upon the round surface are represented by [u]points[/u] on the map-plane, and vice versa,
2nd. [del]A[/del] Continuous series of points on the round surface are represented by continuous series of points on the map-plane.
Let us first imagine that
(1) Every point on the round surface is represented on the map-plane;
(2) Every point on the map-plane represents a point on the round surface;
(3) No point on the round surface is represented by more than a single point on the map-plane;
(4) No point on the map-plane represents more than one point on the round surface.
