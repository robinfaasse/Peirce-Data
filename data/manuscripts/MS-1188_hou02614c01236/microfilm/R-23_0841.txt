I. Spellings of the Vowel Sounds.

Short Vowels

The wide round high back vowel, or sound of short [u]oo[/u]. In Ellis's palaeotype [u]u[/u].
There are but a few words which have retained this sound since Shakespeare's
[del]time[/del]; and these are spelled with a [u]u[/u]; as, [u]Put[/u], [u]bush[/u], [u]push[/u], [u]puss[/u], [u]bull[/u], [u]full[/u], [u]pull[/u].
[u]Wolf[/u] is an exception. The other words now having this sound formerly had that
[del]of [u]oo[/u] long, and were spelled with[/del] [ins]all but [del]could[/del] [ins]would[/ins], [del]should[/del] [ins]should[/ins][/ins] tooke a final silent [u]e[/u], [del]except [u]could[/u][/del]
[del]and[/del] [ins]those[/ins] which has been dropped. They mostly end with [u]k[/u] or [u]d[/u]; as, [u]book[/u], [u]cook[/u],
[u]hook[/u], [u]look[/u], [u]nook[/u], [u]rook[/u] (also pronounced long), [u]took[/u], [u]shook[/u], [del]brook[/del], [u]crook[/u], [u]good[/u],
[del]hood[/del], [del]wood[/del], [del]stood[/del], [u]foot[/u], [u]wool[/u].
