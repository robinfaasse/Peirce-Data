Let us imagine the whole of space to be filled with
an optical image, which we will speak of as a body,
whose parts can move subject to the following rules:

1[u]st[/u], Every part occupying at one instant a homaloid
occupies at every instant a homaloid, of the same dimen-
sionality; [del]and no part whatever[/del] [ins]in any finite time[/ins] changes its dimensionality.
[ins]and the motion is always continuous[/ins]

2[u]nd[/u], A steady motion (that is, one during which [del]every[/del] [ins]all[/ins]
parts that arrive at a given place move through that place
[del]in the same way[/del] [ins]precisely[/ins]) is possible [del]by[/del] which the particles generally
[del]in[/del] [ins]of[/ins] any [del]straight[/del] [ins]ray[/ins] filaments that may be chosen move along
[del]that[/del] [ins]the[/ins] ray, while every flat film containing that [del]filaments[/del] [ins]ray[/ins]

point.

These rules give room for further restriction if we
choose to impose it.
