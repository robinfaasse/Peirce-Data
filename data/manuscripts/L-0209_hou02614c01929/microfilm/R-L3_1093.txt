[u]Capt. Edward S. Huntington[/u]
[u]Dies Suddenly in Nova Scotia.[/u]

[u]Was a Veteran Soldier, a Noted Au-[/u]
[u]thor and a Famous Chess Player.[/u]

Mr. Charles Parkhurst of Glouces-
ter Passes Away in His 82d Year—
Was Formerly in Fishing Business.

Intelligence was received in Quincy
last night of the sudden death in Truro,
N. S., of Capt. Edward S. Huntington,
a well-known and highly respected cit-
izen of Quincy and a Nationalist of
national fame.

He had during several months been
a severe sufferer from a nervous malady

[fig]Portrait of Capt. Edward S. Huntington[/fig]
THE LATE CAPT. E. S. HUNTINGTON.

and went to Truro a few weeks ago
for treatment by a specialist.

Capt. Huntington was a member of
the same branch of the Huntington
family to which the eminent bishop
of New York belongs, and was a nephew
of that divine. He was born in North-
ampton 53 years ago, and was the son
of Judge Charles P. Huntington of that
place. He was connected with the va-
rious prominent Boston families by
blood, and a sister of his is the wife
of Mr. J. P. Quincy of Quincy.

In 1861 he enlisted in the United States
regular army as a private in the 11th
regiment, U. S. I., and was rapidly
advanced to the position of captain.
He was engaged in 17 battles during
the rebellion and was captured by the
rebels and spent 11 months in Libby
and Andersonville prisons. He remained
in the army until 1867, when he retired
and located in the West, where he
married Miss Pratt, daughter of Sen-
ator Pratt of Indiana.

He took up his residence in Wollas-
ton 12 years ago and had made that
place his home since.

He had marked gifts as a writer, and
contributed to various newspapers and
periodicals. He had an excellent lit-
erary style, clear and delicately imag-
inative, and a few years ago a weird
romance from his pen, "Dreams of the
Dead," attracted unusual attention, not
only in this country, but in Europe,
where it was honored with an Italian
translation.

Capt. Huntington, like his uncle, the
bishop, and other leading members
of the family, took a keen interest in
all social and industrial questions of
the day, and was prominent in connec-
tion with the Nationalist movement.
He was a popular member of the Bos-
ton Chess Club and of the Boston Press
Club, and was secretary of the lat-
ter association. At one time he was
known as a great traveller, and passed
several years in Europe. His death was
due, it is believed, to heart failure.

He leaves a widow and one son,
