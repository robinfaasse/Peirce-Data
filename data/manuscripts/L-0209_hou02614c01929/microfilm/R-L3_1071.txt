I suppose, now, that Mary will come home,
as she will hardly stay in Florence now
that Mother has gone.

I enclose you one of my new circulars,
with the slight dread that you may not like
the publisher's reference to the eminent reviewer in
the Nation. I sincerely hope you will not have any
sensitive feelings on the subject, especially when you
remember how few people ever read these book
circulars; and those who do will scarcely connect
you with the subject.

When you have a spare moment, bear in
mind a Cousin of idle life who will be very
glad for even the briefest note informing him
of the good health and happiness of yourself and
wife.

With kind regards to Mrs Peirce,

Your aff. Cousin
C. S. Huntington
