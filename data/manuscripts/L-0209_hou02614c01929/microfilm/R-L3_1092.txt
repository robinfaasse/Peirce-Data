Intelligence was received in Quincy
last night of the sudden death in Truro,
N. S., of Capt. Edward S. Huntington,
a well known and highly respected cit-
izen of Quincy and a Nationalist of
national fame.

He had during several months been
a severe sufferer from a nervous malady

and went to Truro a few weeks ago
for change of scene and air.

Capt. Huntington was a member of
the same branch of the Huntington
family to which the eminent bishop
of New York belongs, and was a nephew
of that divine. He was born in North-
ampton 50 years ago, and was the son
of Judge Charles P. Huntington of that
place. He was connected with the va-
rious prominent Boston families by
blood, and a sister of his is the wife
of Mr. J. P. Quincy of Quincy.

In 1861 he enlisted in the United States
regular army as a private in the 32d
regiment, U. S. I., and was rapidly
advanced to the position of captain.
He was engaged in 17 battles during
the rebellion and was captured by the
rebels and spent 11 months in Libby
and Andersonville prisons. He remained
in the army until 1867, when he retired
and located in the West, where he
