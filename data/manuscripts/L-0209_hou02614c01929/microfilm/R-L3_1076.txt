Dear Lilla,

[del]I have been waiting until[/del]
[del]the last moment before deciding[/del]
[del]whither I should be able to take[/del]
the anticipated pleasure of
visiting you next week when
your letter arrives. By George,
I nearly, I really believe you
will be disappointed! But see
my wife will not let me
go, nor does the physician
(attending me) think it wise
to make the venture. I am
terribly weak, and find it
rather difficult to wander
about here: so the watchful
"old lady" is afraid I should
get into trouble, or very,
very, low. You can see
mine, by going over for
three or four minutes.

Give my love to Mrs Peirce
and tell her that my wife
and I much regret your
making this little tour
with us.

Our boy Charley who is
an excellent chauffeur, a
fearless good driver of
the old state stamp – goes
with us.

With repeated regrets at
missing our expected pleasure,
but with hopes for the fulfillment
of the suggested journey to Europe
of J. Watson [del]I[/del] [ins]she[/ins] is twenty
S. F. L. ?
