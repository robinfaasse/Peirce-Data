Dear Charley,

I have been damning
myself as a perfect ass ever
since sending my previous letter,
with its silly request; for now
your cordial, characteristic, note
relieves my mind. I cannot
tell you how glad I am to
hear from you after all these
years, and to find, in reading
between the lines of your writing,
you are the same old Charley
with whom I [del]for an hour[/del] [ins]am always[/ins] [del]felt[/del]
[ins]ready[/ins] to touch glasses [del]and[/del]!!

Every word you say about
the methods of editors and
the "cut and dried" judgments
of that ilk, touches a responsive
chord in my own mental organism.

I shall send you an author's
copy of Tavernier's [u]via[/u] [del]il[/del] [ins]it[/ins] but
beg you will not take any trouble
about the matter. Your cordial,
very pleasant letter and the
fact of your favorable opinion of
the book is reward enough.

I wish I could see you
the next time you come
in this way. We are living,
very quietly and most stupidly,
here in Wollaston, but would be
delighted to see you here, for
a few days at any time. You
know we are close by Quincy.

Yours faithfully
[u]C. H. Hartshorne[/u]
