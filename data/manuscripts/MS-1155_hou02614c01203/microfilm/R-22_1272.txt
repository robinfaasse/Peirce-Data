The following are the definitions of all the colors on the plate, thus expressed in equations.

A color sensation is any sensation due to the excitation of an elementary portion of the
retina. Every color produces different color-sensations under illuminations differing in inten-
sity and quality, There are three modes of variation of color-sensation, namely, variation in
hue, in luminosity, and in chroma. Hue is the qualitative mode of a color-sensation, the respect
in which the sensations excited by the different parts of the spectrum most strikingly differ,
the mode of difference of red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet, and purple. [del]It is the mode of[/del]
[del]difference of red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet, and purple.[/del] After purple, we come back to red again, thus completing the
color-circle. Luminosity is the degree of difference of a color-sensation from that of black, or
if this be the mere negation of sensation, the intensity of the sensation. Chroma is the decided-
ness of the hue of a sensation, the fullness of the color, its departure from a neutral tint or
grey. Thus, artificial ultramarine has a most intense chroma, though its luminosity is small.
The sky in dry weather has intense luminosity but low chroma. High colors give sensations of
great chroma, low colors sensations of little chroma. An increase of intensity of illumination
always increases the luminosity of the color-sensation. An admixture of grey always diminishes
the chroma. [del]A change of wave-length[/del] always modifies the hue. But there is no exact relation between
these causes and their effects. Thus, after a color has once attained a certain luminosity,
a further increase of the intensity of the light has for its main effect the addition of a yel-
low sensation, called the color of brightness. For great intensities of illumination, this is a
pure yellow; for lower intensities, it is an orange-yellow. Consequently, if it is required to
find by the mixture of colors one [del]which[/del] [ins]a color-sensation[/ins] which shall differ from a given color-sensation in lumi-
nosity only, we must mix yellow with the black. The black pigments used by painters are generally
yellowish, and and ivory-black which is not very much so is called a blueblack. So when colors
are mixed with grey, although the reduction of the chroma is by far the most prominent effect,
yet they always appear more violet and less yellow.
