COLOR OF A BODY, the color of the light which shines from the body when it is exposed to day-'
light. When light strikes upon a body, practically the same proportion of all parts of the spec-
trum is reflected. If the reflection is regular, in consequence of the body being polished, the
reflected light is only seen in certain directions; but if the body is rough, the light is re-'
flected irregularly, and then communicates a dull, whitey appearance, which will be in great mea-
sure removed by wetting. Of the light which is not reflected, but penetrates the body, a part is
absorbed. Different substances absorb very different proportions of different parts of the spec-'
trum. If the body is in fine powder, the light is reflected from the further surface of the par-'
ticles, and thus emerges on the same side at which it entered the body. If the body is in mass
and is sufficiently thick, the light will nearly all be absorbed and the body will appear nearly
black; but if it is thinner part of the light will be transmitted and will emerge from the other
side. If the body contains foreign particles, these will of course reflect a part of the light..
This light which is the residue not absorbed determines the color of the body. Since each stratu
of the body of any given thickness absorbs a given proportion of the light of each part of the
