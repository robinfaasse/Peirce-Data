A COLOR-SENSATION is a specific sensation due to the excitation of an elementary portion of
the retina. Color-sensations vary in three different ways, namely, in hue, in luminosity, and in
chroma. HUE is the qualitative mode of a color-sensation, the respect in which the sensations
excited by the different parts of the spectrum most strikingly differ, the mode of difference of
red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet, and purple. A COLOR-CIRCLE is a self-returning [ins]continuous[/ins] series
of hues, as in running [del]through the spectrum and then[/del] [ins]from one end of the spectrum to the other and then[/ins] through purple back to the starting-point.
LUMINOSITY is the degree of difference of a color-sensation from black, or if that be regarded
as a mere privation of sensation, the intensity of the sensation. CHROMA is the decidedness of
the hue of a color-sensation, the fullness of the color, its departure from a neutral tint or
grey. Thus, artificial ultramarine [Plate, No. ] has a most intense chroma, though its lumino-
sity is small. The sky in dry weather has strong luminosity but little chroma. High colors are
such as give sensations of great chroma. LOW COLORS, such as give sensations of little chroma.
