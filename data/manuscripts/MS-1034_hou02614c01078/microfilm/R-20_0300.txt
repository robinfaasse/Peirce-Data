of the Carbon Spectrum."
In this and a paper sent in next day

of acetylene. But I find nothing.

[date]1880 April 15[/date]. Prof. E. Ludwig presented
to the Vienna Academy (Sitzungsberichte
LXXXI Abth ii p 756) a work from
his laboratory by Dr. Hans Jahn. Studies
on decomposition of simple organic substances
by zinc dust

At first nothing turns up about acetylene
Alcohol gives C₂H₆O = CH₄ + CO + H₂
also C₂H₆O + Zn = ZnO + C₂H₆ + H₂
Both propyl alcohols gave the same propylene
The zinc dust splits (at 300° to 350° C) the higher
alcohols from ethyl alcohol up into the cor-
responding olefines and hydrogen.

But on [date]July 15[/date] the second part was presented
(Sitzungsberichte LXXXII Abth ii p 511)
Ether gives (C₂H₅)₂O = C₂H₄ + H₂O
H₂O + Zn = ZnO + H₂
(C₂H₅)₂O = C₂H₄ + C₂H₆O + H₂O
C₂H₆O = CH₄ + CO
