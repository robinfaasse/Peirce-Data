[date]1895 Dec 30[/date]
[u]Henri Le Chatelier[/u] ([u]CR cxxi. 1144[/u]) [pn]96[/pn]
[u]his paper on combustion of acetylene[/u]

Mixtures of C₂H₂ with air of less than 7.74
percent of volume being C₂H₂ burn to CO₂ and H₂O
giving a yellowish dim flame.

For percentages between 7.74 - 17.37 the flame
is pale blue with a feeble yellowish aureola. The
products are CO₂, CO, H₂O, H

The equation of proportions is
[frac]5000/t+273°[/frac] + Log [frac]Vol[sub]H₂O[/sub] · Vol[sub]CO[/sub]/Vol[sub]CO₂[/sub] · Vol[sub]H₂[/sub][/frac] = const = 5

With percentages above 17.37 the products
are CO, H, C, C₂H₂. The precipitation of C
is very marked above 20% The flame then
becomes luminous, red and smoky and more
and more so as the combustible gas augments.
After the passage of the flame there is a [del]black[/del] [ins]dark[/ins]
cloud of precipitated soot.

The limits of inflammability that is in
which fire being set at one point extends through
the whole are
