[u]Doctor X.[/u] The best of your screed in the April [u]Monist[/u] was about
the singleness of symbols. It is in truth bad morals to use words in other
than their original senses. But apply [ins]this[/ins] rule to your own use of the verb
"is." When the child first uses this verb, he applies it to some [u]sensory[/u] reality.
How, then, in the face of your code of terminological ethics, do you ever dare
to use the verb "to be" for indicating anything not sensory? I, for my part,
stick to the [u]one[/u] meaning of "is" rigidly. When you talk of "general objects
being real," and the like, you seem not to be aware that the verbs "to be,"
"to be real," and "to exist" have [u]ever[/u] precisely one and the same significance.
