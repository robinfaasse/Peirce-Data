The mark that I find it convenient to describe first is that the Critical Common-sensist holds that all the veritably indubitable beliefs are vague, — often in some directions highly so.

Logicians have too much neglected the study of vagueness, not suspecting the important part it plays in mathematical thought. It is the antithetical analogue of generality. A sign is [u]objectively general[/u], in so far as, leaving its effective interpretation [del]indeterminate[/del], it surrenders to the interpreter the right of completing the determination for himself. "Man is mortal." "What man?" "Any man you like." A sign is [u]objectively vague[/u], in so far as, leaving its interpretation more or less indeterminate, it reserves [del]to[/del] [ins]for[/ins] some other possible sign or experience the function of completing the determination.
