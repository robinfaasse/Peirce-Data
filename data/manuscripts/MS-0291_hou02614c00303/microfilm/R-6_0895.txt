[pn]P 63[/pn] Humanism is to be a philosophy not purely intellectual
because every department [del]part[/del] of man's nature must be
voiced in it. For my part, I beg to be excused from having
any dealings with such a philosophy. I wish philosophy
to be a strict science, [del]cool and severe[/del] [ins]passionless and severely fair[/ins]. I know very well
that science is not the whole of life, but I believe in the division
of labor among intellectual agencies. The apostle of Humanism
says that professional philosophers "have rendered philosophy
— like unto themselves, abstruse, arid, abstract, and abhorrent."
But I conceive that some branches of science, and not in a
healthy state if they are not abstruse, arid, and abstract; in
which case, like the Aristotelianism which is this gentleman's particula[r]
[u]bête noire[/u], it will be as Shakespeare says (if I remember):
[ins]Not harsh and crabbed as dull fools suppose
But musical as is Apollo's lute, etc.[/ins]
