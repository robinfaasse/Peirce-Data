Doctor X. The best of your screed in the April Monist was about the singleness of symbols. It is in truth bad morals to use words in other than their original senses. But apply that rule to your own use of the verb "is". When the child first uses this verb, he applies it to some sensory reality. How, then, in the face of your code of terminological ethics, do you ever dare to use the verb "to be" for indicating anything not sensory? I, for my part, stick to the [u]one[/u] meaning of "is" rigidly. When you talk of "general objects being real" and the like, you seem not to be aware that the verbs "to be", "to be real", and "to exist" have every precisely one and the same significance.
