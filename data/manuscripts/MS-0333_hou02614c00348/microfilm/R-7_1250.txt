are greater than its advantages. He will say, "I
hold steadfastly to the truth, and the truth is al-
ways wholesome." And, in many cases, it may
very well be that the pleasure he derives from his
calm faith overbalances any inconveniences re-
sulting from its deceptive character. Thus, if it be
true that death is annihilation, then the man
who believes that he will certainly go straight to
heaven when he dies, however he may have behaved
in this life, has a cheap pleasure which will not
be followed by the least disappointment. A similar
consideration seems to have weight with many per-
sons in religious topics, for we frequently hear it
said, "O, I could not believe so and so, because
