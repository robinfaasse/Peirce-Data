It is not merely in pronunciation, however, that the vernacular is so mobile.
It has, no doubt, a solid and indestructible core; but all the surface of it is
in a fluid condition, — a thing of fashion. We read Bacon's Essayes without the
slightest difficulty in [del]one of the original editions[/del]; but for the vernacular parts of

the point. Let me not be understood as disparaging the spoken dialect: All this is
