Our third principle is the psychological law [ins]of assimilation,[/ins] that there is a gentle tendency for mental creations which are somewhat alike to become more alike. Of course, all the regularities of language are due to this. In particular, it causes words that are spelled alike to be pronounced alike, and conversely. Thus, the spelling [u]league[/u] is unnecessarily complicated. But having thus spelled [u]legue[/u], an alliance, we proceeded to spell [u]liege[/u], in Old French, [u]legue[/u], in the same way; and then even the Dutch [u]leger[/u] was spelled [u]leaguer[/u]. For this reason, although a counter-tendency to distinguish in the spelling words likely to be confounded, occasionally operates, it has seemed to me that words that are both spelled and pronounced alike, ought, for the present purposes, to be considered as the same word.
