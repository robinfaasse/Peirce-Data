The Greek word for induction, of which our word is simply the Latini-
zation, — [u]ἐπαγωγή[/u], — was apparently introduced by Socrates. It ex-
presses the reasoning by the metaphor of a body of soldiers led up to
make an attack on a position. Socrates was the first to employ induc-
tion systematically, He offered no theory on the subject, but simply
relied on common sense.

Aristotle shows that [del]an inductive[/del] an induction is simply a
syllogism; only that which is the conclusion of the syllogism becomes
in induction one of the premisses, while the major premiss of the syl-
logism becomes the conclusion of the induction. Further, this syllo-
gism of which induction is a transposition has for its minor term, i.e.
the subject of the syllogistic conclusion an enumeration of instances.
