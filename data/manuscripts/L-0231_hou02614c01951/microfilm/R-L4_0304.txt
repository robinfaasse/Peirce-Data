Exceptionally powerful intellects, — mathematicians, logicians, economists, famed for their great reasoning powers, — who having found some curious way of reasoning to work all right in many instances have been led to apply it to others and so to reach very false conclusions; and the reason has been that their familiarity with cases in which the reasoning has worked well has seduced them into thinking that a conclusion so drawn evidently must be true, when the truth is that in all the cases where it worked something that their attention never had been drawn to had been true and this unnoticed truth had rendered the conclusion a necessary result, not of what they had considered the premises, but of these together with the unnoticed circumstance. Euclid is full of such fallacies. (The 16th proposition of the first book is one) The greatest modern mathematicians have fallen into them.
