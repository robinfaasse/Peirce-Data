There are two conditions of this reasoning
being legitimate that are apt to be over-
looked; and they need mention here
because [del]they are[/del] corresponding condi-
tions apply to Quantitative Adduction.
The first is that the fact that it was the
probability of the [del]other[/del] balls carrying a
letter E and not any other distinguishing
mark ought to be decided upon before the
drawings. [del]This[/del] [ins]It[/ins] is the reason it was men-
tioned in the premises that there were many
other letters on balls. [del]For[/del] Among so many,
it is very likely that some will occur of-
en on the 125 drawn than the rules of pro-
bability indicate and others less often. If then
it were not decided what the conclusion
was to relate to, one might be tempted either
to use it for some character which had been
remarked to be frequent on the balls drawn, or
the reverse, according as one either trusted
or distrusted calculations of probability
more than one ought to do.
