I will now proceed to Induction. My
doctrine here is the only correct one, and
will have to be received. By induction I
mean any reasoning from a part to a
whole. Of course, it cannot be a neces-
sary reasoning. Nor is it a probable de-
duction. Though its conclusion may be
more or less false, yet it is justified by
the circumstance that through the conclu-
sion may be more or less erroneous, yet
one has only to persist in following the
same method and the conclusion will
get corrected. It is the only way there is
of assuring oneself of real truth beyond
what direct perception [del]furnishes[/del] [ins]furnishes[/ins].

[u]I recognize three important Classes of Inductions[/u], or
perhaps they should rather be called Orders, since they differ
chiefly in the complexity of their precautions against error.
I know no way of measuring the probative force of
Inductions. If there be any such way I suppose I shall have
to leave the discovery of it for my successors. But the three
Orders of Induction [del]consist of[/del] I call respectively Quantitative,
Qualitative, and Crude Induction.
