Dear Mr. Peirce,

I am much gratified that you can "do" the logic.

Mrs. Franklin is to send you the paged proofs through the folio S. (p.272). I am sending herewith the [del]galleys[/del] [ins]paged[/ins] Folio T, (through p.288) and also the galleys from there[ins]on[/ins] through the letter J., together with certain later matter -- all I have in duplicate. The logic already written is substantially there, seeing that after the letter I, the logical terms were left over for supplementary setting up (when the paging is done). The additional topics written are Inference (psychology of)-- the logical treatment being referred to [del]Reasoning[/del] [ins]Proof[/ins]-- and Induction (mainly psychological)-- with [ins]etc. &c.[/ins] reference to Baconian Method, and Scientific Method for logical and other distinctions. Both of these topics I had to do myself, as the I [del]letters[/del] [ins]only[/ins] are returned for paging.

I am following your suggestion regarding the supplementing of the list of topics, asking you however to observe the following requirements:--

A. Supplementary topics before H. can go in only as cross references to later letters: but I can get in such cross references after C. if the [ins]terms[/ins] [del]topics[/del] be sent to me in the early letters at once.

B. In H. and I., I. J. and K. supplementary matter should come at the earliest possible day: say through K. (where the first vol. may end) in one week, and through L. in two to three weeks from now. In later letters follow the dates already sent you, [ins]please.[/ins]

C. Send me as soon as possible the entire list of supplementary topics as you propose them, that I may advise you after consulting other writers' lists.

D. I am not sure that I can secure for you the full amount of payment for the enlarged list; I am limited to payment for so many topics all told, and have [ins]any way[/ins] to distribute them [del]--[/del] [ins]as I[/ins] have!-- but I shall do my best and hope you will go ahead just the same.

With these limitations just take a free hand with the list.

In the matter of treatment, also, use [ins]your[/ins] good judgment, keeping to the general pace-- as to lengths, range, &c, [ins]set[/ins] by Adamson. I should be more than glad, also, if you would pass on his articles, sending me suggestions, revisions, &c, on his proofs and in general signing your initials with his (a plan generally followed where two authorities are working in the same field).
