My dear Prof Baldwin [marginalia]VB[/marginalia]

Herein enclosed is a letter I started to write you a fortnight ago; but violent neuralgia, putting me back in every way, and especially depriving me of the power of normal judgment, prevented my finishing it. I now send it as it is. Nothing is stated more strongly than I feel it, but still I recognize that accommodations must be made.

In view of the reasons therein given, and others, if I am to be debarred from the use of symbols, I think Mrs. Franklin should employ them most sparingly. In any case, I only consented to her writing certain articles, — the most important of those on my list by far — on the understanding that I was to revise and if need be supplement them. She is in my opinion too much disposed to make a toy of logical algebra, and therefore some restraint in the use of symbols is wholesome.

An obolus or line over a term to negative it is an ancient sign mentioned in Diodorus Hispalensis. It is therefore the best of all the logical signs except one. That one is writing one term after another to signify [del]logical[/del] [ins]relative[/ins] multiplication. It is older than the alphabet by very far and is founded in the human mind. Those two are solid rocks.
