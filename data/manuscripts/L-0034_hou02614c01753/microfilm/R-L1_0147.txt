[date]1900 Oct 23[/date] CSP to J. M. Baldwin [pn]4[/pn]

<u>Abduction</u>. This very interesting word, the meaning of which requires some discussion, and of which an explanation here was wanted, is omitted.

<u>Abelard</u>. This and [del]and[/del] all the other short biographical articles fail to meet people's needs. What there ought to be here is an account of the three books in which the writings attributed to Abelard are contained, and of the literature relating to his doctrines, from John of Salisbury down; some slight statement of his peculiar shade of opinion, and a reference to his own <u>Historia Calamitatum</u> and to some easily accessible modern account of his life.

I recommend you to decide upon some Addenda to make up for the deficiencies in the [del]only[/del] early letters.

As to the money question, you may be sure that having put my hand to the plough, I shall not turn back merely because the money runs out. At the same time, I am sure you will use such endeavors as you can, to get an additional grant; since I shall have to give up remunerative work, upon which my bread and butter depends, to do this. In particular, the haste that is requisite in the first weeks will prevent my doing work [ins]from[/ins] which I expected to [del]get[/del] complete the sum needed to pay my tax-bill [ins]which will be increased by a penalty after election-day;[/ins] and therefore I hope you will, as it were, meet me half way, by paying sight down [ins]after delivery[/ins] for the first batch of articles which I intend to send you a week from today. It will be somewhere from 25 upwards. You can deposit directly in the Fifth Avenue Bank, 5th Ave. and 44th St, New York City, to the credit of "C. S. or Juliette Peirce."

very faithfully C. S. Peirce
