I expect to be allowed to use your dictionary to put before those who [ins]consult[/ins] it scientific notions about logic, meaning by "scientific" such as the class of men called men of science can regard as rational. You must not cut me down to defining all the nonsense with which logic is infested, exclusively.

Logic is, from the nature of things, always has, and always will be, behind the times. It was least so in the Middle Ages. At present, it is far more so than ever it was. This is partly because the neglect of logic gave room for its revival in quite the most idiotic form it ever assumed [ins]further idiotized and[/ins] in the first Kantian logic, which Hamilton & Mansell have made widely accepted by our professors. It is also partly owing to the fact that the thought of our age [del]is[/del] has got so far away from the ideas of theological seminaries. Men of science think in their laboratories. But logical chairs are still mostly held by men who move in the atmosphere of the seminary. Moreover, Germans are naturally for the most part confused reasoners. Their copious language tends that way. Of course, I don't deny that Kepler and Weierstrass and a lot more are superb. Still, that is a national characteristic, & so far as logic is not dominated by theology, it is largely dominated by [del]one of[/del] the German professors of logic, who is never a really clear cut thinker. Schröder is only a Hoch Schule man. Dedekind is a professor of mathematics. And so on.

Such being the state of logic, you wont ask a scientific man and a man of original ideas of a real kind trained too in the subtleties of scholasticism, to represent [del]what[/del] [ins]what[/ins] is accepted by logical professors at large.

For the most part they don't deny what we exact logicians affirm. They only ignore it, or call it extralogical. Whatever is not needful [del]for the[/del] professorial bread and butter is extralogical.
