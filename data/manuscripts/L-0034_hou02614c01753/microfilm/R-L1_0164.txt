[marginalia]C.S.P. to Prof Baldwin 1900 Dec 1.[/marginalia] [pn]6[/pn]

In my opinion, it will seriously injure the dictionary to
exclude all logical symbols. But I will not consent to the
admission of any symbols (except on occasion, to illustrate
a special point) which violate the ethics of science; and I expect
my wishes on this vitally important point to be respected.

As to graphs, I care less about them; but still I think you
make a mistake in excluding them, because I have no doubt
they would be popular, as Euler's diagrams have proved to be.
My article on graphs [del]is late[/del] (which is already written, except for
an addition I intended to make concerning Kempe's graphs) is
largely taken up with showing how Euler's diagrams can be put
very easily to uses not yet suggested and which instantly clear
up puzzling points. Kempe's work is extremely important;
and according to the promises of your prospectus, ought
to be noticed. The same promise applies somewhat less
decidedly to exact logic generally.

I have, however, very willingly cut down my article on
exact logic to extreme brevity, notwithstanding that I supposed
it was mutually understood that I was to have some freedom
there. I comply because I wish to be compliant, but I
remain under that impression.
