Prof Baldwin
Princeton, N.J.

Dear Sir:

I will do the work called for in yours of the 9th, which just reaches me.

It is indispensable that I should have [u]all[/u] the preceeding articles on Logic before me, — very desirable that I have the whole so far as in type.

The list of topics sent omits sundry well known terms of the traditional logic, as well as pretty much all that are at all out of the way, many terms of modern systems which have had great vogue (as Quantified Predicate) and all other that have not had great vogue [del]except out of[/del] a narrow line, and substantially the entire terminology of "exact" logic. In addition, many of the topics require sub-topics. I should recommend your allowing me to add what seems to me necessarily, under such general instructions as to fullness as you may give or which I may derive from the earlier proofs.

The French synonyms are without importance in Logic, while the Greek ones are highly so.

I shall not be able to get fully to work till you send me the proofs of all of Prof. Adamson's work.

Yours very truly C S Peirce
