!!! The same principles apply to algebraic notation. Thus, I
used $+$, as the sign of logical aggregation. Later I found that
Jevons had used $+$ before me, and I gave up my sign and
used his, with misgivings. Shortly after, Jevons saw the
impropriety of using $+$ in that [del]way[/del] [ins]sense[/ins], since Boole had already
used it in a different logical [del]sense[/del] [ins]way[/ins], more closely analogous to
that of [del]algebra[/del] [ins]common algebra[/ins], and he substituted for it $\cdot|\cdot$ ! But this is
liable to be mistaken for three signs, and in fact could be so
read and make sense. It was inadmissible. Hence, to change
it as little as possible, I simply join the dots to the upright
thus: $\Psi$.

As another illustration, I must use [u]connote[/u] in Mill's sense,
because it was not only used in a different sense in medieval
logic, but the words [u]signify[/u] and [u]denote[/u] were then used as
he uses [u]connote[/u] and [u]denote[/u]. I will therefore say [u]signify[/u],
for it is noisomely bad.

I propose to submit an article on [u]Logical Terminology[/u]
in which I shall say that these rules have been proposed and
give the reasons for adopting them.
