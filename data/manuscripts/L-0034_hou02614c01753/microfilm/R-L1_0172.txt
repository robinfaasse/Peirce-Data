My dear Prof Baldwin

Newcomb's articles are excessively bad. His notions of philosophy are such as an [ins]intellectual[/ins] person about Cambridge, not a special student of philosophy, would pick up [ins]about 1855.[/ins] [del]They have not progressed.[/del] He is not a mathematician, or at any rate has only become so late in life, — he is only a mathematical astronomer. He is therefore very narrow both on the philosophical & on the mathematical side, and I propose from this on to pitch into his articles and try to render them serviceable to students of philosophy. A few years ago he was engaged in writing for the Standard Dict'y. Like all writers for that work he simply revised & condensed the articles in the Century Dict'y. He wrote to me and said my views about limits were 40 years behind the time. The correspondence which followed showed he had [del]not[/del] read any of the remarkable works on the logic of mathematics of late years & in short what it came to was that I was returning to a view which the nominalism of forty years before had persuaded him was wrong & he had not advanced a step since that time. Now, you know, no amount of vigor of intellect will make a man an up to date philosopher; and the ideas current in Cambridge 40 or 50 years ago and those now held by advanced thinkers are as far apart as South Africa and Berlin.
