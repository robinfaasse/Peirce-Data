My dear Professor Baldwin

Though you doubtless hate long letters, I am obliged to beg your careful reading of this.

You expect me to assent to the rule that "personal views except when generally adopted" are not to be expressed in the dict'y.

Distingua "Views" are, properly, disputable opinions. "Generally adopted" by whom? Not by the public at large, of course. By professors of logic? Professors of logic are, for the most part, Dunces. That is to say, they are simply obstructionists because of their ignorance, their conceiving that their duty is to uphold orthodox opinions, and the fact that they are intent on nothing more elevated than bread and butter. If you do not know that this is true in this country, you do not know the state of things. In England, it is quite as true, though there is a refined and cultured atmosphere there that our colleges lack. My characterization of German professors [marginalia]Monist Vol VII, p. 20,[/marginalia] has brought me voluntary and uncalled for assent from persons in a situation to judge intelligently.

As I told you before, logic is about where Psychology was thirty years ago, when Volkmar's first edition, — mild enough, — sent a shiver down the backs of professorial chairs, & Fechner was held to be a crank & Wundt had hardly been heard of at all. If you were old enough to appreciate what it was to speak the language of science about psychology then, you would sympathize more with scientific logicians now.

But if we are to understand by "generally received", generally admitted, or sure to be admitted as soon as presented, by [del]scientific[/del] [ins]competent[/ins] logicians, then, I grant, you are quite right. Neither do I deny that I may sometimes sin in this respect, and I am perfectly willing to be called down when I do.
