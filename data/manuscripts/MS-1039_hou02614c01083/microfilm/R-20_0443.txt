The word [u]substance[/u] is used in a special sense in chemistry, [del]whi[/del] [ins]which[/ins] has become its most ordinary meaning. Properly, a substance is anything having real being [u]per se[/u], although it may have been created. But in chemistry, two things having mass (inertia and weight) are said to be composed of the same "substance" or "body" if their smallest sensible parts have the same physical [del]qualities[/del] [ins]properties[/ins], or if [del]either[/del] [ins]each[/ins] can by the ordinary action of pressure, heat, light, and electricity be made to assume the physical properties of the other. They are also said to be the same substance if one is simply the crystalline (or certain other) form of the other. A body is also considered to be chemically unchanged when it is in solution, provided it can be recovered [ins]unchanged[/ins] by evaporation, or by [del]precipitation by cold or by admixture of a third[/del] [ins]another menstruum[/ins], [del]unchanged[/del].
