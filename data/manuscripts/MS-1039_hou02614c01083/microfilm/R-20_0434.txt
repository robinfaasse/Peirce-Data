[u]Chemical Element[/u]

The so-called chemical elements are the kinds of bodily matter which our present means of analysis do not enable us to decompose. They are so-called, because in chemistry they take the place of the four Elements, [u]Fire[/u], [u]Water[/u], [u]Earth[/u], and [u]Air[/u], which according to Empedocles were the first matter of the world, and according to Aristotle, [del]each contributes to anything containing it two of the four fundamental qualities[/del] [ins]each contributes to anything containing it the qualities of being hot and dry[/ins]

Our elements [ins](as we know them)[/ins] are almost certainly mere mixtures, whether their chief ingredients are chemical compounds or not. For according to the law of [del]mass[/del] [ins]mass-action[/ins] in chemical action, which is now recognized, all our means of analysis, distillation, precipitation, etc. can only afford mixtures. (See [u]Ostwald[/u], Scientific Foundations of Anal. Chem. Tr. by M'Gowan. Macmillan: [date]1895[/date].) Namely, one clause of that law is, that in two contiguous spaces the concentration of each substance must be in a constant ratio.
