Chemistry

the [del]law of multiple proportions[/del] [ins]or "the stechiometrical (or stoicheiometrical) law"[/ins] suggested to Dr. John Dalton the revival of the atomic theory, which in the light of the kinetical theory of gases and the phenomena of Crookes's radiometer becomes fairly certain. In consequence of this law, chemists regard all combinations of substances in which the constituents are in atomic proportions and seem in some sense to be held together to be [u]chemical compounds[/u], although, according to the theory now usually entertained, in many such compounds a large part, in some cases [del]the[/del] almost the whole, of the constituents are not even joined into the same molecules.

Mendeleef's [u]Periodic Law[/u] is that the properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic weights; but the periodicity is evidently of a very complicated nature. In consequence of this circumstance, together with the fact that by virtue of the "law of mass action" the elements as we know them must all be [del]mixtures[/del] [ins]mixed with[/ins] impurities, we find that no regular relation between the properties of elements and their atomic weights is by any means exactly fulfilled.
