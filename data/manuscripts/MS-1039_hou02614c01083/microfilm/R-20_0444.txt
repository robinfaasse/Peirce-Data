Chemistry

A chemical compound of the most perfect type is a kind of physical matter such that every molecule of a body composed of it contains two or more kinds of matter, either of which might by itself compose a body. In every such compound the proportionate masses of the different constituents, each divided by a number which is constant for that kind of matter and is called its [u]atomic weight[/u], are in simple numerical ratios.

Any intimate mixture of different kinds of matter in simple atomic proportions, if it cannot be even partially decomposed by evaporation nor by dissolving out one of the ingredients by a solvent which by evaporation would leave that ingredient pure, is likewise considered to be a [u]chemical compound[/u], because the constituents are held together in the proportions of the most perfect type of chemical compound.
