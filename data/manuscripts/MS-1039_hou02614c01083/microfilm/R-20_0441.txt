Their sulphates and bisulphates, such as $\text{K}_2(\text{SO}_4)$ and $\text{K}\text{H}(\text{SO}_4)$ are easily soluble.

Their carbonates and bicarbonates are soluble, but the former the more so.

Their phosphates, of which the commonest is of the form $\text{K}_2\text{H}(\text{PO}_4)$ are all soluble, except that one kind of metaphosphates (commonly called after Fleitmann the monometaphosphates) are nearly insoluble in water, though soluble in dilute acids.

Among their more insoluble salts are the tartrates and platino-chlorides. $\text{Cs}$ is [del]a[/del] by $\text{SbCl}_3$ in concent $\text{HClAq}$, and both $\text{Cs}$ and $\text{Rb}$ by silicotungstic acid.

Their halides have a tendency to form double salts with those of relatively electronegative metals such as
$$\begin{array}{l c l}
\text{Rb}_2\text{PtCl}_6 & \quad & \text{K}_3\text{IrCy}_6 \\
\text{Rb}_2\text{SbCl}_5 & \quad & \text{K}_4\text{MnCy}_6 \\
\text{Rb}_3\text{Sb}_2\text{Cl}_9 & \quad & \text{K}_3\text{MnCy}_5 \\
\text{Rb}_2\text{CoCl}_4 & \quad & \text{K}_2\text{FeC}_5\text{N}_5\text{O} \\
\text{RbAuCl}_4 & \quad & \text{K}_4\text{OsCy}_6 \\
\text{Rb}_2\text{FeCl}_5 & \quad & \text{KTeF}_5 \\
\text{RbHg}_2\text{Cl}_5 & \quad & \\
\text{RbHg}_2\text{Cl}_9 & \quad & \\
\text{Rb}_2\text{UO}_2\text{F}_4 & \quad & \\
\text{Rb}_2\text{SiF}_6 & \quad & 
\end{array}$$
and many others.

Among their double salts, the alums are characteristic. These are double sulphates or selenates, having $4(\text{SO}_4)$ combined with two [ins]trivalent[/ins] atoms of $\text{Al}^{\text{vi}}$, $\text{Cr}^{\text{vi}}$, $\text{Mn}^{\text{vi}}$, $\text{Fe}^{\text{vi}}$, $\text{Ga}^{\text{vi}}$, $\text{In}^{\text{vi}}$, and two univalent atoms of $\text{K}$, $\text{Rb}$, $\text{Cs}$, [del]$\text{H}_4\text{N}$[/del], $\text{Na}$, $\text{Ag}$, [del]$\text{H}$[/del] $\text{NH}_4$, $\text{NH}_3(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)$, $\text{Tl}$, and finally with $24$ molecules of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. They form handsome crystals isomorphous with one another.
