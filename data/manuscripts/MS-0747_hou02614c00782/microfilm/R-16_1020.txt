The multiplication of such individual collective relatives must follow the analogy of that of individual non-collective relatives. This leaves room for no indeterminacy in the rule except a perfectly inessential one. The following are examples of multiplication
[del]$(A:D|E) \bullet (E|D|F:G)$[/del]
$(A:D|E)(E|D|F:G) = (A|F:G)$
$(A:D|E|F)(F:G) = A:D|E|G$
$(A:D|E)(E|F:G) = 0$

The rule is this. Unless the last correlate of the multiplier is the same as the first relate of the multiplicand, the second last correlate of the multiplier the same as the second relate of the multiplicand, [del]and[/del] [ins]and[/ins] so on [del]until[/del] [ins]to[/ins] either the first relate of the multiplier or the last relate of the multiplicand, the product is zero. If, however, this condition is fulfilled the relates of the product are the relates of the multiplier followed by the unmatched relates [ins](if there are any)[/ins] of the multiplicand [del]cor[/del] in unchanged order and the correlates of the product are the correlates of the multiplicand preceded by the [del]unmatched[/del] [ins]unmatched[/ins] correlates of the multiplier (if there are any) in unchanged order.
