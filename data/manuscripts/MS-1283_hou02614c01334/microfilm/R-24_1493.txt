Finally, Galileo makes an inclined plane of a
plank nearly forty feet long. He cuts a groove
the whole length and lines this with the finest
vellum smoothly glued down and allows a ball
to roll down. To measure the time he makes
a sort of clepsydra. The aperture is closed with
[fig]diagram of a funnel-shaped clepsydra with a beaker below[/fig] the finger. It is opened when
the ball begins to roll and closed
when a marked place is passed
and the quantity of water which
escapes is weighed on a fine balance. In
this way he establishes the fact that the
spaces are as the squares of the times, or the
velocities acquired to the times since the motion
commenced.
