Let us turn to the consideration of Galileo's
scientific achievements. It is marvellous how
little he positively accomplished. His [del]astronomical[/del]
telescopic discoveries were easy enough. He did
not invent the telescope independently. He had
heard of such a thing, and, by the known principles
of optics, he succeeded in reproducing it. Once
he had it, he turned it to the heavens. The
first thing he remarked was the moon was
covered with mountains, very like many
volcanic cones in Italy. The whole Bay of
Naples is not unlike a lunar crater with
Vesuvius as a small cone superposed upon
the edge of it. There was one heavenly body, then,
not altogether unlike the earth. The planets
all appeared to [ins]show[/ins] [del]be[/del] disks; but the fixed stars
appeared quite as small as with the naked eye.
