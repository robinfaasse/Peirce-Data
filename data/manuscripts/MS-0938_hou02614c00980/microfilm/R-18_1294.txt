There is, however, besides, a little logical question that may be
propounded in regard to this and some other theories; and for the en-
tertainment of those of our readers who delight in such problems
we will state it, inviting the others to skip to the beginning of the
next paragraph, where we shall resume plain narrative.
Imagine, then, that all objects of a certain familiar kind, which, to
avoid confusion, we will call the K-kind, are explained under a
given hypothesis as having a highly complicated occult constitution;
and imagine that among the elements of that constitution there
occur occult objects of that very K-kind. Then the question is whether
the hypothesis is not bound in consistency to explain these objects too
in the same manner in which it has deemed it necessary to explain
familiar objects of the K-kind. The vortex-theory explains all ordinary
liquids as consisting of a crowd of [del]the[/del] vortices in a substrate liquid.
Ought it not, then, to suppose an endless series of liquids each
explained by the next following one of the series?
