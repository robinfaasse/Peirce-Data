7[/marginalia] The theory which is, just now, most in favor is the electron-theory, which does away with all three laws of motion. Some persons, again, suppose the vortex-theory and the electron-theory to be welded together. Another theory, whose logical foundations have been more thoroughly elaborated than any of those mentioned is that of Hertz, who proposes to banish the ordinary idea of a moving force acting on a free particle, and to suppose that all perceptible bodies are connected by something like a link-work with other concealed bodies (presumably in a fourth dimension of space) which latter move as if along constraining guides, so that the velocity of each entire thing remains constant. The one law resembles [ins]the principle of least action of[/ins] Maupertuis. It is that every complete thing moves uniformly along the straightest path it can.

A new theory has been outlined in the introductory volume of Dr. Herbert Nichols's "Treatise on Cosmology." It is founded upon a slight modification of the ideas of the great experimental and sensationalistic psychologist, Wundt, incontestibly one of the very most influential minds of our age. A decidedly singular circumstance of philosophical history is connected with this new theory. Wundt, as [del]everybody[/del] [ins]philosophical student[/ins] knows, has been the chief propagator of the doctrine of psychophysical parallelism, roughly describable as the doctrine that matter and mind are the two sides of one shield. This doctrine has found an extraordinary following among thinkers of every stripe from the Hegelians at one extreme to Huxley, Fiske, and Haeckel, at the other.
