[u]Logarithm[/u]

An artificial number, or number used in computation, belonging to a series [ins]or system of logarithms[/ins] having the following two properties. First, every natural, or ordinary, [del]number[/del] [ins]integral or fractional[/ins], [ins]in each system of logarithms[/ins] has a logarithm, and conversely, [del]every[/del] [ins]each[/ins] logarithm belongs to a natural number or antilogarithm. Secondly, the logarithms corresponding to any geometrical progression of natural numbers are in arithmetical progression. This is shown in the following tables, where two kinds of logarithms are given for three different geometrical series. Logarithms are [ins]generally[/ins] irrational numbers, so that they can only be expressed approximately; they are here given to 5 places of decimals: hence, the differences are sometimes in error by one in the last place.
