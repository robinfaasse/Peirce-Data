We have defined a finite class as one in which if
every individual has any one-to-one relation to some,
then every individual is also correlate of the same [del]re[/del]
relation. Classes that are not finite ought etymologically
to be called infinite; they are generally speaking of the
kind which I have called super-infinite, and intermediate
between the finite and the super-infinite are those which
I will now call the barely infinite. A barely infinite
class is one in which if every individual has a certain
one-to-one relation to some onethen every character of
which it is true, 1st, that if it belongs to any individu-
al it belongs to the one to which this individual has the
one-to-one relation, and 2nd, that it belongs to every
individual to which none is in this relation, such a char-
acter necessarily belongs to the whole class. In other
words, Fermat's mode of reasoning applies to such a class.
