We have defined a finite class in this way. Let c
be a one-to-one relation, and let x be the class. Then the
definition is that if every x is c of an x, every x is
also c'd by an x. Classes not finite ought etymologically
to be called infinite. These are generally of the kind
I have called super-infinite, but intermediate between
these and the finite are the barely infinite, which are
