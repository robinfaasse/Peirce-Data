ual significance only so far as they prove themselves to other
thoughts. So that thoughts are themselves signs which stand
for other objects of thought. And since, on the other hand, there
is no sign which the mind may not make use of in reasoning,
it follows that the science of thought in its intellectual sig-
nificance is one and the same thing with the science of the
laws of signs. Now there are many general truths with regard
to signs which hold good for all signs whatever, of necessity;
being involved in the essential nature of signs. The origin of
these principles is undoubtedly the nature of the mind. But they
are involved in so much of what is true of the mind as, implied
in our capability of reasoning at all and which may therefore be
said to be implicitly taken for granted by all men, that is, to be
deducible from what everybody agrees to and must agree [ins]to[/ins] before
