[u]The doctrine of substantive possibility[/u]

If I say.
# $\Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{i} \Sigma_{j} \bar{a}_{i} + r_{\alpha ji} \cdot b_{j}$

I assert the existence of $b_{j}$ which is matter of
fact, and not necessary. But if this is
not so, then
$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i} \cdot (\bar{r}_{\alpha ji} + \bar{b}_{j})$

whence it will follow that
$\Sigma_{\beta} \Pi_{j} \Sigma_{i} \bar{b}_{j} + r_{\beta ji} \cdot a_{i}$

Let $c_{\alpha ji}$ mean $j$ is in the one to one correspondence $\alpha$ to $i$

If
$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{i} \Sigma_{j} \bar{a}_{i} + c_{\alpha ji} \cdot b_{j}$
is not true, then what is true is that
$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i} \cdot (\bar{c}_{\alpha ji} + \bar{b}_{j})$
From this, how am I to infer
$\Sigma_{\beta} \Pi_{j} \Sigma_{i} \bar{b}_{j} + c_{\beta ji} \cdot a_{i}$ ?
[del]To say[/del] [del]$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{j} \Sigma_{i} \bar{b}_{j} + a_{i} \cdot \bar{c}_{\alpha ji}$[/del]
[del]would not be so much as to say[/del] that there are [del]two[/del] $b$s.
would be merely to say that if there is a $b$ there are more than one $a$s.

Therefore if there is no $b$ or if there are more than one $a$s
it is always false that
$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Sigma_{j} \Pi_{i} b_{j} \cdot (c_{\alpha ji} + \bar{a}_{i})$

!!!

To say that every collection of exclusive pairs
consisting of an $a$ and $b$ omits an $a$ and a $b$
is absurd; yet [del]to[/del] how it is so is not clear.
Let Pair$_{ij}$ means that $i$ and $j$ form [del]a[/del] a pair of
the collection of mutually exclusive pairs $\alpha$
Then the absurdity is
$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{j} a_{i} \cdot b_{j} \cdot \bar{\text{Pair}}_{ij}$
Because, I having selected a [del]pair of[/del] collection of pairs $\alpha$
you say I have omitted the pair $a_{i} \cdot b_{j}$. But I might
have included this. You cannot name any that
I might not have included.
No the point is
$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} \Sigma_{k} \text{[del]Pair[/del]}_{ik} \cdot \bar{a}_{i} + \bar{b}_{j} + \text{Pair}_{ik} \cdot b_{k} + \text{Pair}_{kj} \cdot a_{k}$
the absurdity is
$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{j} \Pi_{k} a_{i} \cdot b_{j} \cdot (\bar{\text{Pair}}_{ik} + \bar{b}_{k}) \cdot (\bar{\text{Pair}}_{kj} + \bar{a}_{k})$
and the absurdity consists in there being here a description
of a collection of pairs of which it is not true.
