[ins]✗[/ins] The following scheme, for example, would embrace all these distinctions.

I. The end is to superinduce upon feeling a certain quality, pleasure.

II. The end is to extend the existence of a subject,

1. Of something psychical, as a conscious life;

2. Of something physical, as a race.

III. The end is to realize a general idea,

1. This idea is a uniformity among feelings, as that of the greatest pleasure of the greatest number of persons.

2. This idea is a definite character [ins]to be impressed[/ins] upon a definite subject,

(a). This is psychical character capable only of psychical realization, as [del]the subject being of the nature of thoughts, or[/del] altruistic sentiments [del]or conscious motion[/del].

(b). This is a physical character capable only of physical realization. [del]This subject being of a physical nature, as outward society[/del]
