The most serious defect of this classification lies in the confusion resulting from its subdivision of rationalistic theory into only two main branches splitting upon the relatively insignificant question whether the end is completely attainable, or not. It would be preferable to recognize three grand classes of rationalistic moralists differing from one another concerning the more important question of the mode of being of the end. Some have made the end to consist in a [del]mere[/del] [ins]simple[/ins] quality of feeling, pleasure; others made it to consist in the mere extension of some fact, whether this be psychological, such as a prolongation of conscious life, or physical, such as the multiplication of the race;
