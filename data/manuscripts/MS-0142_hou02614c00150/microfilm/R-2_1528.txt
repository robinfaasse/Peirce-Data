[u]§1. On Multitude.[/u]

By a [u]thing[/u] I mean an object whose occurrence in any universe of reality or fiction is not [u]ipso facto[/u] constituted by any law or reason, but is an act, which however agreeable to law and reason, is, as act, arbitrary. An act takes place [u]hic et nunc[/u] and cannot be generalized without losing its vitality as act. A generalized act is a law; but a law, without an arbitrary act to carry it out in each instance, remains an impotent formula. It is a legislature without an executive, a judge without a sheriff.
