NOTES ON TOPICAL GEOMETRY.

§1. [del]On[/del] [ins][u]Multitude[/u][/ins].

By a [u]thing[/u] I mean an object whose occurrence in any universe of reality or fiction is not [u]ipso[/u] [u]facto[/u] constituted by any reason, but is an act which, however reasonable, is [del]not[/del] as an act arbitrary. [del]An act is something which[/del] takes place [u]hic et nunc[/u] and cannot be generalized without losing its character as an act. For this reason every thing is an individual.

The doctrine of some logicians that an individual is determinate in every respect is wrong. For whatever may be conceived to be determinate in every respect a description may be conceived to be determinate in every respect and yet it remains a mere description. A law may specify precisely what shall take place; but without the vital act that carries it out, it remains an impotent generality. Nor need an individual, — as far as any [del]law[/del] [ins]requirement[/ins] of logic goes, — be determinate in every respect. It is commonly assumed that in the physical universe atoms are determinate in every respect. But [del]there[/del] [ins]there[/ins] is no proof of this; and were it so, it would not be by logical [del]for[/del] necessity. Besides, atoms are not determinate in every respect, since their velocities are different at different times. It is true that this indeterminacy is supposed to be governed by law; but it is none the less indeterminacy.
