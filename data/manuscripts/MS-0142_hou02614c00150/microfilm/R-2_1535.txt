We may conceive a thing and its color to constitute a collection
of two units; but we do so only by creating an ideal world in
which the color exists independently of its being attached to
any thing. We may call a succession of minutes a collection,
[del]and yet[/del] though no minute is possible until those before it have
gone. But here there is only an observed dependence; and when
we speak of a collection of minutes we conceive them as logi-
cally independent of one another. [ins]For a[/ins] collection remains iden-
tical, no matter how the characters of its members may change;
and were the minutes to change to monkeys, it would remain the
same collection.

However metaphysical these considerations may seem, they
will be found, later, to be pertinent to a mathematical question.
