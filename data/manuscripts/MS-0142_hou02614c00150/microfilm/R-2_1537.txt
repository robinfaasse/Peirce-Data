The following is a particular case of an important proposition
in topical geometry. Suppose an empire without cyclosis on
a spheroidal surface (having all the coigns trihedral) con-
sists of sub-empires without cyclosis. Let E be the number
of outward coigns of the great empire, I that of its inward
(not interior) coigns. Let $e_n$ and $i_n$ be the same for any one of
the subempires. Then
$$I - E + 6 = \Sigma_n (i_n - e_n + 6).$$
If the subempires are single regions $i_n = 0$, and we have
$$E = I + 6 + \Sigma_n (e_n - 6).$$
In order to illustrate the use of this, note that the equation
$$4N_2 + 3N_3 + 2N_4 + N_5 = 12 + N_7 + 2N_8 + 3N_9 + \text{etc.},$$
would permit a map to consist of 13 pentagonal regions and one
heptagonal region. But in that case there would be an empire
consisting of the heptagon and two pentagons, which would have,
$3 + 6 + (1 - 1 - 1) = 8$ [del]outward coigns[/del] [ins]inward coigns[/ins]. [del]Hence, if the boundaries
between these three regions were erased, the result would be a map
consisting of an octagon, 3 tetragons, and 8 pentagons[/del] The rest of
the map would, therefore, be an empire of 11 pentagons with 8
inward coigns and 3 outward coigns. That is
$$3 = 8 + 6 - 11$$
