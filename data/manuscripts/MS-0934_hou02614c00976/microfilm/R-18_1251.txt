§24. One thing cannot be related to another, without they are brought
together by some mediating representation or reason. Suppose one man
murders another by giving him a poisonous drink. This supposes
that there is some regularity, or tendency to regularity, with which people
who [del]take[/del] drink similar potions [ins]experience[/ins] [del]undergo[/del] similar effects; otherwise, the
drink and the death are mere coincidences. True, the coincidence itself
is a relation, though another kind of relation. But one thing's being
subsequent to another implies a general [del]relation is[/del] system of relation-
ship among events, constituting time. Even the relations of similarity
and contrast imply regularities in the way the objects affect the com-
paring mind.

The reason is not necessarily a [u]good[/u] reason; it is simply
a representation according to which one of the two related things cor-
responds to the other.
