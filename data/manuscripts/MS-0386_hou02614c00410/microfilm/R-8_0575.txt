[u]Chap VIII. Of the Copula.[/u]

Every idea occupies time. In the time in which one is thought another may be thought. In this case [del]the latter[/del] is thought as a special case of the former. [del]This is called[/del] [del]the subsumption of the latter under the former.[/del]

Every judgment [del]consists in the comparison of ideas[/del] [del]for only by who[/del] express a relation of ideas, and consequently involves a comparison of them, and a thinking of them together. Every thinking of ideas together is a [ins]process of[/ins] subsumption. Consequently every proposition or expression of a judgment, may be put in the form "[del]A[/del] is a case under B." [del]This is what[/del] Thus, when we say "[del]man[/del] [ins]every man[/ins] is an animal," we say that man is [del]a case[/del] [ins]included[/ins] under animal as a special case of it. I shall use the symbol [del]$\prec$[/del] to [del]express[/del] [ins]declare[/ins] that [del]the[/del] [ins]the object of[/ins] the term written [del]before it[/del] is included under the object of the term written after it. Thus "man $\prec$ mortal" will mean "man is mortal," and "$-\surd \prec \surd$" will mean that the negative of a square root is itself a square root of the same quantity. The symbol $\prec$ or its equivalent in words, [ins]"is"[/ins] is [del]termed[/del] the copula by logicians. [ins]The term which precedes the copula is called the subject of the proposition & that which follows it the predicate; the latter is said to be predicated of the former.[/ins]
