of the various forms of Non-Euclidean
Geometry destroy Kant's argument from
the existence of Euclid to the apodeictic
certainty, universality and necessity
of the foundations of Euclid and of Euclid-
ean Space. There is, however, an Apriori
element in every notion of Space, a sort
of differentiating principle in the Schopen-
hauerian sense upon which rests every
form of geometry and to which it must
appeal as the guaranty of its possibility.
