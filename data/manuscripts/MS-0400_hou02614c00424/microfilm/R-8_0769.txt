"The qualities from which this association arises and by which the mind
is after this manner convey'd from one idea to another, are three,

Hume's giving a prime place to Cause and Effect here, has been
condemned by every critic as directly counter to his own views, ac-
cording to which causality is simply an effect of association by
contiguity. But Dr. Thomas Brown remarks that Hume probably made
the indenture for the convenience of postponing his main contention, un-
til his materials should have been gathered and his preparations made
for the battle. It may be remarked that Spinoza in his Ethica, [date]1677[/date],
seems to recognize no other principle of concatenation of ideas except
that of cause and effect. Hume's striking remark about association
being a gentle force has been quoted above. Another remark of
value is the following: "Here we have a kind of [u]Attraction[/u], which in
the mental world will be found to have as extraordinary effects
as in the natural, and to shew itself in as many and as various
forms."
