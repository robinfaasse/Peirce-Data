Primary Arithmetic

Suggestions to Teachers.

It must not be supposed that so long as children learn [del]numbers[/del] [ins]arithmetic[/ins], it makes no difference how they learn [del]them[/del] [ins]it[/ins]. Many persons know numbers; yet cannot [del]think of[/del] [ins]imagine[/ins] them without such fantastic [del]accessories[/del] accompaniments of shapes and colors, that they are greatly hampered in endeavoring to operate upon them with rapidity.

Many persons, [ins]even[/ins] professors of mathematics, — have no clear idea of what numbers are; though they have been using them all their lives.

The first thing to be done is to convey to the [del]child[/del] [ins]pupil[/ins] as clear an idea of number as a child can have, [ins]and[/ins] to teach him [del]the digits, without fantastic[/del] [ins]to think of[/ins] [del]in[/del] [ins]them in[/ins] plain, simple, and useful and flexible diagrammatic images; and to associate with these the Arabic figures.
