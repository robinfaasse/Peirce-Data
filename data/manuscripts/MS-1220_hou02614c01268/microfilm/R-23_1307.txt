"[u]True eloquence does not indeed consist in mere speech[/u]."
Were this "indeed" taken in the sense in which Baltimoreans
so often use [u]indeed[/u], or [u]indeedy[/u], no comma would be
needed. As it is, the flow of meaning is violently broken
and it should be printed
[u]True eloquence does not, indeed, consist in mere speech[/u].

"United, we stand; divided, we fall."
The semicolon should be a colon. The commas are [ins]perhaps[/ins] superfluous.
[u]United, we stand: divided, we fall.[/u]

"Any departure from the route named in the policy to a port
or place not named, and any delay in prosecuting the voyage,
without necessity or just cause, or any delay at a port named
in the policy for the prosecution of business not connected with
the business of the voyage, or any unreasonable delay at such
port in prosecuting the business of the voyage, is a deviation."
Strange that a port should be "named in the policy
for the prosecution of business not connected with the
business of the voyage." But it must be so, or he would have
put a comma after "policy."

"Our opinion is, that the trust should be terminated."
I see no good in this comma.
