[u]From Ruskin[/u]

All true happiness is both a consequence and cause of life;
it is a sign of its vigor, and a source of its continuance.

No soul can be perfect in an imperfect body; no body perfect
without perfect soul.

The world is not to be cheated of a grain; not so much as a
breath of its air can be drawn surreptitiously.

Christ's way is the only true one: begin at the feet; the face will take
care of itself.

I do not see the use of underselling them: cotton-cloth is already
twopence a yard, or lower; and yet bare backs were never more
numerous among us.

It will pay; not at first in currency, but in that which is the
end and source of currency, — in life; (and in currency richly
afterwards).

A poor boy need not always learn mathematics; but, if
you set him to do so, have the farther kindness to give him
good compasses, not cheap ones, whose points bend like lead.
