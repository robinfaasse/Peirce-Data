The pause that any good reader will make after the
word "ignorant" here is quite as long as that after "deny".

Waiving this point, however, it is not to be required of a
writer, not a speaker, that he should know how [del]best[/del] to
read what he writes for the eye.

The colon and semi-colon are extremely effective
when judiciously used; and what is required is a rule
that shall define the conditions under which the one
and the other are in place, be the proper pauses in reading
what they will.

It seems to me that when clauses are such that they
do not make complete sense, or not the sense intended,
when separate should ordinarily have a comma between
them; but when they make, indeed, sense, and the sense
intended each in itself, yet that they must be taken
in connection to bring out the idea, a semicolon is
wanted; and when the idea of the first is perfect, but
an immediate comment upon the matter of it puts it
into a new light, then a colon is wanted; while when
sentences are not so intimately connected, or when
there is no occasion specially to call attention to this
connection, a period is wanted. A dash is used when
