1. State in precisely what cases the [del]eight[/del] proposition [del]below[/del]
[ins]$P \prec \overline{C}$[/ins]

the same as with $C \prec \overline{P}$.

2. Do the same with $\overline{P} \prec C$ and $\overline{C} \prec P$.

3. State and prove whether
$J \prec (P \prec C)$ and $P \prec (J \prec C)$
are [del]the[/del] equivalent.

$(J \prec P) \prec C$ and $(J \prec C) \prec P$

6. Whether $(P \overline{\prec} C) \overline{\prec} J$ and $(J \overline{\prec} C) \overline{\prec} P$ are equivalent.
7. Draw up a scheme of all the equivalences between
propositions containing $J$, $P$, and $C$ in different
orders. Make it as symmetrical as possible.

8. In what case will one of the following be true
and the other false
$J \prec (P \overline{\prec} C)$ and $(J \prec P) \overline{\prec} C$

9. Same for the following
$J \overline{\prec} (P \prec C)$ and $(J \overline{\prec} P) \prec C$.

$(A \overline{\prec} B) \prec (C \prec D)$
and to $(A \overline{\prec} B) \overline{\prec} (C \prec D)$.
