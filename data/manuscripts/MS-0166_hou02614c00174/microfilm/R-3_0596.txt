[u]Definition 28[/u]. A [u]number[/u] is a vocable, character, or
other sign, belonging to a sequence of such signs which
are applied with reference to their places in the sequence.

[u]Illustration 21[/u]. Thus the words
[del]Eeny meeny mony mi[/del]
[del]Pasca lona[/del]
Bona lena bona stry

are a kind of numbers.

[u]Definition 29[/u]. A [u]quantity[/u] is whatever number answers
a given question. Its [u]value[/u] is the particular number which
answers the question.

[u]Illustration 22[/u]. Thus, a person's age is a quantity; and
it is the same quantity whatever his age turns out to be.
Two persons may be of the same age. That is, [del]the values of[/del]
[del]the two quantities are equal[/del] [ins]the two[/ins] have the same value. As a
man grows old the value of the quantity, his age, increases.

[u]Notation 1[/u]. In algebra instead of writing out the descrip-
tion of a quantity in words, or giving its name, we use a letter,
or combination of letters. This is not only a saving of trouble,
but it also facilitates the construction of arrays, by the study
of which we find out mathematical truths.

[u]Notation 2[/u] The sign of equality, =, means that the
the quantity written before it [ins]equals, or[/ins] has the same value as, the quantity
written after it. The whole expression is called an [u]equation[/u]
and the expressions of the two quantities are called the [del]first[/del]
[del]and second[/del] members of the equation.
