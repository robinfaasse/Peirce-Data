As we are still free to put the trace where we choose, we will [del]f[/del] make it the junction of the intersections of the $1^{\text{st}}$ and $4^{\text{th}}$ sides and the intersection of the $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $5^{\text{th}}$ sides. Since the first side is $\{C_0 D_0\}$ and one of the vertices of the fourth side is $[D_q]$, it follows, from the principle of the last corollary, that the fifth vertex is $[C_q]$. In like manner, since the second side is $\{C_{-p} D_0\}$ and one of the vertices of the fifth side is $[C_q]$ it follows that the sixth vertex is $[D_{p+q}]$. Since, then, the third side is $\{C_{-p} D_q\}$ and the sixth is $\{C_0 D_{p+q}\}$, it follows that these also intersect on the same trace. Q.E.D.
