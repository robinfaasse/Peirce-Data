[u]Art. 126. Definition 171.[/u] The [u]junction[/u] of two homaloidal places is [del]the[/del] [ins]the[/ins] homaloidal place of lowest dimensionality in which they are both contained; just as the [u]intersection[/u] of two homaloidal [del]spaces[/del] [ins]places[/ins] is the homaloidal place of highest dimensionality which they both contain.

[u]Scholium 61.[/u] The junction and intersection are like the least common multiple and greatest common divisor [del]of[/del] two integers.

[u]Scholium 62.[/u] The eight Corollaries 96-103 express all the relations between homaloids involved in Definition 162, except two outlying circumstances.

A part of mathematical thought more essential than its demonstrative procedure is the observation of the symmetries involved in the propositions. In graphics this is of peculiar utility.

The Corollaries 96-103 may be arranged in the following scheme.
