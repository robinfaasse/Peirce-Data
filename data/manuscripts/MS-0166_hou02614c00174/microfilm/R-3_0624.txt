[u]Chapter IV. Factors[/u]

[del]Art. 22.[/del] [u]Definitions[/u]. 39. A [u]part[/u] of a whole number, N, is a whole number unequal to that number, which, multiplied by a suitable whole number, called a [u]divisor[/u] of the first number, N, gives that number as the product. A [u]factor[/u] of a whole number, [ins]N,[/ins] is any [ins]whole[/ins] number which multiplied by a whole number gives the number, N. Thus, the parts of 6 are 3, 2, 1; its [u]divisors[/u] are 2, 3, 6; its [u]factors[/u] are 1, 2, 3, 6.

[ins]By the greatest common divisor of two [del]or more[/del] [ins]whole[/ins] numbers is meant that divisor of both of which every other divisor of both is a factor.[/ins]

40. A [u]prime[/u] number is number with a single part.

41. A [u]prime factor[/u] of a whole number is a prime number that is a factor of that number.

42. A [u]homogeneous[/u] number is a whole number [del]which[/del] [ins]having[/ins] a single prime factor. Thus, 81 = 3.3.3.3.1 is a [del]prime[/del] [ins]homogeneous[/ins] number, for 1 is not a prime number. The multiplicity of the factor is the number of times it [del]must enter[/del] [ins]occurs[/ins] as factor in the product.

A [u]heterogeneous[/u] number is a whole number having more than one prime factor.

44. A [u]composite[/u] number is a whole number which [del]is[/del] [ins]has[/ins] [del]more than one divisor.[/del] [ins]a part which is a divisor.[/ins] the product of two or more homogeneous parts.

45. Two numbers are said to be [u]prime[/u] to each other if they have no common divisor.
