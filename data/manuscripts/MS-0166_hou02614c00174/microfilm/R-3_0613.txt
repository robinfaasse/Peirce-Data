The word [u]negative[/u] was used in this sense by the celebrated
educationalist Pierre de la Ramée, known as Ramus (b. 1515-d. 1572).
What suggested it was that two negatives make an affirmative, that
is, "not no" is "yes". There is not enough analogy between the ideas
[del]to make the choice of the word judicious.[/del] Negative (from [u]negare[/u], containing
the root NA, whence our [u]no[/u]) came in in Late Latin to translate Greek [u]ἀποφα-[/u]

[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Notation[/u] . The sign of multiplication is a cross $\times$;
that is $a \times b$ means the product of $a$ and $b$. A dot can be used
instead; or all sign may be omitted and the factors simply written
one after the other. In this case, the parenthesis about them is
[del]always omitted.[/del] That is, $ab+c$ means $(a \times b)+c$, never $a \times (b+c)$. In
reading omit the sign, if it is omitted in writing, or say [del]"dot" if there[/del]
is one, or "times" if there is a cross.
