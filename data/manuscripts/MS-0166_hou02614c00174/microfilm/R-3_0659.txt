[u]Art. 74. Definition 83.[/u] A set of objects is said to be arranged in a [u]ring[/u], when they are all connected together but so that taking any four of them, [del]two[/del] [ins]each[/ins] of the four [del]is[/del] [ins]has[/ins] one of the others opposite to it, so that if the connections [del]through[/del] [ins]at[/ins] the other two are [del]both[/del] severed, the two opposites are no longer connected.

Thus, if A is joined to Z, the letters of the alphabet in their order are arranged in a ring; for taking any four, as E, I, R, V, [del]two of these[/del] [ins]there are two[/ins] pairs of opposites, as ER and IV, [del]which are[/del] [ins]each pair being[/ins] completely severed, if the immediate connections are cut at the other pair.
