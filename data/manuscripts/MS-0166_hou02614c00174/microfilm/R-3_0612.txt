[[u]Memorandum[/u] . Negative terms were recognized by the
Greek Arithmetician Diophantus [ins]only[/ins] in the fourth century [del]A.D.[/del] [ins]of our era[/ins]. But
negative [del]quantities or[/del] numbers were first recognized by the Hindoo
[del]algebraists[/del] algebraists, especially Brahmagupta, in the seventh
century [del]A.D.[/del] [ins]of our[/ins] era. They called the positive numbers "possessions", the
negative "debts." They also illustrated them by the account of
time, and by measurements in the two senses along a line.
The Arabians seem never to have had the conception; but the earliest
practical arithmetician of Western Europe, Leonardo of Pisa, who
wrote in 1202 has two problems [del]in[/del] which have such quantities
for their solutions. He calls them debts. [del]Others from time to time[/del]
We hear no more of them until [ins]near the end of the fifteenth century (1489)[/ins] Johannes Widmann [del]who[/del] gives
such problems as this: "A man bought 6 eggs and [u]minus[/u] 2 pence,
paying 4 pence and 1 egg. [del]How much[/del] What was the price of an
egg?" The absurdity of the transaction only brings out more strongly
his distinct conception of a negative. Others from time to time
caught the idea. Thus
6x - 2 = x + 4
5x = 6
