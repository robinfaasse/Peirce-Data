Notation 17. We will [del]write[/del] [ins]use[/ins] $\Upsilon$ (the initial of the Greek
$\dot{\upsilon}\pi\alpha\rho\chi\epsilon\iota\nu$, to happen) before the description of a place,
to denote [del]how many[/del] [ins]the number of[/ins] places answering the description
there are. But in many cases this letter $\Upsilon$ may be
omitted, without causing any confusion.

Illustration 81. Thus, putting for the moment $\Theta$
for any description of place,
$\Upsilon\Theta > 0$
will mean there is at least one such place,
$\Upsilon\Theta = 1$
that there is precisely one such place. If $\Lambda$ is another
description of place
$(\Upsilon\Lambda)(\Upsilon\Theta) = 0$
will mean that both kinds of places cannot exist.

Corollary 108. It follows from Postulate 9 with
the definitions
$\Upsilon\{AB\} = 1$ if $\Upsilon[A] = \Upsilon[B] = 1$, unless $A = B$, that is,
[del]unless $\Upsilon[AB] = 1$. This will be expressed[/del]
[del]or[/del] $\Upsilon\{AB\} = \Upsilon[A] \times \Upsilon[B]$.
This holds even if $[A]$ and $[B]$ be descriptions possibly
applicable to a number of points, provided it be understood
that the different ways falling together are not to cause
them to be numbered as fewer, which is the usual understanding
in mathematics.
