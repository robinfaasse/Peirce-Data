If we add to the number of a year, any other number,
say 10, we have the number of the year 10 years later; or, to
express [del]it[/del] [ins]the matter[/ins] algebraically, the number of a year plus [ins]any number,[/ins] $x$ is the
number of a year $x$ years later. In the same way, it ought to
be the rule that if from the number of any year any number,
$y$, is subtracted, we get the number of the year $y$ years earlier.
This is the case with our ordinary reckoning as long as the
remainder is one or more. But 5 from 5 leaves 0; and
therefore the year 5 years before A.D. 5 ought to be called
the year zero, [ins]as astronomers do call it,[/ins] and not 1 B.C., as historians call it.
