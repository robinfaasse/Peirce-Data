It may also be made evident as follows.
If there is a point $[b\ e]$, that point
lies in $b$, which we may write
$[b\ e] \prec \{b\}$
And if there is a plane $(b\ c)$, that
plane contains $b$, or
$\{b\} \prec (b\ c)$
Hence,
$[b\ e] \prec (b\ c)$
But $[b\ e]$ is also contained in $\{e\}$. Hence
$[b\ e] = [(b\ c) e]$
And in like manner
$[c\ d] = [c (d\ e)]$.

Hence
$\{[b\ e][c\ d]\} = \{[(b\ c) e][c (d\ e)]\}$.
