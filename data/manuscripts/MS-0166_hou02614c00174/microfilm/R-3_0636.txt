<u>Scholium 18</u>. Several of the definitions of this article ought in strictness to be stated, as definitions, only so far as previous statements do not determine their truth or falsity. Thus, Definition 59, in case M is divisible by N and N by I, only states what can be proved from previous definitions and axioms; and so far it is not a definition. But so far as it relates to irreducible fractions, it is necessary to explaining what is meant by the multiplication and division of such numbers. No doubt, many teachers will prefer to consider [del]it[/del] [ins]this and some other definitions[/ins] as axioms; it makes no practical difference. Similar remarks apply to Definition 60, in case the numerators are divisible by the denominator, as in [del]6[/del] [ins]$\frac{6}{2} + \frac{10}{2}$[/ins].

[del]Definition 62.[/del] Definition 62 is a part of the definition of the system of rational numbers. Or it may be considered as an axiom. But it contains little which cannot be proved from definitions 59 and 60.
