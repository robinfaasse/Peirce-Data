164. Upon a globular surface what are the [ins]simplest[/ins] [del]varieties[/del] of monohedra?

The whole surface being globular $1 + X = 1$, $K = 0$, $\Pi = 1$, $A = 0$. Hence its valency is $1 - 0 + 1 - 0 = 2$.

This valency may be made up by a single face without summit [del]or[/del] [ins]nor[/ins] edge. It will be [del]an anac[/del] a globular anacral monohedron.

It may be made up of a single face without periphraxis, together with a single summit. [del]It[/del] [ins]This[/ins] will [del]be[/del] give a globular monacral monohedron, obtained by marking a point on a globe.

It may be made up by any number of summits [del]connected by edges one fewer in number[/del] and edges, so long [ins]as[/ins] the network of edges have no cyclosis. This will be formed by drawing lines and dots on a globe so as not to enclose any face.

165. [del]Upon a globular surface what are the simplest dihedra?[/del]

Two faces may be separated by one edge of cyclosis 1. The valency of the edge is 0, and that of each face 1.

Or, the two faces may be separated by a polygon of any number of sides. There will be $N$ summits, $N$ simple edges and the total valency, $\Lambda = 2$, will be made up by $\Lambda_0 = N$, $\Lambda_1 = N$, $\Lambda_2 = 1 + 1$. $N - N + 2 = 2$.
