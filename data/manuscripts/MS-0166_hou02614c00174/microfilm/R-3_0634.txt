[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Definition[/u] . A collection of objects are said to be arranged in a ring, when [del]their[/del] [ins]their[/ins] connections are such that of any four of the objects, one pair is separated by the other pair, so that it is impossible to pass from one object of the pair to the other along the line of connection without passing through one or other object of the other pair.

[u]Illustration[/u] . Thus, if the letters of the alphabet are arranged in a ring, then taking any four letters, say [del]F[/del] [ins]E[/ins], K, O, A, the pairs EO and KA are such that you cannot move round the ring from E to O without passing A one way, and K the other way.
