[u]Art[/u] . [u]Definition[/u] . The chorisis of a place is the
number of particles that can so lie in it that no two
can come together by moving in the place.

[u]Gloss[/u] . [u]Chorisis[/u], pronounced koarisis, is simply
the Greek word [greek]χώρισις[/greek], separation.

[u]Illustrations[/u] . The chorisis of two points is two.
A group of particles have no chorisis; because particles
are not places.
The chorisis of three points is three.
The chorisis of a chain is the number of its links.
The chorisis of a nest of boxes considered as places
equals the number of separate places.

[u]Art[/u] . [u]Definition[/u] . The cyclosis of a place is the
number of times it has to be [del]broken[/del] [ins]cut[/ins] through before every
simple filament in it can contract to nothing without
leaving the place.

[u]Gloss[/u] . [u]Cyclosis[/u], prononounced sikloasis, is the
Greek word [greek]κύκλωσις[/greek], enclosing, introduced into topology
by Listing, in 1861.

[u]Illustrations[/u] . A [ins]point, or[/ins] group of points, can have no cyclosis.
A simple bounded stretch has cyclosis, 0.
The line of Figure 115 has its cyclosis equal to 1.
The line of Figure 116 has its cyclosis equal to 2.
