[del]Art[/del] [u]Definitions[/u] . A line, or [u]simple line[/u], is described by a single motion. A [del]complex line, or linear configuration[/del] [ins]or linear configuration[/ins] is described by two or more distinct motions.

[del]Art[/del] . [u]Definition[/u] . [del]An[/del] [ins]An[/ins] [u]extremity[/u] of a line[del],[/del] is a point of it which in every description of the line must be at the beginning or end of [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] motion.

A [u]furcation[/u] of a line[ins],[/ins] or linear configuration is a point which is the limit of three or more distinct portions of the line or linear configuration. Thus, in Figure 111, the point O is a furcation, because it is the common limit of the three portions OA, OB, OC.

A [u]free extremity[/u] of a line or linear configuration is an extremity that is not a furcation.

A [u]self-returning[/u] line is a line whose [del]generation[/del] [ins]description[/ins] is completed by the return of the describing particle to its initial position.

[u]Corollary[/u] . A simple self-returning line has no extremity.

[u]Definitions[/u] . A [u]stretch[/u] is a [ins]simple[/ins] portion or a whole of a line which extends as far as the form of the line permits without passing a furcation.
