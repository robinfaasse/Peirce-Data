[u]Theorem[/u] . [u]The negative of the sum of two numbers[/u] [u]equals the sum of their negatives[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. For let A and B be the two numbers. Then the sum of their negatives is (-A) + (-B). The negative of the sum is the number which added to the sum gives 0. But (A + B) + [(-A) + (-B)] = A + {B + [(-A) + (-B)]} = A + {[(-A) + (-B)] + B} = A + {(-A) + [(-B) + B]} = A + {(-A) + 0} = A + (-A) = 0. Therefore, A + B added to (-A) + (-B) gives zero; and consequently (-A) + (-B) = -(A + B). Q.E.D.
