<u>Scholium</u>. If we mark the points of a ray by numbers
in any convenient way in their order

[fig]A number line from -9 to 9 with tick marks[/fig]

there will be some point marked $\infty$. That will naturally, though
[ins]not necessarily,[/ins] be very far off, and will be called <u>the point at infinity</u>. In
that sense, graphics recognizes a point at infinity. But graphics
makes no hypothesis whatever about measurement. None of the
propositions of graphics would be disturbed [ins]in the least[/ins] if there were two
different points at an infinite distance in opposite directions,
nor even if the whole circuit of the ray had a finite measure.
We may as well say, at once, however, that there can be no
sensible error introduced in perspective if we assume that there
is on each ray but one point at an infinite distance, and
that all the points at an infinite distance, or say at the firma-
ment, lie on a plane.
