[u]Theorem[/u] . [u]The negative of the sum of two numbers[/u] [u]equals the sum of their negatives[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. For the sum of two numbers added to the sum of their negatives is, by the associative and commutative principles, equal to zero.

[u]Art[/u] . [u]Notation[/u] . The sign of multiplication is [u]x[/u], or a dot; or the factors are written one after the other, [del]with[/del] the multiplying sign being omitted. In reading, [ins]the sign[/ins] [del]it[/del] is omitted, called "dot," or "times". The product of two letters A and B may, then, be expressed AB = A.B = A x B. When the sign of multiplication is omitted, the parenthesis about the product is omitted. Thus ab+c is the same as (a x b) + c, and never a(b+c).

[u]Notation[/u] . The sign of division is [u]÷[/u], the dividend coming before and the divisor after; but / is also used. Therefore, a ÷ b = a/b; and a/b = c means that a = cb.

Division is also expressed in the form of a fraction; so that [fig]fraction a over b[/fig] = a/b.

The sign of a ratio [ins]∷[/ins] is also frequently used instead of a [del]mark[/del] [ins]sign[/ins] of division. In fact the ideas of ratio and quotient are generally confused together in the minds of [del]modern[/del] [ins]advanced[/ins] mathematicians, although it is important to begin by distinguishing between them.
