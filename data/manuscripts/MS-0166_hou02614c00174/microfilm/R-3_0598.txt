ALGEBRA

[u]Art 25[/u]. [u]Definition 27[/u]. [u]Algebra[/u] is generally understood to mean a branch of mathematics which studies arrays of letters and signs; in opposition to [u]geometry[/u] which studies configurations of lines.

[u]Common algebra[/u] is that branch of mathematics by which we work out rules for performing arithmetical computations, especially when this is done by studying arrays of letters, as it usually is in modern times. But there are are geometrical [del]ways[/del] (commonly called graphical) ways of doing this, [del]to which a great[/del] and some mathematicians call the study algebra, even when conducted in that way. If we call [del]it[/del] [ins]that method[/ins] [u]graphical algebra[/u], [del]the[/del] as opposed to [u]literal[/u] algebra, there can be no mistake in our meaning, although it is not algebra, in the ordinary sense of the word.

[u]Illustration 20[/u]. When we say that [ins]by[/ins] algebra [del]works out[/del] [ins]we[/ins] rules for performing computations, we do not refer to the fundamental operations merely. Thus, by means of arithmetical analysis we can ascertain at what times the two hands of a clock are in conjunction. But algebra will furnish a rule applicable not merely when one hand moves 12 times as fast as the other
