[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Definitions[/u] . A [u]hamma[/u] (plural, [u]hammata[/u]) is a point specially marked on a line and regarded as terminating every stretch which extends up to it on the line.

A [u]dihamma[/u], [u]trihamma[/u], [u]tetrahamma[/u], [u]pentahamma[/u], [u]hexahamma[/u], etc. is a hamma of 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6 radiating stretches.

The [u]valency[/u] of a hamma is one less than the number of its radiating stretches.

Let the word [u]edge[/u] be used to mean [del]the[/del] [ins]a line or[/ins] portion of a line [del]either[/del] [ins]non-[/ins]intersecting itself or another, [del]and if ending[/del] [ins]unless self-returning,[/ins] ending at a hamma and extending from that to a hamma [del], or to a free extremity[/del].
