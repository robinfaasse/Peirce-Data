But if logic is thus to precede philosophy,
will it not be unphilosophical logic? Perhaps
logic is not in much need of philosophy. Ma-
thematics, which is a species of logic, has never
had the least need of philosophy in doing its
work. Besides, even if logic should require
subsequent remodelling in the light of philo-
sophy, yet the unphilosophical logic with
which we are obliged to set out will surely
be better than no logic at all.

intelligible. The object of philosophy is to render
everything intelligible. Philosophy thus postu-
lates that the processes of nature are intelli-
gible. [u]Postulates[/u], I say, not assumes. It may
