Three questions, at least, I think it must
be admitted, ought to form the subject of studies
preliminary to the formation of any philo-
sophical theory; namely, 1[sup]st[/sup], the purpose
of the theory, 2[sup]nd[/sup], the proper method of
discovering it, 3[sup]rd[/sup] the method of proving
it to be true. I think, too, it can hardly be
denied that it will be safer to consider
these questions concerning the particular
theory which is to be sought [del]ought to[/del], in the
light of whatever one can ascertain regarding
the functions, the discovery, and the es-
tablishment of sound theories in general.
