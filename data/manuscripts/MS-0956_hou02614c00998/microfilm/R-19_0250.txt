The two words [u]logic[/u] and [u]reason[/u] take their
origin from two opposite views of the nature
of thought. [u]Logic[/u], from [u]λόγος[/u], meaning
word and [u]reason[/u], embodies the Greek no-
tion that reasoning cannot be done without
language. [u]Reason[/u], from the Latin [u]ratio[/u],
originally meaning an account, implies that
reasoning is an affair of computation, re-
quiring, not words, but some kind of diagram,
or abacus, or figures. Modern formal logic,
especially the logic of relatives, shows the
Greek view to be substantially wrong, the
