[u]Footnote, continued[/u]

Feest is doubted by Gil in two passages. It is unnoticed by other
orthoepists of the period. While it is spelled as now in the XVIth century
in the great majority of cases, the spellings feest and fest are not
rare. The poets pretty uniformly rhyme it with short syllables. The
rhyme with guest was a favorite one. Shakespeare uses it for in
five passages. Here is one:

[u]Balthazar[/u]. Small cheere and great welcome makes a merrie feast.
[u]Antipholus of Ephesus[/u]. I, to a niggardly Host, and more sparing guest.

Shakespeare also has -

My cake is tough, but I'le in among the rest,
Out of hope of all but my share of the feast.

Giles Fletcher has,

The jolly priest, in ivy garlands drest,
Chanted wild orgials in honour of the feast.
