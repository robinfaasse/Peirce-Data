[u]Deaf[/u] was drawled by [u]Cheke, 1550[/u], and [u]Smith, 1568[/u] (fol. 24). From that time, the orthoepists are dumb concerning this word, until the "Expert Orthographist", in 1704, tells us that it was in his time pronounced [u]deef[/u]. In 1766, the Scot Buchanan and in 1780 the Irish Sheridan [del]make[/del] [ins]pronounce[/ins] it [u]def[/u]. The spellings show us that these were dialectical variations which had existed all along; for the word is found spelled [u]deefe[/u] [u]deefe[/u] and [u]def[/u], though rarely, in the XVI century. As long as it was drawled, there were no rhymes for it except [u]sheaf[/u] and [u]leaf[/u].

[u]Dread[/u] was drawled by [u]Smith, 1568[/u], and [del]by[/del] [u]Butler, 1633[/u], (p. 129). It was shortened by [u]Cooper, 1685[/u], and by [u]Jones, 1701[/u]. The spellings show that this was the revival of a dialectical variation; for [u]dred[/u] occurs, though rarely, in the XVIth century, as does also [u]dreed[/u]. It is generally [del]rhymed[/del] [ins]drawled[/ins] with [del]long[/del] syllables. Thus Shakespeare has,
"Why hunt I then for colour or excuses?
All orators are dumbe when beautie pleadeth;
Poor wretches have remorse in poor abuses;

Affection is my captaine, and he leadeth."
