By Smith [ins]1569,[/ins] [del]A[/del] Gil, 1619, set down this pronunciation as dialectical. Nevertheless, it is supported by frequent cases of the spellings bet, and bettre, and by rhymes. Donne has,

Our two souls, therefore (which are one)
Though I must go, endure not yet
A breach, but an expansion,
Like gold to airy thinness beat.

Deal, the present tense of the verb, was in the XVIIth century uniformly spelled with ea or the earlier equivalent dele. It was therefore chanted. The noun [ins]was[/ins] too often spelled dell, and the preterit delt. The regular spelling dealt or dealed was, therefore, merely etymological. Price, 1665, chanted deal, and Miege, 1688, made dealt short. Shaks. The Passionate Pilgrim has,

My shepherds pipe can sound no deal;
My wethers bell rings dolefull knell.
