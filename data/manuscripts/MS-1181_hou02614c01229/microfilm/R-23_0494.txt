Indeed, it is not necessary to go back earlier than Pope, to find heath short:

Oft, as in airy rings they skim the heath,
The clamorous lap-wings feel the leaden death.

The indications are that [u]heath[/u] was usually pronounced short.

[u]Bequeath[/u] seems to be noticed by no early orthoepist. The spelling [u]bequeth[/u] occurs in the XVI th century, not only in wills but in books printed with some care. It is usually rhymed with short syllables, the th being atonic, as it was in the verb [u]breathe[/u], then pronounced [u]breth[/u], and sometimes so written. For rhymes, Drummond has

This Life, which seems so fair,
Is like a bubble blown up in the air
By sporting children's breath,
Who chase it everywhere,
And strive who can most motion it bequeathe.
