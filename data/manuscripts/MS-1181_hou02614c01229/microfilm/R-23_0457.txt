Anglo Saxon was formerly often printed with peculiar
forms for some of the letters; namely, [fig]g-like letter[/fig] for g, [fig]i-like letter[/fig] for i, [fig]r-like letter[/fig] for r, [fig]s-like letter[/fig] for s, [fig]t-like letter[/fig] for t, [fig]y-like letter[/fig] for y. But
these convey a very false idea of the character of Anglo Saxon
writing. Every country has always had its peculiarities
of handwriting. In Anglo-Saxon England two general
styles of minuscule prevailed, one in Kent and Wessex,
derived from the Gallican cursive of the 6th century, the
other in Northumbria, which was based on the Irish "run-
cials" or minuscules, which in its turn came from the Gallican
cursive. The two styles were [del]very much alike[/del], and did not
differ materially from contemporary writing in France. The
