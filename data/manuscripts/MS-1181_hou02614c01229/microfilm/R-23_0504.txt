For the testimony of the orthoepists is as follows:

<u>Pleasure</u> was drawled by Gil, 1619, (pp. 89, 144), and Butler, 1633, (pp. 19, 24, 46, 55, 104, etc.); while it is [del]short[/del] [ins]was made short[/ins] by Price, 1668, and by Lediard, 1725.

<u>Treasure</u> is shortened by Salisbury, 1547, and [del]by[/del] Cheke, 1555, [ins]drawled by[/ins] Smith, 1568, and by Gil, 1619, in one passage (p. 124), though in another (p. 77) he makes it short. It is short according to Price, 1668, Jones, 1701, and Lediard, 1725.

<u>Leisure</u> was drawled by Price, 1668, Miège, 1688, Jones, 1701, the Expert Orthographist, 1704, and Buchanan, 1766. Benjamin Franklin shortened it. Sheridan, 1780, pronounced the first syllable 'lee'.

<u>Measure</u> was made short by Price, 1668, and by Jones, 1701. At an earlier date, [ins]say in the XVIth century and later,[/ins] when these words were accented on the last syllable, which was pronounced like -ure in French, the first syllable of measure was probably short.
