These are examples of a way of rhyming beast so common, that one
can hardly doubt that it represented a common pronunciation,
especially as vowels have a particular tendency to shortening
before s followed by a mute. All the orthoepists, however, drawl
the vowel, as Palsgrave, 1530, Bullokar, 1580, and Gil, 1619 (pp. 12, 124).
Hodges, 1643, says that best and beast sound so near alike as
often to be confused, — a remark which certainly implies that
beast ought to be drawled, but perhaps that it was often shortened.
Price, 1668, and later authorities drawl it.
