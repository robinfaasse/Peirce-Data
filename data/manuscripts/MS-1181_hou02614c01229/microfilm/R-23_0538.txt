7th. In the pages of books printed about 1600, we [del]often[/del] find consonants inserted which we omit; as in hability, abhominable, preheminent, proheme, cuckhold, whaule-eied, accompt, anduile, costardmonger, woodbind, eccho, snible, margent, orphsant, stalant (for stallion), Portingall, sclander, sindge, squince.

But much more often consonants are omitted which we now insert; as estarre, gost, reume, reumaticke, rubarbe, baptime, cost-card, crum, Duch, deum, graffe (for graft), hoise (for hoist), holsome, iaumes (for jambs), iland, mariage, num, oute (for woof), plummer, pompion, rampire (for rampart), sackloth, saphire, stick, stockin, wensday, wasted. These spellings are mostly phonetic.

In other cases the consonants are different from those we use; as in abricote, to ake, catharre, dragagant (for tragacanth), eglogue, glisters, libberd (for leopard), marmalet, mastine, salet (for salad), petigree.
