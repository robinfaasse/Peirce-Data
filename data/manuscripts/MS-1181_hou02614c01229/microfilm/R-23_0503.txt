This is from a little piece in five stanzas by Quarles.

Thy babbling tongue tells golden tales
Of endless treasure;
Thy bountie offers easy sales
Of lasting pleasure;
Thou ask'st the conscience what she ails,
And swear'st to ease her.

This shows the drift; and three two other of the five stanzas
contain the triple rhyme, treasure-measure-pleasure. Thus,
all these words were certainly dranted. But Milton probably pro-
nounced the words short when he wrote,

And add to these retired Leisure,
That in trim gardens takes his pleasure;

and,

Straight mine eye hath caught new pleasures,
Whilst the landscape round it measures.
