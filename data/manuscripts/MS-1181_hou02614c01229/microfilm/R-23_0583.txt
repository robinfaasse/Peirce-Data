[u]Pleasant[/u] had it first syllable drawled by Gil (p. 142) and Butler (pp. 27, 51, 76, 160.)

[u]Pheasant[/u] had the first syllable shortened by Salisbury, about [date]1550[/date].

[u]Leaven[/u] was regularly spelled with [u]ea[/u].

[u]Treasure[/u] was drawled by Cheke, [date]1555[/date], and Smith, [date]1568[/date]; also by Gil, [date]1619[/date], in one place (p. 126); but in another (p. 71) he shortens it.

[u]Measure[/u] was regularly spelled with [u]ea[/u]. It was pronounced short by Price, [date]1668[/date], rather weak evidence considering his date. But then it naturally would be short; and the [u]ea[/u] is partly to be accounted for by the general practice of using this digraph to represent a French [u]e[/u] not acute; and partly from the notion that [u]measure[/u] was connected with [u]mete[/u], spelled about [date]1600[/date] regularly [u]meate[/u], and so pronounced according to Hodges ([date]1643[/date]).

[u]Realm[/u] was drawled by Gil (p. 122). Gil sounds the [u]l[/u], with his usual pedantry. Hodges, [date]1643[/date], tells us that it was silent.
