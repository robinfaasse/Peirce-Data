[u]Foot-note, continued.[/u]

Who hath left his darling, th' east,
To wanton on that spicie nest.

Crashaw, [del]has[/del], in his Hymn to the Name of Jesus, has,

What did their weapons but with wider pores
Enlarge thy flaming-breasted lovers,
More freely to transpire
That impatient fire
The heart that hides thee hardly covers?
What did their weapons but set wide the doors
For thee? fair purple doors of love's devising,
The ruby windows that enrich'd the east
Of thy so oft-repeated rising.
Each wound of theirs was a new morning,
And re-enthron'd thee in thy rosy nest,
With blush of thine own blood the day adorning.

A perfect rhyme was certainly desirable here. It seems, on the
whole, probable that the pronunciation "est" was common.
