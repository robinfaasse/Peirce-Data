The digraph ea seems to have been the invention of
[del]XVIth[/del] century printers. It was intended to represent a
vowel-sound now lost from the standard speech, but
which is preserved in the drawling New England pro-
nunciation of the word bread. This sound was, in
Shakespeare's day, heard in [ins]most[/ins] many words in which ea now
has the sound of ee, such as ease, each, beam, creature, fear,
grease, leave, least, leaf, lean, meat, neat, preach, peace,
please, read (present tense), reason, rear, sea, season, seam, teach, treason,
veal, wheat, weak, year, zeal. The same sound was also then heard
in many words which have since been shortened, as bread,
dead, deadly, death, dread, health, lead (metal), meant, seamstress,
spread (present tense), sweat (present tense), threat, tread, weapon.
