unphonetic.

[u]Spread[/u], in the present tense, was drawled by Butler (pp. 90.118); in the preterit, it was shortened by Gil (p. 106) and Butler (pp. 92, 95, 97, 109, 118). Thus, the spelling of the preterit was again perfectly unphonetic.

[u]Steal[/u] was drawled by Cheke (1550); but [u]stealth[/u], which in Middle English was [u]stalþe[/u], was almost certainly short, although positive evidence is wanting.

[u]Sweat[/u], in the present tense, was drawled by Smith (fol. 20) and by Gil (pp. 48, 111). But in the preterit it was shortened by Gil (p. 48) and [del]also[/del] [ins]Smith shortened it[/ins] when it was a noun (fol. 20). Consequently, the spelling of the preterit and noun were unphonetic. The drawl of the present tense is illustrated by Shakespeare:

By this, the love-sicke queene began to sweat,
For where they laie the shadow had forsook them,
And Titan, tired in the mid-daye heat,
With burning eie did hotlie overlooke them.
