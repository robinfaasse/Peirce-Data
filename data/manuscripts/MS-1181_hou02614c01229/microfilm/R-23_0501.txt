* The words [u]leisure[/u], [u]treasure[/u], [u]measure[/u], [u]pleasure[/u] are so con-
tinually rhymed together, and these rhymes are such favorites
with all the poets, that there can be little doubt that they
were pronounced with the same sound of [u]ea[/u]. A very few ex-
amples may be given of the great multitude that occur.
Shakespeare has,
Haste still paies haste, and [u]leasure[/u] answers [u]leasure[/u];
Like doth quit like, and [u]Measure[/u] still for [u]Measure[/u].
A perfect rhyme was here indispensable. Shakespeare further has,
What hast thou din'd. The Tailor staies thy [u]leasure[/u]
To decke thy bodie with his ruffling [u]treasure[/u].
Also,
That god forbid, that made me first your slave,
I should in thought controule your times of [u]pleasure[/u],
Or at your hand th' account of houres to crave,
Being your vassail bound to stay your [u]leasure[/u].
