pronunciation of three centuries ago. The reply is, that no sooner
was a relative state of fixity reached in the spelling, than half
a dozen elaborate proposals of spelling-reform were put forth
in the interest of phoneticism. These books, together with
some others in which English sounds were carefully described,
backed up by various other sources of information, enable us
after systematic and careful study, to ascertain the pronun-
ciation of the language in Shakespeare's time with conside-
