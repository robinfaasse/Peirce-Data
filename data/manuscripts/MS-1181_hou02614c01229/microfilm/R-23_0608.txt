[u]10th[/u], Calligraphic spellings are done away with except in
monosyllables. In particular, consonants are not doubled
without other reason than ornament, k is not employed when
c would do as well, ck or cke is changed to c when this an-
swers as well, tch is often changed to ch, and dge to ge; as
[del]sin (not sinne)[/del]
[ins]in[/ins] tolerable (not tollerable), civil (not ciuell), arsenic (not arsenike,
nor arsenicke), achieve (not atchieve), [del]allege (not alledge)[/del] [ins]college (not colledge).[/ins]

[u]11th[/u], Etymology is more strictly followed, the Latin spelling
being usually preferred, but sometime the French, as bdellium
(not bedellium), biscuit (not bisket), virtue (not [del]vir[/del] vertue),
alien (not aliant).

[u]12th[/u], The apostrophe is used to mark the possessive case.
