In the middles of words, while consonants were often
doubled about 1600 which are not doubled now, as in
ballance, intollerable, etc., yet about as often we find con-
sonants doubled now appearing then as single, as in opor-
tunity, balast, cofer, cabage, arasie, hisope. In particular, it
was not then considered necessary to double a consonant
following a short vowel and preceding a syllabic l; and
we frequently meet with such spellings as adle, dazle, frisle,
medle, nible, peble, sadle, throttle.

Of single consonants, the most ornamental in the writing
of the XVth and XVIth centuries was k; and a decided
inclination to use this letter is manifest. Words which now
end in c, then ended in cke, or at an earlier date in ke. We
also find k [del]printed where c[/del] [ins]where c[/ins] would even then have been more
usual, as in akorne, akre, kankar, kembe (for combe), kiuer
(for couer), maskle, reskew, skaffold, skritch owle. We also find
anker, oker, and bisket, stomack.
