Tread was drawled by [del]Ascham[/del] [ins]Coke[/ins], 1550, [del]Smith[/del] [ins]and by Bullokar[/ins], 1580. Butler, 1633, sometimes drawled it (pp. 81, 85, [ins]sometimes shortened it (pp. 117, 118, 119).[/ins] Cooper, 1685, makes it short. Agreeably to these testimonies we find the word usually rhymed with drawled syllables by the [del]earlier[/del] [ins]some[/ins] poets, and with short syllables by [del]the later poets[/del] [ins]others[/ins]. Thus,

[del]Drayton[/del] [ins]Sidney[/ins] has,

And every little grass
Broad itself spreadeth,
Proud that this bonny lass
Upon it treadeth.

Herrick has,

This done, then to th' enamell'd meads
Thou go'st; and as thy foot there treads, etc.

Nezgrovell has the same rhyme. On the other hand Ben Jonson has,

Pacuvius, Accius, him of Cordova dead,
To live again, to hear thy buskin tread.
