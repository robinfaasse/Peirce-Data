Lovelace has

Who hath left his darling, th' east,

It would be easy to multiply such examples. The word is by Cheke, 1550, in one place made short, in another long. But during the XVIIth century all the evidences show that the drawl became more prevalent.

Feast, the noun, was sometimes spelled feste; and is generally rhymed with words in est, especially with guest. Thus, Shakespeare has the following:

<u>Balthazar</u>. Small cheere and great welcome makes a merrie feast.
<u>Antipholus of E.</u> Ay, to a niggardly Host, and more sparing guest.

This is one of five passages where Shakespeare has this rhyme.

<u>Gremio</u>. My cake is dough, but I'le in among the rest
Out of hope of all but my share of the feast.

Giles Fletcher has
The jolly priest, in ivy garlands drest,
Chanted wild orgials in honour of the feast.
