It may be mentioned that while breathe is by Bullokar, 1580,
pronounced with the drawl of ea followed by the sonant th, by
Gill, 1619, it is pronounced with the same vowel followed by the surd th,
and the rhymes bear out Gill, except that they often shorten the
vowel, so that breathe and breath [ins]were sometimes[/ins] pronounced precisely alike.
Thus George Wither has,

And the vapors that do breathe
From the earth's gross womb beneath.

[del]The[/del] Breath is sometimes rhymed with faith; but it is probable
that this word was sometimes shortened, since it [ins]was[/ins] occasionally
spelled feth in the XVth century.

Ben Jonson has,
I sent thee late a rosy wreath,
Not so much honouring thee,
As giving it a hope that there
It could not wither'd be.
But thou thereon didst only breathe,
And sent'st it back to me;
Since when it grows, and smells, I swear
Not of itself but thee.
[u]Perfect harmony was essential to the effect here.[/u]
