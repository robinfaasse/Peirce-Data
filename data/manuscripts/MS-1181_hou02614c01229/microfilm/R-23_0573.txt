Why hunt I, then, for colour or excuses?
All oratours are dum when beautie pleadeth:
Poore wretches haue remorse in poore abuses;
Loue thriues not in the heart that shadows dreadeth:
Affection is my captaine, and he leadeth:

It is true that we find the noun [u]dread[/u] rhymed with [u]bred[/u] in Spenser; but
this stanza is so difficult that we can [del]not[/del] [ins]scarce[/ins] attach little weight to his rhymes.
Still less can we notice the rhymes of the excessively difficult stanza of
the [u]Nut Brown Maid[/u]. It must be admitted however that the noun [u]dread[/u] is
rather frequently rhymed with syllables in [u]ee[/u]. Thus Sackville has

Stoi'd and amazed at his own shade for dread,
And fearing greater dangers than was need.

It may possibly have been sometimes so pronounced; but even then, it
is long. [u]Dreadful[/u], however, was in the XVIth century not infrequently
spelled [u]dredful[/u], and in the previous century [u]dreful[/u]; so that the evi-
dence is that it was short.
