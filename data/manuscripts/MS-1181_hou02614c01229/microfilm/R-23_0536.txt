These duplications are still retained without the final e, except
that final l is not doubled in polysyllables. Indeed, the double l
letters are now retained before the termination s, where they
were suppressed in 1600. Thus bills was formerly bils.

Another pleasing ligature was for rre, and accordingly in
words accented on the last syllable, r usually became rre; as
conferre, enterre, incurre, scarre, furre. The calligraphic
origin of this duplication, which has now disappeared, is
confirmed by the fact that in black-letter books the ligature is
imitated by setting up the two rs in differently shaped types.
Two gs looked well together; and accordingly, final g was
often duplicated, as in bigge (more usually big), bragge, bugge,
clogge, cragge, egge, hugge, jugge, legge, logge, rugge.

Other final consonants were occasionally doubled, especially
[ins]and [u]p[/u][/ins] before short u; as bunne, gunne, runne, tunne, summe, humme,
plumme; also hoppe (flower), rippe, toppe, robbe, notte, rodde,
branne, inne, sonne, tinne, twinne.
