That [u]steal[/u] was drawled we know on the authority of [u]Choke[/u], [date]1550[/date].
That [u]weal[/u] was so the rhymes show. That [del]wealth[/del] [ins]wealth[/ins], however,
was short, we are told even by the antiquated Bullokar, [date]1580[/date], as
well as by Gil, [date]1619[/date], (pp. 39. 77. 85. 87. 89), and by Butler, [date]1633[/date], (pp. 23, 24,
138, 139, 141), and by later orthoepists. [u]Stealth[/u], then, must have been also
short, though we have no orthoepical testimony about it earlier than
Cooper, [date]1685[/date].

[u]Seamstress[/u] had its first syllable drawled by Price, [date]1668[/date].

but [u]meadow[/u], very often spelled medow, was no doubt, short as
