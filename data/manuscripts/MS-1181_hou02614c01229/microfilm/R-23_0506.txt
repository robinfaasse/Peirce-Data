Pleasant was drawled by Gil, 1619, (p. 42) and by Butler, 1633, (pp. 27, 51, 76, 160), but was shortened by Jones, [date]1701[/date].

Treachery is not noticed by any orthoepist before Cooper, [date]1685[/date], and Miège, [date]1688[/date], who both shorten the [del]ea[/del] [ins]ea[/ins]. But it would seem to have been short in the [u]XVI[/u] th century. For Shakespeare has this isolated couplet:

Against such Lewdsters and their lechery,
Those that betray them, do no treachery.

Lecher, from the Old French lescheur, and spelled in English without the ea, was, undoubtedly, short.

Leaven was shortened by Cooper, [date]1685[/date], Miège, [date]1688[/date], and Jones, [date]1701[/date]. Being from French levain, it would naturally be short.
