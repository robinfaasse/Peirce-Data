Some editors [del]of[/del] adhere to the following principles.

1. The spelling of all words that are quite English, and particularly of all that carry English terminations, should be [del]one of[/del] confined to the strict English alphabet, without æ, œ, or &.

2. An unanglicized word borrowed from a language that is printed in any modification of the Latin alphabet ought to be spelled as it would be spelled in a well-edited book in that language.

3. An unanglicized Greek word should be spelled either in its correctly Latinized form, or else in Greek letters.

These rules would permit pean, or paean, or παιάν, but not 'pæan'. They would condemn 'Bakchik', and require, in place of it, either Bacchic or back-sche, according to the meaning. Whether they ought to be favored or not will remain to be considered later.
