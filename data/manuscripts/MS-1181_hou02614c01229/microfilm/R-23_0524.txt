Dead was drawled by Cheke, [date]1550[/date], and Smith, [date]1568[/date]. Gil, [date]1619[/date], tells us that it was equally well drawled or shortened. Butler, [date]1633[/date] (p. 50), in one place drawls it, but in many others (pp. 3, 4, 5, 9, 24 etc.) makes it short. Later orthoepists shorten the word. It was regularly spelled as now in the XVIth century, pretty often deed, not rarely ded or dedde. Rhymes with drawled syllables are somewhat infrequent. Ben Jonson has

To offer them a surfeit of pure bread,
Whose appetites are dead.

A piece better known than its author has

King Pandion he is dead;
All thy friends are lappt in lead.

Lord Brooke has, but exceptionally,

In peace erect your thrones; your delicacie spread,
The flowers of time corrupt soon spring, and are as quickly dead.
