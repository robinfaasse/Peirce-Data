Chapter I. Spelling.

The sole object of spelling is to render words recognizable.

The best orthography is that which renders them [del]or[/del] cognizable with the [del]greatest ease[/del] [ins]least distraction of[/ins] the reader's attention from the substance of what he reads.

An ordinary reader will read some hundred thousand words a day. If only one in [del]a[/del] [ins]every[/ins] hundred of these [del]are[/del] [ins]is[/ins] so spelled as to cause a hitch in the current of his thought, though it be almost imperceptible in a single case, yet when it comes to be multiplied by a thousand, it will be a sad draught upon his fund of energy for the day. If there are ten thousand readers, this must be multiplied by that number, to measure the importance of the very best [ins][u]strict[/u][/ins] orthography.
