<u>The Alphabet.</u>

We use the 23 Roman letters (including Y and Z) toge-
ther with W, V, and J. No accents or other diacritical
marks are employed. They could not render reading more
facile, and would only put more shapes before the reader
to be noticed and thus draw upon his fund of attention.
This simplicity is only qualified by the occasional em-
ployment of the diaeresis and of the characters æ and
œ. The use of the first is nearly abandoned. Few proof-readers
now favor such spellings as coöperate, coëxist, poët,
aërial, meänder, heroic, hemorrhoids. As for æ and œ,
they are exclusively Latin diphthongs; and these charac-
ters are banished from Latin books. Nor are they used
