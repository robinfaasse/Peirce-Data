[u]Foot-note, continued[/u]

Heath has been passed by, without any statement of its pronunciation, by all the early orthoepists. Palsgrave, 1530, has the word, and spells it hethe; but this proves nothing. Heath was the regular spelling; but heth is frequent and is used where careful spelling would be expected; and heeth is also found. It is rhymed with short syllables, notwithstanding the difficulty of finding such rhymes. Thus, Shakespeare has,

[u]1st Witch.[/u] Where is the place?
[u]2nd Witch.[/u] Upon the heath.
[u]3rd Witch.[/u] There to meet with Macbeth.

Of course, Macbeth would be pronounced according to the spelling.

Richard Corbet has,

But since of late Elizabeth,
And later, James came in,
They never danc'd on any heath
As when the time hath been.
