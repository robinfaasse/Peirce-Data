[u]9th[/u], The vowels have also sometimes been changed. Where we
use [del]e[/del] [ins]i[/ins], it is not unusual in well-printed books dated about
1600 to find e, and vice versa. Thus, we meet with basen,
cestern, empaire, encresse, enterfier (for interfere), enterlude,
enterre, frankensence, grediron, kedney, letanie, orpement,
pippen, rense, Rhene, satten, sence (for since), spet, tendrell,
weuell; and, on the other hand, condiscend, dimilance, indevour,
imbarke, imbosse, imbrace, incounter, indow, inlarge, intrals,
inuiron, lionon, president (for precedent), stinch.

In place of ea we find e and vice versa; as in begle, beyer,
ech, endeuor, ernest, fether, ielous, medow, pesant, repe,
weuell; and in alleage, bell-weather, bequest, compleat,
hearon, ieat, leave, prestie, etc.

In place of oa we find o and vice versa; as in bemone, doke,
flote, fole, fome, grone, horde, lothe, reproch, rore, rost, taste;
and in to boar, cloath [ins]for clothes[/ins] [del](verb)[/del], coape, doa, drone, pole, poare.
