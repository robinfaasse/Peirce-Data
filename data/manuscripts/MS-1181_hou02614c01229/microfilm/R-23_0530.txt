<u>Footnote, continued</u>

<u>Feather</u> was made short by Butler, 1633 (pp. 6, 9, 154, 157). It was not rarely spelled fether in the XVIth century. It was rhymed with weather. Drayton has

Their arrows finely pair'd, for timber, and for feather,
With birch and brazil piec'd, to fly in any weather.

<u>Weather</u> was pronounced short by Cheke, 1550, by Butler, 1633 (pp. 2, 3, 4, 9, 11, 15, 16, etc), and by Price, 1665. It was rhymed with short syllables including hither, thither, whither. Shakespeare's song rhyming hither and weather will be remembered. Lord Brooke has

Or, when this feare hath tied Mens mindes together,
Proves this a storme, or constant Winter-weather?

<u>Leather</u> was pronounced short by Gil, 1619 (p. 38) and by Butler, 1633 (p. 10).

<u>Heaven</u> was drawled by Cheke, 1550, [ins]but[/ins] shortened by Bullokar, 1580. Gil, 1619, often drawled it (pp. 22, 99, 128, 129), but often shortened it (pp. 23, 24, 98, 110). The frequent spelling heven may represent the short pronunciation. [del]This word is most commonly rhymed with short syllables.[/del]

Herrick has,

Leaping with thee, from seven to seven,
Till that we both, [del]being[/del] [ins]were[/ins] from earth,
Fly hand in hand to heaven.
