The drawled sound of [u]ea[/u] was precisely that of short [u]e[/u] lengthened. The two sounds were thus nearly alike than [del]any[/del] of our short and long vowels are today, — closer than the long and short sounds of [u]oo[/u]. Now, we all know how much they are confused. Everybody gives the short sound to stood, wood, look, took, brook, wool, foot, and the long sound to food, mood, spook, cool, fool, boot. But there are a number of words which, if we are asked, we shall all say are long, and yet which many persons pronounce short, as soon, soot, broom, hoop, roof, hoof. Nobody [ins]not on the look-out[/ins] would [del]be[/del] remark that the rhyme root, foot was not as good as root, boot. Still, less, then, can we expect [del]in Elizabethan English[/del] uniformity of pronunciation or accuracy of rhyming, as between the drawled [u]ea[/u] and the shortened [u]ea[/u].
