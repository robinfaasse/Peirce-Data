[u]X Foot-note, continued[/u]

Sweat, in the present tense, was doubled by Smith, 1568, (fol. 20), and by Gil, 1619 (pp. 45, 111). Shakespeare has,

[u]Pompey surnam'd the great[/u]:
"That oft in field, with Targe and Shield, did make my foe to sweat.

And again,
By this, the love-sick queen began to sweat,
For where they lay the shadow had forsook them,
And Titan, tired in the mid-day heat,
With burning eye [ins]were[/ins] did hotly overlook them.

Sweat, noun, is made short by Smith (fol. 20) [del]through [unclear]imitation[/unclear][/del] [ins]by Butler, 1633 (p. 58);[/ins] and sweat, [del]past[/del] [ins]preterit,[/ins] is [del]also[/del] shortened by Gil (p. 48).
