* Bread seems to be made short by Salisbury; but some mistake
seems to be involved, for Cheke, 1550, Smith, 1568 (foll. 11. 41), Gil, 1619,
(pp. 24. 37. 73), and Coote, [del]15[/del] [ins]15th Ed.[/ins] 1624, all drawl the vowel, as de-
scendents of Massachusetts settlers of 1630-1640 do. Hodges,
1643, is the first to make it short, as do Cooper, 1685, and subsequent
orthoëpists. Its uniform spelling was bread or breade. Only Cover-
dale, among other peculiarities, spelled bred. It was almost always
rhymed with drawled words. Thus Herrick has,
A little buttery, and therein
A little bin,
Which keeps my little loaf of bread
Unchipped, unflead.
By "unflead" he means unflayed, with the outer crust intact.
