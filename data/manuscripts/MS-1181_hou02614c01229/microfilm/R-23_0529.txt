[u]Footnote, continued[/u]

Death was drawled by Gil, 1619 (pp. 12, 116, 118, 119, 122), and Butler, [date]1633[/date] (pp. 13, 15, 20, 22, 24, etc.); but it was shortened by Cooper, [date]1685[/date], and later orthoepists. It was not infrequently spelled deth in the XVIth century, and sometimes deeth. These pronunciations, therefore, must have existed. It was rhymed [del]also[/del] with short syllables, perhaps in default of better rhymes [ins]most often[/ins] with breath, also with beneath, underneath, bequeath, [ins]heath[/ins] and with no long syllable except faith which was probably sometimes pronounced [del]short[/del] [ins]sheth[/ins]. [del]Shakespeare rhymes it with Macbeth[/del] The poetical pronunciation of the word must, then, have been deth.

Health was drawled by Gil, 1619 (p. 21), but shortened by Butler, [date]1633[/date] (p. 138). It was occasionally spelled helthe in the XVIth century. It was rhymed with short syllables, especially wealth and stealth.

Sir Thomas Wyatt has
I trust some time my harm may be my health
Since every woe is joined with some wealth.

Sir John Suckling has
And when 'twas nam'd another's health,
Perhaps he made it hers by stealth.
