Least is really the same word as lest. There is, therefore, no need of examples. But George Herbert has

Yet let him keep the rest —
But keep them with repining restlessness —
Let him be rich and weary; that, at least,
If goodness lead him not, yet weariness
May toss him to my breast.

The word was drawn led by, [del]S[/del] [ins]Choke (1550), Smith (1568), Bullokar (1580), and[/ins] Gill (1619). But Hodges (1643) tells us that lest and least are pronounced exactly alike.

Heath was sometimes spelled heth. Shakespeare has

[u]1st Witch[/u] Where the place?
[u]2d Witch[/u]. Upon the heath
[u]3d Witch[/u]. There to meet with Macbeth.

Richard Corbet has

But since of late Elizabeth,
And, later, James came in,
They never danced on any heath,
As when the time hath been.
