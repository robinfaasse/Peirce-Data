Endeavor had its [del]first[/del] second syllable drawled by Gil, 1619, (p. 82),
by Butler, 1633, (p. 49), and by Price, 1668. But it was shortened by Cooper,
1685, by Lediard, 1725, by Buchanan, 1766, and by Sheridan, 1680.
The Expert Orthographist, 1704, gave the vowel the sound of [u]ee[/u]. Since
it was derived from the French devoir, it is possible that the [del]short[/del]
sound always existed. At any rate, it is usually rhymed with short
vowels; but then, other rhymes were in use not to be had.

Realm was drawled by Gil 1619, (p. 122), and by Hodges, 1643. It
was made short by Cooper, 1685, Miege, 1688, Jones, 1701, and Lediard,
1725. Though Gil sounded the l, this must have been pedantic.
Hodges [del]says[/del] [ins]did not[/ins] sound[del]s[/del] the l, and the rhymes confirm his
statement. Thus, Drayton has,

The lusty salmon then, from Neptunes watery realm,
Whenas the season serves, stemming my tideful stream.

And Lord Brooke has,

So as with peace, or warre, if you adorne one realme,
In both, through other Climes againe, you runne with barren streame.
