Middleton ends a play with the single couplet,
On, brides and bridegrooms! to your Spanish feasts
Invite, with bent knees, all these noble guests.

Habington has,
She holds that day's pleasure best,
Where sin waits not on delight;
Without masque, or ball, or feast,
Sweetly spends a winter's night.

Herrick has,
Shall we, thy guests
Meet at those lyric feasts?

Lord Brooke has,
To whom of incense Phillip made a feast,
And gave pride scorne, and hunger to digest.

Milton has,
The virgin quire [del]at[/del] [ins]for[/ins] her request
The god that sits at marriage feast.
