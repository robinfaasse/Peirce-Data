[del]Portugese, who have borrowed the word from the English, seem all to[/del]
have heard 'est'. The poets usually rhyme it with short sylla-
bles. Thus, Shakespeare has,

O weary night, O long and tedious night,
Abate thy houres, shine comforts from the East,
That I may backe to Athens by day-light,
From these that my poore companie detest.

And again,

Lord, how mine eies throw gazes to the East!
My heart doth charge the watch: the morning rise
Doth cite each moving sence from idle rest.

In these cases, slightly imperfect rhymes would be unobjection-
able. But Herrick has,

Each flower has wept and bow'd toward the East
Above an houre since, yet you're not drest.
