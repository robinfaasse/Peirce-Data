Dream, the present tense of the verb, was in the XVIIth century [ins]rarely[/ins] spelled dreame, [del]sometimes[/del] [ins]was[/ins] [del]dreem[/del], [ins]never[/ins] dreem. The preterit is usually spelled dreamed, but sometimes [del]dree[/del] drempt.

Heat, the present tense of the verb, was drawled by Cheke, [date]1547[/date]; and that was probably the correct pronunciation of the [del]latter part of the XVIth century[/del]; and the spelling heat or heate was universal. The preterit and past participle were either heat or heated. The former was [del]so[/del] [ins]often[/ins] spelled het, hett, or hette, that it is probable it was always pronounced short, as it was in [date]1840[/date] by old fashioned descendents of Massachusetts settlers.

Leap [ins]present tense of the verb,[/ins] was drawled by Smith, [date]1568[/date], and that probably remained the correct pronunciation until the Great Rebellion. Miege, [date]1688[/date], makes it short, but perhaps he means the noun. However, the rhymes show that the short pronunciation existed in the XVIth century. Thus, Sir Thomas Wyatt has,

Forage with stealing steppes
Hath clawde me with his crouche,
And lusty lyfe away she leapes
As there had been none such.
