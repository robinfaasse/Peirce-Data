* The evidence in regard to many of these words is almost wanting; but we judge of their pronunciations by analogy.

Nothing is more certain than that break was drawled. Salisbury, 1547, and Gill, 1619, (pp. 51, 83), tell us so; and in the XVIth century there was no other spelling than with the ea, except the earlier breke, which [del]indicated[/del] [ins]indicated[/ins] the same pronunciation. But the relative frequency of the spelling brekfast in the XVIth century, and breckfast in the XVIIth show that this was the pronunciation of that word. It is unfortunately not mentioned by any orthoëpist before Cooper, 1685, who makes the first syllable short, as do Miège, 1688, [ins]apparently[/ins] Jones, 1701, the Expert Orthographist, 1704, etc.

[del]But[/del] Clean seems to be one of the words which, though properly drawled, not infrequently was pronounced with the ee sound. Corbet has,
We came to Paris on the Seine;
'Tis wondrous fair, 'tis nothing clean.
