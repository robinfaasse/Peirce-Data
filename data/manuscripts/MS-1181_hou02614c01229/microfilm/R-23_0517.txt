And Shakespeare says,

Sometime he trots, as if he told the steps,
With gentle majesty and modest pride;
Anon he rears upright, curvets and leaps,
As who should say, 'Lo, thus my strength is tried'.

Leaper is pronounced short by Hodges, [date]1643[/date], Price, [date]1668[/date], Cooper, [date]1685[/date], etc. Leaped was so often spelled lept that it was probably short.

Mean, the verb, was pronounced 'meen' by Smith, [date]1568[/date], but was drawled by Gil, [date]1619[/date], (pp. 77, 94, 95, 99, 109). The latter pronunciation is supported by the rhymes. Meant [del]is o[/del] [ins]was[/ins] drawled by Butler, [date]1633[/date] (p. 5); but the rhymes seem to prove that it was short. Donne has,

Moving of th' earth brings harms and fears,
Men reckon what it did, and meant;
But trepidation of the spheres,
Though greater far, is innocent.
