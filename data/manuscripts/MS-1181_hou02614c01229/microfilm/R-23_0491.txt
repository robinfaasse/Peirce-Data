So now is come our joyful'st feast;
Let every man be jolly;
Each room with ivy-leaves is drest,
And every post with holly.

Middleton ends the [u]Spanish Gipsy[/u] with this single-rhymed couplet,
On, brides and Bridegrooms! to your Spanish feasts
Invite, with bent knees, all these noble guests.

This must have been a perfect rhyme. Every poet down to Milton
and later abounds in such rhymes. It seems almost certain that
[u]feast[/u] was commonly pronounced short.

[u]Least[/u] was doubled by Cheke, [date]1550[/date], Smith, [date]1568[/date], Bullokar, [date]1580[/date],
and Gil, [date]1619[/date], (p. 34). Hodges, [date]1643[/date], shortens it. It would evidently,
easily be confused with lest. It is generally rhymed with short
syllables. Shakespeare, for example, has,
Wishing me like to one more rich in hope,
Featur'd like him, like him with friends possest,
Desiring this mans art, and that mans scope,
With what I most injoy contented least.
