It seems to have been a principle of the [u]XVIth Century[/u] proof-readers
that [u]ea[/u] was the proper representative of any French [u]e[/u] or [u]ai[/u] [ins]as[/ins]
in beadle, beak, [ins]cease, ease[/ins] league, [del]preach[/del] [ins]preach, seal[/ins], [del]treat, zeal.[/del] [ins]treat, zeal.[/ins] Of course, if the word was
recognized as ultimately Latin, that would supersede its French
derivation. Many words from the French then took [u]ea[/u]'s that have
since lost it, as [del]alledge[/del] [ins]allege[/ins], [del]chear[/del] [ins]cheer[/ins], [del]leave[/del] [ins]leave[/ins], [del]searge[/del] [ins]serge[/ins], [del]beavel[/del] [ins]bevel[/ins]. Others, though
really of French origin, were not recognized as such, owing to their
changed signification, as rail, blemish, and were consequently not
spelled with ea. Some of the vowels of French origin represented by
ea were drawled in English, while others were shortened; but that
did not affect their regular spelling.
