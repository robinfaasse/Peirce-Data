The Earl of Surrey, who was beheaded in [date]1547[/date], has,

The wide vales, eke, that harbour'd us each night,

Wherewith, alas, reviveth in my breast

The sweet accord such sleeps as yet delight,

The pleasant dreams, the quiet bed of rest.

Richard Edwards, who died [date]1566[/date], has,

She sighed sore and sang full sweet, to bring the babe to rest,

That would not cease, but cried still, in sucking at her breast.

Passing by Spenser, with his difficult stanza, Shakespeare has,

Throwing the base thing from his bending crest,

Enfranchising his mouth, his back, his breast.

And again

Must for your victory us all congest,

As companied love to physic your cold breast.

Indeed, there is scarcely and Elizabethan poet who might not
be cited, and scarcely an instance of breast being rhymed with
a drawled syllable.
