The Anglo-Saxons never had a character enabling them to
discriminate v from f. They had z at hand in the Gaul-
ish cursive which they copied; but they found s sufficient,
and did not use z. And though they had two characters for
the two th sounds, they used them without discrimination.
These distinctions were not necessary. The Roman
Alphabet was brought into Kent, as an adjunct of
christianity about A.D. 600. In adapting it to Anglo-
Saxon, q and z were discarded, the vowel-character
æ was introduced, to take the place of the rune "[u]æsc[/u],"
two runes were retained "wen", ƿ, for w, and "thorn", þ,
for th, while another character, ð, for th, probably
for the sonant variety, was introduced. It is singular that
they did not retain the rune "ing", ᛝ, for ng; but they
