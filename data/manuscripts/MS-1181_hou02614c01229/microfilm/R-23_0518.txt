Lord Brooke, the friend of Sidney, has,
A farre lesse seruile course for me, that meant
To steale in change into this Gouernment.

And again:
To Mustapha she opened mine intent;
For she had tries, but could not turne my heart.
Yet no hurt to me she in telling meant,
Though hurt she did me to disclose my art

Sir Roger L'Estrange has,
Malice, I see, wants wit; for what is meant
Mischief, ofttimes proves favour by th' event.

Milton has,
I knew this quest of yours, and [ins]a[/ins] [del]f[/del]ee intent
Was all in honour and devotion meant.

It would seem, therefore, that the ea was pure & etymological.
