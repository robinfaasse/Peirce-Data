[u]Books V. VI. VII of the Republic[/u] are, [del]therefore[/del] [ins]strangely[/ins], of the nature
of an addendum. The first thirteen pages of Book V relate to
women and children. Men and women are to have the same occupa-
tions. Here we note, by the way, that the best of proverbs is said to
be, that the useful is fine and the hurtful vile: κάλλιστα γὰρ δὴ τοῦτο καὶ
λέγεται καὶ λελέξεται, ὅτι τὸ μὲν ὠφέλιμον καλόν, τὸ δὲ βλαβερὸν αἰσχρόν.
(457 B.) The wives of the Defenders are to be in common and their
children, too; and no parent is to know its own child nor the child
the parent. The breeding is to take place by careful selection. Lots will
be drawn; but there is to be cheating about it. Thus the state will
be one complex family. Children to be taken to the wars. [Diogenes
Laertius quotes an author who says that Plato "served" at the
battle of Tanagra. He was at that time six years old.]
Those who are courageous are to have to be worshipped after
death. No Greek to be enslaved. Kings must be philo-
sophers and philosophers must be kings.
