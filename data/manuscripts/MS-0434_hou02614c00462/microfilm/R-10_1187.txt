[del]You[/del] [ins]You[/ins] assert that your answer to my question was true, he
would be telling the truth, you would be obliged in consistency
to say he would be; would you not? [u]Velian[/u]. Yes.

[u]C.S.P.[/u] Now this [del]was[/del] answer of yours would relate to
what Richard Roe said or [del]was[/del] [ins]was[/ins] supposed to be about to
say, would it not? [u]Velian[/u]. Yes.

[u]C.S.P.[/u] But your answer to my first question said nothing
at all about Richard Roe, or about any assertion other
than the assertion about the Ass; is that not so? [u]Velian[/u]. Correct.

[u]C.S.P.[/u] So this new answer which you would be obliged to
make would be distinct from what you actually asserted
before; would it not? [u]Velian[/u]. Yes.

[u]C.S.P.[/u] And thus it makes no difference whether an
assertion be actual or virtual; in either case, it
implies a virtual assertion distinct from itself; does
it not? [u]Velian[/u]. Yes, it certainly does.
