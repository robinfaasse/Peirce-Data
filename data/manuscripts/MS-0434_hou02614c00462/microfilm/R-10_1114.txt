The [u]Theaetetus[/u] is a logical dialogue; when we pass from a
series of Plato's ethical dialogues, even if they are largely meta-
physical, we find the logical dialogues, we find it tedious
stuff. In ethics, he is so far advanced beyond most people
whom we meet, even cultivated people, even moralists,
that we find it most refreshing, instructive, elevating.
We quite drop the historical attitude. This metaphysics, on
the other hand, is very bad; but then the metaphysics
of today is [del]one[/del] hardly enough better to deprive what he
has to say of all interest. But of logic, he knew hardly
anything, while we know much; and what he says is too
