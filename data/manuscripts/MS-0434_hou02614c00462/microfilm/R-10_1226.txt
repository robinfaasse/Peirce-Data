The Timaeus is [ins]generally[/ins] supposed to be in form a continuation of the
Republic, with a resumé of which it seems to open. But it appears
to me better to suppose that it is a continuation of some dialogue
which has not come down to us, probably for the excellent reason
that it never was written or, at least, never published. For the
summary of the previous dialogue, nearly like the Republic, if
not identical with it [del]as to do[/del] [ins]after[/ins] Socrates has expressed his longing
to see his political ideas put into practice, the story of the island of
Atlantis is told.

What follows has no connection with what precedes except
that it is supposed to be a continuation of the same conversation,
or speech-making meeting. Timaeus starts off and with a
single of brief interruption speaks through 65 pages of Stephanus
or 75 of Teubner's edition ([del]Hermann[/del] [ins]Herrmann[/ins]). It is a discourse about the
nature of the universe of no interest except as showing
the infantile state of science in Plato's time.

The Critias is a mere tale.
