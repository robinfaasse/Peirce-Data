— Cicero would have [del]made[/del] [ins]inserted[/ins] a mention of the fact
when he said "audisse te credo Platonem, Socrate mortuo,
primum in Aegyptum discendi caussa, post in Italiam et in
Siciliam contendisse ut Pythagorae inventa perdisceret."
But what is here enumerating? Those journeys of Plato which were
at once, made of great effort ([u]contendisse[/u]), and for the sake
of learning ([u]discendi caussa[/u]), and which were celebrated
([u]audisse te credo[/u]). This change of domicile, not to be called
a journey, was neither any undertaking, nor motivated by the
wish to learn, nor a matter of high renown.
