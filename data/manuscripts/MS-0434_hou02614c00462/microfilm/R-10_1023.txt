[u]Plato[/u].

[ins]As to Plato's birth,[/ins] There is a dispute as to whether it took place, 428 B.C., 428 B.C., or 427 B.C. [del]Yet[/del] [ins]But[/ins] the hypothesis adopted ought to explain all the testimony. The life of Plato by Diogenes Laertius is by far the most careful of all his biographies. [del]In the[/del] [ins]He[/ins] reports Apollodorus (a chronologer of high repute) as saying that Plato was born in the 88[u]th[/u] Olympiad, and on the 7[u]th[/u] day of the [del]eleventh[/del] [ins]month[/ins] Thargelion. This sounds as if made up of two statements; one concerning the Olympiad, the other concerning his birth day. Now Plato was, as it were, identified by his followers with Apollo, whose birth day was 7[u]th[/u] Thargelion; and both Plutarch and Apulleius tell us that his birth was celebrated on that day. It is, therefore, more likely than not that his real birth day was not that. According to Ideler's Tables the 7[u]th[/u] Thargelion ended at the evening of 22[u]nd[/u] May 429 B.C., 9[u]th[/u] May 428 B.C., 27[u]th[/u] May 427 B.C. The 88[u]th[/u] Olympiad is supposed to have begun 27[u]th[/u] July 428 B.C.
