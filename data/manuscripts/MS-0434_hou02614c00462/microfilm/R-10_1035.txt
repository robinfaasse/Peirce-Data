[u]δικαιοσύνη καὶ σωφροσύνη[/u]. Another little point is that the meaning of the word dialectical is developed. Were Socrates answering τῶν σοφῶν τις καὶ ἐριστικῶν τε καὶ ἀγωνιστικῶν "one of the disputatious sort of savants," he would answer in one way; but if we were to merely in friendly conversation as you and I now are, "εἰ δὲ ὥσπερ ἐγώ τε καὶ σὺ νυνὶ φίλοι ὄντες βούλοιντο ἀλλήλοις διαλέγεσθαι," then it would be proper to reply more gently and conversationally, δεῖ δὴ πραότερόν πως καὶ διαλεκτικώτερον ἀποκρίνεσθαι. "For it is more conversational (dialectical) not merely to speak the truth but such things as the questioner would acknowledge to be so." Meno, quoting "the poet" says that virtue is desiring and being able to provide τὰ καλά; thus making moral beauty a good. A little further on, Meno turns upon Socrates with a rather pertinent question of logic. Socrates proposes to inquire what virtue is.
