[del]be indestructible[/del] For a principle being destroyed neither will itself ever
be generated from anything nor anything else from it; although it must
be that all things are generated from a principle. [del]So[/del] This is the way in which
on the one hand a principle of motion is the self-moving itself, and
on the other hand this [del]is never[/del] can neither be destroyed nor generated
or [del]the[/del] all things, heaven and all Genesis would collapse and come to
a stand and never again will have whence it should be set in motion.
But [del]that[/del] self-which is moved by itself having been proved immortal, he
who says that this the essence and very meaning of [del]the[/del] a soul will
not be put to shame. For every body, [ins]in[/ins] [del]to[/del] which motion is from without is
soulless, but that in which it is [ins]from[/ins] within in itself from itself, is animate,
such being the nature of the soul. But if this is thus, [ins]namely that[/ins] [del]nothing else is[/del] [ins]nothing else[/ins] itself moves
itself than the soul, of necessity the soul would be ingenerate and immortal. (245CDE)
