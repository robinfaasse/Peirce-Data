[u]C.S.P.[/u] Consequently, no one of that series of assertions
could be the last virtual assertion implied in your
original assertion about the sun. [u]Velian.[/u] That is true.
[u]C.S.P.[/u] And in like manner, any assertion whatever

[u]C.S.P.[/u] Thus there is a distinction between an actual

[u]C.S.P.[/u] The latter is merely what adherence to truth would
compel one, if he were to answer a question, to assert.
[u]Velian.[/u] Well, not so much truth as consistency.
[u]C.S.P.[/u] My expression, however, is correct, I think. For
what other motive could a man have for being self-consis-
tent in spite of the truth than that, if he were telling the
truth, [del]as he desires[/del] which is what he wishes to imitate, he,
would be obliged to be consistent? So that after all, what
[del]compells[/del] [ins]impells[/ins] him to be consistent is that the truth requires
consistency. [u]Velian.[/u] That is true.
