(δικαιοσύνη)* After a long and forcible eulogium of δις -

* Though the Greek conception of morality contains precisely
the same elements as our own, these were differently
grouped in their minds and consequently it is really
impossible to find English equivalents for δικαιοσύνη
and σωφροσύνη. They are often called Justice and Temper-
ance. I prefer Honesty and Self-Command as coming
nearer in our modern vernacular to the Greek con -
ceptions, although they do not fit with anything like
exactitude. The theologians and lawyers, between them,
have made Justice the name of a moral elephantiasis;
and the loss of all moral significance of the word.
Temperance is the price we have paid for an improve -
ment [ins]in[/ins] [del]of[/del] the habits of our people. The price [del]is[/del] [ins]was[/ins] by no
means inconsiderable; though, no doubt, the reform
[del]was[/del] [ins]came[/ins] dirt cheap.
