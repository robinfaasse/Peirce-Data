IV.38

The Meno, which seems intended to [del]show[/del] give an idea of how Plato would propose to advance beyond the stand-point of Socrates in the Protagoras, is nevertheless rather of logical, than of ethical interest. In the beginning we meet with the question δ δε μη οἰδα τι ἐσου, πῶς ἂν ὁποῖόν γε τι εἰδείην; "when I do not know what a thing is, how should I know how it may be?" [del]How[/del] True, we cannot tell whether a predicate is true of an object unless that object be in some measure determinate. And a little below: "So it is with the virtues. No matter how many and various they may be, yet some [ins]and the same[/ins] one species they every one possess, which makes them to be virtues." ἐὰν εἰ πολλαὶ καὶ παντοδαπαὶ εἰσιν, ἐν γέ τι εἶδος ταὐτὸν ἅπασαι ἔχουσιν, δι' ὃ εἰσιν ἀρεταί. And this essence is called οὐσία. (ἡ μεν ἐρομενον μελίττης περὶ οὐσίας ὅ τι ποτ' ἐστι.) It is that in respect to which all [del]the bees are[/del] are the same. τοῦτο τοίνυν μοι αὐτὸ εἰπὲ, ὦ Μένων, ᾧ οὐδὲν διαφέρουσιν ἀλλὰ τωὐτόν εἰσιν ἅπασαι.
