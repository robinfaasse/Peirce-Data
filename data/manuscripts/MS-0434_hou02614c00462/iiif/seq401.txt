neither would a life of knowledge wholly divorced from pleasure
be so eligible as one in which the knowledge afforded pleasure.
Plato fails to observe that, since pleasure is [ins]a species of[/ins] the recognition
of something as good, this last state supposes more knowledge;
since it [del]is[/del] not only knows, but knows this knowledge to be
good. For to suppose that one knows but takes no pleasure
in knowing, supposes that one does not feel knowledge to be good.
