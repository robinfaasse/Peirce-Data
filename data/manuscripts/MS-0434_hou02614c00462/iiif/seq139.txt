The next year Epaminon (Επαμείνων) was archon;
in the third year Diotimus. Now Athenaeus says Plato
was born in the archonship of Apollodorus. [ins]the text of[/ins] Diogenes
Laertius [del]says[/del] reads that he was born ἐπ’ Αμεινίου. This
is usually translated "in the Archonship of Ameinias", which
began 423 B.C. But since ἐπί in stating Archonships
always takes the genitive, at any rate in writers of the age
of Laertius, this must be wrong and may easily stand for
a parenthetical Ἐπαμείνων, which would best agree
with the second [del]date[/del] [ins]year[/ins], though [ins]it is[/ins] [del]not[/del] contrary to the first, if we
suppose that Archon took office a month before the time
or that Plato was born later in the summer. But in the
very same sentence Diogenes Laertius says that Plato [del]died[/del] [ins]was[/ins]
born in the year Pericles died. Now Pericles did die under
the archonship of Epameinon in the autumn of 429 B.C.
Thus, this statement would suit either of the first two years.
