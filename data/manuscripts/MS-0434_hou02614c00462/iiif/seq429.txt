Plato's preference for dichotomy is evident, and plainly,
for the purposes of definition, it is the most economical, in
general. We shall see, however, that he does not absolutely in-
sist upon it.

It seems a very slouchy method which does not stop to inquire
the meaning of tame and wild. The Greek word for tame, ἥμερος,
seems to mean sitting, opposed to ἄγριος, wild, roaming the fields.
