[ured]Plato's thorough detestation of the tyrant,[/ured] – his old friend
Dionysius, – appears in his description of the tyrant and of
the tyrannical man, which reminds one of the Verlohrne
Handschrift. The tyrant has the soul of a slave, and the only
part of him that is free is maniacal. The men whom he
commands, – for it cannot be called government, – are
miserable enough. But there is one more miserable
still, – the tyrant himself. He is obliged to be the lickspittle
of the vilest of mankind and lives in continual terror. The
son of Ariston (the best. This was the name of Plato's father, so that
the son is Plato himself) proclaims, – from having seen all
sorts of men, – that he is the happiest man who is king
over himself, whether seen or unseen by men or by gods.
