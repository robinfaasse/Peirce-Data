Logic
IV. 234

The Timaeus [del]is[/del] [ins]generally[/ins] supposed to be in form a continuation of the Republic, with a resumé of which it seems to open. But it appears to me better to suppose that it is a continuation of some dialogue which has not come down to us, probably for the excellent reason that it never was written or, at least, never published. After the summary of the previous dialogue, nearly like the Republic, if not identical with it & after [ins]the Pseudo-[/ins] Socrates has expressed his longing to see his political ideas put into practice, the story of the island of Atlantis is told.

What follows has no connection with what precedes except that it is supposed to be a continuation of the same [u]conversazione[/u], or speech-making meeting. Timaeus starts off and with a single & brief interruption speaks through 65 pages of Stephanus or 75 of Teubner's edition (Hermann). It is a discourse about the nature of the universe of no interest except as showing the infantile state of science in Plato's time.

The Critias is a mere tale.
