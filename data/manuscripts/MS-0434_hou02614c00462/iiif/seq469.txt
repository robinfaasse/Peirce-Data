[del]do not shine[/del], it does not shine, whether I assert that it
shines or not. But it seems to be different in regard to
the reality of my telling the truth.
C.S.P. Let us see. Suppose, first, that the sun shines,
and that you [ins]really[/ins] assert that it shines; and that you then
add to this assertion, [del]“and what I say is true”[/del] [ins]“and I am
telling the truth.”[/ins] There was no need of actually saying this,
since it was already virtually said. But you were telling
the truth; and should you now say, with Stesichorus,
οὐκ ἔστ’ ἔτυμος λόγος οὗτος,
your recantation would not be true, being in conflict with
your first assertion which is really true, just the same, since
the sun [ins]really[/ins] shines. If, on the other hand, the sun were [del]not[/del]
[ins]not[/ins] [del]really[/del] shining, and you [ins]really[/ins] assert it shines, this assertion is
[del]really true[/del] not true, and its implication that it is only adds
to its falsity; and if you recant, the recantation will be true,
