It must probably have been during the sojourn of Plato
in Megara that he wrote the following ten dialogues:
The [u]Charmides[/u] discusses the question whether we
are to say that nothing is good but knowledge and no
knowledge unless it be of the good; so that there would
be a knowledge of knowledge. There is no definite conclusion.
The [u]Laches[/u] is intended to show that knowledge alone is
good by showing that even courage is knowledge.
The [u]Lysis[/u] endeavors to make readers feel that the
inquiry after truth is good even if no great progress
is made. It looks up the good from the point of view
of attractions.
The [u]Euthydemus[/u] ridicules the common sophists as
really not conveying or possessing any knowledge.
