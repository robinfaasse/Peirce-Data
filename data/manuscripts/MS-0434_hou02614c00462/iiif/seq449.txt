Logic
IV. 188.

    For never may you subdue this, that nothings are;
    But keep your mind from the path of this inquiry.

The first line here is all right; the second is nonsense. Nothing is simply
that of which everything is true. That is wherein its nothingness consists.
It therefore exists. That is true. It also does not exist. Why not? The
principle of contradiction does not stand in the way; [del]for that only says that[/del]
[ins]for the[/ins] [del]things of which contraries are true are non-existent.[/del] The principle of con-
tradiction does not say that of that of which contraries are true contraries
are not true; but merely that these things of which contraries are true are
nothing. Here [del]as is[/del] [ins]follows his[/ins] first and chief reasoning, mark it well:
