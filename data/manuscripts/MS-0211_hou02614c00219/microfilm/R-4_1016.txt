Pursuing that method, we must begin by asking, what is the use of calculating probabilities? The answer is, that by that means we are assured of a result in the long run, although we cannot tell what it will be in any single instance. Whether or not a given house will burn down within a year is what neither its owner nor the Company [del]who[/del] that insures it for him can know. But the company does know by experience that year in and year out, such and such a proportion of houses insured by them will burn. That fraction they call the probability that [del]any[/del] one of them will burn. Calculating [del]for[/del] upon that basis, they make every policy-holder pay his proper quota and are able to insure him, [ins]practically[/ins] without danger to themselves.

Ignorance, therefore, is an essential element of it. The probabilities with which they deal do not belong to the facts themselves.
