It is now held that infinity is a positive conception concerning which
there is no difficulty, with proper care, in reasoning without any con-
tradiction. In order to define an infinite collection, it is best to conceive
of arrows, or lines having their two ends unlike, [ins]and having no branchings,[/ins] but capable of being
curved round so as to bring the two ends together. Then an infinite collection
is such a collection that an arrow or arrows may [del]be drawn[/del] [ins]point[/ins] from every
unit [ins]of the collection[/ins] to [del]some[/del] a unit of the collection, without any two arrows pointing
to the same unit, and without all the units having arrows pointing
to them. Thus, take all the whole numbers. An arrow may point from every
number to the number next greater than it.
