If we suppose the resources as well of the bank as of every player to be limitless and the game to be perfectly fair, sundry curious situations may arise. Suppose, for example, a player to sit down with a determination to win one franc net, and then to stop playing forever. If his staying power is unlimited, and the game is even, he is sure to succeed. [For if he bets one franc each time, the odds that he will make a net gain of one franc before he has, in the process, at any time suffered a net loss of any given number of francs, are that number again in his favor against one in favor of the bank.] As soon as he has retired with his franc, let his place be taken by another player, who does the same thing. Suppose that all the players pursue the same policy. Then every man of them will come away a winner. Nevertheless, the game being even, the bank in the long run will neither win nor lose.

This seems highly paradoxical. Yet there is no contradiction in it.
