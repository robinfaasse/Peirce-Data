If we suppose that both the bank and every player are possessed of limitless resources, then, especially if the game be perfectly fair, sundry curious situations may arise. Suppose, for example, a player to sit down with the determination to win one franc net, and then stop playing for ever. If his staying power is unlimited, and the game is even, he is certain to succeed at last. For example, out of [ins]every[/ins] 1024 players who should bet a franc each time, 512 would win the first time, and go home. Out of the 512 who remained, 256 would recover their loss at the second bet, and of these 128 would have netted a franc on the payment of the third bet and would go home. Of the 384 who remained, 64 would [del]have[/del] [ins]have[/ins] gained a franc net on the payment of the fifth bet, and go home. Of the 320 who remained 40 would have gained on the payment of the seventh bet, [del]and[/del] [ins]and go home.[/ins] Of the 280 who remained 28 (or [u]7[/u]/10 of 40) would retire with their francs at the end of the 9[u]th[/u] bet. Of the 252 remaining, 21 (or [u]3[/u]/4 of 28) would retire at the end of the 11[u]th[/u] bet.
