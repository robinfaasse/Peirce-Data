Introduction on the nature of Probability.

§1. That there really are Laws of Nature, and such as we can understand.

Many false notions are current concerning the degree of certainty of scientific [del]conclusions[/del] propositions. Many persons, who have not read much about natural science, have the idea that a conclusion cannot be more certain than the premises from which it is drawn, and that no kind of evidence is better than [ins]that of[/ins] one's own eyes, and [ins]that[/ins] after that, none is better than the direct testimony of credible witnesses. If this book succeeds in elucidating that doctrine of probability, which has been the light to the feet of science, the reader will see clearly before reaching its end that many scientific truths are far more certain than anything resting merely upon human testimony, and that in particular they are [del]so[/del] [ins]almost[/ins] infinitely less erroneous than any of the observations upon which they rest.
