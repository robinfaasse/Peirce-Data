The problem to ascertain what the average number of bets a player will make is really an easy one; but I will here attack it so as to illustrate how to solve [del]an equal[/del] a linear equation of finite differences with constant coefficients when the known term is not zero.

Giving $x$ and $y$ the same significations as above, let $w_{x,y}$ be the average number of bets after the player with hoard $x$ has lost $y$ francs.

In $\frac{31}{60}$ of the cases the number of bets will be $1 + w_{x,y+1}$
and in $\frac{29}{60}$ " " " " " " " $1 + w_{x,y-1}$

Hence, on the average,
$$w_{x,y} = 1 + \frac{31}{60} w_{x,y+1} + \frac{29}{60} w_{x,y-1}$$

That is
$$w_{x,y+1} - \frac{60}{31} w_{x,y} + \frac{29}{31} w_{x,y-1} = - \frac{60}{31}$$

or
$$\left(E_y^2 - \frac{60}{31} E_y + \frac{29}{31}\right) w_{x,y} = - \frac{60}{31}$$

But we have seen that this is
$$\left(E_y - 1\right) \left(E_y - \frac{29}{31}\right) w_{x,y} = - \frac{60}{31}$$

Hence
$$w_{x,y} = \frac{1}{\left(E_y - 1\right) \left(E_y - \frac{29}{31}\right)} \left(- \frac{60}{31}\right)$$
