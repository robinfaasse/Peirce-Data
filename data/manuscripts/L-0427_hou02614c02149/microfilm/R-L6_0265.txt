When a pragmatist describes himself as a kind of idealist,
what he means is that he holds that reality is of an
intellectual nature. I do not think that any nominalist
is properly describable as an idealist. I refuse to call Berkeley
an idealist because he does not believe in concepts at all; and
the only modes of being according to him are arbitrary acts
of God and images produced thereby. True, [del]to[/del] [ins]he[/ins] says he believes
in the existence of [del]souls[/del], beginning with himself. But I fail
to see that he means anything intellectual [ins]or cognitive[/ins] by it. It is a mere
emotion [del]he attaches[/del] to the word "soul" or "spirit." At any rate, if
he does mean anything he utterly fails to make it clear. Now
Berkeley was very apt to make his meaning clear when he
had any.

So when you call yourself an idealist, I suppose you at-
tach some emotion or feeling to the word; but of cognitive mean-
ing I am able to extract none. You are a hylozoist, I my-
self incline to that. But when you say that mind is real
and matter an appearance, I doubt greatly whether that means
anything. It seems indeed strange to say that matter is an
appearance. I don't see by what sense or how otherwise
it appears.
