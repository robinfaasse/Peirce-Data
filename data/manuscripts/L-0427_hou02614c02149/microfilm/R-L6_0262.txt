I may as well go on to jot down some observations upon your fol-
lowing pages, as I re-read them. I regard any metaphysics not based
on pragmatism as a mere form of words to which no meaning
whatever can be attached. In particular, I do not believe that you
attach any true meaning to the word [u]reality[/u]. For the pragmatist the
real is that which would enter into the ultimate opinion resulting
from an indefinite, or sufficient, prolongation of inquiry. As far
as I can see, we have no reason to believe that any amount of
inquiry would settle every question (that it would not settle [del]all ques-[/del]
[del]tions, most people would, I suppose, incline to think. But I go further,[/del]
[del]and doubt whether it would settle every question.[/del] [ins]The distinction is a
familiar one in the logic of relations.[/ins]) If so, the conception of reality has but
a limited validity. But it is sound logic to assume that it is valid for any
given question that may be in hand. You will therefore observe that for
the pragmatist reality consists essentially in what [u]would be[/u], [del]except that[/del] [ins]or would not be.[/ins]
Thus, reality is [del]for him[/del] essentially [u]general[/u]. There need not be any ab-
solute uniformity; it is sufficient that there should be such a kind of
uniformity as [del]makes[/del] the [u]would be[/u]. The most absolute uniformity does not
of itself alone make any [u]would be[/u]; for it may be a mere uniformity among
[u]faits accomplis[/u]. It is the conditional futurity that is required to make
reality and not mere uniformity however unbroken. I am much
