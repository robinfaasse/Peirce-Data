When I speak of "logic," I mean semeiotics, the doctrine of the
necessary principles of signs. It has three parts; 1, speculative
grammar, by which I mean the study of the main genera of signs
and the analysis of their natures; 2, critic, which studies the
conditions under which [del]logic[/del] signs can refer to their objects; and
3, methodeutic which considers the necessary conditions of signs
fulfilling their purposes. In this third [del]part[/del] [ins]part[/ins], I allow some use
of facts of history, and of facts concerning the mind which may
be the discoveries of psychology. But I only admit them as checks
upon possible blunders in severer methods.
