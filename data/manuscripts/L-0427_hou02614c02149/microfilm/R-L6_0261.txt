Your proposition No 3 is the most monstrous of all. "We can have
immediate intuition only of our mental states". Really, I am inclined
to wipe my pen and put the whole [del]theme[/del] discussion aside! The
idea of our having an immediate intuition of our mental states!
Can you tell, then, what your mental state was in [date]1880 March 4[/date]?
You have, however, as immediate an intuition of that, as you have
of your mental state [ins]$\frac{1}{10}$[/ins] second ago. Oh, but that doesn't begin
to touch the absurdity of the position. Just look at my "Questions
concerning certain faculties claimed for man" in Vol 2 or 3 of the
Journal of Speculative Philosophy. The only direct knowledge we
have is, for example, such as that the inkstand on my table appears
to be round, shiny, and tolerably well-filled. My mental states are [ins]known[/ins]
only inferentially.

As to your No 4, it appears that you call an inkstand
a "mental state". This is grossly contrary to the usages of speech.
But let that pass. Then you go on to say that if there are realities
"outside the mind" — and what you mean by that I don't know and
do not believe you mean anything by it, — they can only be known
"through the medium" of our "mental states", that is, through the
medium of [del]inks and[/del] [ins]inkstands[/ins] and other material objects, "that is, re-
presentatively." Now if all you wish to conclude is that
concepts are only known through symbols, that is manifestly
true. But if you had stated the matter in terms of pragmatism
it would have been plainer.

I won't go further. That sufficiently illustrates how little you meet
the pragmatist.
