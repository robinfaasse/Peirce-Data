My dear Mr. Peirce,

I want to thank you most
sincerely for your kind appreciation
of my write-up in regard to my Kant;
I value this most highly as coming
from you.

Of course I have no idea that
the psychophysical solution of the mind-
and-body problem is final, in the
sense of being free from difficulties
or in that of being adjusted to details
(such, for instance, as the molecular
structure of the cortex). On the other
hand the theory seems to me to ex-
plain the relation intelligibly, as it
stands, and this is to be the only one
consistent with sound principles
that is now in the field.

I could readily admit that
interactionism, automatism, and
parallelism express aspects of the facts,
though it would be necessary to take

else is it than the nexus, or rather the
"substance" of the Kantian schema?
Is the fact that all consciousness
is referred to a subject, other than a
stance, we perceive the visual objects
we are occupied exclusively with the
objects & oblivious of ourselves. But
if at the next moment we reflect, we
always distinguish between the "we"
who were conscious & the objects we
were conscious of. And we consider
this distinction to be valid for the
past moment, when we were exclu-
sively occupied with the objects.

But now, how can it be valid?
How can there, at that past moment,
