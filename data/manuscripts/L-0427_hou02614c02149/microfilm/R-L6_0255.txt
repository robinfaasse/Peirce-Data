The three essential elements of a sign are its own
qualities, its reference to its object represented, and its
reference to its interpretant, or the new sign that it is its
purpose to determine. There is a great deal to be said about
the nature of these three sides of signs in general. My way of
attaining a comprehension of them is to take advantage of my
categories of Firstness, Secondness, and Thirdness, to produce
a division of signs in each of the three aspects, and thus
help me to make sure that my generalizations are not
restricted to any particular variety of signs. The number of
different species of signs that I find it necessary to recognize
is very large. But I will just illustrate my procedure by
showing how I get ten genera[del]ls[/del]. In the first place, in respect
to the Firstness, or material nature of the sign, it will either
