I do not pretend to understand how the mind acts. I am fully
convinced that the true explanation, when it is found, will be as
unlike metaphysical verbiage as can be. I think there is some
reason to conjecture that an endless series of logical transfor-
mations [del]is[/del] [ins]maybe[/ins] followed by a beginningless series of physical transfor-
mations. But [del]I[/del] it seems to me that pragmatism, by putting
us in possession of the idea of the mind as a legisign, a sym-
bol, an argument that somehow is organized to address itself
puts us in possession of conceptions likely to be required in
solving the problem. For pragmatism makes the essence of
reality to consist in a [u]would be[/u] or law. Now we see that it
is the mind's power of correcting its own habits which
makes its inward operations important, and therefore it
must be somewhere in that neighborhood that the next
step is to be taken. Logical thought is simply thought self-controlled
& its purpose (for there can be no logical criticism where
thought is uncontrollable) and therefore in the study of logic
we may expect to find conceptions of value in the study of the
psychophysical problem.
