There will never be a require-ment of steam machinery to supplement the water power. The amount of water available will always be far in excess of what can be required.

The fall available at Sault Ste. Marie is about 20 feet. At Niagara the direct fall upon the turbines is 140 feet. This latter fall, while giving a great amount of power with a moderate volume of water, demands turbines of special construction and of extraordinary strength, and also requires very long vertical shafting and novel arrangements of machinery and appliances in various directions.

At the place now under consideration, Massena, the fall upon the turbines will be about 40 feet. This fall is great enough to devel-ope a large power with a moderate volume of water, but is not so great as to require turbines of excessive cost, or appliances different from those in general use. No tunnel will be necessary. The mills will be built upon rock foundations and the turbines will be at the level of the base of the mills, and without requiring long shafting or other ar-rangements for power transmission.

In my opinion, the developement of a very great water power at this place is feasible, at moderate cost, and with characteristics of favorable use, equal to any, and superior to nearly every other known power.
