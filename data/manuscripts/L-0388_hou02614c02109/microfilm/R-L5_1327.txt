In addition to these statements, as to the proposed improvement
at this place, reference may also be made to certain general features
of successful development of water powers, so that the peculiar features
of the one in question may be considered.

The development in very large amounts, of power from the fall
of water has received special attention during the last few years. This
has been to some extent due to the new uses of electricity in manufacture,
its transmission to considerable distances, and its application in var-
ious directions not before attempted. The great expansion of the manu-
facture of wood-pulp for paper, has also required large powers, which
can be afforded economically only by the utilization of water. Many
other industries are finding it advantageous to consider favorably the
establishment of their works at points where they can be sure of having
constant and regular power without the expenditures and annoyances con-
nected with the provision and use of fuel.

The powers derived from falls on ordinary water courses have
certain limitations which, in many of the older manufacturing localities,
have become very annoying. The principal disturbing element in such
cases is the great variation in the flow of the streams. The volume of
flow follows more or less closely the rainfall on the drainage basins
of these streams, and, unless there are very large storage reservoirs,
