My dear Mr. Stewart

I beg to acknowledge with thanks the receipt of $25.

Magnesia has been used as a lining for the electrical furnace since the first invention of this furnace. Thus, the new [del]matter[/del] [ins]invention[/ins] is merely [del]one[/del] [ins]a matter[/ins] of detail. The investigation of its value will be easy as soon as I can get such a furnace. It has no bearing upon the question of the use of magnesian limestone.

I repeat that it is known there is a carbide of magnesia. It is not formed in the furnace from magnesia owing to the refractory character of that earth. There seems to be evidence (requiring confirmation) that it is formed from magnesian limestone. It certainly produces more acetylene than the carbide of calcium. At first sight, it strikes a chemist as likely that its cost would be much greater. Careful examination shows that the presumption is that the increase of cost would be slight.
