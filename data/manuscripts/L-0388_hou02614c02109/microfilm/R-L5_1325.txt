required in piercing the high banks at other points.

It also permits the canal to leave the river at a slight angle
away from the current, this being a very desirable feature in avoiding
the entry of floating ice into the canal. This point also, all things
considered, is the best for the diversion of water from this branch of
the St. Lawrence River. It is sufficiently below the deep channel above
Long Sault Island, to prevent any effect upon the flow of that channel, [del]x[/del]
and it is at a point where the full difference in elevation between the
St. Lawrence and Grass Rivers can be most economically utilized.

The route of the canal after leaving the St. Lawrence, follows
for a short distance the line of the small creek and thence its course
toward the valley of the Grass River is determined by the topography of
the country. No short curves have been made in the line which has been
adopted, and thus a maximum flow of water can be secured. The canal
reaches the high banks of the Grass River at a distance of 17,400 feet,
(3-1/4 miles) from the St. Lawrence. From this point, it will be con-
tinued on these high banks and parallel to the Grass River for as long a
distance as may be found desirable. The location and estimates which
have been made carry the canal to a total length of 20,700 feet, affording
3300 (more than 3/5 of a mile) feet of head race, parallel and adjacent
to the Grass River. The topographical conditions at this point are
peculiarly favorable to effective and economical use of the power. Grass
