R E P O R T

AS TO A PROPOSED DEVELOPMENT OF WATER POWER BY THE CONSTRUCTION OF A CANAL

BETWEEN THE ST. LAWRENCE AND GRASS RIVERS,

IN THE TOWN OF MASSENA, ST. LAWRENCE COUNTY, NEW YORK.

By JOHN BOGART,

Consulting Engineer.

[del]- -1-9-0-2-[/del]

The power, the practical development of which is the subject of
this report, will be produced by water from the St. Lawrence River, trans-
ferred by a canal to the valley of the Grass River, and restored through the
Grass River to the St. Lawrence. The topographical conditions which
make this power practicable, are peculiar. The St. Lawrence, the outlet
of the whole system of the Great American Lakes, flows easterly from Lake
Ontario, between Canada and Northern New York, to a point about 60 miles
above Montreal, and thence through Canadian territory to the Atlantic Ocean.
The volume of water carried, twenty-six thousand million cubic feet per day,
is enormous. There are no vertical falls in the course of the St. Law-
rence but there are a number of rapids where the stream makes considerable
descents in moderate distances. One of these is at the Long Sault Island
and directly below and above it, where in a distance of 17-1/4 miles (from
Cornwall to upper end of Croils Island) the river falls about 53 feet.
Navigation is here provided for by the Cornwall canal, with seven locks and
a rise of 48 feet, and the Farran's Point Canal, with one lock, and a rise
of 3-1/2 feet; both canals are on the Canadian side. Boats do, at favor-
able stages of the water, run down the river, but must always use the canals
on their upward trips. The Long Sault Island divides the River into two
parts. The northerly branch is navigable to the head of the Cornwall canal
and for the whole distance for descending boats, when they care to risk
the descent. It is the international channel, through which runs the
boundary line between the United States and Canada. It carries the great
volume of water of the river. [u]The Long Sault Island is entirely in the[/u]
