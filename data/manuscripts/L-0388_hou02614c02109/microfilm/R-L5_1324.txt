South of this part of the St. Lawrence there extends a table-
land of about the elevation of the bank of that river. This country has
moderate variations in its topography, with some hills, not of very great
height. At about 2-1/2 miles South from the St. Lawrence, at the point
of nearest approach, the valley of the Grass River is reached. This
stream has its source in the Adirondack forests of Northern New York,
and empties into the St. Lawrence two miles below the lower end of Long
Sault Island. Its course for several miles above its mouth is substan-
tially parallel with that of the St. Lawrence. It is a stream of mod-
erate volume, and not of rapid flow. At the village of Massena, 7 miles
from its junction with the St. Lawrence, there are two dams, and the
falls created by these dams, are utilized for small industries.

On the Southern bank of the St. Lawrence, about 7/8 of a mile
below the head of Long Sault Island, a small creek flows into the river.
This creek has only a storm season flow, being substantially dry in other
seasons. But it affords a depression in the bank of the river, very
favorable for the construction of the inlet to a canal. The surface of
the [del]river[/del] water at this point is 46 feet above the surface of the Grass
River at the foot of the lower dam at Massena.

Before my personal examination of this locality, some prelimi-
nary measurements and trial surveys had been made. Since then, by a more
careful survey and by instrumental leveling, a route for a canal has been
located and the observations requisite for estimates of cost have been
made.
