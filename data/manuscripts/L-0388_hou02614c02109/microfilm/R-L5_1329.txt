The most desirable characteristics of effective water power is
regularity of flow to the maximum amount required for the machinery to
be used. Very few rivers which have very large volumes of flow, possess
also descents where the power of this great volume of flow can be util-
ized. As has been indicated above, the flow of ordinary rivers upon
which falls may exist, has too great variations to permit the utilization
of great powers, without enormous and expensive storage. No storage
basins of fresh water in the world are equal to the Great American Lakes,
the outflow of which is of very great volume and of very great regularity.
At two points, this large volume and regularity of flow have been already
very successfully developed, namely, at Sault Ste. Marie, the outlet of
Lake Superior, and at Niagara Falls, the outlet of all the lakes except
Ontario. The writer has been the consulting engineer in the successful
development of each of these great powers. At Sault Ste. Marie, the
fall is about 20 feet, and within the past year this has been utilized
in the developement of a very large power and extensive mills are now in
operation. At Niagara, while the descent is very much greater, the lo-
cal circumstances made necessary the construction of a tunnel, a mile
and a half in length, and of large interior area, for the developement
of 100,000 H. P. This also is in successful operation and has demanded
a considerable investment of capital.
