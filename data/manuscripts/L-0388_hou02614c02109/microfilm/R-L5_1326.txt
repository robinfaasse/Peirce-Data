developement, and will be retained for temporary use, but will probably
finally be taken away as the developement of power from the St. Lawrence
proceeds.

The whole route of the canal, from the St. Lawrence to its
terminus, is upon lands which have been secured by the projectors of this
enterprise. This route is the most feasible, and really the only econ-
ical route by which the difference in level between the two rivers can
be utilized. Various other lines have been examined, but none afford
similar advantages.

Head-Gates at the St. Lawrence end will control the flow in
the canal. It will be of such dimensions as to afford transportation
from the St. Lawrence to the mills, and should such provision ever be
found desirable, locks could be placed so as to connect it with the Grass
River. This latter river, from Massena to its mouth, has a width of
over 400 feet, and will serve admirably to return to the St. Lawrence the
water which has passed through the canal and given mothion to the wheels.

The country through which the canal will pass is of alluvial
formation. No excavation of rock will be required. It will probably be
advisable to construct the canal at once of such dimensions as to carry
sufficient water to develope at Massena fifty thousand horse power. The
canal can afterwards at a moderate cost, be enlarged so as to develope double
that amount of power. The conditions are so favorable and the direct
