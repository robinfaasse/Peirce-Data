[date]1896 Aug 20[/date]

Messrs Stewart & Company
Gentlemen
The objection you mention to the working of my invention must strike any competent person at first sight; and I have already dealt with it in a letter to you. I have also explained that a very excellent method of remedy was probably patentable. I had hopes you would have taken steps about that before this.

I also offered to go to your shop, make the apparatus and demonstrate its practicability. Had that offer been accepted, this long delay ending in this obvious question would have been avoided.

I am inclined to think my letter was either not received or not read attentively, as it contained other things I should have expected some reply to.

If you will refer to my letters, you will find your question satisfactorily answered. But I must say I think the other course of simply making the thing under my direction, (or I will go and do it myself) will be far the speediest.

I yesterday very unexpectedly received notice [del]from the Patent Office[/del] of a probable [del]infringement[/del] interference. This would not have occurred had the Examiner lived up to his promise. [del]But[/del] As it is it cannot amount to anything, except some weeks' delay. Yours truly [ins]C. S. Peirce[/ins]
