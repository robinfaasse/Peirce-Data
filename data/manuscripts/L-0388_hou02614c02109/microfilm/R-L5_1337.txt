Office of
Stewart & Company,
40 WALL STREET,
New York, March 18th, 1897.

C. S. Pierce, Esq.,
84 Broad St., City.

Dear Sir:-

Your esteemed favor of Feb. 18th, I have just received, having missed me in London.

I was greatly disappointed to find on my arrival home that a rumor had gotten abroad to the effect that the stock of the St. Lawrence Power Company was being offerred on the Street prior to its being issued which rumor arose presumably from your negotiations, and I am very glad to notice that you have arranged matters so that no further negotiation of this kind will take place.

I also regret to advise you that our friend, Count D'Aulby utterly failed to carry out the provisions of his proposition, but the Crown Company have made us a proposition which I believe we will accept, but contemplates underwriting only one half of the bonds on their part, which will make commissions pro rata just fifty per cent of what they would be had they underwritten all of the bonds as represented by the Count that they would do. As a matter [ins]of fact[/ins] the information received here was a tissue of misrepresentations of fact, due in some cases to ignorance of business, and in others to wilful misconstruction, all of which is to be regretted.
