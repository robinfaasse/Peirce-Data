84 Broad St. [date]1897 Jan 1[/date]

My dear Mr. Stewart:

Mr. Easton yesterday left a card here to say you wanted my Digest in time for some gentleman to carry it with him to Europe tomorrow. I was at the Astor Library and only received the card today. Not knowing there was any immediate need of the Digest, I have been occupied for some days in getting ready a memoir for the next volume of Memoirs of the National Academy of Sciences which has to go to press tomorrow.

Under the circumstances, the best I can do will be to send you the [del]Digest[/del] [ins]text[/ins] in parts as fast as ready. I can mail you tonight Part I, relating to the History of our knowledge of acetylene. The other parts will follow in a few days. They will be

[u]Part II.[/u] Properties of acetylene.
[u]Part III.[/u] §1. Chemi[del]cal[/del] actions producing acetylene.
§2. Methods of preparation of acetylene (chiefly obsolete; but brief account necessary to show how pure or impure the substance was in determinations of its properties.)
[u]Part IV.[/u] Chemical reactions of acetylene.
§1. Explosion per se.
§2. Combustion and explosion with oxygen, with all the facts relating to its flame, the force of its explosion, etc.
§3. Reactions upon metallic solutions.
§4. Other reactions.
[u]Part V.[/u] Properties of its [del]compounds[/del].
§1. Acetylides and carbides.
§2. Haloid compounds.
§3. Other compounds.
[u]Part VI.[/u] Possible uses of carbide of calcium.

Yours very truly, C. S. Peirce
