84 Broad St. [date]1897 Jan 1[/date]

My dear Mr. Stewart:

I find here Mr. Easton's card with a message written upon it that you want my "Digest" so that "Capt. Mead" may have it when he sails tomorrow. I was yesterday at the Astor Library and therefore only received the card today. I have been occupied in getting ready a memoir for the National Academy of Sciences which had to be in Washington tomorrow in order to get into the annual volume. I was not aware that there was any special haste about the Digest. Under the circumstances, the best thing that I can do will be to mail to you tonight the first part of the Digest, containing a general sketch of the history of the investigation of acetylene from first to last; and I will then at once finish up my digest, which has still to be arranged so as to bring similar facts together, and that I will have ready for an early steamer just as quick as it can be drawn up. I can even send it [del]to you[/del] by part, as finished.

The synopsis of contents of the Digest will be as follows:

[u]A.[/u] History of the knowledge of acetylene.
[u]B.[/u] Physical Properties of Acetylene, including heat of combustion, etc.
[u]C.1.[/u] Chemical actions producing Acetylene.
[u]C.2.[/u] Methods of Preparation of Acetylene.
[u]D.[/u] Chemical reactions of Acetylene, especially all the facts relating to (1) its explosion by itself, (2) its combustion, its flame etc. and its explosive mixtures, (3) its combinations with salts & metals, etc.
[u]E.[/u] Properties of acetylides, carbides of metals, chlorides, bromides, and iodides of Acetylene.
[u]F.[/u] Uses of acetylene and of carbide of calcium.

Yours very truly,
C. S. Peirce
