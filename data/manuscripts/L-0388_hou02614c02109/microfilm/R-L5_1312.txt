[u]Copy of Letter to Stewart[/u]

[date]Nov 22.[/date]

Gentlemen: Though it is not certain that Magnesian limestones will [del]produce[/del] [ins]yield[/ins] carbide of Mg at no very high cost in the electrical furnace, [del]yet[/del] I think they will, not merely from the rough experiment which it is difficult otherwise to explain, but also from my knowledge of the behavior of Magnesium compounds, to which I have paid special attention. I also believe that not much more power will be called for.

I think it would be advisable

1st, to analyze that carbide specimen that gave so high an amount of acetylene more accurately, & to get up a ready method of determining the acetylene given by a carbide, which method shall be reliable. The present method of direct measures is not so.

2nd, to set up an electrical furnace & compare the production of carbide from pure limestone, from dolomite (or extra magnesian limestone) & from a selected magnesian limestone from St. Lawrence County. Two questions are to be answered by these experiments

1st. Is MgC₂ produced in the electrical furnace?
2nd Is its production much more expensive than that of the carbide of calcium?

3rd It would be necessary to analyze the limestone & carbides.

As to the total expense I can make an accurate estimate except of the work with the electrical furnace. If you give me the use of the room in Old Slip with gas and electricity, I would agree to pay for all the apparatus & chemicals & do all the work at these rates

Non electrical work $150
Electrical work [u]$250[/u]
$400
