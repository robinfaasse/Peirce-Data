The volume contains half a dozen portraits of the American
Socrates. There is the characterless thing in the Harvard
Memorial Hall, an unsatisfactory miniature, a work of
the eminent portraitist Martin, very beautiful and aimi-
able but not inspiring confidence as a likeness, a drawing
in profile by Carmontelle that shows us something of the
wit, a profile sketch by West that must be very accurate,
and, best of all, a [del]fantasy[/del] [ins]portrait[/ins] by West in an unfinished
painting of the peace commissioners. One naturally makes
a mental composite of these and combines with them
[del]features[/del] remembered features from some of Franklin's
descendants, and thus gets a tolerably life like image of
the outward man. Analogous supplements to the glimpses
Mr. Ford's innumerable anecdotes present help us to an insight
into the man himself.
