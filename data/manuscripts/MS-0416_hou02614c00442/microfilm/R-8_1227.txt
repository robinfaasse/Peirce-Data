be expressed by any proposition of another species. The following are
these forms. $S$ means an object of the class $S$; while $\pi$ means
a quality of the group $\pi$; [del]and[/del] "has" means possesses as a
subject an attribute; "is had by" means inheres in, "wants" and "is
wanted by" are negatives of has.

1. Every $S$ has every $\pi$.
2. Some $S$ has [del]every[/del] [ins]all[/ins] $\pi$'s.
3. Every $\pi$ is had by some $S$.
4. Some $\pi$ is had by all $S$'s.
5. Every $S$ has some $\pi$.
6. Some $S$ has some $\pi$.

7. Every $S$ wants every $\pi$.
8. Some $S$ wants all $\pi$s.
9. Every $\pi$ is wanted by some $S$.
10. Some $\pi$ is wanted by all $S$s.
11. Every $S$ wants some $\pi$.
12. Some $S$ wants some $\pi$.

[del]These are the variations produced by the different connections of the verb without
taking account of not applied to $S$ and $\pi$ which would of course multiply them by four.[/del]

These I term [ins]the above[/ins] the forms of the [u]first order[/u], because they
contain "has" or "is had by" "wants" or "is wanted by"
once, and once only. The following is an example of a
proposition of the [u]second order[/u]:

"Every $S$ wants some character possessed by every $P$."
