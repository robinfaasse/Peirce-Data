§156. It has been explained in the [del]former[/del] [ins]last preceding[/ins] chapter that
(speaking with sufficient accuracy for the purpose) "Some"
means that the speaker is to select an instance, while
"Every" or "Any" means that a second person is to perform
the selection. Of course, it is easier to satisfy the conditions
of a statement if one can select one's own example, ex-
cept for this, that he who undertakes to find an example
[del]therewith[/del] guarantees that there is one, while if he
leaves the selection to another, and there is none, is
statement is not broken down. Consequently, from
<u>Every</u> we can infer <u>Some</u>, provided only we have some
other premise which assures us that something exists.
Thus, if, and only if, we are assured some S exists we can
infer [del]from 2 from 1, and[/del] 8 from 7, 6 from 5, and 12 from
11. In like manner, if, and only if, we are assured some U
exists we can infer [del]4[/del] from 1, 6 from 3, 10 from 7, and 12,
from 9.
