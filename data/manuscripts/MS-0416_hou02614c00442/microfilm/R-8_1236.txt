[u]§158. General Canon of Syllogism.[/u]

It has for ages been customary with logicians, in addi-
tion to establishing valid forms of syllogism, to put
forth certain rules independent of figure, or the
special forms used, [del]by[/del] which [del]the[/del] were supposed
to be form of themselves sufficient criteria for the
ascertainment of the validity or invalidity of a
syllogistic argumentation.

true. The [del]as[/del] only such canon that really holds is that
in order that there should be a syllogistic conclusion there
must be two premises containing a middle term "dis-
tributed" in one, but not in the other, and the con-
clusion will be drawn by [del]putting[/del] [ins]compounding[/ins]
the two premises in such a way that this middle
term may be dropped upon the same principle
upon which it is dropped in Barbara.
