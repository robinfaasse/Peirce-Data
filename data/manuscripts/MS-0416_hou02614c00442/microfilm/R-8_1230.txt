§157. In this system syllogisms hold between any two propo-
sitions having a middle term of different phasis in the
two premises. We should therefore expect [del]21[/del] [ins]in each figure[/ins] different
species of syllogisms. [del]But[/del] Two of these infer no more than
might be inferred from weaker premises. I am indis-
posed to admit them as distinct species ([del]for[/del] [ins]these[/ins] species
are more natural classes than are moods). Each species
by metatheses of the three terms and two verbs gives 32 moods,
and if the premises are of different form, 64. I enumerate
the species in case the middle term is a group of qualities.
I state them, when convenient, in the second figure, but
regard figure little. I usually take the phasis of the verb different
[ins]in the two premises.[/ins]

[u]1[sup]st[/sup] Species[/u]. Every P has no quality but [del]μ's[/del] [ins]μ's[/ins],
Every S has every μ;
∴ Every S has every quality in any P.

[u]2[sup]nd[/sup] Species[/u]. Every P has μs as its only qualities,
Some S has all μ's;
∴ Some S has every quality of any P.

[u]3[sup]rd[/sup] Species[/u]. Every P has μs for its only qualities,
Every μ is in some S or other;
∴ Qualities of Ss are alone possessed by [ins]any[/ins] Ps.
This conclusion follows from the last.
