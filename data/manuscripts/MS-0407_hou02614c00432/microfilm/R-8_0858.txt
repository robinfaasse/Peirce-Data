truth, which is distinguished from falsehood simply by this, that if acted on it will carry

the truth and seeks to avoid it, is in a sorry state of mind indeed.

Yes, the other methods do have their merits: a clear logical conscience does cost some-
thing- just as any virtue, just as all that we cherish, costs us dear. But we should not
desire it to be otherwise. The genius of a man's logical method should be loved and reverenc-
ed as his pride, whom he has chosen from all the world. He need not condemn the others: on the
contrary, he may honor them deeply, and in doing so he only honors her the more. But she is
the one that he has chosen, and he knows that he was right in making that choice. And hav-
ing made it, he will work and fight for her, and will not complain that there are blows to
take, hoping that there may be as many and as hard to give, and will strive to be the worthy
knight and champion of her from the blaze of whose splendors he draws his inspiration and
his courage.
