That people which, in point of native intellect, far out-
classed any other that has trod this planet never had the first
notion of it; and to one who remembers how it was not until the
middle of the proud XIXth century that modern geometers succeeded
in placing themselves in every respect in advance or abreast of
Euclid, who knows how late it was before modern architects possessed
themselves of the [del]secret[/del] [ins]secret[/ins] of the Parthenon's beauty, and who in
reading Plato,---quite apart from his poetic flights,---has here
and there been struck breathless at the involution of the thought
even in the simplest sentences, at the subtlety of the syntax,
the opulence of the vocabulary, is amazed when he comes to reflect
that in the huge lexicon of Greek the principal ingredient of the
meaning modern science attaches to the word "probability" is
neither expressed nor implied by any noun, verb, adjective, or adverb.
