Addition and multiplication, and
their cognate words and algebraical signs
are used in such senses that [u]x[/u] + [u]y[/u] means
that either [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is true (without excluding
the possibility of both being true) and [u]xy[/u]
means that [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] are both true.
More explicitly, the meanings of the sum
and product are summed up in the following
propositions, which, for convenience of reference
are lettered, A, B, C, U, V, W.

[del]A. Either x is false or x+y is true.
B. Either y is false or x+y is true.
C. Either x+y is false or y is[/del]

A. Either [u]x[/u] is false or [u]x[/u]+[u]y[/u] is true.
B. Either [u]y[/u] is false or [u]x[/u]+[u]y[/u] is true.
C. Either [u]x[/u]+[u]y[/u] is false or [u]x[/u] or [u]y[/u] is true.

U Either [u]xy[/u] is false or [u]x[/u] is true.
V Either [u]xy[/u] is false or [u]y[/u] is true.
W Either [u]x[/u] is false or [u]y[/u] is false or [u]xy[/u] is [ins]true[/ins].
