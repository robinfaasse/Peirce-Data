The reader must not be surprised that $x \prec y$
etc, which are expressions of the relative mag-
nitude of two quantities, are themselves consi-
dered as quantities again; for this is the very
essence of the algebra. By Rule 2, we have
$$\underline{(5-A)(5-B) = 0};$$
for this equation is true if either of the fac-
tors of the first member vanishes, that is, if
either $A=5$ or $B=5$, that is, if either $x \prec y$
is true or $y \prec z$ is true; [del]which[/del] [ins]which[/ins] is required by
Rule 2. In like manner, the same rule yields
the following equations
$$(5-B)(5-C) = 0$$
$$(5-C)(5-A) = 0$$
$$(5-e)(5-b) = 0$$
$$(5-b)(5-a) = 0$$
$$(5-a)(5-e) = 0.$$
