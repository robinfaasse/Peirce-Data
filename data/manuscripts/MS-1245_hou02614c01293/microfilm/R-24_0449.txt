The above examples refute the assertion that the separable [u]um-[/u] is markedly more literal than the inseparable [u]um-[/u]

Weisse's saying that [u]um-[/u], [u]über-[/u], [u]unter-[/u], [u]durch-[/u], [u]voll-[/u] and [u]wider-[/u] are separable when taken literally and inseparable when taken figuratively is one of the many instances of his mistaking vagueness for generality. Figurative uses of words are merely metaphors that have passed into general use and it is contrary to the spirit of metaphor to use different constructions from what would be used in speaking literally.

I have examined the difference between [u]um-[/u] separable and inseparable, and find that all verbs in which it is inseparable and transitive, the thing surrounded or covered being the direct object, while the corresponding verbs in [u]um-[/u] separable, when transitive, take the surrounding or covering thing as direct object, and when intransitive take the same thing as subject.
