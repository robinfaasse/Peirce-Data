[u]Notes on Laurent's Calcul des Probabilités.[/u]

$A_m^n$ is the number of arrangements on $m$ objects taken in $n$'s

The arrangements of four objects taken in 2's
are AB AC AD BC BD CD
BA CA DA CB DB DC

To get the arrangements $A_m^{n+1}$, it suffice to put at the
end of each arrangement $A_m^n$, each of the objects
that does not occur. Thus [del]with[/del] to get arrangements of
4 objects in 3's
ABC ACB ADB BCA BDA CDA
ABD ACD ADC BCD BDC CDB
BAC CAB DAB CBA BBA DCA
BAD CAD DAC CBD DBC DCB

Thus $A_m^{n+1} = (m-n) A_m^n$

[del]so that $A_m^n = $[/del] Hence $A_m^n = m^{\underline{n}}$ that is $\frac{m!}{(m-n)!}$
But $A_m^1 = m$
Hence if $P_m$ is the number of permutations of $m$ things
$P_m = A_m^m = \frac{m!}{(m-m)!} = m!$

If $C_m^n$ is the number of combinations of $m$ things taken in $n$'s, that
is the arrangements regardless of the order
$C_m^n = \frac{A_m^n}{P_n} = \frac{m!}{(m-n)! n!}$

Or if $\alpha$ is the number of things taken and $\beta$ the number left
[del]The[/del] and $M_\beta^\alpha$ is the multitude of collections [del]of[/del] taking $\alpha$ things and
leaving $\beta$ things
$M_\beta^\alpha = \frac{(\alpha+\beta)!}{\alpha! \beta!}$
