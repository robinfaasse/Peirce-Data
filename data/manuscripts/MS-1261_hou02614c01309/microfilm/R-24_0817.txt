Tenses ought to be as follows:

[u]Order 0.[/u] With no reference to time: [u]houses sometimes fall[/u].

[del][u]Order 1.[/u] [u]Simple aorist, present, future.[/u][/del] [del]The house[/del]
[del]fell, the house falls, the house will fall.[/del]

[u]Order 2.[/u] [u]Pluperfect[/u]. [u]The house had fallen[/u]

[u]Imperfect[/u]. [u]The house was falling[/u]

[u]Past future[/u] (Conditional?) [u]The house [ins]was[/ins] about to fall[/u]. [ins](The house would fall)[/ins]

[u]Perfect[/u]. [u]The house has fallen[/u].

[u]Double present[/u]. [u]The house is falling[/u].

[u]Present future[/u] (Subjunctive?) [u]The house is about to fall[/u].

[u]Future perfect[/u]. [u]The house will have fallen[/u].

[u]Future present[/u]. [u]The house will be falling[/u].

[u]Future future[/u]. [u]The house will be about to fall[/u].

Other tenses can be imagined as: I know not whether the house had fallen or was falling, but if not it certainly was about to fall, for it had [ins]indeed[/ins] been about to fall, sixty years before, and, according to a prediction, had been about to have fallen before the time we are speaking of. But the above 13 are required to fill out the scheme of those we commonly use.
