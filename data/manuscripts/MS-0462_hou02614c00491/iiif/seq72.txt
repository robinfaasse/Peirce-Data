[del]It is difficult to determine with
certainty how far the present appearance
of the heavens, and the actual arrangement
of the stars in the Milky Way, is the result
of a slow and gradual process of
aggregation, and how far it may be traced
back to the primitive condition of things,
when the stars were first created.
But there are various considerations that lead
us to think that the Milky Way is not a
stable and permanent structure, but that
it is undergoing a slow and gradual change,
which will in the course of ages lead to its
total disruption and the formation of a
number of isolated and independent
clusters. The first consideration is derived
from the known motions of the stars.
We know that all the stars are in
motion, and that these motions are not
altogether irregular, but that there are
certain general tendencies or drifts,
which lead the stars in certain directions.[/del]
