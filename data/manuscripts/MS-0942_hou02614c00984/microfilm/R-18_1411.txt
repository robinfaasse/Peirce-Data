[del]Hence[/del] [ins]Thence[/ins] the first dimension of the continuum of possible [del]quality[/del] [ins]qualities[/ins] is of the nature of a hyperbolic sequence. [del]That is taking any two points[/del] [del]one will be after the other but none after itself.[/del]

What will it be in two dimensions? This is not by any means to be answered off-hand. The mere mathematical question is not entirely easy because that two dimensional continuum may be artiad or perissid. By an artiad surface I mean one [ins]like a sphere[/ins] which has two sides [del]not containing points[/del] [del]with one another[/del] [del]and it is necessary to draw an even number of nonsingular lines[/del] [del]on it to prevent such a line being drawn upon it which cannot shrink to a point[/del] [del]into two regions[/del], while by a perissid surface I mean one which has but one like [del]a[/del] [ins]the[/ins] plane which has but one side, and it is necess[ins]ary[/ins] to [del]draw[/del] [ins]cut[/ins] an odd number of topically nonsingular [ins]linear[/ins] cuts in it to prevent a topically nonsingular line being drawn on it which cannot shrink to a point by continuous variation within the surface. A line is topically non singular if [del]at[/del] every point on it [del]there[/del] can move [del]two ways[/del] back and forth. [del]In other words[/del] it is a line with no isolated points, no extremities, and no points of branching or crossing itself.

If there is a sequence in a two dimensional continuum there must be [del]or[/del] through every point a line of [del]not say[/del] progression. The figure shows this, for a point not at the beginning or end of the sequence. If the sequence is hyperbolic, it is plain no line of sequence can cut the line of no sequence but once, between its beginning and end. But generally every such line will cut it once. Three such lines cannot cut at three places for then there would be different ways of passing from one stage to another contrary to the nature of a sequence.
