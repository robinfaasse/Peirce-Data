The intensity of the quality is one thing, the intensity of the feeling
that represents it is another. I dimly remember when I was
a boy going aboard a naval vessel with the gentleman who
[del]then and there took[/del] [ins]was to take[/ins] command of it, and a salute was fired with the
brass Dahlgren guns then used in the navy. They were horribly
noisy and I still have a very faint consciousness of a sound which
I can call to mind as being of [del]most dreadful intensity[/del]. I do
not suppose two different intensities are present to me, but it
is like a painter's scale of values, where the brightest color his
pigments afford represents the brightest color in nature, and
the rest are scaled down [del]correspondingly[/del] [ins]accordingly[/ins].

Quality, however, in its infinite degree is at a point of
discontinuity. Every infinity is recognized [ins]accepted[/ins] by mathematicians
as a discontinuity, and is truly such by the strictest logical
analysis. The infinite degree of a quality is thus broken off
from the continuum. It is in no possible comparison with other
degrees or other qualities. It is just that quality in and for itself
which we have seen is not represented in any consciousness
other than its own. That explains why the two regions of the
continuum are at one in their maxima.

limits. The last consequent is the very reality itself. That is
[del]my very conception of[/del] [ins]the essence of the word,[/ins] reality, [del]namely,[/del] what we should believe
