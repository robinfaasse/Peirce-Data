§ 1. [del]Three Grades of Clearness[/del]. [del]After studying[/del] Since the beginnings of logic, relations have been recognized as forming one of the proper subjects of its study. But, notwithstanding a few treatises [u]De relatives[/u] in the middle ages, nothing of importance was done until Augustus De Morgan, an eminent mathematician and the greatest formal logician of his day by far, laid the foundations of the logic of [del]relations[/del] and showed that it was the general form of logic of which common logic is so extremely specialized a branch that the main features of logic are not exhibited in it. In [date]1870[/date], I brought the method of Boole to bear upon it, and in subsequent writings gradually penetrated to the heart of the theory. America is a bad place for original work; and not one word of cheer came from [del]the[/del] any person in this country, except from President Gilman and the Johns Hopkins University, where I had a class of splendid minds for three years. After that, I ceased to publish, although my studies were continued whenever I was able to get the leisure.
