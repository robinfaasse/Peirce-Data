The method of representing relations by diagrams consisting of spots and simple lines of connection was developed by Mr. A. B. Kempe in a very profound and masterly [u]Memoir on the Theory of Mathematical Form[/u] published in the [u]Philosophical Transactions[/u] for [ins]1886[/ins]. Though he does not say how he would represent such a relationship as A gives C to B, I [del]concei[/del] apprehend that he would approve of Fig [del]4[/del] [ins]3[/ins]; while Fig 4 represents the definition of the last section. The difference is that Mr. Kempe conceives that the relation is capable of perfect representation by simple connections all of one kind, while I hold that the connections [del]&[/del] must be branching connections. Mr. Kempe conceives that his connections are all of one kind; but they are really of two kinds, those that are marked by lines, and those that are not so marked. Thus, A is [ins]in[/ins] that negative way connected with the second party and to the idea of a gift. My lines are of different kinds. Thus, I distinguish by quality of the lines, "by," "to," "of."
