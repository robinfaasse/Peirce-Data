[u]The Logic of Relations.[/u]

§1. [u]Three grades of Clearness.[/u] The purpose of this article is to explain what the logic of relatives, or better, of [ins]relationships,[/ins] is, how two "algebras" or systems of diagrammatical representation by means of letters and other characters, have been invented by the present writer for the study of that logic, and how Schröder in an immortal volume has shown how one of these algebras, especially, [del]with the aid of[/del] [ins]with some aid from the other and from[/ins] a notation of his own, may be applied to the solution of certain problems.

The first step [del]is to[/del] must be to make the conception of a relation clear. Now there are three grades of clearness of [ins]our[/ins] apprehension of the meanings of words. The first consists in the connection of the word with familiar experience. The second consists in the abstract definition of it, involving an analysis of the conception. The third consists in such a diagrammatical representation of the idea that it can be applied in fruitful thought and [ins]help to[/ins] determine how we shall act in practical conjunctures.

I call it essential because no language can exist without prepositions, either as separate words, as case-declensions, or as [ins]syntactical[/ins] arrangements of words, or something equivalent.
