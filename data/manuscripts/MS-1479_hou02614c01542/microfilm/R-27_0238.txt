Morgagni died Dec 5, 1771 and Van Swieten, another of the eight, June 18, 1772, [del]and in that year there[/del] [ins]no letter[/ins] These were the only two vacancies in 1772 when Franklin and the great mathematician La Grange were elected. Van Swieten, a philosophical physician, had succeeded De Crousaz, a philosophical mathematician, who was considered to be the successor of Leibniz (Wolfius having been elected considerably later than De Crousaz). It is probable that Franklin was regarded as a successor of Leibniz and Lagrange of Newton.
