[date]1793[/date] Bonnet d. (May 20). Hunter d. (Oct. 16).
[date]1799[/date] De Saussure d. (Jan. 22). Black d. (Nov. 26)
[date]1800[/date] J. A. Euler d. (Sep 18)
[date]1802[/date] The Institute created. There were five vacancies. Maskelyne, Rumford, Pallas, and Cavendish were elected. The astronomer Maskelyne followed the astronomer J. A. Euler, the naturalist and traveler Pallas [del]filled[/del] [ins]very appropriately[/ins] the place of De Saussure, Cavendish evidently succeeded to Black, and the [del]great[/del] Rumford, as a patron of science, filled the vacancy from Bonnet's death.
[date]1804[/date] Priestley d. (Feb 6). Volta and Klaproth were elected. The latter obviously took Priestley's place and therefore Volta must be regarded as succeeding Hunter.
[date]1811[/date] Cavendish d (Feb 24) and A. von Humboldt took his place.
[date]1812[/date] Maskelyne d. (Feb 9) Pallas d. (Sep 8). Jenner and Werner were elected, the latter evidently succeeding Pallas, and so German took the place of German, and Englishman that of Englishman.
[date]1814[/date] Rumford d. (Aug 21). Woll. was elected in his place.
[date]1817[/date] Klaproth d. (Jan. 1) Werner d. (June 30). Scarpa and Piazzi elected.
[date]1819[/date] Watt d. (Aug. 25) Davy elected in his place.
[date]1820[/date] Banks d. (May 19) Gauss elected in his place.
[date]1822[/date] Sir Wm. Herschel d. (Aug. 25). Berzelius elected to the vacancy.
[date]1823[/date] Jenner d. (Jan. 26) Wollaston elected in his place.
[date]1826[/date] Piazzi d. (July 22) A. P. De Candolle elected
[date]1827[/date] Volta d. (March 5) Young elected.
[date]1828[/date] Wollaston d. (Dec. 22)
[date]1829[/date] Young d. (May 10). Davy d. (May 29). Olbers elected. This astronomer may perhaps be regarded as taking the place of Wollaston whose place had been [del]who was[/del] the indirect successor of several astronomers. Besides, this was the only vacancy not very recent.
[date]1830[/date] Dalton and Blumenbach elected. Dalton evidently made up for the loss of Davy. Blumenbach, therefore must have succeeded to Young's chair.
[date]1832[/date] Scarpa d. (Oct 31) Robert Brown elected.
[date]1840[/date] Blumenbach d. (Jan. 22) Olbers d. (March 2). Von Buch and Bessel elected, Bessel evidently the successor of Olbers, and Von Buch appr. not inappropriately, following Blumenbach.
[date]1841[/date] A. P. De Candolle d. (Sep 9)
[date]1842[/date] Oersted elected in De Candolle's place.
[date]1844[/date] Dalton d. (July 22) Faraday elected in his place.
[date]1846[/date] Bessel d. (March 17). Jacobi elected.
[date]1848[/date] Berzelius d. (Aug 7) Brown
[date]1849[/date] Brewster elected to supply the place of Berzelius
[date]1851[/date] Jacobi d. (Feb 18) Oersted d. (March 9) Tiedemann elected, following Oersted rather than Jacobi
[date]1852[/date] E. Mitscherlich elected following Jacobi, if Tiedemann succeeded to Oersted.
[date]1853[/date] Von Buch d. (March 4). Lejeune Dirichlet elected
[date]1855[/date] Gauss d. (Feb 23) Sir John F. W. Herschel elected in his place.
[date]1858[/date] Brown d. (June 10)
[date]1859[/date] Dirichlet d. (May 5) Humboldt d. (May 6) Richard Owen elected to succeed Brown.
[date]1860[/date] Plana and Ehrenberg elected, the former in place of Dirichlet, the latter in place of Humboldt.
