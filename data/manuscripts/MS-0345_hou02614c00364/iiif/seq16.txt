Consider, what a word or symbol is; it is a sort of representation. Now a representation is something which stands for something. I will not undertake to analyze, this evening, this conception of standing for [del]another[/del] [ins]something[/ins] – but, it is sufficiently plain that it involves the standing [u]to[/u] something [u]for[/u] something. A thing cannot stand [u]for[/u] something without standing [u]to[/u] something [u]for[/u] that something. Now, what is this that a word stands [u]to[/u]? Is it a person? We usually say that the word [u]homme[/u] stands to a Frenchman for [u]man[/u]. It would be a little more precise to say that it stands to [del]that[/del] Frenchman’s mind – to his memory. It is still more accurate to say that it addresses a particular remembrance or image in that memory.
And what image, what remembrance? Plainly, the one which is the mental equivalent of the word [page 15] [u]homme[/u] – in short, its interpretant. Whatever a word addresses then or stands [u]to[/u], is its interpretant or identified symbol. And conversely, [del]every[/del] [ins]any[/ins] interpretant is addressed by the word; for were it not so, did it not as it were overhear what the [del]what the[/del] word says, how could it interpret what it says. There are doubtless some who cannot understand this metaphorical argument. I wish to show that the relation of a word to that which it addresses is the same as its relation to [ins]its[/ins] equivalent or identified terms. For that purpose, I first show that whatever a word addresses is an equivalent term, – [del]the[/del] [ins]its[/ins] mental equivalent. I next show that, since the intelligent reception of a term is the being addressed by that term, and since the explication of a terms implication is the intelligent reception of that term, that the interpretant or equivalent of a term which as we have already seen explicates the implication of a term is addressed by the term.
