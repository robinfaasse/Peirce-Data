Lecture VII.

We have now made a pretty thorough analysis of the syllogism, [ins]both[/ins] in its proper deductive form and also in the forms of induction and hypothesis. Without troubling you with a recapitulation, I will say that we have found the syllogistic system so symmetrical and perfect; that it is not impossible that we now know the whole of it. But so far, we have conducted our investigation purely in the spirit of Aristotle. I am sure it must have struck you, how peculiar, how idiosyncratic, [del]the[/del] the whole character of this logical research has been. In other sciences, we discover principles and then apply them, but this has not been our course; we have enunciated no principles, we have merely erected [u]types[/u] to which other reasoning has to be reduced.
