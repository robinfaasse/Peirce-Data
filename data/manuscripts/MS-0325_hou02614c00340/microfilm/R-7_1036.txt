To the Editor of the Sun, Sir:—

It is a well-settled rule among scientific men that every step in science, every new result, shall be credited to the name of him who first publishes it. It is a sensible rule, because the [del]date of[/del] [ins]first[/ins] publication is easily ascertained, and because while it is convenient to credit a discovery to some name, it makes no difference, for most purposes, whether strict justice be done or not; and the presumption is that the man to whom this rule works injustice was too intent on his scientific work to take care to secure priority, and from the same cause will take little heed or care for the mention of the vocable, that has served his fathers for a family name.
