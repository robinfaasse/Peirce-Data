In the Roman world, there never had been
any science worth mention, and [del]this world[/del] [ins]the empire[/ins]
was sinking always into deeper and deeper
barbarism, until almost universal illiteracy
was reached. Finally, the crusades in one way
and another brought about a fructification
of the genius that lay dormant in the
modern peoples.

In the 13[ins]th[/ins] century, there began to
be quite a promising revival of science;
but it was followed by a great wave of
theological study which drove science
out of the field. This is a historical
fact little recognized. It is, however,
positively stated by Roger Bacon, and
there are many facts to bear out
his assertion.
