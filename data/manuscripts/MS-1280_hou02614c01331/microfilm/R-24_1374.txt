By the time of Socrates, however, the Greeks
were pretty well convinced of the futility of physical
inquiries, which they regarded as something
as absurdly unattainable as people nowadays
general consider metaphysics to be.

But they went on with the study of
geometry and arithmetic; and when these
were sufficiently advanced they were able,
as we have seen to take considerable steps
in astronomy, optics, and statics.

I have said nothing about the optics,
because its value is small. I also pass by
a great deal of valuable mathematical work.
