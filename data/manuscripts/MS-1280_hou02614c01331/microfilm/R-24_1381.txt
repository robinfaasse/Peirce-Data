But when they found themselves masters
of great empires, with treasures pouring
in quantities they had never dreamed
of, and pouring out in almost equal
measure, it became necessary that
they should keep books; and in every
country, they learned arithmetic from
the peoples they had vanquished. But the
Khaleefeh Almamoon found that the
arithmeticians of Khiva were the best;
and the book of Muhammad bin Moosa
was written under the orders of that Khaleefeh
who directed the author to prepare a
popular account of the Khiva system
of arithmetic. There is, therefore, nothing
original about the book, which expressly
professes to be [u]popular[/u], not a learned
work, and expressly professes to teach
