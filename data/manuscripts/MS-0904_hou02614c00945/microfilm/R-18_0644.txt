The familiar conceptions of First, Second,
Third, which are used by everyone a thou-
sand times a day, have a fullness of
significance which is commonly little
thought of. By the First, I do not at all
mean the One, of which Parmenides spoke,
which is a synthetizing unity or whole. I am
thinking simply of what presents itself as
first, immediate, fresh, unsubjected to any-
thing that went before, and therefore sponta-
neous and free. Of these words, [u]fresh[/u], I
think, nearest expresses the idea in its
virgin purity. For the others [ins]seem to[/ins] imply a series
