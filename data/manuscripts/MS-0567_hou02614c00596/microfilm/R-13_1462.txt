In multiplying [del]a[/del] proposition into itself
we have the right to choose any point
we please in the quantifier and to leave
all the indices to the left of that unchanged
while changing all to the right of it. To
prove that this is so, let $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ stand
each for one of the quantifying letters $\Sigma$ and
$\Pi$, and let $*$, corresponding to $\Phi$, and $\circ$,
corresponding to $\Psi$, stand each for one
of the signs $+$, $\times$, according as the quanti-
fying letter is $\Sigma$ or $\Pi$. Then let the pro-
position to be multiplied into itself be
$\Phi_{i} \Psi_{j} u_{ij}$,
and suppose that when [del]it[/del] [ins]this[/ins] is multiplied
into itself $i$ is identified and $j$ diversified
in the two factors, so as to give
$\Phi_{i} \Psi_{j} \Psi_{k} u_{ij} u_{ik}$.

Now the original proposition is equivalent
to
$(\Psi_{j} u_{1j}) * (\Psi_{j} u_{2j}) * (\Psi_{j} u_{3j}) * \text{etc.}$
