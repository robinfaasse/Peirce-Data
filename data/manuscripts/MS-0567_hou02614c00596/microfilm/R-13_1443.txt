The following proposition is universally true
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{\beta} \Sigma_{\gamma} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} \left\{ \overline{\Upsilon}_{\gamma i j} (\overline{\Upsilon}_{\alpha i j} + \overline{\Upsilon}_{\beta i j}) + \Upsilon_{\gamma i j} \Upsilon_{\alpha i j} \Upsilon_{\beta i j} \right\}.$$
To prove this, note first that if we eliminate $\gamma$ at once, we have an identical proposition. If we raise the whole to a power, there will be no additional mode of elimination, unless $i$, $j$, and $\gamma$ be identified in different factors; but then all the indices must be alike and nothing will be changed. As a special case of this formula we put $U_{i}$ for $\Upsilon_{\beta i j}$. This we can do, because
$$\Sigma_{\beta} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} \left\{ U_{i} \Upsilon_{\beta i j} + \overline{U}_{i} \overline{\Upsilon}_{\beta i j} \right\}$$
which is true by the same reasoning as that just used. The product of these two formulae gives
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{\gamma} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} \left\{ \overline{\Upsilon}_{\gamma i j} (\overline{\Upsilon}_{\alpha i j} + \overline{U}_{i}) + \Upsilon_{\gamma i j} \Upsilon_{\alpha i j} U_{i} \right\}.$$
