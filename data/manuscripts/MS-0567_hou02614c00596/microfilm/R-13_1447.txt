$\Pi_{i} a_{i} = a_{1} a_{2} a_{3} \text{ etc} = \Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i} a_{j}$

$\Sigma_{i} a_{i} = a_{1} + a_{2} + a_{3} + \text{etc}$
$= (a_{1} + a_{2} + a_{3} + \text{etc}) (a_{1} + a_{2} + a_{3} + \text{etc})$
$= \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{j} a_{i} a_{j}$.

Next consider the first two indices. The proposition is of one of the four forms

$\Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i j} \quad \Sigma_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i j} \quad \Pi_{i} \Sigma_{j} a_{i j} \quad \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{j} a_{i j}$.

For the first and last of these, we have only to apply the formulae just obtained [ins][of step two][/ins] with the first two [del]of p. 17[/del]. That is,

$\Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i j} = \Pi_{j} \Pi_{i} a_{i j} = \Pi_{i} \Pi_{k} \Pi_{j} a_{i j} a_{k j}$
$= \Pi_{j} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{k} a_{i j} a_{k j} = \Pi_{j} \Pi_{l} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{k} a_{i j} a_{k l}$.

$\Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{j} a_{i j} = \Sigma_{j} \Sigma_{i} a_{i j} = \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{k} \Sigma_{j} a_{i j} a_{k j}$
$= \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{j} \Sigma_{k} \Sigma_{l} a_{i j} a_{k l}$.

For the other two forms the proceeding is not more difficult. By the formulae for a single index

$\Sigma_{i} (\Pi_{j} a_{i j}) = \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{k} (\Pi_{j} a_{i j}) (\Pi_{j} a_{k j}) = \Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{k} \Pi_{j} a_{i j} a_{k j}$

$\Pi_{i} (\Sigma_{j} a_{i j}) = \Pi_{i} \Pi_{k} (\Sigma_{j} a_{i j}) (\Sigma_{j} a_{k j})$
