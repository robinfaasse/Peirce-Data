In multiplying a proposition into
itself we have the right to leave all the
indices unchanged at the left of any point
in the quantifier and to change all to
the right of that point. To prove this,
let $\Phi_i, \Psi_j$ represent each one of the
quantifying letters $\Sigma, \Pi$; and let $*$, $\circ$
stand [ins]each[/ins] for the operations [del]either[/del] of addition
or multiplication. Suppose, then, [del]the[/del]
proposition multiplied into itself is
$$\Phi_i \Psi_j u_{ij}.$$
This may be represented thus
$$(u_{11} \circ u_{12} \circ u_{13} \circ \text{etc}) * (u_{21} \circ u_{22} \circ u_{23} \circ \text{etc}) * (u_{31} \circ u_{32} \circ u_{33} \circ \text{etc}) * \text{etc}$$
Now let it be multiplied into itself, leaving
[del]i unchanged[/del] with identification of $i$ and
diversification of $j$, and we have
$$\Phi_i \Psi_j \Psi_k u_{ij} u_{ik}$$
$$(u_{11} u_{11} \circ u_{11} u_{12} \circ \text{etc}) \circ (u_{12} u_{11} \circ u_{12} u_{12} \circ \text{etc}) \circ \text{etc}$$
$$* (u_{21} u_{21} \circ u_{21} u_{22} \circ \text{etc}) \circ (u_{22} u_{21} \circ u_{22} u_{22} \circ \text{etc}) \circ \text{etc} * \text{etc}$$
