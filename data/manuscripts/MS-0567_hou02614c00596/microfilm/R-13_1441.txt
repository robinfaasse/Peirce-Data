$$\Sigma_{a}\Pi_{b}\Sigma_{c}\Pi_{d}\Pi_{e}\Sigma_{f}\Pi_{g}$$

$$\{\bar{x}_{a} + r_{ab} y_{b} (\bar{r}_{cb} + I_{ac})\} \{\bar{x}_{d} + r_{de} y_{e} (\bar{r}_{fe} + I_{df})\}$$

The application to the Boolean of the common
formula
$$(a+x)(b+y) \prec a + b + xy$$
gives
$$\bar{x}_{a} + \bar{x}_{d} + r_{ab} r_{de} y_{b} y_{e} (\bar{r}_{cb} + I_{ac}) (\bar{r}_{fe} + I_{df}).$$

We now identify $f$ with $a$; when the first
and last factors of the last term become
$$r_{ab} (\bar{r}_{ae} + I_{ad}).$$

But we have
$$r_{ab} \bar{r}_{ae} \prec I_{be}.$$

Thus, we reach the conclusion
$$\Pi_{a} \Sigma_{b} \Pi_{d} \Sigma_{e} (\bar{x}_{a} + \bar{x}_{d} + I_{ad} + y_{b} y_{e} I_{be});$$
that is, there are either not two $x$'s different
from one another or there are two $y$'s dif-
ferent from one another. A little examina-
tion will show that no other conclusion could
be reached by elimination from the square.
