Kant professes to "beat idealism at its own
game." The idealistic reasoning turns upon
the proposition that all we know immediately,
that is, otherwise than inferentially, is what
is present in the mind, as things external to
the mind are not. But we apprehend our
own ideas only as in the stream of time, and
since neither past nor future, however near
the present instant, is present, no time is present;
and thus the mere imagination of time is an
immediate perception of time, and so of that
which is not absolutely present. This simple
remark, not explicitly made by Kant, but con-
tained in his thought, refutes that conception
of the "present" upon which subjective idealism
essentially depends.
