[u]Definition[/u]. The directrix of a circle is the ray [ins]in its plane[/ins] [del]which is distant a quadrant from its centre.[/del] [del]The director plane of a sphere is the plane which is the metrical conjugate of its centre.[/del]

[u]Corollary[/u]. From any point on the directrix of a circle two tangents can be drawn to it, because [del]through that point[/del] there are two rays in the plane inclined by the requisite angle to the directrix.

[u]Theorem[/u]. Every ray cuts the [del]circle[/del] [ins]any sphere[/ins] in two points or none.

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let $\sigma$ be a sphere centred at $[C]$ and let $\{r\}$ be a ray cutting $\sigma$ in $[A]$. Then, I say that there is one [ins]other[/ins] point, $[B]$, and no [del]others[/del] [ins]third[/ins] in which $\{r\}$ cuts $\sigma$.

[fig]A circle with center C, a secant line cutting it, and an external point D.[/fig] For the section of $\sigma$ by $(rC)$ is, by Definitions and , a circle. Let $[D]$ be the point in which $\{r\}$ cuts the directrix of that circle. Then, the ray $\{CD\}$, by Axiom 3, cuts the circle in two points; and owing to the continuity of the motion of the medium (First Property) [del]as[/del] [ins]if[/ins] the straight filament in $\{CD\}$ is turned about $[D]$ in the plane of the circle, the number of points in which it cuts the circle must continue the same until it comes into the position of a tangent. But after that, it cannot cut the circle at all; for [del]the line between the two points of tangency of tangents from [D], by the second of the last theorems, passes through [C].[/del] Hence this line the number must then be increased or diminished by two, and if it [del]did so[/del] [ins]were +[/ins] a ray through the centre could cut the circle in more than two points, which is impossible. Hence the ray $\{r\}$ cuts the sphere in two points. Q.E.D.
