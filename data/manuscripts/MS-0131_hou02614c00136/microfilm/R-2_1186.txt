This does not prevent the length of one circuit being finite while that of another is infinite [ins]or imaginary,[/ins] nor the angle round one ray being finite, while that round another is infinite or imaginary. For example, in our ordinary system of measurements, sensible lengths, [ins]as[/ins] [del]e.g.[/del] a mile, on an ordinary ray are infinitesimal parts of the whole; while infinitesimal parts of a ray in the sky are insensible. Thus, the whole ray in the sky is finite, namely, 180°. But different units of measure have to be employed for the two kinds of rays; and one degree is an infinity of miles.

[u]Art[/u]. [u]Theorem[/u]. [u]The motion has just six degrees of freedom.[/u]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. For [del]every[/del] [ins]any[/ins] particle can be carried by [del]translation[/del] along any ray through it as axis of rotation. Now there is a doubly continuous multitude of such rays. And it can be carried on such ray to any point, and there is a singly continuous multitude of such points. This makes three degrees of freedom. The motion may then be rotated round any ray through the particle, and there is a singly continuous multitude of planes through such axis of rotation. Hence, there are six degrees of freedom in all.
