In solving the main problem of the effect of the live load, it is equally [del]important[/del] [ins]indispensable[/ins] that the method selected should leave no room for serious doubt as
to whether every part of the effect is substantially taken into account and that it
should be no more complicated than the nature of the problem necessitates. Two different
methods approve themselves to me, of which one is more satisfactory except that it
involves the numerical solution of a large number of equations, requiring a
very competent computer, while the algebraic developments it involves are so
extended that I hesitate to undertake them until I can have an algebraist
duplicate every step concurrently with my own calculation. The other method
in my opinion is fully sufficient, but I do not know that there is no room for
doubt [ins][pn][u]p.41[/u][/pn][/ins] in the minds of others about that; and it is also pretty complicated,
though less so than the other.
