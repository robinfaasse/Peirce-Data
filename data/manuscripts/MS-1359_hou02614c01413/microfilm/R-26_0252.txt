[pn]p.24.[/pn] [u]Development of the Formulae.[/u]

We have a stiffened suspension-bridge. The cable between the outermost suspenders takes under the dead load the parabolic form. The outer parts of the truss rest, at each end, upon two supports, of which the inner is a good deal inside the attachments of the cable. The cable does not pass over pulleys, but is attached to towers to which are likewise attached back stays. The vertical compression of the towers and the truss-supports under the live load is nil. But the cable attachments have an elastic horizontal freedom. The live load may lift the truss entirely off its outer support at one end, which will give an entirely distinct problem having its own equations.

Two things have to be determined: first, the changes of the vertical of the different parts of the truss under the live load, and second, the increase of the pull upon the cable. Any moments of bending and shearing stresses that may be wanted are readily calculated from the deformations.

I think it ought to be assumable that the extensions of the suspenders under the live load can be neglected. For if we imagine inextensible suspenders to become extensible, it is manifest that the truss would sink and the cable rise in consequence, and the rise of the cable would relieve the back stays, showing that there would be less pull on the cable. Thus, the effect of the extensibility of the suspenders would be to increase the load on the truss and di-

[pn]p.25[/pn] minish that on the cable. Now the economy of the suspension bridge seems to consist in making the cable bear as much of the load as possible.
