[u]Second Part of the Effect due to Flexure without stretching of the Cable[/u]

In forming the equations, let us assume that all the bending, as well as all the weight, is concentrated at joints which occur wherever [del]$\frac{n}{B}$[/del] [ins]$\frac{n}{S}$[/ins] is a whole number. We will assume that $\theta_n$, $\varphi_n$, $\psi_n$, etc refer to links extending from $n-S$ to $n$. Then, the energy of the bending of the supported truss
$$\frac{1}{2} \frac{B}{S} \sum_{-N+S}^{+N-S} (\theta_{n+S} - \theta_n)^2$$
where the precise limits are not of much consequence so long as $\theta$ is assumed zero beyond $\pm N$. The energy of lifting the live load is
$$w \sum_{1}^{J} y_n S.$$
The energy of the forces and couples at $\pm N$ is
$$\frac{1}{2} L (y_{-N}^2 + y_N^2) + \frac{1}{2} L (\theta_{-N+S}^2 + \theta_N^2).$$
The energy of displacement of the two tower-heads is
$$K \xi_R'^2.$$

The number of degrees of freedom is $2\frac{N}{S} + 1$, equal to the number of $\eta$s. Hence we have only to equal to zero the variations of the energy relatively to the [u](p. 51)[/u] $\eta$s, in order to obtain sufficient equations for determining the deformation under the live load when the truss rests on four supports.

As explained above we may assume $y_n = \eta_n$. We have $\tan \theta_n = \frac{y_n - y_{n-S}}{S}$. But it is assumed that the $\theta$s are so small that their squares and products can be neglected in comparison with unity. Hence, $\tan \theta_n = \theta_n (1 + \frac{1}{3} \theta_n^2) = \theta_n = \frac{y_n - y_{n-S}}{S}$. We have also the right-angled triangles giving
$$(\frac{R^2-N^2}{R} - \eta_N)^2 + (R-N + \xi_R'' - \xi_N)^2 = (\frac{R^2-N^2}{R})^2 + (R-N)^2$$
$$(\frac{R^2-N^2}{R^2} - \eta_{-N})^2 + (R-N + \xi_R' + \xi_{-N})^2 = (\frac{R^2-N^2}{R})^2 + (R-N)^2.$$
