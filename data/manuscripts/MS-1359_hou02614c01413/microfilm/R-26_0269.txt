The above formulae apply only to the part of the bridge where the live
load is, but we can make them apply to the other two parts between the extreme
suspenders by simply putting $w=0$. The formulae for these two parts will
differ from one another only in having different values for the
constants of integration. For the three parts there will be 12 such
constants. But at the point J, the equations for the live-load part
will involve $\eta_{J+s}$ and $\eta_{J+2s}$; and will therefore apply to these $\eta$s,
while for the equations for the part beyond J will involve and apply
to $\eta_{J}$ and $\eta_{J-s}$. There are thus four consecutive $\eta$s to which
which both formulae apply. Hence the values of $\eta_{J}$, $D \eta_{J}$, $D^{2} \eta_{J}$, and
$D^{3} \eta_{J}$ are given equally by either set of formulae; and we
thus have four equations between the integration-constants for
the two parts. A similar state of things exists at I; so that there
are 8 equations thus given between the 12 constants, leaving
but 4 independent unknowns. These four must be determined
by the conditions at $\pm N$. Now we have treated the truss as
cut off at these points, representing the effect of the
unsuspended parts by the force and couple whose
expressions involve $\mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{I}$. Hence the
differential coefficient of the bending energy relating
to $\eta_{N}$ is
$\frac{\mathcal{B}}{s^{3}} (\eta_{N} - 2 \eta_{N-s} + \eta_{N-2s})$
and that relative to $\eta_{N-s}$ is
$\frac{\mathcal{B}}{s^{3}} [-2(\eta_{N} - 2 \eta_{N-s} + \eta_{N-2s}) + (\eta_{N-s} - 2 \eta_{N-2s} + \eta_{N-3s})]$
