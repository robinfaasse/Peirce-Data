As long as the truss continues to rest on all four of its supports and is
not lifted from any of them by the live load, we can, in calculating
the deflections caused by the live load, imagine the whole structure to be
weightless and unstrained. For elastic energies are proportional
to squares of linear displacements, which displacements, as long as they are
very small, are proportional to vertical displacements of the parts of the
live load. Now it is true that the cable and truss really have enormous
weight and are under large strains. But the dead load and the elastic
stresses being in equilibrium, any possible further deformation (consistent
with the fixity of the four bearings of the truss) which is very small will
develop only a force of restitution very much smaller. Consider, then,
any particular kind of deformation, consisting in certain
linear displacements, all proportional to one another. Let $s$ be a
linear parameter measuring the deformation. The vertical displace-
ments of the centres of gravity of the dead load and live load, respectively,
will be two functions of $s$, which we may denote by $F's$ and $f's$ respectively.
Three functions will have their second derivatives very small. There
will be just as many such kinds of deformation as there are degrees of
freedom in the structure. Then, if $W$ and $w$ are the weights of the
dead and live load, the energy will be with the dead load only
$$W \Sigma_i F'_i s_i + \frac{1}{2} \Sigma_i E_i s_i^2$$
and there will be as many equations as degrees of freedom all of the form
$$W F'_i + E s = 0$$
so that
$$s = - \frac{W}{E} F'_i.$$
