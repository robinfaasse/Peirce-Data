[u]Practical X Ray Work[/u]. By Frank T. Addyman. London:
Scott, Greenwood & Co. New York: The Van Nostrand Co. 1901. 8[sup]vo[/sup]. pp.
207. With eleven twelve photographic plates and 52 cuts.

This book deserves mention in a literary journal as
being really well-written and put together with some skill.
It treats amply of every part of the art of using the X Rays,
without being swamped by inutilites. As a curious example
of how imperfect the art still is, it may be mentioned that
the author of this handbook has a piece of glass lodged in
his thumb which he has never been able to see either on the
screen or in a radiograph, although he has repeatedly located
pieces of glass in the bodies of patients by these means.
