$$\begin{array}{rcccl}
A & + & B & + & \Gamma & + & \Delta & + & \cdots & - & N & = & S_{0} \\
A\alpha & + & B\beta & + & \Gamma\gamma & + & \Delta\delta & + & \cdots & - & N\nu & = & S_{1} \\
A\alpha^{2} & + & B\beta^{2} & + & \Gamma\gamma^{2} & + & \Delta\delta^{2} & + & \cdots & - & N\nu^{2} & = & S_{2} \\
\multicolumn{13}{c}{\text{---------------------------------}} \\
A\alpha^{n-1} & + & B\beta^{n-1} & + & \Gamma\gamma^{n-1} & + & \Delta\delta^{n-1} & + & \cdots & - & N\nu^{n-1} & = & S_{n-1}
\end{array}$$

Now according to the rule, $r$ being any number, from $n$ consecutive values of the $S$'s, we calculate one more, either $S_{r}$ or $S_{r+n}$, from the equation

$$a_{0} S_{r+n} + a_{1} S_{r+n-1} + a_{2} S_{r+n-2} + \cdots + a_{n} S_{r} = 0,$$

where the coefficients $a_{0}, a_{1}, \dots a_{n}$, are those of the equation to be solved,

$$a_{0} x^{n} + a_{1} x^{n-1} + a_{2} x^{n-2} + \cdots + a_{n} x^{0} = 0.$$

The second principle is that [del]the[/del] [ins]any[/ins] roots of this equation satisfies every equation of the form

$$a_{0} x^{r+n} + a_{1} x^{r+n-1} + \cdots + a_{n} x^{r} = 0,$$

where $r$ is a positive or negative integer.
