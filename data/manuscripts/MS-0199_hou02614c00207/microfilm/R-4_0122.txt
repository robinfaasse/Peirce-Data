D.62[/pn] [u]Example 2[/u]. Required both roots of the equation $x^{10} - 101x + 101 = 0$.

Since the discriminant $(101)^{10} - 101\cdot 100 = 11001 - 10100$ is positive, the roots are real. Since the last term is positive, they have the same sign; and since the second term is negative, that sign is that of plus. The sum of the roots is five. Comparing the different values of the product $\xi(101-\xi)$ in the following table, we see that the ratio of the greater root to the lesser one is not much more than that of [del]11[/del] [ins]11.1[/ins] to [del]1.1[/del] [ins]1.11[/ins], say, about 1000 to 11.
