[u]Example 2.[/u] Requires both roots of the equation $x^{10} - 101 x + 10 = 0$.

I intend to treat this in such a manner as to illustrate a certain point
about the method. Since the two roots, evidently both positive, are roughly
in the ratio of [del]3 to 8[/del] [ins]11 to 1000 (tentatively)[/ins], the equation ought to be transformed so as to make the
ratio of
their moduli much more extreme. We may put [del]$x = \frac{y}{2}$ when $\frac{y^{10}}{1024} - \frac{101}{2} y + 10 = 0$[/del]

$x = y + 11$, when $x^2 = y^2 + 22y + 121$, and the equation will be $\frac{y^{10} - 19y}{101} = 1$. The
problem

power of five by a circulant of five times as many places as the next lower
power; and in fact five is no [del]exception to this general rule, though[/del]
