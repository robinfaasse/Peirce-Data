Subtraction can be variously performed. The method of the arithmetical complement is
explained in every good school arithmetic; for an arithmetic-book that did not explain it would
not be a good one. [del]They are[/del] [ins]Rare[/ins] [del]rare[/del], good arithmetics are. The one I learned from, by Pliny Earle Chase did not
reach a second edition, because it made the scholar think, and, what was worse, made the teacher think
too. Oh, American pedagogy! It is the art of teaching what you don't know. The Newtonian books are
little better. [del]When I[/del] [ins]myself[/ins] come to consider division I shall be forced to give some account of the arithmetical
complement; but whether I shall earn the prizes of being pronounced obscure by the American
[fig]black circle[/fig] teacher, who, I believe, holds a diploma guaranteeing that he or she has never been guilty of
thinking, and probably never will be, — [del]Yet, mind, I[/del] [ins]sincerely[/ins] [del]much admire the arts of the teachers, would they only[/del]
put a little intellect into it, — or would they only not lay a prohibition upon all real thinking.

One great advantage of the arithmetical complement is that it affords a simply
way of summing a collection of numbers of which, while most are of one sign,
several carry the opposite sign.
