The purpose of this Essay is, First, to show that there are three entirely different ways of passing from [del]Premisses[/del] [ins]Premiss[/ins] to Conclusion*;
[Foot-note: * I say from [u]Premiss[/u] to Conclusion, instead of from [u]Premisses[/u] to Conclusion, simply because if a Reasoning is based on more than one Premiss, I consider them as forming together a Copulative Proposition, which is the true Premiss of the Reasoning. A Copulative Proposition is a [ins]Hypothetical[/ins] Proposition (i.e. a Proposition composed of other Propositions, [fig]figure[/fig] termed its Members) whose Members are united as if by the conjunction "and", so that [del]each[/del] each is asserted when the Proposition is asserted. This is the Scholastic nomenclature, which I think ought to be followed when there is no scientific reason for not doing so.
