[u]THE RATIONALE OF REASONING.[/u]

The purpose of this Essay is, First, to show that there are three entirely different ways of passing from Premisses to Conclusion[del]s[/del][ins]*[/ins];
[[ins]Foot-note:[/ins] * I say from [u]Premiss[/u] to Conclusion, instead of from [u]Premisses[/u] to Conclusion; simply because if a Reasoning is based on more than one Premiss, I consider them as forming together a Copulative Proposition, which is the true [del]Hypothetical[/del] Premise of the Reasoning. A Copulative Proposition is a [del]Hypothetical[/del] Proposition composed of other Propositions, termed its Members) whose Members are united as if by the conjunction "and"; so that [del]all[/del] each is asserted when the Proposition is asserted. This is the Scholastic nomenclature, which I think ought to be followed when there is no scientific reason for not doing so. I remark that most writers have a tendency to overlook the fact that "and" has a meaning of its own. I sup]
