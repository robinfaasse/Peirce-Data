DEPARTMENT OF PHILOSOPHY

[date]October 1, 1931[/date]

Professor Paul Weiss,
Harvard University,
Cambridge, Mass.

My dear Weiss:-

I have only just returned from Europe, and consequently have not had time as yet to look over the copy of your biographical article on Peirce, which you were good enough to send me. I shall write you again shortly about this matter.

I am sorry to say that I have nothing of any importance to send you regarding Peirce at Johns Hopkins. I only dropped in to some of his lectures, but was never a regular student of his, and was not prepared at that time, either by my dominant interests or my previous training, to appreciate the significance of his work. I could see that he was a very learned and original man, but never got further than that. On the intellectual side I have the recollection - though unduly vague - of excursions of his into psychology in which he stated something which, as it now comes back to me, was practically identical with James' stream of consciousness.

As you doubtless know, Jastrow did have direct contact with Peirce, and I presume you have already communicated with him.

Sincerely yours,

John Dewey
