FOUNDED BY JOHN D. ROCKEFELLER

Chicago, Dec. 23 1903.

Mr. C. S. Peirce,
6 Prescott Hall,
Cambridge, Mass.

My dear Mr. Peirce:-

Mr. Moore has called my attention to your wish to have a copy of the Studies in Logical Theory. I understand that a copy sent to The Nation would come to you. A copy was sent by the publishers, the University Press, soon after the book came out, in October. If you can not through The Nation get in possession of this copy I shall be glad to have steps taken to send you a copy direct. I should do so in any case were it not that the editorial supply is so limited that I have to purchase all further copies myself.

I am asking the Press to send you a copy of another document of mine. I had your name on my previous list, the copies for which were sent out during the summer in my absence, and it is possible that this may duplicate what you already have. In this monograph I refer incidentally to the apparent similarity between the point I was making and your Monist article. I should have made my acknowledgment of indebtedness much stronger than I did, were it not that I got at the point of view in question by such a different route, I have to confess that I did not fully get the force of your position until I had worked out a somewhat similar one myself.
