[u]Chapter V.[/u]

[u]That the significance of thought lies in its reference to the future.[/u]

In every logical mind there must be 1[ins]st[/ins], ideas; 2[ins]d[/ins], general rules according to which one idea determines another, or [del]habitual connections[/del] [ins]habits of mind[/ins] [del]mind-connec-[/del] of ideas; and, 3[ins]d[/ins], processes whereby such habitual connections are established.

A belief is an habitual connection of ideas. For example, to say that I believe prussic acid is a poison is to say that when the idea of drinking it occurs to me, the idea of it as a poison with all the other ideas which follow in the train of this will arise in my mind.
