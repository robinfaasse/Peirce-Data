Prag. 24 form of the following rule for attaining clearness of
thought:
"Consider what effects that [u]might[/u] [u]conceivably[/u] have practical
bearings you conceive the object of your conception to have.
Then conception of these effects is the whole of your conception
of the object."
When I came to apply this rule to the concepts of philosophy,
I should certainly, — but for the inklings I had derived from
my studies of Kant and of Berkeley, — have been amazed
to find what a considerable majority of the hard knots of
[del]metaphysics[/del] no sooner receive[ins]d[/ins] their pragmatistic interpre-
tations by the application of this rule than they fall apart
at the lightest touch.
