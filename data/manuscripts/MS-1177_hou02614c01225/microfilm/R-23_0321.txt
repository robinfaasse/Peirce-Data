Logic was, in this sense, formerly, termed a
directive science; and certainly the word 'normative'
is not irresistibly charming. However, there is such
a verb as [u]norme[/u], to square, in the dictionary; and the
new adjective has the advantage of not seeming to
imply that logic is a merely practical science.
A normative science may be defined as the theory
of the facultative conditions under which a
desirable result may be brought about. It neces-
sarily has practical [ins]mainly[/ins] applications; but it is not
necessarily studied with reference to these, or with
a view to the economies of the problem.
C. S. Peirce
