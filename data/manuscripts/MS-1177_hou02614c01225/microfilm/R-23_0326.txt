Latin, English, & French have the two words
[u]liquid[/u] & [u]fluid[/u], which are carefully distinguished.
German has but one & considerable confusion
results.

A [u]fluid[/u] is any body which does not statically
resist a force tending to bend it. Fluids are
either [u]elastic fluids[/u] or gaseous bodies and vapours,
or [u]inelastic fluids[/u], that is, fluids not tending
to expand without separation into two parts,
that is, [u]liquids[/u]. A liquid has an upper surface,
provided the pressure is not too great, this surface,
separating the fluid into two parts one elastic,
the other inelastic. The lower part is [u]liquid[/u],
the upper part vapor. Above the critical point
the liquid cannot exist. If a liquid is subjected
to such a pressure that it has no upper surface,
its distinction from a vapor is potential, only, so
to say; and it may pass into the gaseous state
without any sharp line of demarcation being apparent.
