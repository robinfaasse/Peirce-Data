I.

Human life is made up of experiences, of actions, and of thoughts. Philosophers differ whether the purpose of life ought to be conceived as the attainment of satisfactory experiences, of satisfactory performances, or satisfactory thought; and in [del]this[/del] [ins]this[/ins] regard to this question all men are philosophers. Indeed, mankind may be divided into three bodies consisting of those for whom experience is the great thing (the majority of men), those for whom achievement is the great thing, and these for whom knowledge is the great thing. Although this classification is based on a great fact, the three classes melt into one another somewhat. There will be three corresponding classes of human vocations, of which the third (or its most important subdivision) will be the vocations of science.
