My next quotation will be from the work which is the [del]Aristotelian[/del] [ins]Aristotelian[/ins] collected works follows after the Organon, that is the Φυσικῆς Ἀκροάσεως, or [u]Lectures on Physics[/u]. The first book of these lectures is occupied with the question What are to be the principles upon which physical phenomena are to be explained. The First Book has 9 chapters. The 1[sup]st[/sup] is short and introductory, He begins by remarking that the question is how to proceed from things better known to us, to things better known γνωριμώτερα by nature and σαφέστερα more manifest. He also says it is necessary to proceed from the general to particulars: ἐκ τῶν καθόλου ἐπὶ τὰ καθ' ἕκαστα δεῖ προϊέναι. (184 a 23) For this he says is the way in which the mind of children developes. He does not discuss the question more seriously [ins]evidently[/ins] because there was nobody to urge the opposite view. This is a pity.
