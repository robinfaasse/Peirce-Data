How to Reason —
to read. I have read all
these pages with great ad-
miration — I do not say
with full assent, for I differ
somewhat in my views —
but with wonder at your
learning, industry, and native
ability. Your distinction of
icons, indices, and symbols,
as three classes of signs, is
a masterly piece of work.
I want the whole in print
without delay — if I could
get it!

I get gravelled on one
point. If we never know
what goes on in conscious-
ness except as a memory
of the event, what do we
know? The reminiscence
itself is an event in con-

sciousness — we must know only a [del]memory[/del]
[ins]of the[/ins] memory, only a memory of that,
and so on. That is, we know nothing.
I don't think you can get away from
acknowledging that, if you know anything
immediately (e.g. the medium by which
you know mediately), you must know
what goes on in consciousness at the time.

Nevertheless, you must go your own
thought-way, and I do not cavil at a
difference of results. On the contrary, I
delight to recognize the genius and origi-
nality of it. I wish I could be of some
real help to you. Faithfully, your old friend,
F. E. Abbot.
