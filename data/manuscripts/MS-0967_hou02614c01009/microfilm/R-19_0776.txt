The doctrine opposed to nominalism has for many
centuries been called [u]realism[/u]. The word comes from
[u]realis[/u], real, from [u]res[/u] a thing. [u]Realis[/u], then, is thingy;
and the realist is he that holds that laws and types
are thingy not merely nominal, or namy. The
word [u]realis[/u], though ancient, came into ordinary use
in its now ordinary sense, in consequence of its
use in the discussion about universals. The word
[u]reality[/u], now so familiar, was at first a technical
term of realistic metaphysics. So there can be no
doubt that the word realism has been preempted
ages ago to mean the doctrine opposed to nominalism.
It is therefore a gross abuse to use it now as it
is often used to mean that spirit of art which is opposed
to idealization. In its proper meaning [u]realism[/u] is a
kind of [u]idealism[/u]. It is the doctrine that ideas play a
part in the real world; and it naturally allies itself [del]most[/del]
with an idealistic art. The opposite tendency in art
would much better be called experientialism, because
it seeks to check in a measure the riot of the imagina-
tion & bring [del]the[/del] artists down to sober representation
of ordinary experience. So much for the word realism.
