between the soul & the body. To show that this is a me-
taphysical distinction: the soul is that which moves it-
self, the body is that which moves but does not move
itself. Now when I say that the soul moves itself, I
do not mean that it originates any force, but that
a certain amount of force which is in it always
moves in it & never out from it [ins]⁕[/ins], so that every force
passing through it is subject to [del]the[/del] modification
by a force within it & belonging to it. In this respect
it is that the soul differs from the body, that in the
body every force which is at one instant in one atom at the
next instant is in the next atom, whence forces in bodies
are always in motion; all force however must manifest
itself in change of some kind, & it is only forces in
substances that make those substances manifest; hence,
the soul is that which is manifested out of space.
the body is that which is only manifested in space —
obviously a metaphysical distinction.

⁕ As Plato explains in one word in Phaedrus, where he calls
the body τὸ ἄλλο κινοῦν καὶ ὑπ’ ἄλλου κινούμενον
but the soul τὸ αὑτὸ κινοῦν, ἅτε οὐκ ἀπολεῖπον
ἑαυτό, οὔ ποτε λήγει κινούμενον — that which moving
itself, never ceases to be moved, since it does not
abandon itself.
