[u]Dogmatism[/u]. The two other classes of dialecticians have [ins]now[/ins] been reduced to dogmatists. An abstraction such as the perfect which contains a statement of its own [del]existence[/del] is a proposition, an axiom. A reasoning power which shall transcend its experience must do so by virtue of some other premises which is an axiom. They are, in fact, dogmatists who turn aside for the [del]very[/del] purpose of being assailed by skeptics. The objection is just the same for all, What right have we to assume these axioms?

I have thus shown that we can have no dialectics [del]except that which has been[/del] until we have had a Transcendental Philosophy.

[u]On the probability of Error[/u].
Probability is measured by the ratio of the possibilities of its being true to all possibilities. For instance, from an urn containing 100 balls 30 of which are red & the rest white, [del]the in[/del] one being taken out there are 30 possibilities of its being red & 100 is the whole number of possibilities & [fraction]30/100[/fraction] is the probability of its being red. Let [u]w[/u] be the whole number of possibilities, in any case, & [u]e[/u] the number of possibilities of the particular event we consider the probability of. [del]Since[/del] [u]e[/u] can never be greater than [u]w[/u] & hence the probability ([del]=[/del] [ins]or[/ins] [u]p[/u]) can never be greater than 1. In case of impossibility [u]e[/u] = 0 & [u]p[/u] = [fraction]0/w[/fraction] = 0. In case there is just as much probability of the events happening as of its not happening [del]e = w - e[/del] [ins]e = [fraction]1/2[/fraction] w[/ins] & [u]p[/u] = [fraction]1/2[/fraction].
