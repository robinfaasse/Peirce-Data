Chapter II
On the Insufficiency of Dialectics
§ 1. [del]Of the Position of the Dialectician[/del].

The former chapter attempted a developement of the true idea of metaphysics. The triplicity of the conception and the impossibility of achieving the synthesis of it creates naturally three [del]three[/del] [ins]true[/ins] [del]genuine[/del] schools of metaphysics; the dogmatical, the psychological, and the logical schools. [del]Each of these[/del] The procedure of each of these, depending as it does on a partial [del]conception[/del], is liable to abuse, although that must [del]be[/del] in reality be contrary to the normal mode of the very view assumed. I shall designate these errors as follows
The abuse of dogmatism I call dialectics
— — psychology — transcendentalism
— — logic — rationalism

To the two former I shall devote one chapter each of this introduction; but as I myself belong to the logical school, Rationalism must be the constant under-topic of Book I, & need not be specially considered here.

When the notion that metaphysics is philosophy antecedent to all other science (which is, [ins]indeed[/ins] based upon it) is carried so far as to produce a denial that is the science of our own consciousness merely, and also that it adds nothing to our knowledge but only analyzes it, this notion produces [u]dialectics[/u], that is to say, a system which seeks to investigate truth by elaborate reasoning upon first principles.
