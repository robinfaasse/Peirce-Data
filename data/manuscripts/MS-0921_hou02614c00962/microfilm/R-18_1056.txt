Chapter II. Nature of the Perfect.

§1. Composition of Abstractions

[del]Prop.[/del] There are [del]simple[/del] [ins]elementary[/ins] conceptions.

[u]Proof[/u] Everything is either [del]simple[/del] [ins]simple[/ins] or composed of parts
[del]The parts of conceptions[/del]
[del]A complex conception must be conceived as such[/del]
A conception is simple or complex according as it is composed of other conceptions or not
A simple conception is an elementary one.
[triple_dot_therefore] Every conception is either elementary or composed of elementary conceptions.

[u]Proposition[/u] Every conception is of boundless complication in its own nature.
[u]Proof[/u]. The only ways in which two thoughts can be compounded is [del]by[/del]
1 by being both thought at the same time
2 by one's being a thought of the other.
3 by both being thought [del]through[/del] [ins]by[/ins] a third
[del]If[/del] In the last case the third thought thinks of the others in the second mode. It can only think of two through their being compounded [triple_dot_therefore] the 3[sup]d[/sup] way is reduced to the first & second.
