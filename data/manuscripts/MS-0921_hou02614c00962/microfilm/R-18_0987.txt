The Orders of Mathematical Infinity.
[date]1860 July 13[/date].

It now becomes perfectly easy to remove the objection which per-
haps was felt against my making Mathematical & Theological
Infinity the same, because the former having orders would seem
either not to be infinity at all or at least a very different
kind of infinity from the latter. For it can now be seen how
this idea of order of infinity arises in mathematics without
giving infinity any different definition from that which it has
in the Category, which I showed in the essay just referred to
was the definition it had in theology - both Natural & Rational.

In mathematics then the term order, applied to infinity, implies
that infinite quantities are not all equally large & that one may
even be infinitely larger than [del]the[/del] [ins]the[/ins] other. The reasoning by which
these conclusions are reached is as follows

Since infinite extensive quantities are still extensive quantities,
whatever is true of extensive quantities in general is true of [del]those[/del]
that are infinite, so that

$1^{\text{st}}$ Since if $a$ & $b$ are extensive quantities & if $b > 0$, $b + a > a$
it follows that since $\infty > 0$ we may make $a = \infty_{\text{No 1}}$, $b = \infty_{\text{No 2}}$ & then
$\infty_{\text{No 1}} + \infty_{\text{No 2}} > \infty_{\text{No 1}}$; but from the nature of infinity ([del]that it[/del]
[del]is beyond degree[/del] [ins]Perfect in degree[/ins]) $\infty_{\text{No 1}} + \infty_{\text{No 2}} = \infty_{\text{No 3}}$ $\therefore \infty_{\text{No 3}} > \infty_{\text{No 1}}$, or all
infinity is not equal.

$2^{\text{nd}}$ In the same way from the formulae $a > b$, & $i^a : i^b = i^{a-b} : 1$
we deduce $\frac{\infty_{\text{No 1}}}{\infty_{\text{No 2}}} = \infty_{\text{No 3}}$

All which reasoning is in accordance with the principles of
the last essay.
