[u]The Fundamental Distinction of Metaphysics.[/u]

It is usually conceded or assumed that all me-
taphysics is based on some fundamental distinction.
The fact is that every new system of metaphysics has some
particular merit, which is based on some particular
distinction. The distinction, for instance, upon which my
system is based, obviously is between the thought & the thought-
of, or rather between the thinkable & the thinkable of = A.

Above these partial views, however, it is obvious that
the possibility & the utility of philosophy in general & of every
science in particular must be based upon a metaphysi-
cal distinction.

Thus, the possibility & the utility of philosophy in general,
that is of the application of the understanding to ex-
perience, depends on our having besides the facts of
experience, certain universal [u]a priori[/u] truths on objects
of experience; that is it depends on the distinction be-
tween sense & reason; what this distinction is I dont
stop to inquire.
