[u]Summary[/u]. [date]1880 December 16.[/date]

The foregoing Essays contain an explanation of my conception of metaphysics.

1 What it deals with — namely our thoughts
2 How it deals with them.
a. It regards them as [del]thoughts of things thought of[/del] [ins]phenomena[/ins].
b. It does not collect them or elucidate them but takes so much for granted but
[del]c. Investigation into the nature[/del] It [del]defines and analyzes them and shows their relations[/del] [ins]treats them purely à priori[/ins].

As to how this is to be done.

1[u]st[/u] with regard to investigations into the relations of the thought to the thought-of these must either be investigations into the correspondence of the thought and thought-of which I hold to be unnecessary, or examinations into the psychological processes, which [ins]except[/ins] so far as [ins]the results[/ins] [del]I hold[/del] [ins]they[/ins] relate to [ins]the[/ins] objective validity of thoughts, may also be obtained by analysis

2[u]nd[/u] with regard to [del]investigations[/del] [ins]searches[/ins] into the relations between thought and the unthought-of, I hold such to be impossible.

Accordingly I reduce metaphysics to the Analysis of Conceptions. The Perfect is the special subject herein [del]to be considered[/del].
