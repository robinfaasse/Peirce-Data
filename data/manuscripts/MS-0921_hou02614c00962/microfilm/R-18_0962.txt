Sir William Hamilton's [date]1854 July 27.[/date]
[u]Theory of the Infinite.[/u]

The whole theory of the Unconditioned falls with that of the infinite.

I admit with [del]Sir W. H.[/del] [ins]him[/ins] that we have no [ins]positive[/ins] conception of the infinite which is in a certain sense [del]but the[/del] negation of limitation.

Hamilton, however, says that no special faculty gives us the infinite & that we have no special idea of it. [del]For[/del], he says, the infinite is the mere negation of limitation & the knowledge of contraries is one. Besides, he says, infinite expresses a mere inability to think. Moreover does he not say [del]that[/del] the infinite — neither infinite wholes nor infinite parts — never manifests itself to us? (He admits, I am aware, that finite parts of the infinite may manifest themselves.)

These three propositions I controvert.
