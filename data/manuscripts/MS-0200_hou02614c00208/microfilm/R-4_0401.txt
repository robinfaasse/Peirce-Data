E 84[/pn] A "class" is an ens rationis differently related to its singulars
from the relation of a "kind" to its singulars. The singulars are
simply determinations of the kind, which retains its whole Being,
whether the singulars exist or not. [del]The Being of[/del] [ins]The essence of[/ins] a
class consists [ins]in two conditions, first,[/ins] in its being in a certain transitive relation to sin-
gulars, called "containing" them; and secondly, in its being so
related to a certain general character that it contains at any time
all the singulars that at that time possess that character, and
that it contains nothing else.
