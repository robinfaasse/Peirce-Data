Like other things of a general nature, it has two orders of Beings, [del]an essence and an existence.[/del] [ins]the essence of a thing [del]do[/del] consists in its being rendered intelligible by being regarded from a certain general point of view. This gives it an intellectual nutritiousness. For instance, I may be shown a [del]piece of machinery,[/del] [ins]complicated[/ins] [del]and[/del] while recognizing how many parts of it move or may move together, I may be utterly at a loss to understand it, or to discern what kind of a [ins]machine[/ins] thing it is until some body tells me for what purpose it is intended to subserve. Then I see. That [del]I[/del] see gives it a function, an office in the world. But this essence does no more than determine the generic mode of being of the thing.
