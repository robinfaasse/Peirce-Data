It might be objected that perhaps the chair vacated at the table
will not at once be filled, so that the banker is deprived of the
opportunity to recoup himself for a loss he must have sustained, if
the player goes out at the moment of a net gain. If there is [del]an[/del]
a percentage of chance in favor of the bank, there is this much
force in this objection, that it is for the banker's interest that there
should be no vacant places round the table. But if the game is even
the opportunity to recoup his losses is of no value, since he is just as
likely to lose as to gain. There is a widely current notion that chance
so acts that future results balance past results. But that phenome-
non would be the phenomenon which is described by saying that the past
influences the future. It is like a storm which only lasts a few days, be-
cause it spends itself. What we mean by chance is a large collection of
[del]its[/del] facts which do not influence one another.
