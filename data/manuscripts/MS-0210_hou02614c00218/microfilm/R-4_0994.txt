No. 1.

Should the [del]head of the house of[/del] Rothschild, during a sojourn at Monaco, take it into his head to earn one franc at roulette each day, he could not prudently prolong his visit into the third week. For supposing the odds on any bet are as 30 in favor of the player to 31 in favor of the bank, the odds of M. de Rothschild gaining his first day's franc would only be as 30 to 1. To find the chance of his holding out for 3 weeks, or 21 days, we must raise 30/31 to the 21[ins]st[/ins] power, which [del]is[/del] [ins]gives[/ins] 0.5023; [del]and[/del] [ins]so[/ins] that the odds in his favor are only as 5023 to 4977; [del]a[/del] [ins]that is, it is[/ins] very near an even bet that, if he could crowd so many one franc betts into a single day, on some one of those 21 days he would rise from the table a ruined man.

As long as his plan was to bet one franc each time, it would make very little difference how rich he was. The man with a fortune of only 100 francs to draw upon would be nearly as advantageously situated in regard to the betting. The odds of his [del]gaining[/del] [ins]netting a gain of[/ins] 1 franc is 29 to 1; and the odds in favor of his gaining 21 francs are 4833 to 4977, numbers differing little from those for Rothschild. But the case is far otherwise if a martingale be played, that is, if 1 franc be bet and, in the event of its loss, the bett be doubled every time, until the first winning gives a net gain of 1 franc.
