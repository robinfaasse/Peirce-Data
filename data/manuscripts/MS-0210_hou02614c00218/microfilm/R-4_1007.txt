But [del]a man's[/del] [ins]the player's[/ins] fortune makes a great difference if he is permitted to
play a "martingale", that is, to bet 1 franc at first, and to double his
bet after every loss, so that his first winning shall make him a
net winner of 1 franc. Were a man with 1023 francs to play in this
way, the odds against him at each bet being as 31 to 30, then the odds
in favor of his winning a franc would be as 869 to 1. That is, out of
every 870 such players, 869 would in the long run win each his 1
franc, while the 870[ins]th[/ins] would lose his entire fortune of 1023 francs,
leaving a profit to the bank of 154 francs on every 870 such players
or 18 centimes per player. But if the player's fortune, instead of being
