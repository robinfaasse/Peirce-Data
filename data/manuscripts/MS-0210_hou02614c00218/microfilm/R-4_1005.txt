is a definite conception concerning which, with due care, we can
reason without falling into any contradiction. In order to define
an infinite collection, it is best to imagine arrows, or lines with their
two ends unlike, and having no branches, but capable of being bent
into any form. Then an infinite collection is a collection such that
between its units arrows may exist, no two arrows pointing to the
same unit, but with at least one arrow pointing from each unit, and
yet with some units to which no arrows point. Thus, take for example the
