Comments on the language of these 5 verses.

[u]ang[/u] is an article neither definite nor indefinite.

[u]n[/u] in [u]nang[/u] is the sign of a word governed. As there is little or no distinction between a noun and a verb, it makes no difference whether the nang is governed by [ins]the[/ins] one, making a genetive, or by the other, making an accusative. But proper names [del]are[/del] [ins]form a[/ins] distinct part of speech and when governed take [u]ni[/u] as in the first verse, or [u]si[/u] [del]who[/del] in case the [del]proper name[/del] [ins]noun[/ins] is the agent, as in the fourth verse, or [u]cay[/u] when proper name is a secondary object, as in the title.

[u]ayon[/u] I have translated "according to"; but I suspect it is a pronoun.

It is evident that reliance is placed on pronouns to show that words are to be taken as nouns; and this is the function of the articles.

[u]Pasimulá[/u] plainly means 'beginning'. The root I suppose is [u]simul[/u]. I don't know what that means. But [u]pa[/u] is a common prefix signifying tendency toward, or causality, etc. Most of the roots a dissyllabic, and letters are inserted into them in Shemitic fashion.

[u]Pagcásulat[/u]. [u]Sulát[/u] is the root, meaning write. [u]Pagca[/u] is a prefix meaning the substance or essence.

[u]sa[/u] This [del]s[/del] seems to have a locative value.

[u]manghúhúla[/u]. Reduplication of the first syllable of the root signifying manifold action or futurity is common. [u]Mang[/u] is a common prefix signifying multitude or magnitude etc. etc.

[u]pinadadala[/u] is a passive, the root apparently being [u]padal[/u]. I don't know what it means. Perhaps 'send'? The reduplication means I think futurity. "Will be sent". There is a similar formation in verse 4. "ipinangangaral". Perhaps the [u]p[/u] does not belong to the root. But the latter may have a quadriliteral root.
