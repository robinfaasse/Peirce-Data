[u]unahán[/u] is like [u]harapán[/u] in the same verse. It is a sort
of passive, where the agent is the place or person to whom.
The roots appear to be [del]ma[/del] unah and harap. There
seems to be no objection to a preposition being put in the passive.

[u]Sumisigáo[/u]. I take it the root is sigáo. The m inserted in the
first syllable seems to give an ideal [del]cl[/del] or non-assertory
character to a verb.

[u]Thandâ[/u] I suppose this is a passive the root being hand or
something.

see what. 'Εγένετο Ιωάννης is the Greek. Fuit Joannes the
Vulgate. I think the [u]i[/u] is a relative pronoun.

[u]Pagsisisi[/u], I suppose pag is a prefix marking a verbal noun,
