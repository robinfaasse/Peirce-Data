The observations of Tycho in Uranienburg
occupied 15 years from 1582 to 1598. After
the death of Fredrich II he suffered great
inconveniences, and finally the learned though
not very wise Emperor Rudolph II invited
him to [del]Prague[/del] [del]Austria[/del] [del]Bohemia[/del] Germany.

Tycho was a marvellous skillful and
perfectly indefatigable observer. But he
was not very much of a mathematician
and still less a philosopher.

But Johann Keppler it was who discovered
the form of the planets' paths in coursing round
the sun, and the law of their varying speed.
