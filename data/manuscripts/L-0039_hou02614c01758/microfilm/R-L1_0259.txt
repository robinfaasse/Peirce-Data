My dear Charles.-

The applications of analytical mechanics to geology pure and simple are not numerous, so far as I know. I enclose citations of 4 memoirs by William Hopkins and Darwin. A.Langel wrote a short paper in Comptes Rendus vol. 40, 1855, page 978. Another paper by the same man in Bull.Soc. Geol. vol. 12 1854-5, page 363, states results, but gives no analysis.

I give you no references to Forbes, Hopkins and others on glacial motion, a subject I have never yet earnestly tackled, and I dont' need to mention such matters as are embraced in Pratt's Figure of the earth. On the subject of the earth's rigidity I wrote a paper which reaches Kelvin's results to all intents and purposes, but by an extremely simple method. The fundamental proposition is that the ellipticity of a tidally strained sphere is necessarily greater than if the strain were homogeneous. I compute the deformation of the homogeneous sphere and show that it must be more rigid than glass to account for semidiurnal tides of considerable amplitude (Amer. Jour.
