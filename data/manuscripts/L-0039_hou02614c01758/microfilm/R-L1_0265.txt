Considering viscosity merely as a resistance which [del]diminishes[/del] [ins]diminishes[/ins] with increasing time, it must reinforce the rigidity more effectually during a brief period of strain than during a longer one. Hence, under the action of rotational stress, yielding will take place more readily on one system of planes of max. tangential strain than on the conjugate system.

That is my case.

Anent the constitution of matter, it would please me if you would read my remarks on "Relation of plastic solids to fluids" (Finite Hom. Strain Flow & Rupture pp.46-48) where I think I have shown that the rigidity of a fluid is not merely infinitesimal, as Stokes, Kelvin and others assume, but infinitesimal of the second order. Thus in my opinion there is no transition except a state of mixture.

If I manage to get a more permanent arrangement made with you, as I hope to do, the time paid for would have to be spent directly on geological questions. The amount of money which was formerly spent on the Survey for pure paleontology caused unpleasantness, and the policy now is to subordinate everything to geology. I hope however that it will give you a lift in the grosser part of your philosophy.
