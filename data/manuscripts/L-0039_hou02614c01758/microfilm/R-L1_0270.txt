Washington D.C.
[date]Mar 25. 1897[/date].

My dear Charles;

I have to thank you for your recent paper in "The Monist". I received from you the copy of my paper on homogeneous strain, with a letter, yesterday.

I am not annoyed with you, for it is impossible that under the circumstances you should wish to annoy me. I confess however that I think you are a trifle positive in your assertion of the valuelessness of my theory. You propose to me an investigation which is complete and ready for the press. I have made fresh examinations of thin sections of slate under the microscope. The cleavage of slate is by no means infinitely close but quite the contrary; the cleavage [del]planar[/del] surfaces are seldom at [ins]absolutely[/ins] microscopic distances from one another. The structure, whatever it is, affects only these surfaces and there is a limit beneath which slate can be split no more. I have made 15 experiments on the artificial production of cleavage, and I have solved the corresponding problem in heterogeneous, finite stress and strain.
