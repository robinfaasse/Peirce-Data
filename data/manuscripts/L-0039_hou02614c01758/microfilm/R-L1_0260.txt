You make the suggestion that Van Hise and others may simply have in mind cleavage perpendicular to the actively pressing plane. I took up this view on p. 83 of my memoir. Finite, Homogeneous Strain etc. and showed it [ins]to[/ins] be untenable So far as I can see Van Hise and Hopkins are even less logical. They suppose cleavage to be due to the flattening of particles and to arise in the plane of flattening. They don't explain how cleavage can arise from flatness, they don't see that in rotational strains the flattening is not perpendicular to the external pressure, they disregard viscosity, they fail to account for the "grain" of slate and for any distortion except in lines parallel to the force. Understand that these remarks are not criticism's , but a mere statement of the inadequacy of Sharpe's theory.
