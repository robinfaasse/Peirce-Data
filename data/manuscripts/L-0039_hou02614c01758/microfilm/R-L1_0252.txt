DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR
UNITED STATES GEOLOGICAL SURVEY

Washington D.C. [date]Jan 5, 1895[/date]

My dear Charles;

Your card gives me a shock. It reads as if that dear old man Bunsen were dead but, if so, I haven't heard of it.

I enclose you a copy of my little note on Bunsens discovery which was not, as you will see, Kirchhoff's law but the fact that metals give characteristic spectra. It is noteworthy that this discovery was made in 1844, or 13 years before Bunsen and Kirchhoff published their invention and 5 years before Stokes claimed to have lectured on the principles of spectrum analysis.

Bunsen's most striking characteristic to me [del]was[/del] was his unselfishness. He took the same interest in a pupil making his first qualitative analysis as in a post-graduate carrying out an original investigation. He once asked a pupil (now a famous chemist) to make two carbonic acid determinations for him. Some weeks later he brought the young man the MS of [del]the[/del] a paper in which these determinations were used, and [ins]incidentally[/ins] requested him to publish the inquiry as his own. This dishonest, altruistic offer was of course declined.

He practically spent [del]the[/del] [ins]the[/ins] entire day with his pupils, his own work being done at night and in the early morning.
