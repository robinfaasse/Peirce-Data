These special cases seem to prove that $y dy/x dx$ does not
vary with $x$ and $y$, however great the strain may be. In other
words $\sigma$ is a constant which expresses the ratio of infinitesimal
lateral contraction to infinitesimal longitudinal extension,
irrespective of the state of strain prior to the small change
of strain supposed.

Hence
$$x y^{\sigma} = x_{0} y_{0}^{\sigma}$$
is a perfectly general expression for the absolute motion of
any particle in an isotropic cube under tensile stress.

[u]Relations of distortion and dilation.[/u] Let $\alpha$ be the ratio of a
shear and $h$ the ratio of linear dilation, so that $h^{3}$ is
the volume of a strained unit cube. Then if such a
cube is stressed in the direction of $oy$,
$$x = \frac{h}{\alpha} \text{ and } y = \alpha^{2} h.$$
Substituting these values in $x y^{\sigma} = 1$
$$h^{\frac{1}{1-2\sigma}} = \alpha^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}};$$
and if $\sigma$ is expressed in terms of $n$ and $k$
$$h^{9k} = \alpha^{6n}$$
which shows that $h$ is the same function of $9k$ as $\alpha$ is of $6n$.
