For Mr Peirce's father I felt
great affection as well as intense
admiration. His last serious effort
was the preparation of a course of
lectures for the Lowell Institute
upon "Ideality", and there is no one
whom I should more cordially welcome
than his son.

I should be glad to receive
from him, should he accept this
invitation some idea of the subject
he would select, the number of

lectures he would prefer to give, and
the time of their delivery approximately,
upon all which points I should wish
to consult his choice.

As I cannot write to Mr Peirce
directly because I am ignorant of
his exact address, may I ask you
to convey to him this invitation, and
in rendering me this service, believe
me to be,

Yours very truly

Augustus Lowell.
