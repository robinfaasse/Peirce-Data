Confidential

New York, May 18, [date]1905[/date].

Dear Sir:

A conference was held in New York, May 3, [date]1905[/date], to consider a proposition received from Mr. Andrew Carnegie, to provide funds to promote the simplification of English spelling. Prof. Brander Matthews was elected chairman, and Dr. Charles P. G. Scott secretary.

Mr. Carnegie makes it a preliminary condition that a sufficient number of men of distinction shall agree to use in their personal correspondence twelve simplified spellings adopted by the National Educational Association in 1898, and now employed in the Educational Review, the Independent, and certain other periodicals of prominence. These twelve simplifications are program, catalog, decalog, prolog, demagog, pedagog, tho, altho, thoro, thorofare, thru, thruout.

With the funds supplied by Mr. Carnegie, it is proposed to organize a board, to engage a secretary, and to enter on an active campaign to win a wider acceptance for these simplifications and to arouse the interest of the public in the whole question of English orthography. The opportunity must be seized at once; and we appeal to you to lend your aid by agreeing to use the twelve simplifications in your _personal_ correspondence. The promise we ask you to give is not onerous. It does not extend to writings that are to appear in print. Even if you do not approve of all of these twelve simplifications, the sacrifice of prejudice is small, and the help you give to the cause, which is the main thing, is immediate.

We may add that the signers of this letter have agreed to do themselves what they ask you to do.

A form of promise is enclosed. Please sign it, and return it in the accompanying stamped envelop, addressed to the secretary of the conference.

BRANDER MATTHEWS (Chairman)
