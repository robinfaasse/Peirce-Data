With all the likeness between Mozart and Goethe, there was
this remarkable difference between them, that Mozart was not in-
fluenced by moods. They had no part whatever in [del]his[/del] [ins]the process of[/ins] artistic produc-
tion. Art was not for him the means of expression of his own
sensations as it was with Goethe; he could direct the play of
his fancy by outward associations and adapt them to motives
of the outer world; so that Mozart strongly reminds one of Schiller.
But he could set his fancy any problem he liked, and it would
promptly proceed to resolve it. Mozart could compose to order; and
many of his works, especially Canons, were produced in that
way. In consonance with this was Mozart's power of setting
foreign texts of of operas to music which corresponded precisely
to the character and mood of the words, although he did
not warm up to them.
