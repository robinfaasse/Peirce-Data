/ Fancy stands half-way between dreaming and a train of thought.
The latter depends directly on the will; while in the former the will
is in total abeyance. Purposive thought is like a ship with
a good rudder which follows every touch of the tiller and can
be carried through the narrowest straits. A dream is a
rudderless hulk, [ins]wandering about,[/ins] [del]at[/del] the play of the wind and waves. Fancy
is a disabled ship which with its sails set wends its way
over the deep, moving like a ghost with no visible im-
pulse, yet directed toward its destined port. The will is
not really inactive in fancy, but it confines itself chiefly
to removing hindrances which might confuse the thoughts and
prevent ideas from forming a harmonious whole.

All men exercise the above described action of fancy. In
ordinary men it makes day-dreams, which everybody recognizes
to be hostile to rational thought. All that fancy produces depends on
former impressions of sense. It is powerless to create anything new;
its products are mere combinations of the residua of memory.
They may be unlikely enough; and in that sense it may be true
that its dreams are "original." But this does not conflict with
the fact that it acts according to the simple laws of suggestion.
