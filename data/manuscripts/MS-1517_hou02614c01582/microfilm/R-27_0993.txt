Goethe says; "There is a sense in which it is true that [del]poets, and indeed all true artists, must be born, not made. Namely,[/del] there must be an inward productive power, to bring the images that linger in the organs, in the memory, in the imagination freely without purpose or will to life. Voltaire on seeing one of his tragedies performed, exclaimed: "Was it really I who wrote that?" Lamartine said: "It is not I who think, but my ideas which think for me." Of Werther Goethe said "Having written this little book somewhat unconsciously, like a sleepwalker, I [ins]was[/ins] surprised myself, in lately reading it over, to find that there was something to alter and improve." Schiller writes to Körner: "It is not well in works of creation that reason should too closely challenge the ideas that come thronging to the doors. Taken by itself an idea may be highly unsuitable, even superstitious, and yet in conjunction with others, themselves equally absurd alone, it may furnish a suitable link in the chain of thought. Reason cannot see this [del]until[/del] unless it carefully considers the [del]ideas[/del] in their connection. In a creative brain, believe me, ideas would crowd in pell mell, were reason to withdraw her watch at the doors."
