[del]this side[/del] [ins]They[/ins] will have qualities of moods that there are no words to
express. Just as the musically gifted can distinguish timbres that
ordinary people cannot, so born artists have visceral sensations,
or moods, for which the average man is, as it were, color-blind,
and for which language has no names. Alfieri's "soft fever" and
Schiller's "a certain musical mood of heart" are obviously attempts
to convey some notion of such refined sensation of the viscera.

Another trait remarkable in famous poets is an instinctive
and invincible impulse to express the ideas and feelings within them.
In consequence of this impulse the work of genius is not a voluntary [ins]labor[/ins] [del]task[/del]
but the involuntary product of a psychical need. It is not a [del]han-[/del]
[del]kering[/del] after applause and success, nor a regard for his interests,
which conduces the man of genius to perform his task. It is solely a
[del]to[/del] passion to give shape and a "local habitation" to the idea that
lives in his mind. The true poet does not versify because he would,
but because he must. Goethe has painted the poetic impulse in Tasso.
