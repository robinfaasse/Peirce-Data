[del]Yet I must go on to the world of earth,[/del]
With thee I can become only a slave;
I hanker for freedom,
For freedom, freedom I am athirst;
In battle and combat I am ready to stand,
Be it even to death and overthrow! —
Therefore I must flee thy realm
O queen, goddess! Let me go!

Well he knew the thorny path, which he had thenceforth to tread; and he,
himself says: "With this work I wrote my death-warrant. Before the
[marginalia]p. 280[/marginalia]
modern world of art I could no longer hope for my life." He felt indeed the flight
from Venus:

[u]Thy[/u] shame blossoms out for thee into ignominy;
Under the ban, accursed, scorn follows thee:
Crushed, trodden down, I see thee approach,
Thy dishonored head covered with dust.

Steadily, unmisled, he advanced upon the only path possible to him. He
felt that in taking his present road he alienated the public and many friends who
had hitherto respected him. He felt that he could offer no compensation
for the opera of recreation. He recognized that his works were entirely
unfitted for the artistic stage in its existing state. Thus he says: "A
piece for the repertoire which is to be brought for a long time before
the public or perhaps forever, taking its turn with other pieces, need
have arisen out of no mood and has need of no mood to be under-
stood, or, at any rate, none of any particular personal nature.
