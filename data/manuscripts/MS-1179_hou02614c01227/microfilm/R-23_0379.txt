When we examine the modifications which the
written and the spoken dialects have since undergone,
particularly in the spelling and the pronunciation
respectively, we find that each has described its
peculiar orbit, with little perturbation by its
neighbor. The pronunciation has been utterly
revolutionized[ins], if Mr Ellis's conclusions are to be accepted[/ins]; and so far as we can guess from the
few passages of the dramatists that approach the
vernacular, even if we could understand the pro-
nunciation of a man of Shakespeare's day, his
conversation would remain a puzzle to us, and
though it were made intelligible to our outer under-
standing, our minds would but faintly resound to
its notes. What a contrast to the language of books!
