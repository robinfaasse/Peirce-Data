[u]APPENDIX TO CHAPTER 4.[/u]

[u]THE HISTORY OF THE ARABIC NOTATION.[/u]

The art of notation for numbers is, without doubt, the most beautiful system of expression
which men have ever managed to adopt. It is infinite, more clear and expressive than words.
The Hindu-Arabic numerals have nothing wonderful about them, even the use of the cipher so as to
give five values for four hundred and, 400,000,000 million ones, is not unparalleled. But that,
after this, they should go on with 40 for forty, they should not have insisted, according to the
Hindu-Arabic system, on writing 404 for forty-four, and 404404 for four hundred and forty-
four, is, indeed, a matter for astonishment.

There is a writer, who lived in the sixth century, by the name of Anicius Manlius Severinus Boetius
(or Boethius). He was a Roman patrician, and consul, and was held by the Goths, and who wrote
a book on arithmetic, and also translations of Aristotle's philosophy. Besides this, he wrote
a book on geometry, or rather, on the definitions as they seem now to be. Among the works of Boethius,
there is a treatise on geometry, some scholars do not think
it is his, but others think it is. In this treatise on geometry, there seems to be no
mention of the Arabic numerals.
