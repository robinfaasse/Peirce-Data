There are other kinds of numerals, besides the cardinal. The thing at which in counting
we say "one," is called the first thing, that at which we call "two" is called the second, and
so we go on to the third, fourth, fifth, etc. Formulae these numerals, called ordinal, that is,
showing in what order things are taken, by adding th to the cardinal number. But numbers ending
in one, two, three, form their ordinals by replacing these words by first, second, and third.
If a thing is composed of two things perfect in themselves, it is called two-fold or double;
if of three things, it is threefold or treble or triple; and so we go on to fourfold, or quad-
ruple, fivefold or quintuple, sixfold or sextuple, sevenfold or septuple. A system, say of
counting, which collects things into twos is called binary, if into threes ternary, if into fours
quaternary, etc. A set of two things is called a pair, couple, dual, or dyad; a set of three is
called a trio, triad, leash; a set of four, a tetrad, quartet, quaternion; etc.


CARDINAL COMES FROM THE LATIN


CARDINAL comes from the Latin cardinalis, like a hinge, from cardo, a hinge, and the
cardinal-words are so called because they are the words on which all the other kinds of numeral
words depend or hang, and about which every other system of numerals, as the ordinal, turns.
