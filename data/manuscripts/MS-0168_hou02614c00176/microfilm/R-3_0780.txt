At the end of the Franco-Prussian war, France was compelled to pay four thousand million francs
to Germany, and the public then learned that bankers were in the habit of calling a thousand
million a milliard. This is a very happy word, formed like million, but with a different aug-
mentative suffix. We can see that -ard means great in wizard, a great wise man, a man who knows
more than a man naturally can or ought to know; drunkard, a great drinker; sweetheart, a great
sweet fellow; braggart, placard, custard, etc. When people grow rich enough to want a word for
a thousand milliard, they may very likely call it a milliote [Spanish, millote]; a thousand mil
liote may be called a milliazo [Spanish, millazo], and a thousand milliazo, a milliaton [Italian
milliacto.]

After these words had had these meanings for nearly three hundred years, some French schoolmaster
took it into his head he would like to count by thousands, instead of by millions. If he had
looked into the best books on the subject, then written in Italian, he would have found a whole
system of words provided for this kind of numeration. They are as follows:

Not knowing these names, and being singularly deficient in invention, he calls a trilliary a
billion, a quadrilliary, a trillion, etc. The result is that the meanings of these words became
doubtful, and as they were not much wanted, they have been given up.
