But observed facts relate exclusively to the [ins]particular[/ins] circumstances that happened to exist when they were observed. They do not relate to any future occasions upon which we may be in doubt how we ought to act. They, therefore, do not, in themselves, contain any practical knowledge.

Such knowledge must involve additions to the facts observed. The making of these additions is an operation which we can control; and it is evidently a process during which error is liable to creep in.
