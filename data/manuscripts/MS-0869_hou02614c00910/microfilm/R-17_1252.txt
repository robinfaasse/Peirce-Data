The miracle remained nothing more than a great wonder, until the scholastic doctors, in their desire to give exactitude to theology, began to define it metaphysically. Aquinas said that a miracle [del]was an violation[/del] [ins]interruption[/ins] of the order of nature; and that remained the regular definition for the scholastics. When Hume took up the subject of miracles, he endeavored to conform to the definition of the theologians, although for the purposes of his argument it was a matter of indifference how a miracle should be defined. All he needed was, that it should be something the like of which was perfectly unexampled in experience. He was perfectly willing to adopt whatever definition the theologians preferred.
