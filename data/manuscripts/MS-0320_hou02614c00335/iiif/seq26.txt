I have often remarked that a man of true mental alertness (a 
rare quality among philosophists) is apt to be so struck with the brilliancy of 
his own ideas, that he expresses them too hastily; in consequence of which
refutations cause his powers to be underrated. The most numerous class is that 
of minds that are hasty, — especially, in denying novel propositions, — without 
any originality. A third type is that of a mind whose frequent blunders have 
rendered him indisposed to express thoughts [del]that have not been subjected[/del] [ins]until[/ins]
[fig]figure[/fig] multiplied and multiform reëxaminations have given him such 
a familiarity with all the ramifications of the subject to its remotest twigs
as pretty effectually to mask from ordinary observation the real slowness 
and dullness of his apprehension. The bearings of these ere observations 
lies in [del]the[/del] application thereof (which, by the way [ins]may pass as[/ins] one way of 
enunciating the principle of pragmatism.)
