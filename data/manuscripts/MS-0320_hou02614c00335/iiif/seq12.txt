If the reader imagines that the propounder of the crystal-question
will [del]experience[/del] be annoyed, instead of being gratified, at being set
right, he may take my word for it that he does not know the man,
who would incomparably rather undergo the mortification and
[del]the[/del] substantial loss of credit attaching to having been proved a
blunderer, rather than that anybody should be deceived by any
fallacious argument of his. But in any case, he ought to be
somewhat consoled to learn that his remarks on the subject
did contain an application of the pragmatistic principle
that was not only correct but vitally important. Namely, he
quoted that stanza of Gray's Elegy, beginning
Full many a gem of purest ray serene,
The dark, unfathomed [del]pure[/del] caves of ocean bear, etc.
