[pn]Pag 3[/pn] [del]To the Editor: The following[/del] [ins]I have often remarked[/ins] that a man of true mental alertness (a
rare quality among philosophers) is apt to be struck with the brilliancy of
his own ideas, that he expresses them too hastily; in consequence of which
refutations cause his powers to be underrated. The most numerous class is that
of minds that are hasty, — especially, in denying novel propositions, without
any originality. [del]A third type is that of a mind[/del] [ins]whose frequent blunders have
rendered him indisposed to express thoughts until[/ins] [del]many beautiful &[/del]
multiplied and multiform reëxaminations have given him such
a familiarity with all the ramifications of the subject to its remotest twigs
as pretty effectually to mask from ordinary observation the real slowness
and dullness of his apprehension. The bearings of these observations
lies in the application thereof ([del]which by the way[/del] [ins]may pass as[/ins] one way of
enunciating the principle of pragmatism)
