Thus it is plain that any inference is competent from a particular case and result. And so it appears that the explaining syllogism of an induction may belong to any of the four moods of the first figure. That is it may be either

$$\begin{matrix} \text{A} & \text{A} & \text{E} & \text{E} \\ \text{A} & \text{I} & \text{A} & \text{I} \\ \text{A} & \text{I} & \text{E} & \text{O} \end{matrix}$$

and the first premise or rule of the explaining syllogism is the conclusion of the inductive argument.
