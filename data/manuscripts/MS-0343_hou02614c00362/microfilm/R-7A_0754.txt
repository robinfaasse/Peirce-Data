All logicians agree that the word [u]is[/u] in the logical
form of proposition does not imply that the subject exists;
for instance we can say all fictitious animals are non-
existent, with out any contradiction between the [ins]are[/ins]
and [u]nonexistent[/u]. Accordingly it is true that
[del]All dragons[/del] [ins]griffins[/ins] have wings
and that All griffins are quadrupeds; both these
points are part of the definition of griffins. But
from these premises, if our syllogistic forms are

right, it follows that
Some quadrupeds have wings.
Now this is false. Even if we allow it
not to say that any quadrupeds exist, it does say, that
if any exist, some of them have wings.

We shall have in the first and second figures the
corresponding fallacies -

No quadrupeds have wings | No quadrupeds have wings
All griffins are quadrupeds | All griffins have wings
$\therefore$ No griffins have wings | $\therefore$ No griffins are quadrupeds

In all these cases the premises are correct and
yet the conclusions are wrong.
