proposition

$$(a \prec b) \prec a \prec c.$$

By [u]Rule III[/u], from this follows $b \prec a \prec c$. But further from the latter the former follows. For, by prop. B, this is true provided

$$(b \prec a \prec c) \prec (a \prec b) \prec a \prec c$$

or

[del]$$a \prec (a \prec b \prec c) \prec$$[/del]

$$a \prec (a \prec b) \prec (a \prec b \prec c) \prec c$$

But, by prop. A, this holds; for if $a$ and $a \prec b$ are true, $b$ is true and if $a$ and $b$ [del]and $(a \prec b \prec c)$[/del] are true $c$ is true.

Next consider the proposition

$$[(a \prec b) \prec c] \prec a \prec d$$

By [u]Rule III[/u], this follows from $(b \prec c) \prec a \prec d$. But further the latter follows from the former. [del]For by [u]Rule III[/u] from $(a \prec b) \prec c$ follows $b \prec c$. Hence by the principle of contraposition from $[(a \prec b) \prec c]$[/del] For the principle of contraposition with prop A gives us

[del]$$a \prec (a \prec b) \prec (b \prec c) \prec a \prec c$$[/del]

$$(b \prec c) \prec a \prec (a \prec b) \prec c$$

Hence by the principle of contraposition

$$\{[(a \prec b) \prec c] \prec a \prec d\} \prec [(b \prec c) \prec a] \prec a \prec d$$

or by the principle of insertions and omissions

$$\{[(a \prec b) \prec c] \prec a \prec d\} \prec (b \prec c) \prec a \prec d$$
