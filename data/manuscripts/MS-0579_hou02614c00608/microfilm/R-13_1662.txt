Three copulas give the general forms
$$A \prec [B \prec (C \prec D)]$$
$$(A \prec B) \prec (C \prec D)$$
$$[(A \prec B) \prec C] \prec D$$

The first is obviously true when for $D$ we put either $A$, $B$, or $C$. Otherwise, not [ins]necessarily[/ins].

The second can be [ins]necessarily[/ins] true in two ways
$$(A \prec B) \prec (A \prec B)$$
a case of (1), and
$$(A \prec B) \prec (C \prec C)$$
which follows at once from (2)

The third can be [ins]necessarily[/ins] true in two ways
$$[(A \prec A) \prec B] \prec B$$
the principle is
(4) If $A$, then $(A \prec B) \prec B$.
