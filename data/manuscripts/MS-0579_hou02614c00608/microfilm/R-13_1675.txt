[u]Rule 7. Principle of doubled consequent.[/u] Concern-
ing a proposition of the form $(a \prec b) \prec b$, there is
much to note. Its fundamental properties are
$\alpha$. If $a$ is true $(a \prec b) \prec b$ is true.
$\beta$. If $b$ is true $(a \prec b) \prec b$ is true.
$\gamma$ If $(a \prec b) \prec b$ is true, either $a$ or $b$ is true.
This gives rise to the following rules.
$$\begin{array}{lcl}
(a \prec b) \prec b & = & (b \prec a) \prec a \\
\{[(a \prec b) \prec b] \prec c\} \prec c & = & (a \prec b) \prec (b \prec c) \prec c \\
& = & (a \prec c) \prec (b \prec c) \prec (c \prec c) \prec c \\
& = & (a \prec b) \prec (b \prec b) \prec (b \prec b) \prec b \\
a \prec b & = & a \prec (b \prec b) \prec b
\end{array}$$
This makes it possible to subject the consequent
to all the rules given above for the antecedents

Thus, by the principle of insertions and
omissions, we can insert consequents. Thus,
from $a$ we infer $(a \prec b) \prec b$; for this is no
more than inserting an antecedent by
writing the equivalent $(b \prec a) \prec a$. So, we
can omit [del]ante[/del] consequents. Thus from
$(a \prec b) \prec c$ we can infer $(a \prec c) \prec c$.
