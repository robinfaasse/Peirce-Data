The definition of the copula is embraced in three propositions.

I. If B is scriptible A⤙B is scriptible.
II. Either A⤙B or A is scriptible.
III. If both A⤙B and A are scriptible, B is scriptible.

[u]Deductions from this Definition[/u].

(1.) No such formula is necessarily not scriptible. For such necessity could only come from A being scriptible and B not scriptible, and would therefore depend upon something else.

(2). The formula A⤙A is necessarily scriptible. For by II either it is so or A is scriptible, and by I if A is scriptible it is scriptible.

(3). If in the formula A⤙B we substitute any scriptible formula for B it becomes scriptible by I. Thus, A⤙(B⤙B) is scriptible. And hence A⤙[B⤙(C⤙C)] is scriptible, and so on indefinitely.
