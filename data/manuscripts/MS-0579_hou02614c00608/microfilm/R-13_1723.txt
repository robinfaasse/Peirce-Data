Next, it may depend on I alone. If $Z$ is
scriptible $Y \prec Z$ is scriptible and consequently
$X \prec (Y \prec Z)$ is scriptible and so on indefinitely.

Next it may depend on II and III. Thus, if
$A \prec (A \prec B)$ is scriptible $A \prec B$ is scriptible.
For by III if $A \prec (A \prec B)$ and $A$ are scriptible
$A \prec B$ is scriptible, and if $A$ is not scriptible
by II $A \prec B$ is scriptible.

Next, it may depend on I, II, and III. Thus,
if $A \prec (B \prec C)$ is scriptible $B \prec (A \prec C)$ is scriptible.
For if $A \prec (B \prec C)$ is scriptible and $A$ is not scriptible
then by [ins]I and[/ins] II $B \prec (A \prec C)$ is scriptible. But if $A \prec (B \prec C)$
and $A$ are both scriptible then by III $B \prec C$ is
scriptible. And if $B$ is not scriptible by II $B \prec (A \prec C)$
is scriptible, but if $B \prec C$ and $B$ are both scriptible
then by III $C$ is scriptible and if $C$ is scriptible
by I [del]$B \prec (A \prec C)$[/del] [ins]$B \prec (A \prec C)$[/ins] is scriptible.

The following also depends on all three. If
$A$ is scriptible, then $(A \prec B) \prec B$ is scriptible. For
if $B$ is scriptible by I $(A \prec B) \prec B$ is scriptible. But
by II either $(A \prec B) \prec B$ or $A \prec B$ is scriptible and by III
if both $A$ and $A \prec B$ are scriptible, $B$ is scriptible.
