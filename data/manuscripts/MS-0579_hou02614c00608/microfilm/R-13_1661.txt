Examination
of formulae of the Copula of Inclusion

The general principle is that we are justified
in writing $A \prec B$, provided that assuming $A$ to
be true, we find $B$ necessarily is so.

General form with one copula
$A \prec B$

The special form
(1) $A \prec A$
holds. It is the principle of identity. For assuming
$A$ to be true, $A$ is true [del]according to[/del] and the
condition of the general principle is satisfied.

General forms with two copulas
(I) $A \prec (B \prec C)$
(II) $(A \prec B) \prec C$

For (I) is true in two special cases. The first is
(2) $A \prec (B \prec B)$
Assuming $A$ to be true, $(B \prec B)$ is true by (1),
and so the condition of the general principle is sa-
tisfied. The other special form is
(3) $A \prec (B \prec A)$.
