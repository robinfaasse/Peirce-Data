$$(a \prec b) \prec c \prec a$$
This follows from $c \prec a$ by Rule III. But further, the
latter follows from the former. That is,
$$[(a \prec b) \prec c \prec a] \prec c \prec a$$
or
$$c \prec [c \prec (a \prec b) \prec a] \prec a.$$
This is true provided
$$[(a \prec b) \prec a] \prec a$$

$$[(a \prec b) \prec c] \prec a$$
$$(\bar{a} + b)\bar{c} + a$$

$$\bar{a}\bar{b} + a$$

$$c \prec a \qquad (\bar{a} + b)\bar{c} + d \qquad \bar{a} + b\bar{c} + d$$
$$[(a \prec b) \prec c] \prec d \qquad a \prec (b \prec c) \prec d$$
$$[del](\bar{a} + b)(\bar{c} + d)\bar{e} + f[/del]$$
$$\{[[(a \prec b) \prec c] \prec d\} \prec e] \prec f$$
$$[del][(\bar{a} + b)\bar{c} + d]\bar{e} + f[/del]$$
$$(\bar{a} + b)(\bar{c} + d)\bar{e} + f$$
$$[del](\bar{a} + b)(\bar{c} + d)[/del]$$
$$\{[a \prec b] \prec [c \prec d] \prec e\} \prec f$$
$$\{[a \prec (b \prec c) \prec d\} \prec e\} \prec f$$

$$\left\{ \begin{aligned} (a \prec b) \prec (c \prec d) \prec e \\ [a \prec (b \prec c) \prec d] \prec e \\ a\bar{b} + c\bar{d} + e \\ a(\bar{b} + c)\bar{d} + e \end{aligned} \right.$$
