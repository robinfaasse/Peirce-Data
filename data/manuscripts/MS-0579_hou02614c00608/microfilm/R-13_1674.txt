[u]Rule 5 Principle of breaking parentheses[/u]. Taking any
copula enclosed within an even number of parentheses,
we may do as follows:
1[sup]st[/sup] bring the beginnings of the two innermost parentheses
forward to the beginning of that copula.
2[sup]nd[/sup] Repeat all to the left of the innermost parenthesis
[del]out to[/del] [ins]from the beginning of the[/ins] the next innermost parenthesis followed
by one of the antecedents to the left of the break
in the innermost parenthesis. This to be done for
each of these antecedents.
[del]Within an odd number of[/del]
Thus, from
{[a ≺ b] ≺ [c ≺ d ≺ e ≺ f] ≺ g} ≺ h
breaking after d, we can infer
{[a ≺ b] ≺ c} ≺ {[a ≺ b] ≺ d} ≺ {[a ≺ b] ≺ [e ≺ f] ≺ g} ≺ h

Under an odd number of parentheses, the inference is from
the second form to the first.

[u]Rule 6: Principle of identity[/u]. We can always write a ≺ a.
For by [del]β[/del] [ins]α[/ins] either a or a ≺ a is true, and by [del]α[/del] [ins]β[/ins] if a
is true, a ≺ a is true. So that a ≺ a is true in
any case.
