[u]Algebra of the copula[/u]

With one copula we have the general form
$$U \prec V$$

With two copulas the two forms
I. $U \prec (V \prec W)$
II. $(U \prec V) \prec W$

With 3 copulas the five forms
I. $U \prec [V \prec (W \prec X)]$
II. $U \prec [(V \prec W) \prec X]$
III. $(U \prec V) \prec (W \prec X)$
IV. $[U \prec (V \prec W)] \prec X$
V. $[(U \prec V) \prec W] \prec X$

It is a matter of curiosity to know how many of these forms there are.
In general, if $N$ be the number of copulas $\Phi N$ the
number of forms
$$\Phi N = \frac{2(2N-1)!}{(N-1)!(N+1)!} = \frac{1}{N} \frac{(2N)!}{(N-1)!(N+1)!}$$

Such a number is divisible by [del]a power of an[/del] [ins]any[/ins]
[del]odd[/del] prime provided that after division by a power
of that prime, it leaves a remainder [ins]not less than[/ins] [del]exceeding[/del]
half that power [del]but not[/del] [ins]just[/ins] one less than that power,
and it is divisible by a power of that prime whose
exponent equals the number of powers for which
this rule holds. For the divisor two the exponent is one
higher, unless $N$ is [del]just[/del] one less than a power of 2.
