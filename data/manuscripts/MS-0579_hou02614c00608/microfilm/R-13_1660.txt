[u]Study[/u]
On the formal laws of the copula of in-
clusion.

In order to make sure I have every princi-
ple, I propose to consider every formula ne-
cessarily true up to say six copulas inclusive.
(1) $A \prec A$.
[u]The principle of identity[/u]

With two copulas, we have the forms
$A \prec (B \prec C)$
$(A \prec B) \prec C$.

The first of these becomes necessarily true
when $C = B$. That is $A \prec (B \prec B)$. The prin-
ciple is
(2) If $B$, then $A \prec B$.

Also when $C = A$. That is $A \prec (B \prec A)$. The
principle is
(3) If $A \prec (B \prec C)$ then $B \prec (A \prec C)$
so that the parenthesis is unnecessary.

The second of the above forms yields no necessary
formula.
