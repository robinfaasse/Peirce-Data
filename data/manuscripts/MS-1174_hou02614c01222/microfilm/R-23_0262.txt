[u]Absolute good[/u]. "The moral good, which concerns the
highest interest." Kant. Kritik d. Urtheilskraft. §4.

[u]Absolute ground of proof[/u]. A ground of proof not itself pro-
ved or needing proof, but intuitive.

[u]Absolute horizon[/u]. "The congruence of the limits of human cog-
nition with the limits of collective human perfection in gen-
eral." Kant. Logik. Einl V. p. 207. See [u]Horizon[/u].

[u]Absolute identity[/u]. "The system which reduces mind and
matter to phenomenal modifications of the same common
substance." Hamilton.
"Nature should be the visible mind, and mind invisi-
ble nature. Here, therefore, in the absolute identity of the mind
within us, and nature without us, must we solve the prob-
lem how it is possible for a nature out of us to be." Schelling

[u]Absolute impossibility[/u] "The absolutely impossible is that
which involves a contradiction; as, that a man is a
stone." Burgersdicius

[u]Absolute independence[/u]. "That is said to be absolutely

[page break]

independent which does not owe its being to another; and
that, either positively, [del]or negatively,[/del] in which way God
alone is independent, or negatively, in which way non-
ens owes its being to nothing, inasmuch as it has none"
Chauvin.

[u]Absolute indivisibility[/u] "The absolutely indivisible is that
which has no parts into which it can be divided, and so
is a simple ens."

[u]Absolute intellection[/u]. Intellection not performed by means
of comparison. "Absolute intellection can only be of
some simple object contained under the object of the
concept (intellectus)" Scotus. In sentent. lib 2. dist 6. qu 1. [del]p. 272[/del]

[u]Absolute Knowledge[/u]. Knowledge not relative. See [u]Rela-
tively of Knowledge[/u].

[u]Absolute liberty[/u]. "The absolutely free is free from servitude,
from a law established over him by others, from compulsion,
and from natural necessity." Burgersdicii Instit. Log. p. 66.
