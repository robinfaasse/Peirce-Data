<u>ABSOLUTE.</u>

[u]Absolutum[/u] is good Latin & is now used in a great variety of senses.

1. [u]Absolutum a restrictione[/u]. Without restriction, in the logical sense. See below, absolute distribution, and absolute scepticism.

2. [u]Absolutum a conditione[/u]. [del]With[/del] [ins]Un[/ins]conditionate. See below, absolute proposition, absolute necessity, etc.

3. [u]Absolutum a causa[/u]. "The Absolute is that which is free from all [u]necessary[/u] relation, that is, which is free from [u]every relation as a condition of existence[/u]; but it may exist in relation, provided, that relation be not a necessary condition of its existence; that is, provided that relation may be removed without affecting its existence." Calderwood.

Mansell expressly says that this is what he means by 'absolute, in the sense of free from relation.' Phil. of Conditioned. This is its sense in the Limits of Religious Thought; not sense 4.

"<u>Absolutum</u> means what is [u]freed[/u] or [u]loosed[/u]; in which sense the absolute will be what is aloof from relation, comparison, limitation, condition, dependence, etc.; and thus is tantamount to [u]τὸ ἀπόλυτον[/u] of the lower Greeks. In this meaning the absolute is not opposed to the infinite." Hamilton

4. "<u>Absolutum</u> means [u]finished[/u], [u]perfected[/u], [u]completed[/u]; in which sense the Absolute will be [ins]what is[/ins] out of relation, [del]etc.[/del], as finished, perfect, complete, total, and thus corresponds to [u]τὸ ὅλον[/u] and [u]τὸ τέλειον[/u] of Aristotle. In this acceptation, — and it is that in which for myself I exclusively use it — the Absolute is diametrically opposed to, is contradictory of the infinite." Hamilton.

"Absolute expresses the attribute of totality (in composite unity or indivisibility" F. E. Abbot.
