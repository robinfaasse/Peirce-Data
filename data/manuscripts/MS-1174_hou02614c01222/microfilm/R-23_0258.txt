relatively. For if we should make not only immediate ex-
periences but also all that we can discover by means
of them [u]a posteriori[/u], then the concept [u]a priori[/u] [del]would[/del] [ins]could[/ins] only

The following passages show Kant's use of the terms. "The
rules of logic can therefore be regarded as [u]a priori[/u], that is,
[u]independent of all experience[/u], since they contain merely
the conditions of the use of the understanding in general,
without distinction of objects." Logik. Einl. I. "Although all
our cognition begins with experience, it does not neces-
sarily on that account arise out of experience. For it
might easily be that even our experiential cognition
was a compound of that which we receive by impressions
and of that which our cognitive faculties (only stimu-

lated by sensuous impressions) bring forth from them-
selves.... Is there such a cognition independent of expe-
rience and even of all impressions of sense? One
terms such cognitions [u]a priori[/u], and distinguishes
them from the empirical, which have their sources [u]a[/u]
[u]posteriori[/u]. Kritik d. reinen Vernunft. 2[ins]nd[/ins] Ed. Einl I.

[u]A priori employment of a synthesis[/u]. Its employment in
reference to representations which are not empirical. Kant
Kritik p. 99.

[u]A priori knowledge[/u] "embraces those principles which, as
the conditions of the exercise of the faculties of thought,
are, consequently, not the result of that exercise" Ham.

[u]A priori possibility[/u]. "We know possibility [u]a priori[/u] when
we resolve a notion into its requisites or into other no-
tions of known possibility, and know that there is no-
thing incompatible in them. We know [u]a posteriori[/u] the
possibility of a thing, when we actually experience its
existence." Leibniz p. 80.
