[del]Accidental[/del] mode of signifying, is one which belongs to a term from any grammatical or logical accident; thus [u]amo[/u] signifies present time by an accidental mode.

Accidental supposition. A term of the Parva Logicalia; opposed to natural supposition. "Accidental supposition is the acceptation of a [ins]common[/ins] term for all things, [del]for[/del] which it adjuncts requires that it be taken" Petrus Hispanus. Thus, in the proposition "homo erit," homo supposes for all future men. "The supposition of a term is accidental in respect to a copula which is not freed from time; as 'homo fame moritur septimo die' Eck.

Accidental perfection is an addition pertaining to the essence, either for causing it to do or to suffer what is fit for it, or for ornament" Burgersdicius.

Accidental predicable. Property and accident. "They are called accidental because they are not substance, or of the essence of the subjects of which they are predicated. It is to be observed that the real ens is divided into substance and accident; thence taking accident as opposed to substance, property is an accident." Pseudo-Aquinas. Summa Logices.

Accidental quality. An accident in the second sense. Hamilton.
