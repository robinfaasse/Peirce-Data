[u]Absolute truth[/u] "is the agreement of the content of cognition with the actuality." Überweg.

[u]Absolute utility[/u]. "We may call the higher utility, or that conducive to the perfection of a man viewed as an end in himself, by the name of absolute." Hamilton.

ABSOLUTELY.

[u]Absolutely first and last[/u] The very first and last.

[u]Absolutely practical[/u]. What relates to the absolute good. Kant.

[u]Absolutely proper adjunct[/u]. A proper adjunct "which emanates from the essence of the subject; and that either immediately (thus, the faculty of laughing and wondering emanates immediately from the human essence) or by the medium of another property (thus to occupy space emanates from the essence through the quantity of the body." Burg.

[u]Absolutely pure[/u]. "A cognition is termed absolutely pure in which no experience or impression is mixed" Kant.

ABSTRACT.

1. That which signifies or represents an attribute or relation, apart from any reference to a subject.

This appears to be the original meaning of the term. The word is said to be employed by the Roman philosophers and grammarians. It certainly was not in common use in logic before Scotus who uses it frequently. Before him, we meet the expressions, [u]ex abstractione[/u] [u]per abstractionem[/u], [u]inabstractum[/u], [u]abstrahens[/u], [u]ablatum[/u], and [u]remotum[/u], where abstraction might be expected.

2. That which signifies or represents [ins]objects in other[/ins] [del]certain[/del] elements apart from others, whether these latter be the matter, certain forms, or the individuality. See [u]Abstract concept[/u].
