[u]Absolute motion[/u] "is the translation of a body from one
absolute place into another." Newton.

[u]Absolute name[/u]. "Names not connotative. "Merely absolute
names are those which do not signify one thing principally
and another or the same secondarily, but whatever is sig-
nified by such a name is equally primarily signified,
as appears concerning this name, 'animal.'" Occam
Quoted by Prantl iii. 364.

[u]Absolute necessity[/u]. "The absolutely necessary is that whose
negation implies contradiction." Burgersdicius.

[u]Absolute pain[/u]. See [u]absolute pleasure[/u].

[u]Absolute perfection[/u]. Perfection in every respect. "That to

which no imperfection of any sort belongs." Burgersd.

[u]Absolute philosophy[/u]. A philosophy which is ab-
solute knowledge, if true.

[u]Absolute place[/u]. "The part of absolute space which a
body occupies." Newton.

[u]Absolute pleasure[/u]. "Absolute pleasure is all that plea-
sure which we feel above a state of indifference and
which is therefore prized as a good in itself, and not
simply as the removal of an evil." Hamilton. Metaph
Lecture 42.

[u]Absolute power[/u]. The power of a despot or autocrat.

[u]Absolute prescience[/u]. "Is that by which God knows that
things absolutely and without condition will be." Bur-
gersdicii Metaph lib 2. cap 8.

[u]Absolute principle[/u]. Absolute principles are those "from
which in the construction of a science, cognitions al-
together certain not only are, but must be derived." Hamilton.
