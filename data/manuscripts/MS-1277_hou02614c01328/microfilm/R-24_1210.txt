Among all the works of the Greeks which can be classed as scientific, rather than philosophical, Euclid's Elements is the most characteristic; and the most distinctive trait of Euclid's Elements is, as we shall see, its excessive formalism and artificial unity.

The earliest of the scientific men of Greece was Thales the Wise, who was born 640 B.C. and died 548 B.C. aged about 92 years. He is said to have been a merchant of Phoenician descent and to have conversed with Egyptian priests. In his day Egypt was just beginning to be a place of resort for Greek merchants.
