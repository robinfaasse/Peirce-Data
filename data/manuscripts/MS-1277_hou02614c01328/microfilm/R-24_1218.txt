anybody to whom that division was
familiar could fail to see that no
two points on the circle could differ by
more than 180°, nor that a line
through two points distant 180° from
one another would pass through the
centre. But such a line would evidently
have 180° of the circle on each side of
it, and thus would bisect the circum-
ference. If it passes through the centre and
bisects the circumference, it evidently bisects
the circle. The fact, then, that a circle
is bisected by its diameter, even if it had
escaped the unadventurous intellect of the
Egyptian with whom Thales conversed,
must have been one that would be evident
to a Babylonian as soon as it was broached.
