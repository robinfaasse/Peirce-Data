As for Aristotle, though he says nothing of
the secrets of the Pythagoreans in any of the
works that have come down to us, he does
allude to these secrets in two fragments, one of
which is quoted both by Porphyry and by Aelian.

That other [ins]ancient[/ins] authors have not made citations
to this effect from Aristotle is easily accounted
for. Namely, the fact the Pythagoreans kept their
doctrines secret being perfectly notorious, it was
not worth while to adduce any authority to support
it.

But Aristoxenus, a direct disciple of Aristotle,
cites the authority of the Pythagoreans to support
him in the idea that a certain reticence is to
be used (Diog. Laer. VIII. 15). He also mentions
the cryptic modes of expression of the
Pythagoreans.
