The ten point theorem

If three rays $(a), (b), (c)$ in a plane, have a common point $[O]$ and if there be [del]three[/del] [ins]two[/ins] triangles $(i), (j), (k)$ and $(l), (m), (n),$ whose vertices lie upon the first three rays; namely
$[jk]$ and $[mn]$ on $(a)$
$[ki]$ and $[nl]$ on $(b)$
$[ij]$ and $[lm]$ [del]also[/del] on $(c)$
it follows that $[il], [jm],$ and $[kn]$ lie on one ray.

<u>Proof</u>.
For take any points $[Q]$ [del]not in the plane[/del] [ins]$\{OA, B_1\}$[/ins]
There will be a plane $\{Q C_1 C_2\}$ and only one
[del]But there is a ray $(O C_1 C_2)$ by hypoth. and since $[C_1]$ and $[C_2]$ are in $\{Q C_1 C_2\}$ so is $[O]$[/del]
Take any point [del]$N$[/del] $[T_1]$ on $(Q C_1)$ [del]Then hence $[Q]$ and $[C_1]$ are in $\{Q C_1 C_2\}$ so is $[T_1]$[/del]
since $[O]$ and $[T_1]$ are both in $\{Q C_1 C_2\}$ so is $(O T_1)$
since $[Q]$ and $[C_2]$ are both in $\{Q C_1 C_2\}$ so is $(Q C_2)$
since $(O T_1)$ and $(Q C_2)$ are in the same plane, they have a common point, call it $[T_2]$
There is a plane $\{A_1 B_1 T_1\}$ and a plane $\{A_2 B_2 T_2\}$
