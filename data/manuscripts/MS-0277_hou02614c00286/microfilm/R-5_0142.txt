Mathematics wholly relates to hypothetical
states of things, [del]very[/del] [ins]perfectly[/ins] exact, but of a kind that only
affect a few very general characters.

A mathematical [u]operation[/u] is a triadic
relation. Two of the correlates are analogous
to causes. One is the [u]operator[/u] the other the [u]operand[/u].
The third correlate is analogous to an effect,
it is the [u]result[/u]. All [del]the[/del] mathematical operations
are determined by entirely general definitions,
expressive of the essence of the ideas, and
are quite unaffected by any existential
conditions.

Perhaps the most fundamental operation
is that of [u]addition[/u]. The operator is called the
[u]addend[/u], the operand the [u]augend[/u], and the
two indifferently the [u]additive terms[/u] (or [u]parts[/u],
though this is somewhat open to objection) of the
[u]sum[/u], which last is the result. The essential
characters of addition are 1[u]st[/u] that the addend
and augend [del]affect the sum[/del] [ins]and in the same way[/ins] independently
of one another, and 2[u]nd[/u] that the different
quantities, or correlates of operations, may be
conceived as results of the operation of addition
