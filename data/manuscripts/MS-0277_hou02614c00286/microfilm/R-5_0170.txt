1. There may be one [del]simple[/del] [ins]i.e. homogeneous[/ins] being complete in itself.

2. There may be a being that needs one attachment only to complete it

The two are necessarily of different [del]kinds[/del] [ins]parts[/ins]. Otherwise the 2 would make one simple and complete being. Different means of 2 kinds. Every complete being whose parts are merely singly-needing consists of two. If [u]n[/u] being the number of kinds of parts which must be 2 or more, [u]n(n-1)[/u]/2 is the number of different kinds of complete being.
