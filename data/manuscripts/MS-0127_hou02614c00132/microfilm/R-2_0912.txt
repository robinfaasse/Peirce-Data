Let us imagine the whole of [del]s[/del]space to be filled by a [del]solid[/del] body whose parts can move subject to the following rules:

1[u]st[/u], Every motion is continuous;

2[u]nd[/u], Any part occupying at any one instant a homaloid occupies at every instant a homaloid of the same dimensionality;

3[u]rd[/u], The particles of any straight filament can move [ins]by a steady motion[/ins] along its ray, while every flat film containing that filament remains in its plane; and on the other hand the flat films containing any flat filament can move [ins]by a steady motion[/ins] round its ray, while every particle of the filament remains at its point.

Mind, I do not say that in this motion every particle moves upon the straight filament ray or that every plane moves round the [del]place[/del] [ins]ray[/ins]. A steady motion is one in which all [del]particles[/del] [ins]parts[/ins] coming to any given place move in the same way.

These rules give room for a still further restriction of the possibilities of motion when it shall be convenient to impose them.
