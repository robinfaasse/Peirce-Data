Two "primes" are said to be "[u]projective[/u]" to one another (an accepted parlance) if one can be derived from the other by one or several acts of homaloidal production and section. Each element of the one prime so corresponding to an element of the other.

Thus a sheaf and a field are projective if the latter is a section of the former.

A [del]range[/del] [ins]sheaf[/ins] [ins]that is[/ins] a section of a star is projective with that star and a star that is a section of a book is projective with that book.

Two sheafs which have a field as common section are projective with one another.

Two fields which have a sheaf as common production are projective with one another.

Two [del]ranges[/del] [ins]books[/ins] that have a star as common section are projective.

[del]Two stars that have a book as common production are projective[/del]

Two ranges that have a star as common production are projective

Two stars that are sections of one book are projective.

Two stars that are productions of one range are projective.
