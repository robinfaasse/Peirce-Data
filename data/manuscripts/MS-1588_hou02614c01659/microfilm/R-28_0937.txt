Curvature is expressed by ratio of angle
made by tangent with axis compared
with distance passed, i.e. by the ratio of $\Delta \tau$ to
$\Delta s$, or going to the limits by $\mathcal{D}_s \tau$. To find
$\mathcal{D}_s \tau$ we have, in rectangular co-efficients,
$(ds)^2 = (dx)^2 + (dy)^2$, or $(\mathcal{D}_x s)^2 = 1 + (\mathcal{D}_x y)^2$,
$(\mathcal{D}_y s)^2 = 1 + (\mathcal{D}_y x)^2$, and we know $\tan \tau = \mathcal{D}_x y$.
Applying these values to each other
we obtain as a result $\mathcal{D}_s \tau = \frac{\mathcal{D}_x^2 y}{[1 + (\mathcal{D}_x y)^2]^{\frac{3}{2}}}$
which measures the curvature
at any point.
