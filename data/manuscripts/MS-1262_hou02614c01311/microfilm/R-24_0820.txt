[u]Garrulities of a Vulgar Arithmetician.[/u]

The name "Vulgar Arithmetic" is quite out of fashion; it is quite too vulgar for these superfine days.

I shall stick to it, all the same. The school-books call the art "practical arithmetic." But that [del]is[/del] [ins]term is[/ins] too general; [ins]for[/ins] whether computation with the Arabic figures really be the most practical method or not is a matter of opinion. Some highly rational people argue to the contrary; and though I do not agree with them, you will find, dear reader, that there is more to be said on their side of the question than you have been aware of. At all events, an art ought to be called by a name that is appropriate to it independently of any opinion about it. The objection to the word "vulgar" is itself a piece of vulgarity. That is primarily [u]vulgar[/u] which is common among the lower middle class of society. The word [u]vulgarity[/u], however, is restricted to the secondary sense of odiously bad taste mingled with ignoble admiration, both the bad taste and the wrong feeling being due to want of culture. Such, very decidedly, is speaking as if whatever is [u]vulgar[/u] [del]is[/del] [ins]were[/ins] necessarily marked [del]by[/del] [ins]with[/ins] [u]vulgarity[/u].
