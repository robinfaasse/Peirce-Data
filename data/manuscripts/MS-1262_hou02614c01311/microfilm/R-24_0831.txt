[pn]12[/pn] Joost Bürgi (whose family name is often erroneously given as Byrge, Borgen, and otherwise, and his christian name as Jobst, Juste, etc.) was born 1552 in Lichtensteig, Switzerland (and not at Lichtenstein in 1549, as stated in Didot). He was bred to watchmaking, and says himself in his preface to his tables of sines that he knew neither Greek nor Latin. He had, however, a native genius for mathematics. Keppler in his Narratio de [u]Stella Nova in Cygno[/u] says of him, "licet expers linguarum, rerum tamen mathematicarum scientia et speculationis multos earum professores superat." From 1579 he was in the service of William IV Landgrave of Hesse [ins]in Cassel[/ins] as [del]an[/del] [ins]his chief[/ins] instrument maker and as one of his two astronomical observers. That prince died in 1592. Later he was in Prague similarly employed by the emperor Rudolph II. [del]He died in 1632. How[/del] He returned to live in Cassel in 1622, and died ten years later. He was an independent discoverer of logarithms, and Keppler, in the Rudolphine Tables, assigns to him an absolute priority over Napier, but adds: "Etsi homo cunctator et secretorum suorum custos foetum in partu destituit, non ad usus publicos educavit."
