[pn]9[/pn] Moreover, Cantor says that there is in Munich a MS. table of latitudes and longitudes by George of Peurbach in which the degree is divided into a hundred minutes and the minute into a hundred seconds. That was going far toward decimals. Next came the scholar of the Peurbachian, the celebrated astronomer Regiomontanus. About [date]1467[/date] he remarked [ins](what was extremely obvious)[/ins] that calculations with a table of sines would be facilitated if the radius had been taken as 100000; and he himself gave a brief table of tangents (not yet known by this name) which are virtually calculated for a radius of 100000. Next, the [u]Arithmetica[/u] of the Venetian Piero Borgi (not to be confounded with Joost Bürgi) printed in [date]1484[/date] gives this rule for dividing by a number expressed by a digit followed by zeros only: You strike off from the right side of the dividend as many figures as there are zeros in the divisor, and having divided what remains on the left of these by the first figure of the divisor, the quotient of this division is the whole part of the quotient required, while the remainder prefixed to the figures struck off gives the numerator of the fractional part of the quotient.
