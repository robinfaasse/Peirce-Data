[pn]24[/pn] [del]account of Gerbert's stay in Spain with the theory that he went to Cordova,[/del] an account of which Gerbert must have been cognizant. It is by supposing that the latter, whether he took Richerus into his confidence or not, being always a most prudent man, for all the boldness with which he sometimes acted, was so apprehensive of the sequelae of the popular belief that he had learned the black art of the Sarracens, that he determined (at least, so far as was possible without a direct lie,) to convey the idea that he never had had any intercourse at all with the Arabians. I therefore proceed to consider another necessary consequence of the hypothesis. Namely, Gerbert, being so extremely prudent, would never have undertaken the journey to the university of Cordova, unless he were sure of possessing enough Arabic to profit by his instruction there.
