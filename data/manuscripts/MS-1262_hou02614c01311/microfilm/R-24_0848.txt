A book that was very popular in the latter part of the [u]XV[/u]th century, the
[u]Fasciculus Temporum, omnes antiquorum cronicas complectens[/u] (of which I use an
edition of [date]A.D. 1482[/date]) thus sketches his life: "That Sylvester was made pope with
the help of the devil to whom he did homage so that everything should succeed to
his wish. He was called Gilbert; and he procured for himself the favour of the king of
France, though his enemy. So he was made Archbishop of Rheims. Yet he was
immediately ejected by a council at the command of Benedict VII. Then he
acquired the favor of the emperor, and was made Bishop of Ravenna and afterward
Pope. But he quickly came to the usual end of those who put their faith in false
demons. Yet there is hope of his salvation on account of certain signs at his tomb,
as well as the heavy penance he made at last; his members being cut off and given to the devil."
