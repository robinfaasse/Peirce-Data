GAROL THE SECOND.

There is nothing more detestible than a brand new word, never yet
handselled. But when it slips off the pen before one thinks, then when
one stops to think, it may plead that now it has already been used.
After all, what else should I call this? I trust you would not have
me entitle it Twaddle Two. You must remember that these garols
are not to be Milton's
Shameful garrulity, to be excluded
All friendship, and avoided as a blab;
nor is it the "empty garrul[ins]i[/ins]ance of youth." It is simply the natural
note of the thinking-bird calling to its like to come and think with it.
If you are not of us, you can call us gas-birds. A garol may be
defined as such a portion of garrition as suffices for one sitting.
Had I maintained a proper gravity, I should perhaps have called
it a garrite, or garrit, after "audit"; or else a garriment, like
[del]vestment,[/del] "impediment," "muniment," "liniment," "nutriment,"
"condiment"; or since vestire gives "vestment", garrire should
give "garment." [del]The[/del] "garment of words" is not an unheard phrase:
only, unfortunately it commonly refers to a garnement.

But skipping all that, be it granted our vulgar arithmetic
was not the original invention of Ja'afar's father Mohammed the
son of Moses, but was the public common of all his people. What then?
