[u]CAROL THREE.[/u]

I will correct a few blunders in the notice of Gerbert that I took from the [u]Fasciculus Temporum[/u]. That it should be supposed that he dealt with Satan is not surprising. The same thing was said of Roger Bacon, although he lived in the comparatively civilized times of [del]the Plantagenets[/del] [ins]Henry III and Edward I,[/ins] while Gerbert was immersed in the deepest blackness of the dark ages, when reading was an unusual and almost awful accomplishment. Even under Queen Elizabeth, the learned Dr. Dee was obliged to leave home and become a wanderer through Europe because of such imputations. As to his being an enemy of the king of France, that is, of Hugh Capet, there is reason to think [del]the Gerbert[/del] [ins]like most of the men born south of the Loire,[/ins] regretted the decay of the Carlovingian dynasty; and it has been supposed that he encouraged [del]Prince[/del] [ins]Archbishop[/ins] Arnoul in his plot [del]to assist Prince Charles[/del] against Hugh.
