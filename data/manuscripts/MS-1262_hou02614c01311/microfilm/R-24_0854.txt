betook himself to the court of the Ottos where he was, at first, made

vacant, Otto very naturally demanded that Gerbert should be
appointed to that important see, and Gregory as naturally has-
tened to make good the request. Two years later, when Gregory
died, it was a matter of course that the choice of a new pope should
rest upon Otto, and equally so that he should prefer Gerbert, who
had a reign of over four years, or longer than the average, and
