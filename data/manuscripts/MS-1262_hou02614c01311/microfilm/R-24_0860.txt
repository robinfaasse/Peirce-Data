Richerus continues: "Coming to man's estate he would have remained in that convent as he had hitherto intended, but that Borrellus, the Duke of Hither Spain, happened to come [del]to the same[/del] there for a religious retreat" [[u]In quo utpote adolescens cum adhuc intentus moraretur Borrellum, citerioris Hispaniae ducem orandi gratia ad idem coenobium contigit devenisse[/u].] You will remark that the "adolescens" agrees with Malmesbury's "mox cum Pitagoricum bivium attigisset" in representing that he was about 20 years old when he left the convent; so that he must have been born about A.D. 947. It may be doubted whether Borrellus had [ins]at that time[/ins] assumed any such title as the Duke of Gothia, or of Hither Spain. He was a man of great energy, and great diplomacy, and great enlightenment, who labored to found a dynasty, and succeeded in doing so, since the kings of Aragon were his descendents.
