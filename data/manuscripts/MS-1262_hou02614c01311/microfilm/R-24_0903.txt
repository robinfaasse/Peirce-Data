As soon as he returns, therefore, [ins]I[/ins] [del]we[/del] look to see him spreading the knowledge of this through Latin Europe. But nothing of the sort! He comes back utterly without a suspicion of the existence of the Chorezmian computation. He is entirely taken up with a method of working the abacus. Moreover, I am surprised to find that although he must have separated himself from Count Borel in order to go to the university, yet, as soon as he comes back, he appears in the retinue of the same prince and accompanied by the metropolitan of Catalonia; and I remark that from the moment of his return he exhibits a skill which he might have developed in three years passed in the court of a sovereign, but not so easily in a foreign university, the skill in dealing with men of state.

All these difficulties convince me that I did wrong in departing, even for a moment, from the principles of logic by [ins]quite[/ins] gratuitously assuming that Gerbert went to the town of Cordova. Of course he went to the caliphate of Corduba, since a ten mile walk from Barcelona would suffice to carry him there. He may even have gone as far as Tolosa, then a more considerable place than it is now, about eighty miles from Barcelona. Tarragona (an arch bishopric [ins]soonafter[/ins]) was only half as far.
