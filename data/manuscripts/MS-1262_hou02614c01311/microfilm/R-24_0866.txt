CAROL FOUR.

Some of those critics whom I am compelled the most thoroughly to
respect, such as Woepcke for his great learning, and Moritz Cantor for
the sanity of his judgments, are of the opinion that Gerbert's
abacus, with its accompanying figures for the digits, was not
derived in any measure from the book of Ja'Cafar's father
Mohammed the son of Moses, but that a knowledge of it had
existed among the Latin speaking peoples for five centuries
and more before Gerbert's time. Now I am not ready yet to
state their opinion in full. They say that substantially the same
thing is described in a book whose date is A.D. 500 ±. Let us
not prejudge that question one way or the other. But supposing
that it was so, I wish to consider how we ought to explain the
surprising fact that this useful instrument never appears
in the records of the intervening five centuries, while the
very first man that goes to Spain to learn arithmetic comes
back with it, and comes back with a very high respect for
an arithmetical treatise by one Joseph Chacham.
