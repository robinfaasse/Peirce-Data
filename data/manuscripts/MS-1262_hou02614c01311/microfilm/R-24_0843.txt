[pn]24[/pn] The description of the operation of counting which I have just given [del]and[/del] mentions every essential element of the operation. Yet it says nothing about the fingers. I do not believe that many people of my acquaintance or of yours, reader, make any use of the fingers in arithmetic. It seems, however, that all people do in that stage of their development in which their numeral vocabulary is taking root; and to their convenience, or rather to what they stupidly imagined to be such, all the habits of the civilized world have to bend, since to change the base of our numeration now would cost more than the present value of all future inconvenience will amount to. It is true that by the multiplication of the human race the [del]s[/del] total yearly inconvenience is always increasing; and if this increase were more rapid than the increase of money at compound interest, the present value of the inconvenience would be infinite. But, of course, that is very far from being the case. The whole theory of sacrificing present advantages for the sake of future advantages needs to be thoroughly worked over; but I cannot help believing in prudence, and prudent conduct would become impossible if infinite present importance were to be [del]allowed[/del] [ins]attributed[/ins] to the infinitely distant future. Now the value of infinitely distant advantages must either be infinite or be zero. Meantime, it is [del]a[/del] curious to trace out the advantages that might accrue to us from a change of our base of numeration; and in another carol I will do so.
