The reality of continuity once admitted, the next question
is what are we to regard as continuous and what as discontinu-
ous? It is shown that to say anything is continuous is to leave
possibilities open which are closed by asserting it is discon-
tinuous. Accordingly, a regulative principle of logic
requires us to hold each thing as continuous until it is proved
to be discontinuous. But absolute discontinuity cannot be
proved to be real, nor can any good reason for believing it
to be alleged. We thus reach the conclusion that, as a
regulative principle, at least, ultimate continuity ought to be
presumed everywhere.
