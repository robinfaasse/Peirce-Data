"Provisionally", here, means that upon indefinitely increasing
the number of instances the conclusion will be modified so that
its error will be indefinitely diminished. "Probably" is omitted,
because it is held (against the doctrine of Inverse Probabilities)
that no definite value of the probability exists.

[u]Third Figure. (Induction.)[/u]
[u]By the conditions of selection[/u], among $S$s is an [u]$l$ of an $M$[/u];
[u]By observation[/u], we find among $S$s are [u]$ls$ of $k$s of $v$ $P$s[/u];
$\therefore$ [u]Provisionally and approximately, in the long run[/u], each $M$ [ins]will be[/ins] [u]$k$ of $v$ $P$s[/u].

Here is a theory which makes induction, hypothesis,
and all positive synthetic inference to be nothing but
indirect probable syllogism. It is essentially the same as
that given by the author in 1867. If it be admitted, the rules of
such inferences follow with demonstrative certainty. There is one
of these rules which has not usually be sufficiently insisted
upon; and examples are given to show how able men [del]are[/del] [ins]have[/ins]
been led into fallacious conclusions by neglect of it. That rule
is that instances that have gone to suggest a theory cannot
also be counted in as availing to raise the theory above
the rank of a mere suggestion; or, to state the rule more
clearly, a theory can only be supported by verified pre-
dictions, or what are virtually predictions.
