Dear Miss Daniells:

Suppose a person says that if A is true
B is true. Then, if what he says is true, and
if the antecedent, A, is true, the consequent, B,
is also true; is it not?

This granted, suppose a person says:
"If what I am saying is true, black is white."
Then, by the principle just admitted, if what he
says is true (and if the antecedent is true, but
that is the same thing), the consequent is true and
black is white.

But that is all he says, that if what
he says is true, black is white, and thus what
he says precisely accords with the truth.

And so black is white. Though you
would hardly think it, if it were not so
clearly proved.

very faithfully
C. S. Peirce

Arisbe, [date]1891 Sep 12[/date].
