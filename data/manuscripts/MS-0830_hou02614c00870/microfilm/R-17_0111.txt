The first thing to be noticed about reasoning
is that it is a passage from one belief to
another. The propositions embodying the earlier
and later beliefs respectively are called the
premises and the conclusion. The latter is said
to be inferred or concluded from the former by
the process of inference or reasoning.

One belief being subsequent to another
makes no significant connection between
them, unless the one results from or is caused
by the other. Often when this happens we
are unaware that the belief so arising has
been the effect of a previous belief. So long
as we are unaware of it, we cannot watch
the process, and are unable to control it, so that
there can be no art of doing it well.
