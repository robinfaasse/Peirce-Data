So, if philosophy be constructed upon the rock of un-
critical experience, another philosophy must investigate
and criticise this same experience.

Psychology, it is true, like astronomy, for instance,
gives a certain range of facts for the consideration of
the philosopher. This, however, is not the important
consideration. That is this: psychology's facts are of
such a kind that they enable one the better to grasp the
meaning of this same experience to which otherwise, phil-
osophy can only refer for explanation. The philosopher
feels that his work is not the matter of the superstructure
save as that becomes a "Weltanschauung", that his part is
to spend his life in finding one single solitary spot of
certainty - absolute certainty. He feels that if this
be found he can move a world. Hence he must needs
scrutinise this experience of which we have been talking.
If, however, he has studied it, as to its facts, in a
scientific way, he is the better prepared to philosophise
concerning it. This is my meaning when I say that phil-
osophy should be approached by the route of psychology.
It would seem that the whole history of philosophy is a
commentary on the point which has been made of the great
problem of philosophy being this thing of experience.
The early Greeks philosophized about the material world.
