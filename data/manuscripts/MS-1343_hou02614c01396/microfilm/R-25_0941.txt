[del]Accurately[/del] descriptive designations would have been too
cumbrons. What was most desirable, therefore, was that the
names should not be understood as descriptive. They are merely
key-words referring to the following definitions.

[u]The Gambol Instinct[/u] is the general instinct (for some instincts
are more general and rational than others) for exercising body
and mind. It prompts to athletic exercises, dancing, [del]and[/del] games,
and active amusements, and also to the preservation of health, to
cleanliness, and to general personal well-being.

[u]The Gust Instinct[/u] is the instinct which causes us to take pleasure
in sensations, such as agreeable eating and drinking, smoking, and
other sensual gratification.

[u]The Getting Instinct[/u] is the instinct which causes men to amass
treasures, whether of money, books not to be read, cancelled postage stamps
and other equally worthless objects, to engage in business, to speculate
and gamble, and generally [ins]to pursue self-interest with eagerness[/ins] [del]to have two well-marked varieties, the gaining
and the gathering kinds.[/del] and energy.

* Major Powell regards games of chance and calculation as having their
origin on the Ghost-instinct; but his own data are against this conclusion.
