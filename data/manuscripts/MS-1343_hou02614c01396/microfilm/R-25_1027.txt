<u>Definition of a Human Instinct</u>, as the term will here be used. An
animal instinct is a natural disposition, or [ins]inborn[/ins] determination
of his Nature (or that within him who makes his behaviour
to be such as it is), manifested by a certain unity of quasi-
purpose in his behaviour. In man, at least, this behaviour
is always conscious, and not merely spasmodic, and is
almost always partially controlled by the deliberate
exercise of imagination and reflexion; but its adaptation
to its [del]geni-[/del] purpose is invariably in part not so controlled. The
behaviour in question generally involves feeling, the putting
forth of mechanical energy, and cognition. The unity of
quasi-purpose has two parts. [del]In[/del] the first place, the be-
haviour is such as might be expected to lead to a certain
[del]re[/del] result, and if this is frustrated [ins]or appears destined to be frustrated[/ins] a new impulse will
produce another [ins]more or less[/ins] independent line of behaviour which
might be expected to produce the [ins]same[/ins] result in another way.
