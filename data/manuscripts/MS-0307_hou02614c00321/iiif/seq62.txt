The Red-backed shrike or Butcher-
bird (Lanius Collurio)
This bird is a summer visitor,
and is found in most parts of En-
gland. The food of the Butcher-bird
is chiefly beetles, but he is said
to eat small birds and mice. His
peculiar habit of spitting or fork-
ing his prey upon thorns until he
wants them to eat, has given him
the name of Butcher-bird. The nest
is a large one, and is usually placed
in a bush or hedge; it is com-
posed of [del]dry grass and moss[/del]
[ins]dead twigs, moss and roots[/ins] lined with [del]horsehair[/del]
[ins]wool and hair[/ins]. The eggs are of a yellowish
white or pale blue, with brown or 
green spots. The Butcher-bird is a
great mimic, and can imitate the 
notes of almost any bird. His
own song is a low but pleasant 
warble. The male has a gray 
crown and lower back, the mid-
dle part of the back being of a 
chestnut brown; the tail feathers 
are white at the base and black
at the tips. The female is brown
above and white below.
