[marginalia]A[/marginalia] and the Not-me is far more prominent in
Willing than in Sensation, which, one may
say, is hardly anything but Feeling considered
from the point of view of its occurrence. Now
Willing [del]is of the[/del] splits into two forms Active
Willing and Inhibitive Willing, and there is
no corresponding distinction of Sensation.
For what is called the Inner Sense is merely Out-
ward Sensation reflected upon; while in In-
hibitive Willing we have a phenomenon in
which there can hardly be said to be a
Second different from the Willing Ego.

These successions of dichotomies pre-
sent some very curious phenomena; but
they require too close study to be presented in a
lecture. Indeed, I do not think that any man
who eats three meals a day can make much
headway in phenomenology.
