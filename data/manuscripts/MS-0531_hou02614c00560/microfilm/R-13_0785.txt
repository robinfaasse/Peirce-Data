Any relative term may be joined to its [del]copula[/del] [ins]correlative[/ins] in three different ways.
1[sup]st[/sup], Simply, as in "lover of a woman;"
2[sup]d[/sup], Distributively, as in "lover of all women there may be";
3[sup]o[/sup], Exclusively, as in "lover [del]of women[/del] in whatever may he is a lover, of women, [ins]only[/ins]."

The nature of the simple application of a relative [ins]is involved[/ins] [del]may be stated[/del] in six elementary principles, which (as well as most of the following propositions) I shall set forth only in examples, [del]because[/del] no proper terminology for abstract statements exists.

1. If every man is an animal then every head of a man is a head of an animal. If A is B, C of A is C of B.

2. If X is any servant of a man, X is a servant of Y, where Y denotes some particular man.

3. If [del]to be a lover is to be a servant[/del] [ins]the relation of loving is a relation of serving[/ins], then all lovers of women are servants of women. If A is B, all As of Cs are Bs of Cs.

4. If X is any servant of a woman, then X is Y to a woman, where Y denotes some particular relation of serving.

5. All things which stand to women in the relation of servants of lovers are servants of lovers of women.

6. All servants of lovers of women stand to women in the relation of servants of lovers.
