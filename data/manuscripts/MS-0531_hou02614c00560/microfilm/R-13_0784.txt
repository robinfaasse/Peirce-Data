The distributive and exclusive applications of relatives are
each defined by two principles, the two being very similar
in the two cases. [del]For[/del] the distributive application is such that
1. If $A$ is $B$. Whatever is $C$ to all $B$'s is $C$ to an $A$, if there is
any $C$ to an $A$.
2. Let $X$ be any $A$. Then whatever is $B$ to $X$ is $B$ to all $A$'s.
[del]The[/del] exclusive application of relatives is such that
1. If $A$ is $B$, whatever is $B$ only to $C$'s is $A$ to a $C$, or else there
is nothing to which it is $A$. Thus, if every relation of loving is
a relation of serving, then [del]whatever[/del] [ins]whoever[/ins] is a servant only to women
is a lover of a woman if he is a lover, at all.
2. Let $X$ be any $A$. Then whatever is $X$ to a $B$ is [del]A[/del] $A$ to $B$ only.
From the two principles of distributive application it is readily
deducible [del]that, if $X$ be a singular[/del] that,
(Theorem 1) If $X$ is a singular term, whatever is $A$ to an $X$ is $A$ to whatever
$X$ there is.
In the same way, from the two principles of exclusive application we
have,
(Theorem 2) If $X$ is a singular term, whatever is $X$ to an $A$, is $X$
to $A$'s only.
