Reference to an interpretant is rendered possible and justified
by that which remains possible and requires comparison. But this
is clearly the diversity of impressions. It is plain, that if we had
but one impression, this impression would require to be reduced
to unity, and would, therefore, not need to be thought of as a
[del]representation[/del] [ins]to an interpretant[/ins] and the conception of reference to an interpretant would
not arise. But the moment there are several impressions, that is
a manifoldness of impressions, we have a feeling of complication
or confusedness, which leads us to differentiate this impression from
that, and they require to be brought to unity. Now they are [ins]not[/ins] brought to
unity [del]the moment[/del] [ins]until[/ins] we conceive them together as being ours, that
is, [del]until[/del] we refer them [del]to ourselves, that is[/del] to a conception as their
interpretant. Thus the reference to an interpretant arises upon
the holding together of diverse impressions, and therefore it does
not join a conception to the substance, as the other two references
do, but joins, directly, the manifold of the substance itself.
It is therefore the last conception in order, in passing from Being to
Substance.

§ 9 Formal Objects.

We have found between the unity of being and the manifold of
substance, three universal and necessary [del]intermediate[/del] conceptions; namely

Reference to a Ground
Reference to a Correlate
Reference to an Interpretant.
