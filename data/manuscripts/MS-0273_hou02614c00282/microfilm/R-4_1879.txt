It will be convenient, in what follows, to
designate [del]s[/del] places, either by [ins]a[/ins] single letter[del]s[/del], or by
writing together the letters which denote the
places which, by containing or [ins]by[/ins] being contained,
determine the place to be designated. In either
case, but especially in the latter, the designation
of a point will often be enclosed in square brackets,
that of a ray in parentheses, and that of a plane
in braces. Thus if $(P)$ and $(Q)$ are two rays
[del]by[/del] containing a point in common, and therefore
lying in one plane, $[PQ]$ will designate the
point and $\{PQ\}$ the plane. If $(P)$ and $(Q)$ have
one common point, [del]and the rays $(Q)$ and $(R)$ have
another common point[/del] then $\{[PQ]R\}$ will denote
the plane containing $(R)$ and $[PQ]$. This might even
be written $\{PQR\}$, since if $(Q)$ and $(R)$ had a common
point $\{P[QR]\}$ would be simply the plane $\{PQ\}$, and
would naturally be so written.
