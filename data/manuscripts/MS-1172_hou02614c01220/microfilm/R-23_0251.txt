[pn]No 1172[/pn] [u]An English Lesson[/u]
Here are a few passages from some celebrated writers.

"In sober state,
Through the sequestered vale of rural life,
The venerable patriarch guileless held
The tenor of his way." [u]Bishop Porteus[/u].

"Have... the journals of literature, those important publications which must have
great influence on the principles and learning of the times; have these been in
the hands of men who enjoy leisure, and a competency in the sequestered
shades of Oxford or Cambridge?" [u]Bishop Knox[/u].

"His addiction was to courses vain;
I never noted in him any study
Any retirement, any sequestration
From open haunts and popularity." [u]Shakespeare[/u].

"Ye sacred nine, that all my soul possess,
Whose rapture fires me, and whose visions bless,
Bear me, oh bear me, to sequestered scenes
Of bowery mazes and surrounding greens," [u]Pope[/u].

"In shady bower
More shady and sequestered, though but feigned,
Pan or Sylvanus never slept." [u]Milton[/u]

"The statesman, lawyer, merchant, [del]man[/del] [ins]man[/ins] of trade,
Pants for the refuge of some rural shade,
Where all his long anxieties forgot
Amid the charms of a sequestered spot
He may possess the joys he thinks he sees,
Lay his old age upon the lap of ease, etc." [u]Cowper[/u]

These [del]things[/del] extracts show how these authors understood the word [u]sequestered[/u]
[u]as applied to a place[/u]. It has a different meaning as applied to [u]property[/u], and a
third meaning as applied to a [u]person[/u]. The general sense is set apart or [ins]remote[/ins] [del]removed[/del] [del]from other[/del] [del]things[/del]
