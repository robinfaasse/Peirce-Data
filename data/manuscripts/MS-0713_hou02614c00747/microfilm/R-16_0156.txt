[marginalia]Kempe[/marginalia]

My attention has been called to a paper by Mr. Kempe in
Vol XXI of the [u]Proc. Lond. Math. Soc.[/u], which is marked by its
author's genius as well as by that air of a foreigner to
logic which strikes one in other papers by him.

He considers an "infinite number" [which ought to mean a
denumeral collection] of "entities", [u]a[/u], [u]b[/u], [u]c[/u], [u]d[/u]... which are
"not distinguished" from one another, [although in the same breath
he does distinguish them by letters]. Since the word [del]"entity"[/del] [ins]"entity"[/ins] is
always used as an abstraction by realists, it may be assumed
that Mr. Kempe means by entities individual objects.

He considers certain "linear triads", expressed in the form
[u]a[/u]·[u]b[/u]·[u]c[/u] ; and the "distribution" of these through the system is
"defined" by the following laws: —

[u]Law I.[/u] If [u]a[/u]·[u]b[/u]·[u]p[/u] and [u]c[/u]·[u]p[/u]·[u]d[/u], then there is some "entity", [u]q[/u], such
that [u]a[/u]·[u]d[/u]·[u]q[/u] and [u]b[/u]·[u]c[/u]·[u]q[/u]. [Whence also there is some "entity", [u]r[/u], such that
[del]law[/del] [u]d[/u]·[u]a[/u]·[u]r[/u] and [u]c[/u]·[u]b[/u]·[u]r[/u].]

[u]Law II.[/u] If [u]a[/u]·[u]b[/u]·[u]p[/u] and [u]c[/u]·[u]p[/u]·[u]d[/u], then there is some "entity", [u]q[/u], such that
[u]b[/u]·[u]c[/u]·[u]q[/u] and [u]a[/u]·[u]q[/u]·[u]d[/u] [Whence there is some "entity", [u]r[/u], such that
[u]c[/u]·[u]a[/u]·[u]r[/u] and [u]b[/u]·[u]q[/u]·[u]d[/u]]

[u]Law III.[/u] If [u]a[/u]·[u]a[/u] = [u]c[/u], then [u]c[/u] = [u]a[/u].

[u]Law IV.[/u] Whatever entities [u]a[/u] and [u]c[/u] may be, [u]a[/u]·[u]c[/u]·[u]a[/u].

[u]Law V.[/u] "No entity is absent from the system which can consistently be
present."
