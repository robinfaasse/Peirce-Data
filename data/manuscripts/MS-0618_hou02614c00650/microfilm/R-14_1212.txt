For this purpose, I must first explain what [del]signification[/del] [ins]significations[/ins] I attach to the word "science, and why; and then what I understand by "a science" and by "a branch of science", and why.

The word [u]science[/u], with the corresponding Latin and Greek words, [u]scientia[/u] and [u]ἐπιστήμη[/u], has been used at different times in three principal senses. As long as Aristotle [del]reigned[/del] [ins]reigned[/ins], science was defined as "knowledge through principles," which was as much as to say, syllogistically demonstrated knowledge. [del]But[/del] [ins]But[/ins] this manifestly conflicts with modern notions, since, in the first place, it excludes all the [ins]physical[/ins] [del]natural[/del], psychological, philo-logical, historical, and other sciences, which for us constitute science [u]par excellence[/u], and in the second place makes the conclusion of a syllogism science, while its at least as well-known premises may not be science.
