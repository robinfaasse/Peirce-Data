[u]Rule XV[/u]. If one graph can be trans-
formed into a second when [del]unenclosed[/del] [ins]the former ^ is[/ins] unenclosed,
it can be transformed into the second
within any even enclosures;
and the second can be transformed
into the first within any odd enclosures.

Proof.
Suppose A can be transformed into B.
By [u]Rule XIV[/u] A " " " " [fig]a circled letter B[/fig]
By [u]Rule VII[/u] [fig]a circled letter B[/fig] " " " " [fig]a circled letter A[/fig]
Then also AC " " " " BC
And AC " " " " [fig]a circled letters BC[/fig]
Therefore [fig]a circled letters BC[/fig] " " " " [fig]a circled letters AC[/fig]

So that if A can be transformed into B
then in our oval B can be transformed into A
whether the oval contains anything else or not.
But this does not prove that if a—c is transformable in b—c
therefore [fig]a circled letter b, then a dash, then a letter c[/fig] is transformable in [fig]a circled letter a, then a dash, then a letter c[/fig]
Thus from "there is a male dog" we may infer "there is a female dog"
But that gives us no right to infer from
"there is a dog not female" to "there is a dog not male."
