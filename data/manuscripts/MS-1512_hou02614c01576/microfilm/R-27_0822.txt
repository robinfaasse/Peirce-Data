The Sun of January 26 printed a poem hesitatingly attributed to Edgar Poe. We only wish we could think it spurious; for then we evidently we could congratulate ourselves in having among us alive a poet who would overmatch Poe in the very province of Poe's strength, — superadding to that a widely different sort of imagination, to which we should then have to attribute the account of how and where the poem was found, with its highly aristocratic old maiden lady calmly enjoying this piece and retaining it for her own exclusive use and behoof and that of a choice of her nearest kinsfolk and intimates, [del]during[/del] [ins]of course,[/ins] near a life-time; — declining with scorn a handsome cash offer for it. To the same imagination we should have to attribute, if not the graphic description of the spot where it is supposed to have been written, at least, the idea that it was this place which evoked its resonance in Poe's spirit, — an idea that affords ordinary people needful help in sympathizing with much that that strange being published.
