Von Guericke knew only one kind of air. What a
sensation must have been created when Black
in 1755 announced the existence of "fixed air," which
we now call carbonic anhydride! In 1766, Cavendish
discovered "inflammable air," now known as hydrogen.
Gases have remarkable physical properties. Faraday
invented a pretty lecture-experiment to show their
differences of density. Two beakers A and B are
suspended from the two arms of a balance,
so as to be in equilibrium, one mouth up, the other
mouth down. Into the former, [del]the[/del] carbonic anhydride,
which is heavy, can be poured, or into the latter,
hydrogen, which is light; in either case, the
equilibrium is disturbed. [ins]The balance is[/ins] Gases [del]are[/del] easily rendered
directly visible in many simple ways. [ins]\*[/ins]

\* The author refers to modern optical "Schlieren-
methode," but the translator does not understand
this expression. Gases are rendered visible most simply
and satisfactorily in the pneumatic trough.
