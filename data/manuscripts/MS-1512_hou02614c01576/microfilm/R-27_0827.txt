[pn]Kelah.[/pn]

"Kelah! Kelah!" cries my spirit
"Kelah!" to Nirvana bear it!
Bear my cry, o sea-winds sweeping! Echo it forevermore!
Mark my soul and body severed,
Where the quick-sands heaved and quivered,
Where the breakers curled and shivered,
On the shelved and sloping shore.

Particularly characteristic of Poe is the manner in which, without saying so, he puts it into the reader's head that the vocable [u]Kelah[/u] means something, something very great and very mysterious, and thus puts the [del]reader[/del] [ins]mind[/ins] into [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] frame of wonder and puzzle [del]which suits the charm[/del] [ins]that[/ins] adapts it to appreciating the [del]sentiment[/del] [ins]turn of the song[/ins] of the piece, [del]which is[/del] [ins]a piece[/ins] Poëan to the last [del]pitch[/del] [ins]degree[/ins], and more poetic than some of his work. The last stanza, however, is infinitely below that of the Raven. It was an experiment that turned out badly.
