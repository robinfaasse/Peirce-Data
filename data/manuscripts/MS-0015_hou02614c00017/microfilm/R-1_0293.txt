[u]Art. 6.[/u] Modern exact logic shows that [del]all[/del] [ins]every operation of[/ins] deductive reasoning consists of four steps as follows:

1[ins]st[/ins], a diagram, or visual image, whether composed of lines, like a geometrical figure, or an array of signs, like an algebraical formula, or of a mixed nature, like a [del]logical[/del] graph, is constructed, so as to embody in iconic form, the state of things asserted in the premise (there will be but one premise after all that is known [del]to be[/del] and is pertinent is collected into one copulative proposition).

2[ins]nd[/ins], Upon scrutiny [del]of this diagram[/del], the mind is led to suspect that the sort of information sought maybe [del]found[/del] discovered, by modifying the diagram in a certain way. This experiment is tried.

3[ins]rd[/ins], The results of the experiment are carefully observed. This is genuine experiential observation, even though the diagram exists only in the imagination; for after it has once been created, though the reasoner has power to change it, he has no power to make the creation already past and done different from what it is. It is, therefore, just as real an object as if drawn on paper. Included in this observation is the analysis of [del]what is seen[/del] and the [del]clothed[/del] representation of it in general language.
