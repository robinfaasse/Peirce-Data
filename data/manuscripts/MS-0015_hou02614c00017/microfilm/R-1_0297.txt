[u]de inesse[/u] having the zero proposition for its consequent. Thus, the whole proposition will be of the form "If A is true, then if B is true, zero." This proposition is true if A is false and also if B is false; but it is false if both A and B are true. Let this whole proposition be made the antecedent of a consequence [u]de inesse[/u] of which the consequent is zero, and the result is equivalent to [ins]the[/ins] assertion that both A and B are true. We thus have the means of asserting two or more propositions simultaneously by means of the single logical form of the consequentia [u]de inesse[/u].

An ordinary Philonian conditional proposition, [del]asserting[/del] [ins]having[/ins] a range of possibility and asserting that [del]in[/del] "If A is true, B is true," that is, that [ins]in[/ins] every possible state of things in which A is true, B is likewise true, may be regarded as a simultaneous assertion of a multitude of propositions each asserting that if a single state of things [ins]in which A[/ins] is true, B is true, each therefore being a [del]proper consequentia de inesse[/del].
