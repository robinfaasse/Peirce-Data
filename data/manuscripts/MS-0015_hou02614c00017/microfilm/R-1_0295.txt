A [u]consequentia simplex de inesse[/u] is a Philonian conditional proposition which refers to the possible in the sense of that which is not known to omniscience to be false. In other words, in such a conditional, the [del]possibole[/del] possible is merely that which is true. [ins]That is to say,[/ins] [del]Hence,[/del] if supposing the proposition "If A is true then B is true" to be understood as a [u]consequentia simplex de inesse[/u], it merely means, Either A is not true or B is true. This proposition is true if [del]A[/del] B is true, and also if A is false; it is false only if A is true while B is false.

The [u]consequentia de inesse[/u] is, according to [ins]The[/ins] [del]my[/del] analysis [ins](which I have long defended)[/ins], by far the most important of logical forms, because upon the one hand it is the essential element of the illative relation, out of which logic springs, while upon the other hand every other [del]logical[/del] form of proposition is expressible as a combination of consequential simplices [u]de inesse[/u]. [ins]*[/ins] The antecedent and consequent of a [u]consequentia de inesse[/u] may be any propositions whatever. They may, therefore, themselves be consequences [u]de inesse[/u]. In fact, every proposition may be regarded as a consequence [u]de inesse[/u], provided we admit the possibility that the consequent may be such as to have not effect.
