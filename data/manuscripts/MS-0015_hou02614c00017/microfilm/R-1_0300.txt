The second mode of necessary deduction is [u]inference by insertion[/u].
The rule is that in any consequence [u]de inesse[/u], in place of any anteceden-
tal of [del]even[/del] order may illatively be substituted any consequence [u]de inesse[/u]
that has that antecedental for its consequent. For example, from $A \prec B$,
we may [del]either[/del] infer either $(C) \prec (A \prec B)$ or $(A) \prec (C \prec B)$.

The rule is that in any odd constituent, $x$, of any consequence [u]de inesse[/u],
substitution may be illatively be made, the place of any even constituent,

first constituent [ins]$z$[/ins] of $x$ in such a manner that the antecedentalities of $x$ and
of $y$ in the conclusion shall agree in being odd or even with their ante-
