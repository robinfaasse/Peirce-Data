I have learned a great deal about it since then, and have learned that there are various ways of reasoning of which I then took no account, and which I then may have pronounced illogical, but which I have found are not absolutely so, and though usually of little or no value, sometimes rising to high importance. In fact, my whole notion of reasoning and of the mind has been quite remodelled since then. Nevertheless, the general contents of that essay have only been strengthened by my subsequent criticism of it, as far as it goes. In particular what I there say of the utter nonsense of one great division of the modern Theory of Probabilities, as taught in all the text-books, is entirely sound. My natural disposition makes me, as soon as I have once published an essay, pooh-pooh it, [del]try to[/del] [ins]pick[/ins] flaws in it, and search for considerations by which I may escape its conclusions; and therefore when this has gone on for several years, and I can find only confirmations of it, and at length yield to my former reasoning, I am all the more confident that it was correct, either just as it stood or with such subtractions as I have been able to make.
