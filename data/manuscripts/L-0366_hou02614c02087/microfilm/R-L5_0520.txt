My dear Cousin Jo;

I feel that it would be an act of treachery merely to answer your questions without pointing out how little they have to do with your ultimate purpose. I have also a difficulty in giving helpful answers owing to my not knowing how much of your mathematics you remember.

The Doctrine of Chances is a branch of Logic; and therefore it has necessarily been an object of close study with me. As a branch of logic, it must rest, first of all on [u]logical[/u] analysis, and secondly, [del]more[/del] as considering complicated states of things, on [u]mathematical[/u] analysis. Since the epoch of Copernicus, [ins]A.D.[/ins] 1543, the science of logic has been totally neglected. So far as I know, I am absolutely the voice crying in the wilderness, [del]cla-[/del] [ins]vox[/ins] clamantes in deserto on the subject, — with the exception of [ins](of course I don't count imbeciles)[/ins] a few who have studied certain special points. But it is not special studies that are needed [del]as yet[/del] in logic, but a general conception of the science and its possible efficient methods.
