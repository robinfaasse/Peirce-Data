In this formula $\pi$ is the ratio of a circumference to its diameter,
$3.141592653589793$; so that $\log 2\pi$ is $0.7981798684$
$e$ is the Neperian base $2.718281828459$; so that $\log e = 0.4342944819032518$
The B's are the so-called Bernoulli's numbers
$B_1 = \frac{1}{6}$ $B_3 = \frac{1}{30}$ $B_5 = \frac{1}{42}$ $B_7 = \frac{1}{30}$ $B_9 = \frac{5}{66}$ $B_{11} = \frac{691}{2730}$ $B_{13} = \frac{7}{6}$ $B_{15} = \frac{3617}{510}$

You can use as many terms as are each smaller than the preceding one,
with increasing accuracy. But when the terms begin to increase, they will
only carry you farther from the truth. If $n$ is pretty large or you
don't care for great precision, you need not use any of the terms
involving B's. The larger $n$ is, the more accurate is the formula.
Nobody would ever use it for a value of $n$ less than 10, because
direct multiplication would be easier. But for the sake of
illustrating its use, I will first calculate by means of it $2!$, then
$20!$ and finally $200!$

The correct results in the two first cases are
$2! = 1 \cdot 2 = 2$ $\log 2 = 0.301029995663981$
$20! = 2^{10+5+2+1} \cdot 3^{6+2} \cdot 5^4 \cdot 7^2 \cdot 11 \cdot 13 \cdot 17 \cdot 19 = 10^4 \cdot 2^{14} \cdot 3^8 \cdot 7^2 \cdot 11 \cdot 13 \cdot 17 \cdot 19$
$2^{14} = 2^4 \cdot 2^{10} = 16 \cdot 1024 = \begin{array}{r} 1632 \\ \underline{\phantom{1}64} \\ 16384 \end{array}$
$3^9 = 3^4 \cdot 3^4 \cdot 3 = \begin{array}{r} \phantom{18}81 \\ \underline{\phantom{18}81} \\ 181 \\ \underline{64\phantom{1}} \\ 6561 \\ \underline{\phantom{656}\cdot 3} \\ 19683 \end{array}$
$7^2 = 49$
