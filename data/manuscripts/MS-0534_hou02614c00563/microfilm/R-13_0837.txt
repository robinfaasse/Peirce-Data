[u]The Logic of Relatives[/u]
By C. S. Peirce.

The theory of relative terms has since Aristotle been recognized as a branch of logic; but it was little studied before [date]1860[/date], and remains very imperfect. The following is an attempt to state the main results reached by DeMorgan and the writer in one line of inquiry and by Mr. A. B. Kempe in another, with references to allied studies by other mathematicians.

In every piece of reasoning, three kinds of signs are required. First, we must indicate what it is we are talking about; no description can do this, for what we refer to might be precisely like something else except in regard to the characters in question. This indication can, therefore, only be made by some sign which forcibly puts the mind into relation with the very thing meant.
