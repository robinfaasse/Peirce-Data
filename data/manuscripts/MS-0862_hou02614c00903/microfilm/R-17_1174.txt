[del]and that[/del] for three reasons. In the first place,
nobody can know, or form any satisfactory judg-
ment as to whether such a case is really inspi-
ration. I am speaking, you understand, not of
these things which [del]as[/del] our own hearts assure
us are true, — such as the doctrine of love, — but
of doctrines which are proposed for our acceptance
because a small number of men are supposed
to have received an [del]assurance or[/del] inspiration
which gives assurance of their truth. In such
a case, it is very difficult for a cautious and
intelligent mind to satisfy himself that the inspi-
ration was real. In the second place, even if it
were real inspiration, it would be most rash
to conclude with any high degree of confidence
that the dogma is true. The divine mind is in-
scrutable; the purposes of God cannot be fathomed.
How can we tell that He would not choose to
inspire a man with a false belief, — especially when
we see that He has allowed the most ruinous errors
to grow up in every church and every religion.
