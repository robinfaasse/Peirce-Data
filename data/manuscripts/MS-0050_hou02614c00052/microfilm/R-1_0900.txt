Attraction
Ms. 501
[/marginalia]

An Illustration of the fact that [del]one[/del] [ins]two[/ins] series of agents
may act, the agents of each series directly only upon
those of the same series; and yet the agents of each
series may act upon those of the other without any
[u]tertium quid[/u].

(1). I shall suppose a particle to be attracted to a
fixed centre by a force inversely proportional to the
cube of the distance; and I shall proceed to inquire what
the form of the trajectory, or orbit, will be, and further,
how much time will be consumed in passing from
one point on the trajectory to another; after which I
shall point out the bearing of the solution, with the
consideration of certain doubts.

(2) Let [u]r[/u] be the variable distance of the particle from
the centre, otherwise called the radius vector. Let [u]r[/u]₀ be
the value of [u]r[/u] at the starting-point ([u]carceres[/u]).

t is the time elapsed since an arbitrary epoch. t₀ will
the [del]time[/del] [ins]time[/ins] which has elapsed when the particle leaves the starting-point.
