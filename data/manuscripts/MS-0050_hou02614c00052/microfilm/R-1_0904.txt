Let us apply our formula for $r$ to ascertaining what $r$ will be
when
$$t - t_c = \frac{r_a}{\sqrt{k}} \sqrt{r_a^2 - r_c^2} \pm \tau$$

Since $(t - t_c)^2 = \frac{1}{k} r_a^2 (r_a^2 - r_c^2) \pm 2 \frac{r_a}{\sqrt{k}} \sqrt{r_a^2 - r_c^2} \tau + \tau^2$

we shall have
$$r = \sqrt{r_c^2 + 2 (r_a^2 - r_c^2) \pm 2 \tau \sqrt{k} \sqrt{1 - \frac{r_c^2}{r_a^2}} - (r_a^2 - r_c^2) \mp 2 \frac{\sqrt{k}}{r_a} \sqrt{r_a^2 - r_c^2} \tau - \frac{k}{r_a^2} \tau^2}$$
$$= \sqrt{r_a^2 - \frac{k}{r_a^2} \tau^2}$$

This shows that the greatest distance occurs [ins]at the instant[/ins] when $t - t_c = \frac{r_a}{\sqrt{k}} \sqrt{r_a^2 - r_c^2}$
and at any interval $\tau$ before or after that instant the distance of
the particle falls short of $r_a$ by the same amount, whether this in-
stant be before or after that defined.

(20) If the particle had been shot in any other direction
than directly away, but with the same initial velocity, it
evidently could not go further from the centre than when shot
directly away. Hence $r_a$ is [del]at least[/del] the maximum distance that
the particle can go, however it is shot, so long as it starts from
a point at distance $r_c$ from the centre of attraction, and with
the same velocity $v_c = \sqrt{k} \sqrt{\frac{1}{r_c^2} - \frac{1}{r_a^2}}$
