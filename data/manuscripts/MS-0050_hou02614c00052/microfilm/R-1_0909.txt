whence
$dt = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}} \frac{r dr}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{A^2}{k} - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}} \frac{d(r^2)}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{A^2}{k} - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}}}$

Again applying the equation of (14) we get from this last
$d\theta = \frac{A}{\sqrt{k}} \frac{dr}{r \sqrt{1 - \frac{A^2}{k} - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}}}$ .

These two equations have forms specially considered in
all treatises on the calculus. The former, which is quite simple,
gives the law, or regularity, of the times at which the particle
attains different distances from the centre; the latter, slightly
more complicated, gives the form of the trajectory.
