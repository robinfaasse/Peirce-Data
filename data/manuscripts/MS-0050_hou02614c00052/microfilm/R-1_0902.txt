$$\frac{dv}{du} \times \frac{d}{dv} (\frac{du}{dv}) \times 2 \frac{du}{dv} = 2 \cdot \frac{d}{dv} (\frac{du}{dv}) = 2 \frac{d^2u}{dv^2}.$$

Applying this to the case in hand, we have
$$\frac{d^2r}{dt^2} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{d}{dr} (\frac{dr}{dt})^2$$

(7) Substituting, in this last equation, the value of $\frac{d^2r}{dt^2}$ of (5), we get
$$\frac{d}{dr} (\frac{dr}{dt})^2 = - \frac{2k}{r^3}$$

Whence by integration
$$(\frac{dr}{dt})^2 = \frac{k}{r^2} - C$$

where $C$ is an arbitrary constant. The quantity $(\frac{dr}{dt})^2$ is the square of the upward velocity (i.e. outward from the centre). Since in this case it is the [del]resultant[/del] [ins]practical[/ins] velocity, it is the old vis viva.

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{k}{r^2} - C}$$
