[date]1907 June 24[/date]

Dr. Bliss Perry;
Dear Sir:

I today submitted to Mr. Scudder
an article by me entitled "Guessing". As
this is, I fancy, a good deal removed from
the type of Atlantic Articles, I beg to
submit the following remonstrance, to
go along with my proposal.

I myself rarely care to look at a
Magazine article; and I find that the
same is true of the kind of men I find
companionable. I know that the Maga-
zines must have a sale. Naturally,
they must have, considering how much
they offer for 25 or 35 cents. It is their
advertisements that enable them to do
this, of course. [del]I myself, when closely
connected with the Century Company
paid $300 or $350 (I forget which) a month
for twelve months [ins]for that company[/ins], &
I found it remunerative.[/del] They are, the
Atlantic, the Century, just books of
advertisements, with interior pages of

[page break]

I have known since I was ten [del]and the
senior partner Mr. H. O. Houghton (whom
and greatly respected)[/del] is of a different
class from the old publishers, who still
exist in this country. But example is infectious.

I know from forty years' experience as
a writer for the Nation, and from twenty with
The Monist (not to speak of other journals)
that my articles would attract readers, and
[del]would[/del] [ins]be[/ins] talked about among the most
reflective class of readers, and would be
found agreeable by others, — but I much
confess that they utterly fail to satisfy the
[del]ordinary editor's[/del] first requisite that they
should have [del]no[/del] freshness in them.

Apologizing for this long apology,
I remain with great respect
C. S. Peirce
