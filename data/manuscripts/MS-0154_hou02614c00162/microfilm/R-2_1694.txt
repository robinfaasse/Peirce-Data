[u]On the Problem of Coloring a Map.[/u]

[u]Definitions.[/u] An ordinary surface is a surface on which any two self-returning lines that cross one another at one point cross one another at another point.

A map is a figure, covering a surface and consisting of these three kinds of parts: 1[sup]st[/sup], regions, each of which is a [del]connected[/del] [ins]in one piece[/ins] [del]single[/del] surface; 2[sup]nd[/sup], boundaries, each of which is a [del]connected[/del] [ins]single[/ins] line in one piece, being either at the [del]common[/del] [ins]mutual[/ins] limit of two regions
