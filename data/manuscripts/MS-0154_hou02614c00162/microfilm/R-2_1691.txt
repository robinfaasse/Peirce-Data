It will be noticed that according to this definition, there
is one and only one, collection that has no ultimate parts,
being the collection [u]nothing[/u], which is comprised among
the collections of As, no matter what the As may be.
Moreover, according to this definition, given an indi-
vidual object, there is a collection having that object as its
sole ultimate part. The collection is not exactly identical
with this sole ultimate part, since the former has only a
being [u]in posse[/u], while the latter may have a positive
independent existence.

If [del]one collects[/del] every ultimate part of one collection
is an ultimate part of a different collection, then
some ultimate part of the latter is not an ultimate
part of the former (otherwise, the collections would
be the same), and the former is said to be a part of
the latter. Evidently, a part of a part is a part, provided
the word "part" be always taken in the same sense. But
collections being individual objects may become
ultimate parts of collections of collections, and the
parts of the former are necessarily parts of the
latter; [del]in[/del] indeed, strictly speaking, they cannot be so.
For, applying the definition of parts, the ultimate
parts of the former are not among the ultimate parts
of the latter, which are, on the contrary, collections of ul-
timate parts of the latter. For example, every pair of As
is a part of a triad of As; but no pair of As is any part of a
collection of triads of As, not even if it be a part of
every one of the triads that are the ultimate parts of that col-
lection.
