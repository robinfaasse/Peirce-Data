On the [del]Problem[/del] [ins]Theorem[/ins] of Coloring a Map.
By C. S. Peirce.

Some time in the early sixties Augustus De Morgan
mentioned in the [u]Athenæum[/u] that experience had shewn
that four colors would suffice to distinguish confine
regions upon any map, and pronounced it a reproach
upon logic and upon mathematics that no proof had
been found of a proposition so simple. It is plain that
he had attempted the demonstration in vain. In [date]1878[/date],
Arthur Cayley took up the question, but only succeeded
in showing why the proof would naturally be very
difficult. In the following year Mr. A. B. Kempe proposed
a simple demonstration, which is unfortunately fallacious.

it is thrown into algebraical form. Let the regions be
numbered, 1, 2, 3, etc. Let the four colors be A, B, C, D. Let
any of these letters with a number subscribed, as $A_3$, be a
quantity which is equal to one if the region bearing that
number has the color signified by that letter, and which
