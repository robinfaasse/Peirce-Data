If the surface is a plane of projective geometry X
$K=1$, $\Pi=1$,
and we can put
$\chi_R = \frac{1}{2} [7 + \sqrt{25}] = 6$.

Fig. 1 shows this case.

If the surface is an anchor-ring, we have
$K=2$, $\Pi=1$,
and can put
$\chi_R = \frac{1}{2} [7 + \sqrt{24+25}] = 7$.

Fig. 2 shows this case, after the bar of the ring has been cut, then straightened into a cylinder, then slit down the side, and lastly developed into a plane.

If the surface is [del]of[/del] [ins]that of[/ins] a spheroidal body with two holes through it, forming two fornices, or bridges-with-tunnels, we have
$K=4$, $\Pi=1$
and to make the quantity under the radical a perfect square, we must take $x=2$. Then,
$\chi_R = \frac{1}{2} [7 + \sqrt{72-16+25}] = 8$.
