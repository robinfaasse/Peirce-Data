Another general logical remark which will prove pertinent
is that there is another important kind of forms besides
generals, namely abstractions. The abstract and the general
are often looked upon as much the same thing; but that is
inexact logic. The abstract is a form, since its being consists
in the possibility of something else. But no reactional deter-
mination will convert it into an ordinary concrete existence,
