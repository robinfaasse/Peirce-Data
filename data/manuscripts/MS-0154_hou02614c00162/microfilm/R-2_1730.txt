[u]On the Map-Coloring Problem.[/u]

Let us define an [u]ordinary surface[/u] as a surface on which no self-returning filament that so moves as continually to quit altogether every point of its instantaneous place can ever return to its original place.

By a [u]map[/u] is meant a figure drawn on a surface and consisting usually of three kinds of parts: [ins]1[sup]st[/sup],[/ins] [u]regions[/u], each of which is a surface in one piece, that is, such that any two points within it or at its limit may be connected by a line wholly within it; 2[sup]nd[/sup], [u]boundaries[/u], each of which is a line in one piece (that is such that a particle may move [ins]continuously[/ins] from any point within it or at its extremity to any other without leaving the line)
