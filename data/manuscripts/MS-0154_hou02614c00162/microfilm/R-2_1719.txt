For this purpose, I define a [u]collection[/u] as anything
which (if it has any parts) has ultimate parts. The
clause of this definition that I write in parenthesis
has the effect of including [del]a[/del] single objects, as well as
nothing at all, collections. I substantially adopt the
idea of the following definition from Dr. Cantor:

If there is any relation in which every ultimate
part of a collection A stands to an ultimate part
of a collection B, while no two ultimate parts of A
stand in this relation to the same ultimate part of
B, then the collection A is said to be [u]as small as[/u] B.

If A is as small as B and B is as small as A, A and
B are said to be [u]equal to[/u] one another.

If A is as small as B, but B is not as small as A, A
is said to be [u]smaller than[/u] B, and B to be [u]greater than[/u] A.
