This will be found by far the simplest method of calculating a circulating decimal.

Every [del]rational[/del] [ins]expression of a[/ins] fraction [del]expres[/del] in decimals must end in a circulating decimal whose period embraces a number of decimal places which is a divisor of [del]one less than[/del] [ins]the totient of[/ins] the [del]numerator[/del] [ins]denominator[/ins] of the fraction. This is substantially Fermat's theorem. If the [del]circulating decimal is[/del] [ins]places of the circulating decimal are[/ins] one less than the denominator (which of course, only happens when that is prime) the different multiples less than 1 are the different arrangements of the circulating decimal. Otherwise, there is some number of circulating decimals whose different arrangements end the different multiples. These are matters well known; so that I do not stop to prove them.
