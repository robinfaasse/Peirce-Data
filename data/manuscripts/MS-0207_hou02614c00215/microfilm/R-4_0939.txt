For when fractions are equal, the one whose numerator and denominator are doubles of that of lowest terms is the next to the smallest, and all others are inserted between these two in the row. The fractions of any given odd denominator having been [del]thus[/del] inserted in the row, the question arises, where are the fractions of the next higher denominator, which will be even, to be inserted? Let $2k$ be this even denominator. Every even multiple of $\frac{1}{2k}$ will be the equal to a fraction [ins]of lower terms with [del]half its numerator[/del][/ins] and denominator and must, therefore, be inserted directly after it. It will usually happen that that lower fraction is followed by the sign $<$. Hence, the new fraction must be inserted to the left of that sign. These even multiples will occur at every $(4k-2)^{\underline{\text{th}}}$ interval. If the odd multiples are inserted at the $(2k-1)^{\underline{\text{th}}}$ intervals half way between, they will give the right alternation of intermediate fractions. These fractions must each be greater than the fraction next to the left of it and less than the fraction next to the right unless these two are equal to each other. Consequently, [del]when it fall[/del] [ins]whether the sign[/ins] before it be $<$ or $=$, the same sign should be repeated after it. All the fractions of odd denominators may be inserted [ins]in the intervals[/ins] immediately after signs and the same rules will be applicable.
