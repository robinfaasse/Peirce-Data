The testimony of the senses enables us to judge that one thing is more or less than another thing. If A is greater than B, B is not greater than A. If one thing is greater than another which is itself greater than a third, the first is greater than the third. Any relation of which [del]these[/del] [ins]two[/ins] propositions of [del]these[/del] [ins]this[/ins] form[ins]s[/ins] are true may be considered as a quantitative relation.

A system of numbers, or mathematical quantities, is a system of signs by means of which the ordinal place of an object in a quantitative series may be compactly expressed. Quantities express absolutely nothing except the relative place in a succession. Numbers, besides expressing this, also serve as apparatus to ascertain it, by facilitating those experiments on the place of an object in the quantitative series which we call measurement and counting.

Collectional quantity, or multitude, is often evident to sense. Even a cat has a vague idea of [ins]one collection of[/ins] her kittens being fewer [del]than another[/del] [ins]or more[/ins].
