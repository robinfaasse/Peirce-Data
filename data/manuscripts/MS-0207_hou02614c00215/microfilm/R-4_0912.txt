We may say that all the fractions whose red spots lie in one straight line form a [del]row[/del]. A vertical row may be called a [del]column[/del] [ins]file[/ins], a horizontal row a rank. A row which passes through the point of indeterminacy [del]0[/del]/0 may be called a radiant. If the general fraction be [frac]\frac{yM+xR}{yN+xS}[/frac] the equation of a row will, of course, be of the form
[frac]\frac{x}{x_{0}}[/frac] + [frac]\frac{y}{y_{0}}[/frac] = 1
where $x_{0}$ and $y_{0}$ are the values of $x$ and $y$ when $y$ and $x$, respectively vanish. If $y_{0}N = x_{0}S$ the [del]row[/del] denominators of all the fractions of the row will be equal; for multiplying the above form of the equation of the row by $y_{0}N$, and substituting [frac]\frac{y_{0}}{x_{0}}[/frac]$N = S$, it becomes
$xS + yN = y_{0}N = x_{0}S$
If $y_{0}N = x_{0}S = I$, the row marks the limit of fractions used. If on the other hand $y_{0}M = x_{0}R$, all the fractions of the row have the same numerator. The table is not really limited by the radiant rank and file which appear as its limits in the diagram. On the contrary there will be two oblique radiants the equation of one of which will be $yM + xR = 0$ while that of the other will be $yN + xS = 0$. All the fractions in any one radiant have the same value. In the former of these two radiants the value is 0 in the latter $\infty$. Between them, the fractions will be negative, and therefore they are not strictly rational. On the opposite side of both these radiants numerators and denominators are both negative. All these fractions are upon radiants which extend into the rational part of the table.
