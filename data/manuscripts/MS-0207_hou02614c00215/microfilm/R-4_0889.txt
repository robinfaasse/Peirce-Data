But if $N$ is even, of the two numerators $M$ and $M+1$ one is
even, so that either $\frac{M}{N}$ or $\frac{M+1}{N}$ has both numerator and deno-
minator equal to the double of those of another fraction. Count-
ing this as $1/2$, there will be as before $1/2 N - 1/2$ fractions of lower
denominator than $N$ between $\frac{M}{N}$ and $\frac{M+1}{N}$.

We thus obtain the following simple rule for arranging the fractions
in the order of their values.

Within the interval of values adopted insert all fractions of lower
denominators before those of higher denominators; and of fractions of
any one denominator those of lower numerator before those of higher nume-
rator. In [del]writing[/del] [ins]writing[/ins] each fraction draw a line above it and below it. Enter the
fraction $\frac{M}{N+1}$ next below the lower line of $\frac{M}{N}$ and enter [del]so[/del] $\frac{M+1}{N+1}$ $N$ lines
above $\frac{M}{N+1}$.
