[u]In reference to the principles of measurement,[/u]

1. A spread which can be transported so as
more than to cover a former place is infinite.

2. A spread which can be transported so as partly
to fall short of partly extend beyond a former
place but cannot more than cover a former
place is finite.

Commensurable ratios of magnitude are determined
by counting successive superpositions.

Incommensurable ratios depend on approxima-
tions which require us to take notice that of [del]magni-[/del]
tudes not coincident one is more nearly so than
another.
