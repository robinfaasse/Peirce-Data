Denoting their copulas by $\leftarrow$, $\prec$, and $\Delta$, these may
be defined as follows.

If [del]$x \prec$[/del] $x \prec y$ and $y \prec z$ then $x \prec z$. And $x \prec x$
If $x \leftarrow y$ and $y \leftarrow z$ then $x \leftarrow z$. And $x \not\leftarrow x$.
If $x \ \Delta \ y$ and $y \ \Delta \ z$ then $x \ \Delta \ z$.

Here the straight vertical line through a copula sign
negatives the copula.

If the only copula of an algebra is $\prec$ then all its
expressions may be thrown into classes so that

1. If $x$ and $y$ belong to the same class $x \prec y$ and $y \prec x$

2. These classes form an indefinite number of series
so that for the same series
$x \prec y \prec z \prec w \prec$ etc

& these different series may be connected so that
if $x$ & $y$ are expressions in different series we may
have $x \not\prec y$. If there is but one such series we have a
single algebra. If there are more than one, we have a
multiple algebra.

If the copula of an algebra is $\leftarrow$ all its expressions
belong to one series of classes such that [del]if[/del]

1. If [del]$x$ and[/del] $y$ belong to the same class $x \leftarrow y$ and $y \leftarrow x$

2. [del]The[/del] The series of classes are such that
$a \not\leftarrow b \not\leftarrow c \not\leftarrow d \not\leftarrow e$ etc
and if $x \leftarrow y$ then $y \not\leftarrow x$.
