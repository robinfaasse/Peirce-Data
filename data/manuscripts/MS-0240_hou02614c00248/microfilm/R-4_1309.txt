Let [ins]capital[/ins] letters of the alphabet be objects which may differ in respect or have some attributes in common.

Let [del]A_B[/del] be the measure of the attributes which A has in common with B, [ins]to the same amount.[/ins] And so with other letters.

Let [del]A^B[/del] denote the measure of [del]the A[/del] in those respects in which A differs from B.

Let the difference between A & B be denoted thus A^B - B^A

Differences between different pairs of objects may themselves differ in some respects and agree in others. Let (A^B - B^A)_C be the difference between A & B in that respect in which C & D do not differ; and let (A^B - B^A)^C be their difference in those respects in which C & D differ.

Differences of objects may be considered as differing in respects which have nothing in common. Thus that A & B differ in the respect in which they differ by [del]any[/del] [ins]certain[/ins] amount [del]denoted by[/del] may be implied in the fact that they differ in the respect in which E & F differ by the amount [del]E^F - F^E[/del]. Let this amount of this implied difference in the respect in which C & D differ be denoted by (A^B - B^A)^[EF]CD
