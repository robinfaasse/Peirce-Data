Lemmas.

Since number is a sum of equals, whatever two things are
in numerical ratio are sums of equals. Incommensurables may
be said to be sums of infinitesimal equals. Hence, if the total dif-
ferences between two pairs of objects are in numerical ratio,
their differences in every respect are in the same ratio, & vice versa.
Thus, $(A^B - B^A) : (C^D - D^C) = (A^B - B^A)^{XY} : (C^D - D^C)^{XY}$
expresses that $(A^B - B^A)$ & $(C^D - D^C)$ are in numerical ratio.
From the above it follows also that
$(A^B - B^A) : (C^D - D^C) = (A^B - B^A)^{[XY]ZW} : (C^D - D^C)^{[XY]ZW}$
When two differences as the above are in numerical ratio $A_B = C_D$
for did they not agree in the same respects they would not be the sums
of equals. Hence
$(A^B - B^A) : (C^D - D^C) = (A^B - B^A)^{[XY]AB} : (C^D - D^C)^{[XY]AB}$
In general $A_B = B_A$.
From the above it follows that the value of the fraction $\frac{(A^B - B^A)^{MN}}{(A^B - B^A)}$
is independent of any numerical change in the value of $A^B - B^A$.
If $(C^D - D^C)$ & $(O^P - P^O)$ are not in simple numerical relation
to $(A^B - B^A)$ & [del]$(L^M - M^L)$[/del] but such that
$(A^B - B^A) : (C^D - D^C) = (L^M - M^L) : (O^P - P^O)$
And if $(L^M - M^L) = (A^B - B^A)^{L_M}$ & $(O^P - P^O) = (C^D - D^C)^{O_P}$
Then $\frac{(A^B - B^A)^{L_M}}{A^B - B^A} = \frac{(C^D - D^C)^{O_P}}{C^D - D^C}$
That is the value of this fraction is also independent of the
[del]value[/del] of $A_B$ so long as a proper relation [ins]is kept up[/ins] [del]exists[/del] between $A_B$ & $L_M$
