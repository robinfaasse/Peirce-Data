# National Academy of Sciences.

### Office of the Home Secretary.

ARNOLD HAGUE, Home Secretary,
U. S. Geological Survey.

Washington, D. C.,
April 10, 1902.

Prof. Charles S. Peirce,
Milford,
Pennsylvania.

My dear Prof. Peirce;

On my return from Cuba, where I spent the month of March, I find two communications from you; one of March 12th, the other of March 31st. In reply to the latter, I will say that the notices of your three communications to be presented to the Academy have been received and will be printed in the list of papers to be read. I will also say that the reception, given at the Arlington Hotel by Mr. Agassiz on the evening of April 17th, includes ladies as well as members of the Academy.

In regard to Prof. William James I may say, that his name went before the section of Anthropology for their recommendation. The Committee on Anthropology consists of nine members. Before the name of a candidate can be presented to the Academy for election, it is necessary that it should receive the votes of a majority of the members of the committee to which the name was referred. [del]Prof. James only received four votes; and, if I remember correctly, only six votes were cast, as to that, Prof. F. W.
