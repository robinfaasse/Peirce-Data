Introduction.

Of the Place of Logic among the Sciences.

Logic proper is the theory of reasoning. That is to say, it is the study which aims to ascertain what must be the perceptible relations between possible facts in order that the knowledge that certain ones are true may warrant us in assuming that [ins]certain[/ins] others are true.

This definition is opposed to the treatment of logic as a branch of psychology or as founded upon psychology. For according to our definition it makes no difference to the logician whether a fact is thought in this way or in that way unless the distinction will alter our right to draw a conclusion. If it be true that Enoch and Elijah were men and that all men [del]are mortal[/del] [ins]die[/ins], then it is true that Enoch and Elijah die, no matter [del]what we[/del] [ins]how or what we[/ins] think. If I endeavor to separate the fundamental stars into two equal classes [del]of which[/del] [ins]such that[/ins] the stars of the first class shall be generally nearer to us than those of the second by putting into the first all those stars which have any two of the following quantities greater than the majority of the fundamental stars, namely, 1[u]st[/u], their brightness, 2[u]nd[/u], their proper motion perpendicular to the suns path, 3[u]rd[/u], their proper motion along the suns path, then it is logically pertinent to inquire how I came to pick out these criteria, and whether or not, the parallaxes of the stars are not known.
