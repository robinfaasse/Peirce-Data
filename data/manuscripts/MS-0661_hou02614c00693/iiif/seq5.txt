To do that is the business of Logic: the central part of logic,
whose proper name is [u]Logical Critic[/u], has no other business
than that. Now when one considers the immense number
of reasonings upon which [del][fig]figure[/fig][/del] our sciences depend, not to
speak of our affairs, and how novel those of them are which
constitute the great strides of human knowledge, we cannot
fail to be impressed with the vast importance of Logic's being
rendered as certain as possible. For any uncertainty in
a single conclusion of Critic means that a considerable
part of our reasonings are uncertain; and if they are uncer-
tain, some of them will be wrong, and these will be most likely
to be the most important ones. Logic, then, [del]must[/del] needs to
adopt for itself a standard of certainty higher than any other.
