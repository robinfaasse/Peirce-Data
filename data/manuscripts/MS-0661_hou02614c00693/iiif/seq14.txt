The quadrants of this circle illustrate the truth of the four forms of simple categorical propositions [fig]diagram of a circle divided into quadrants labeled A, E, I, O with text inside each quadrant[/fig] [del]con[/del] concerning the Z's therein being capitalized

A = Any Z is capital
E = No Z is capital
I Some Z is capital
O Some Z is not capital.

Aristotle does not shirk, as most modern logicians do the difficult but obligatory task of treating "modals," that is propositions about what [u]can[/u] [u]be[/u], [u]must[/u] [u]be[/u], etc. Possibility and necessity may be taken in several senses.
