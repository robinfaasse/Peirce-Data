[u]The Art of Reasoning.[/u]

[u]Chapter II. What is a Sign?[/u]

§31. This is a most necessary question, since all reasoning is an interpretation of signs of some kind. But it is also a very difficult question, calling for deep reflection.

It is necessary to recognize three different states of mind. First, imagine a person in a dreamy state. Let us suppose he is thinking of nothing but a red color. Not thinking about it, either, — that is, not [del]asking nor answering[/del] [ins]asking nor answering[/ins] any questions about it, not even saying to himself that it pleases him, but just contemplating it, as his fancy brings it up. Perhaps, when he gets tired of the red, he will change it to some other color, — say, a turquoise blue, — or a rose-color; — but if he does so, it will be [del]at will[/del] in the play of fancy without any reason and without any compulsion.
