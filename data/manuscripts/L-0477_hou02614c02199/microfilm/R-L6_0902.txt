I never myself met Peirce. He wrote to me in 1906 a long letter
informing me that he approved of my correct logic in "Heredity in
Royalty". I had never heard of the man who wrote thus [del]on[/del] [ins]or[/ins] [del]methods[/del] [ins]method[/ins].
I hastened to find out who he was, and Professor Sedgwick told me
all about him and that everyone had a great admiration for his intellect
and that I ought to feel flattered to have received such attention.
I always have felt that way.

The necessary thing to do is to have three or four carbon copies
made of that long letter (the other letters have no public interest in
any way) send one to Professor Cohen and one to Dr. Weiss. If either
of them need to see the original letter O.K. but I want to be sure to
have one type-written copy of it in case it might be lost.

Very likely Cohen and Weiss would be interested to read this letter
that I have written to you. So you may pass it on to them in any form
you like either in the original or in typed copies.

You have been very good about this and I feel very flattered that
I have been asked and have not myself asked for this pleasant recall
of bygone days.

Ever affectionately yours,

(Signed) Frederick Adams Woods.
