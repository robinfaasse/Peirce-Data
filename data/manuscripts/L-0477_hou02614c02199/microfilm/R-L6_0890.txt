I was always interested to learn more about the character of this remarkable man, whom so many people seemed to revere and whom I had never met, yet who had done me the honor of writing me such long letters containing such thrilling praise, and it was he who wrote me in the first place.

I was introduced by Mrs. Charles Inches. Becker did not seem very enthusiastic about Peirce. He praised his intellect, but condemned his character, at least in regard to certain weaknesses. He told me that Peirce stole Professor Schaler's overcoat. One evening Peirce was at Schaler's house. Shortly after this, meeting Peirce on the street, he was surprised to see him wearing his coat. "Why that's my overcoat!" he said. "Yes" said Peirce appearing to think this nothing of importance, and they went on talking of something else.

Becker told me that Peirce was always experimenting with drugs and their effect on himself. I remembered he wound up his conversation about Peirce by saying, "We have had good work from Peirce, but we should have had more." Becker, it seems to me, was a man of such an entirely different temperament and mentality that his attitude towards Peirce is easy enough to understand.

Josiah Royce told me a good deal about Peirce and his private life, - of the scandal he caused in Cambridge, which at that time (as Royce expressed it) was a little village where everyone knew everyone else. He said that his second wife was devoted to him. Royce said that Peirce was (as everyone knew) poverty stricken and in debt.
