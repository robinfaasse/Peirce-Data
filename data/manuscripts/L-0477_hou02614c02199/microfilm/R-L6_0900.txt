Dr. Albert Edward Wiggam

Dear Friend Wiggam:

In regard to the letter or letters from Charles Peirce I feel pretty sure there is there in your vault only one of them and that that is a big one of about 50 pages written from time to time as he felt able to do so. He had great difficulty in forming the letters of his words.

I would like very much for Professor Cohen and any others to have copies of this letter as I judge from its content, that this was about his last communication with the outside world. He lived in retirement and I believe (from what Royce said in real financial distress) in a small house in a small town in Pennsylvania. One time I remember Royce said "I suppose he is starving". At another time he said. "He is a hard man to help."

Peirce was a great epicure and loved luxuries. When he had money he would spend it on food and wine. He was partly fond of a good Burgundy and of Rocquefort cheese.

He had nice rugs and furniture in his little house. John Stone Stone the wireless inventor and mathematician, who made a pilgrimage to his home said he had the whole attic made into a comfortable study. He went up by a ladder and pulled the ladder and closed the trap door. Thus he avoided interruptions or could escape his creditors.
