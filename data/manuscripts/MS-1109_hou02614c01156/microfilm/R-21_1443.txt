<u>Experiment No 3</u>. Imagine that while you are at a lecture, a
messenger brings you a note from a friend to whom you have lent
the key of your apartment for a necessary purpose, saying "The
key seems to fit; yet the door will not open." You reflect an in-
stant and then write "Try is a double lock. Turn the key a second
turn and it will open." You give this to the messenger who de-
parts. Now I ask you to observe [del]how[/del] that, while there are here
sundry cases of reaction between yourself and the outward world,
all of which involve the second element of experience, yet it
was the sense of the whole of your friend's note, and not any
individual part of it which caused you to write the reply. Nei-
ther did the note exercise any compulsion or force upon you,
but its meaning made it seem reasonable to you and
conformable to your purposes to answer it. And though in
answering it every stroke and dot required an effort, yet these
efforts had nothing to do with making the whole answer such
as was reasonably sure to lead to the door being opened. [ins]or signified[/ins]
