I see an inkstand and I imagine another standing beside
it. The latter is very faint, but were it as vivid as it would be
to some painters, were it as vivid as the one I see, it would still
be a mere fancy, possessing no self-identity, since I can at will
make it tall or short, large or small, [del]glass or clean or dirty.[/del] It thus
has no individuality. For an individual has self-identity. But
perhaps the inkstand I think I see is only a hallucination. I
call in my wife. She sees it too & cannot unsee it. Perhaps, however,
we are mad together. To test this, I set up a camera before it, &
I predict that if it is a real inkstand the camera will show it.
My prediction is verified; and the [u]insistency[/u] of this image in
retaining definite characters, regardless of who thinks of it,
and for all purposes, [del]leads[/del] compels me to think of it as virtually
individual. Here again it is the compulsive force of the percept
that makes me call it a real individual inkstand.
