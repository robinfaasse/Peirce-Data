CANTOR (?)

Dear Sir:

Let $A$, $B$, $C$, etc be individual objects;

Let $lA$ denote any individual which is in a certain relation, signified by $l$, to $A$; [del]etc.[/del] and so $lB$, etc

Let $\bar{l}A$ denote any individual which is [ins]not[/ins] in [del]a certain[/del] [ins]that[/ins] relation to $A$, [del]and so $\bar{l}A$ etc.[/del]
[ins]Let the relation signified by $l$ be such that if $X$ is $lY$, $Y$ is $lX$, and $X$ is $lY$, $Y$ is $lX$.[/ins]

Let $lA \cdot lB \cdot \bar{l}C$ denote any individual which is at once $lA$ and $lB$ but is not $lC$.

Now call $A, B, C$, etc. the zero-$\lambda$s

Call a set of individuals including just one each of all the classes [del]$\bar{l}A \cdot \bar{l}B \cdot \bar{l}C$ etc, $lA \cdot \bar{l}B \cdot \bar{l}C$ etc[/del]
$\bar{l}A \cdot \bar{l}B \cdot \bar{l}C$ etc
$lA \cdot \bar{l}B \cdot \bar{l}C$ etc
$\bar{l}A \cdot lB \cdot \bar{l}C$ etc
$\bar{l}A \cdot \bar{l}B \cdot lC$ etc
$lA \cdot lB \cdot \bar{l}C$ etc
$lA \cdot lB \cdot lC$ etc
in short, including just one individual which is $l$ to none of $A, B, C$ etc
just one which is $l$ to every one of $A, B, C$ etc
just one which is $l$ to any part and non-$l$ to all the others, — call this set I say, the [u]$1^{\text{st}}$ $\lambda$s.[/u]

[del]Treat the [ins]first[/ins] [del]$\lambda$s[/del] [ins]inclusive[/ins] in the same[/del]
Treat the [ins]all the simple[/ins] $n^{\text{th}}$ $\lambda$'s [ins]in the same way[/ins] to make a set of [u]$(n+1)^{\text{th}}$ $\lambda$'s[/u].

Now I say that however small or large the [del]set of[/del] $n^{\text{th}}$ $\lambda$'s may be, the set of [u]$(n+1)^{\text{th}}$ $\lambda$s[/u] is larger. That is to say, there is no relation, $g$, whatsoever such that every $(n+1)^{\text{th}} \lambda$ is in the relation, $g$, to an $n^{\text{th}} \lambda$ to which no other $(n+1)^{\text{th}} \lambda$ is in the relation, $g$. For, let $g$ be what relation it will, I can describe a $(n+1)^{\text{th}} \lambda$ which is not the sole $(n+1)^{\text{th}} \lambda$ to any $n^{\text{th}} \lambda$ whatever; that is, taking any $n^{\text{th}} \lambda$, my described $(n+1)^{\text{th}} \lambda$ shall either not be in the relation $g$ to any $n^{\text{th}} \lambda$, or else only to such $n^{\text{th}} \lambda$s as some other $(n+1)^{\text{th}}$ are in the relation $g$ to.
