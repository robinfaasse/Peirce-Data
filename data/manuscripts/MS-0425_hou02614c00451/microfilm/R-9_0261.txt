But much more than that, they are thoroughly reason-
able; and that which they call for ought to be. Now
that which they demand above all is the fact and the
admission that the world is reasonable, reasonably
susceptible of becoming reasonable, for that is what it
is, and all that it is, to be reasonable, or in other words,
that man is made after his maker's image.

Such is my rude notion of what the method is
to which Hegel endeavors to impart exactitude.
[del]Stated vaguely,[/del] Vague applications of it recommend them-
selves to my faith; but I have never met with an
attempt to state a transcendental argument with
precision which began to convince me. Of any rate,
when I reexamine the [ins]logical[/ins] more or less Hegelian tendency
which have appeared in the last quarter of a century, I must
decline to allow any weight to such flummery. I do not
mention earlier German works, because they are still worse.
