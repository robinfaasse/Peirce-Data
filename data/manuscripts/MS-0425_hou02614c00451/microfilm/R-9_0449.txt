504. No great attention will, however, be paid to indexical propositions. [del]As[/del] [ins]For[/ins] example, we may take a weathercock, which asserts [del](as far as logical recognition is)[/del] that the wind is in a certain direction. The subject of this proposition is the general appearance and situation of the vane, which, through the law of association by contiguity, forces us to think that it tells something about the direction of the wind. Apart from this subject, the proposition reduces to a Rhema, consisting in [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] weather-cock facing in a certain direction. This Rhema [del]is[/del] [ins]maybe regarded as[/ins] an Icon of the wind, [del]with[/del] of which it is a likeness in respect to direction; and it would face in some direction even if there were no wind; Still, more strictly speaking, it is an Iconic Index, since the wind forces it into this likeness.
