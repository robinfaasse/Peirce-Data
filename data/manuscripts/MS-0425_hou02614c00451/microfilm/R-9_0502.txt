Now definitions do not require to be proved, being merely
announcements of how it is intended to use language.

However, it is unnecessary, at this point, to go into
the question of the nature of logical principles. We shall
find that out in due course. Suffice it to say that some of
them are such that doubts may be, and are, felt as to their
truth, until [del]it is sufficient to[/del] [ins]by some process[/ins] we become satisfac-
torily assured of this by a process which, whether it is
reasoning or not, may be admitted in some sort to "take
for granted" the truth of those logical principles.
