The Criterion of the Inconceivability of the opposite, preserved
from oblivion by Herbert Spencer, comes near enough to a truth
to cause some students still to be deceived by it. There are
certain things which, as a matter of fact, have never struck me
as doubtful. There are certain things that you, Reader, do not
doubt. These are pretty nearly the same things; not only because
you and I view the world from the standpoint of a common epoch,
country, and education, but also because our mutual respect,
dear Reader, is such that were I to learn for certain that you doubted
any proposition that I had never doubted, that news would instantly
set me doubting it myself; and I dare say it might have a
similar effect on you to learn that I doubted something, were
all ambiguity of meaning excluded. There are, however, things
which we do not, in fact, either of us, doubt at all, but are
perfectly satisfied to believe. Now that which we do not doubt
