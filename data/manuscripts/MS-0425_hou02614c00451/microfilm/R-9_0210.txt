[u]MINUTE LOGIC.[/u]
By C. S. Peirce.
Chapter I. Intended Characters of this Treatise.
§ 1. Logic's Promises.

Begin, if you will, by calling logic the theory of the conditions which determine reasonings to be secure. A conception at once more exact and more [del]extended[/del] [ins]description[/ins] may be looked for in the sequel; but this definition will serve for our preparatory studies of the yet unvisited country into which we propose to push our explorations.

Logic, then, is a Theory. The end of any theory is to furnish a rational account of its object. I hope and trust that I shall succeed in animating my reader with a theoretic interest in logic; but that is an attitude of mind belonging chiefly to a stage of intellectual development which comes late in life.
