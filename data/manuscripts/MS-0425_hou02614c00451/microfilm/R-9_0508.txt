As to the second interpretation of the objection, I deny
that a conclusion, C, can never be proved by a process,
P, which is such that if C were not true, P would afford
no satisfactory assurance of the truth. On the contrary,
the great majority of experiments which prove anything
are such that if that which they prove had not been
true they would have proved nothing. Suppose for instance,
that I try touching a lighted match to a mixture of gold
dust and alum and from the result of the experiment
draw the very cautious conclusion, which I think nobody
could gainsay, that touching a lighted match to a mixture of
gold dust and alum [del]will[/del] [ins]does[/ins] not invariably and inevitably
produce such an explosion as to shatter the terraqueous globe
into fragments none of them larger than a billiard ball.
Yet if this conclusion had not been true, the experiment would
probably have afforded me no information at all.
