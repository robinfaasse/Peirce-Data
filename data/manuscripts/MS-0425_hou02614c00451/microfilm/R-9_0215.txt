logic, such as these, never useful, instead of being
frequently so, as they are, yet its indirect utility,
through the useful conceptions with which it supplies
us, would be immense.

Meantime, its highest and greatest value of all
is that it affords us an understanding of the processes
of reasoning. That the Platos are thoroughly right in that
estimate will be more and more impressed upon our
convictions as our acquaintance with the science grows.
