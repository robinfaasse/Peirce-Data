The categories at once point out to us that a thing may
be regarded either in itself as a Quale, or in [del]Relation[/del]
Reaction with [del]other[/del] another thing, or as a Sign; and
I find it well to examine these matters somewhat
carefully.
The categories further guide me to a division of [del]three
kinds of[/del] signs into three radically different kinds, which
is a matter of extreme importance in logic. These three
classes of signs are termed Icons, Indices, and Symbols.
That our thoughts are signs is an old and familiar
doctrine. I show that it is only insofar as as thoughts
are symbols that they become subjects of logic, and
further that all symbols are equally so subject.
Logic then is a science of signs, a [u]semiotic[/u]; and in
particular it treats mainly of symbols. It is a [u]symbolic[/u].
