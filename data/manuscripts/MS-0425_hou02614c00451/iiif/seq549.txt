$\delta$ [* Two references which Erdmann gives serve to remind us
that Socrates was distinguished for his continual seeking
for definitions in general terms. Aristotle ([u]Metaph.[/u] III.iv.) says [del]that[/del]
he was the first to investigate the [u]universal definitions of the virtues[/u]. Aris-
totle repeats [del]this[/del] in several other places and Xenophon expresses
the same idea in untechnical language in the fourth book of the
Memorabilia. I regret that, not having [del]it[/del] [ins]that work[/ins] at hand, I [del]cannot[/del] only speak
from memory. There would not be a word of verisimilitude in
Plato's dialogues if it were not so.
The last sentence is suggested, as Erdmann's reference
shows, by Schleiermacher's remark that 'the awakening of
the ideal of knowledge and its first utterances must have
formed the substance of the philosophy of Socrates.'
