     That our thoughts are signs is an old and familiar
[del]idea[/del] doctrine. I show that it is only in so far as thoughts
are signs, and particularly as symbols, that they become
subjects of logic; and further that [del]all[/del] [ins]the rules of[/ins] logic are applicable
to all symbols. Accordingly by regarding logic as a science
of signs or [u]formal semeotic[/u], and in the main as a science
of symbols, or [u]formal symbolic[/u], we accurately cover its
subject matter, and at the same time insure ourselves
against all risk of being led astray into psychology.
The word [u]formal[/u], in this connection, signifies that only
the [del]fa[/del] general conditions to which signs ought to conform
are to be considered.
