"What right have we to assume that ideas refer to [del]external objects?[/del] [ins]objects out of the mind?[/ins]" (Mit welchem Rechte nehmen wir an, dass sich das [u]Vorgestellte[/u] überhaupt auf ein [u]Transcendentale[/u] bezieht?) Certainly psychology has no dealings with objects out of the mind. Still less has logic. Whether or not there is, at all, any such thing as Reality, the logician need not decide. He cannot hide from himself, any more than another man can, that objects very nearly like real things these are; and he cannot pretend to doubt it. But he sees, perhaps more clearly than other men, that approximation to reality and absolute reality [ins]itself[/ins] are two different things. The mathematicians' [u]i[/u], [del]of[/del] which the square is negative unity, approximates to reality.
