Logic. By C. S. Peirce.
Introduction.

Let us begin by calling logic the theory of the condi-
tions which determine reasonings to be secure. We shall
develop a conception of it, at once, more exact and more
extended; but this will serve for our introductory view.
Logic is a theory. The purpose of a theory is to furnish
a rational explanation of [del]the[/del] its object. I hope and trust
that my reader will come to take a theoretical interest in
logic. That, however, belongs to a stage of culture which
the young man whose energies overtop his intellect has
not yet attained. The healthy boy asks, '[u]Oh, what's the use[/u]
of this or that study?' And if his life is an active one, it
may not be until old age that he begins to set a value on
knowing, independently of practical achievement.
