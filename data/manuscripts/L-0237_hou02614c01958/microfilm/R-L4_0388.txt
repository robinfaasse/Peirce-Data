Moreover, the ordinary
rules of conversion
become entirely per-
verted when it is borne
in mind that [ins]the[/ins] subj. [ins]and
pred.[/ins] may be non-existent.

From
[u]All griffins are blue[/u],
it does by no means fol-
low that
[u]Some blue things are griffins[/u].

This last proposition
may be taken as true,
but only on the hypothesis
that we understand by
blue things the sum of
real and imaginary blue
things, that is, that the
principle of contradiction
is false, & that blue things
can at the same time both
be and not be.

[marginalia]Evidently no such inference is intended by logic.[/marginalia]
