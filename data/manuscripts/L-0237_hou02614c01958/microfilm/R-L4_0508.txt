If you want to get up a logical system based on your
[del]ipse dixit[/del] you are welcome to do so; but if you aim
to prove your propositions, I note that you have not
proved this. My idea is to assert in logic only what
can be made evident, not necessarily in the mathe-
matical sense, but yet quite undeniable by any person
who will go through the indicated processes of thought.
For that reason I should prefer when possible to state
arguments with formal accuracy.

If every universe to which a proposition relates
were the universe of reality which you seem to think,
follows from the fact that something is real, then of course,
nobody will dispute that a proposition which relates to the
whole of its universe relates to something that is real.

But if a proposition may have reference to another
universe than that of realities, as when I ask whether
Hamlet was mad, then it does not seem evident.
Of course to say that the thought of Hamlet is a real
thought is utterly beside the question. That does not
make Hamlet a real man, any more than the
fact that the thought of nothingness is a real thought
makes nothingness a real thing.

one thing & that of [del]the[/del] [ins]implied[/ins] inclusion within the
special universe of discourse is another.
