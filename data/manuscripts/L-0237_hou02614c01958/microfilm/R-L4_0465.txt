[u]*[/u]It happens that it is an error to suppose that the character of [del]the[/del] transitiveness of the copula [u]all... is[/u] (what is equivalent to the [u]dictum[/u]) constitutes a sufficient definition of the relation. It does not suffice to distinguish it from the universal negative non-symmetrical copula, [u]none but ... is[/u], which has exactly the same rule of elimination. Just as we have
[u](a < b) (b < c) entails (a < c)[/u],
so we have
[u](a < b) (b < c) entails (a < c)[/u];
this last statement is: If [u]none but a is b[/u], and [u]none but b is c[/u], [u]it follows that none but a is c[/u]. The formal situation is the same in both cases; and this proof, by instance, of the insufficiency of the usual characterization of the copula [u]all ... is[/u] (or, [u]is-wholly[/u]) serves as justification of my claim that it is necessary to consider th[ins]e[/ins] Complete Scheme of propositions. (See Dictionary of Philosophy and Psychology, Art. Proposition) It is not possible, I believe, to distinguish formally between the copulas [ins]<[/ins] and [ins]<[/ins] without introducing a third term (which may or may not be everything-nothing). The two copulas, while alike in the rule for elimination, differ, namely, in their rule for transposition: while we have
[u]a < b + c . [equiv] . a + b < c[/u]
for the first copula, for the other we have
[u]a < b . [equiv] . a + b < c[/u]
In terms of everything-nothing these become respectively:
[u]a < b . [equiv] . a + b < 0 . [equiv] . [infty] < a + b[/u],
and [u]a < b . [equiv] . a + b < [infty] . [equiv] . 0 < a b[/u]
