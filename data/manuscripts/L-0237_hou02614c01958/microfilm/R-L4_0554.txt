Since writing [del]a[/del] [ins]either[/ins] proposition asserts it, writing both seems, without a special and very strange convention, to be asserting both. But this is non-relative multiplication. Therefore, it is a perversity not to understand the writing of propositions together to constitute their non-relative multiplication. It is, therefore, proper to understand the writing together of terms, which are but indefinite propositions (or may be so regarded) as their composition. It ought not to be confounded with multiplication proper, which is rather relative multiplication (at least, so it is in all linear associative algebra, and therefore in ordinary algebra[del],[/del] [ins]and so it is in the usual notation for operations $\Phi_1 \Phi_2$[/ins]) and therefore multiplication when it is used along with composition (as it is highly important it should be) ought to have a different sign & not be signified by simply writing terms together.

This, you will perceive brings us much nearer together. Addition and aggregation have to be used together. To give them the same sign is simply to cling passionately to falsity. As for your $\in$ etc. I am convinced they will not live.

My [ins]Book[/ins] Logic is now going on rapidly to completion. I think it puts the ethics of terminology and notation into a clear light.

Yours very faithfully
C S Peirce
