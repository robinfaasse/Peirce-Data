[u]C S P to Mrs Franklin 1900 Mar 9[/u]

[u]Miscellaneous rules[/u]

Any graph once written may stand
The graph can be erased
Every rule of illative transformation of a whole graph applies to every unenclosed partial graph.
Any graph may have two ovals drawn round it
[del]Any[/del] [ins]An[/ins] index may be written unenclosed.
Any evenly enclosed partial graph maybe erased
Anything [del]oddly enclosed[/del] [ins]may be inserted[/ins] under odd enclosures.
The same graph cannot be written at once enclosed alone and unenclosed with the same connections.
Every possible graph can be written either unenclosed or singly enclosed with any connections
Any graph may be written enclosed and unenclosed [ins]with[/ins] [del]in[/del] any connections both being oddly enclosed.
If a graph is written outside any number of ovals it makes no difference whether it is also written with the same connections inside any of them.
Any evenly enclosed partial graph may be swallowed by any number of ovals outside of which it stands.
Any [del]enclosed[/del] graph enclosed in two or more ovals may be brought out so as to be oddly enclosed.
If an enclosure contains only enclosures, it makes no difference whether a graph be written outside the whole or inside each of the inner enclosures.
A heavy line may be broken at any point and its two ends joined to mutually transformable indices [fig]loves[/fig] then [fig]loves[/fig]
