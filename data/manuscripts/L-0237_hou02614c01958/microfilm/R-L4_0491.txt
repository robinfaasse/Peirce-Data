[fig]arrow pointing right[/fig] My analysis is as follows: A [u]sign[/u] may be defined as such an object
A₁ that it brings another object A₂ into that same relation to an
object A [ins]in[/ins] which [del]it is[/del] itself stands to A, the object A₂ being
itself a sign. I call A the [u]object[/u], and A₂ the [u]interpretant[/u] of
A₁. Of course, a perfect sign would have to have an infinite
series of interpretants. A sign may represent its object by
virtue of a relation which it would have even if the inter-
pretant did not exist. If so this relation may imply no other
character in the sign than that which it might have even if
the object did not exist. In that case, it is an [u]icon[/u]. But if its
character is such that it supposes an object but no interpretant
it is an [u]index[/u]. It is in a relation of blind force to the object.
But [del]icon[/del] [ins]icon[/ins] and index are imperfect signs inasmuch as they do
not become [del]signs[/del] [ins]signs[/ins] until something inessential to them is done.
The [u]symbol[/u] is a sign whose relation to its object essentially involves
the interpretant. Now a symbol may have such a relation to its
object that it does not determine what the interpretant is to be.
If it does determine that interpretant it is an [u]argument[/u]. Its inter-
pretant is its conclusion. If it does not do so, it still needs some
application in an interpretant to give it any signification,
but it does not specify what that [del]interpretant is to be[/del] [ins]application is to be[/ins]. In that case it may determine what its object
is to be, & not merely leave it to be whatever it may be. In that
case it [del]is[/del] asseverates, [del]that is[/del] [ins]that is[/ins], it is [del]either[/del] [ins]either[/ins] true or false, and is
called a [u]proposition[/u], that is an argument with a vague application.
But if it does not determine what its object is to be, it is a [u]proposi-[/u]
[u]tion with its subject left blank[/u], a [u]ῥῆμα[/u], or [u]predicate[/u], or [u]term[/u], as
"— is a man".
