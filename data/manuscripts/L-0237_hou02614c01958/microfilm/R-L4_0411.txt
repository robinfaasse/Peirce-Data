When our ancestors
spoke of the foci of a
conic, they spoke of two
real points. We include
in the meaning of the term
those two points— and also
two other points, their anti-
points, which (the universe
of discourse being, as it is,
the plane of the conic) < 0.

The two points $\alpha \pm i \beta$ are
two points equally distant from
the plane in the perpendicular
at the point $(\alpha = 0, \beta = 0)$, one
above the plane, the other below.
It might be said, then, that
as soon as we speak of imag.
points in a plane, we have
actually enlarged our universe of
discourse without intending it.
