The following method is demanded:-

(15) $\infty \prec AB$
or [del]$\infty \prec$[/del] $(\bar{A} + \bar{B}) \prec 0$

Every thing is both $A$ and $B$, or there is no not-$A$ nor not-$B$. Observe that when either $\infty$ or $0$ is the subject of a proposition the copula is of necessity positive, for the reason that neither $\infty$ nor $0$ can be taken in an undistributed sense. [ins][u]over[/u][/ins] In the same case, the predicate is also universal, because nothing can be greater than infinity (Principle of Contradiction) [ins][u]over[/u][/ins]

We have
$\bar{\infty} = 0$, $\bar{0} = \infty$;
hence there is no real necessity for introducing both symbols into this Calculus.
