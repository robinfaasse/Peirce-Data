Milford Pa 1901 May 5. [pn]VB[/pn]

My dear Mrs Franklin:

In regard to your sheets about Proposition, I think that it would be well to give references to some of the opinions you quote. Who, for example, can take such ground as you describe about the existence of the subject, and about the definition of a proposition which you say is "usually" defined as a judgment expressed in words? This seems to me a monstrous statement & I wonder what your line of reading in logic has been. Also when you say that the scholastic authors [del]usually[/del] regularly express the particular negative in the form Not all A is B. Petrus Hispanus has 'quidam homo non currit' regularly. The Conimbricenses: 'Quidam homo non est justus'. Ockham follows Petrus Hispanus and specially mentions that such propositions as 'Non omnis homo currit', 'Non nullus homo currit' are particular. The Mainz doctors have 'Quidam homo non currit'. You say it necessarily follows that the scholastics [del]of[/del] regularly took O in the form 'Non omnis A est B' from the premiss that AEIO are derived from Greek words. If this be so, it would seem to reduce that premiss to absurdity.

Some of your terminology is open to remark. Thus you take Postulate in Wolf's sense, which has not been accepted out of Germany & confuses the subject as German terminology generally does. You call analytic & synthetic propositions
