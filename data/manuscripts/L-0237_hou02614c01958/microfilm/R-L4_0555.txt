Milford Pa [date]1902 Jun 12[/date]

My dear Mrs. Franklin:

I write to correct a very important error in my letter of the other day. I can make my [del]idea[/del] [ins]meaning[/ins] more distinctly clear if I revert a moment to some of my first principles. A sign is an object made by a party we will call the [u]utterer[/u], and determined by his idea, which is the sense or [u]depth[/u] of the sign, in order to create in the mind of the interpreter an [u]interpretant[/u] idea of the same [del]object[/del] [ins]meaning[/ins]. The object is itself really of the nature of the sign, too. Every sect of metaphysics admits that; and for our present purpose it matters not what metaphysical doctrine be adopted, only my statement would require some modification unless that there were object behind object [u]ad inf.[/u] The relations of the sign to its depth, object, and interpretant are of utterly different categories. The depth of the sign seems to be nothing but its better self. The sign is related to its depth as an idea to an ideal, as memory to vivid hallucination, as this — A — to the letter A which it is trying to be. The relation of a sign to its object is correspondence in hard fact, so far as this meant and interpreted. Its chief ingredient is brute force. The relation of a sign to its interpretant is the relation of the seed to the plant that grows from it. Is not the plant the better self of the seed, you may ask. We hope so: but the one is mere δύναμις the other ἐντελέχεια. We say that the idea of right is the mightiest force in the world In that sense, the sign has a power of determining an interpretant and is only an actual sign in so far as it exercises that power. If you will just have patience, you will see that I am growing to a point. In order to get these ideas more within our grasp, it will be well to see how they apply to the three kinds of signs, icons, indices, and symbols. Take an icon.
