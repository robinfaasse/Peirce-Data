Kant in the C. d. r. V. says that not one thing has been
added to logic since Aristotle; and that must be understood as
a public acknowledgment that his [u]falsche Spitzfindigkeit[/u] ( a
youthful production) contained nothing not in Aristotle. It
does, however, contain some of the most precious nonsense ever
written about logic; as where he denies a certain syllogism
to be sound reasoning, although [del]if[/del] [ins]is[/ins] the premisses are transposed[ins],[/ins]
it is all right. Kant had evidently great power as a logician[ins];[/ins] [del],[/del]
but he never seriously studied the subject.

Your statement of what "a master of Symbolic Logic" could
do makes me grin. I think you had better not put that in, un-
less (now that Kempe's proof turns out fallacious) you can just
sit down with pencil and paper, and a knife to sharpen the
pencil should it become dull, and just work out a proof that
four colors will color a map on a spheroidal surface. My own
opinion is that non-relative logical algebra is very little
use as a calculus, and relative algebra not gigantic. The
real use of both is in logical analysis.
