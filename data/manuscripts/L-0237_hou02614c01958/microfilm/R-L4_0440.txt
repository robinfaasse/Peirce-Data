The innermost efficient bond between two graphs is inside
their innermost common enclosure.

For instance [fig]graph with a and b in an oval with a line extending out[/fig] is no more than [fig]graph with a and b in separate nested ovals[/fig]

A branch of a line extending into an oval and not joining
any graph in that oval is of no effect.

[fig]graph with a, b, and c in an oval[/fig] is no more than [fig]graph with a-b and c in an oval[/fig]

If two indices are joined by a bond passing into an oval,
it makes no difference whether either or both are within the oval

[fig]graph A-B with oval in between[/fig] [fig]graph A-B with oval around A[/fig] [fig]graph A-B with oval around B[/fig] are all equivalent.

A graph already written may be iterated with the same connections
provided the iteration is not outside any enclosure which the
original graph was within; and being so iterated may be erased

Thus [fig]graph with a and b in an oval[/fig] [fig]graph with nested a and b in an oval[/fig] are equivalent
