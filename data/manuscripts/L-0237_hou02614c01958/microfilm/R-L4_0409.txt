The only sufficient expression for (8) is then
$(0 \prec A) + (A \prec 0)$,
but that is equal to
$0 = A$,
and there is probably no
reason for rejecting the
condensed form. It is
true that we write
$x \prec \overline{x}$ and $\overline{x} \prec x$
instead of
$x = \overline{x}$,
but that is not without
reason.

$(0 \prec A) + (\infty \prec \overline{A}) = (A = 0) = (\infty = \overline{A})$
