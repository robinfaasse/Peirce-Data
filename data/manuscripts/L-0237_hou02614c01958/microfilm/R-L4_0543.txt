My dear Mrs. Franklin:

I have a set of characters for what you call copulas and I operations, — I mean only the four [ins]universal[/ins] [del]affirmative[/del] ones, — upon which, I think, you and I might compromise, as they embody my ethical notions sufficiently, and your idea of turning the character about, [u]as much as that idea ought to be embodied[/u]. I object to your signs, not only as unnecessarily new, but also because although the four relations are in a purely formal aspect, considered in themselves alone, quite on a par, and this ought to appear in the notation, yet, in other purely logical aspects, they are not on a par. It is, for example, utterly impossible for logic to take the first step without compounding two propositions. This composition is the non-relative multiplication; and for this reason, your two wedges $\lor$ and $\bar{\lor}$ are entitled to a certain preeminence; and $\bar{\lor}$ has a peculiarity of a purely logical kind among the four [ins]universal[/ins] copulas. Again, logic cannot take a step without the idea of inference; and the illative relation is the relation expressed by my $\prec$ so that has a peculiarity. This also confers a peculiarity upon the converse relation. The fourth copula expresses the same relation as $\bar{\lor}$; that is, it asserts the disjunction.
