As to my morals of terminology, there may be differences of opinion as to the exact limitations of the rules, but I do think that any decent person who will seriously reflect upon it from the point of view of morals will agree with my general principle. You say "I think that those who follow us in the paths of learning have a right to demand that concepts be given adequate and appropriate names and such as carry their meaning upon their face so far as that can be done & that a certain amount of priority of claim should make way for this consideration" Well, I do not think I have ever said anything contrary to this. Only I have endeavored to [u]precede[/u] a little. But as for Lambert's <, I think it utterly inadmissible, for the reason that long before Lambert this sign had been preempted for a relation which essentially implies that
A < B
and B < A
cannot be true at the same time; and [del]as[/del] [ins]as[/ins] I was very familiar with Lambert when I introduced [fig]copula sign[/fig] I have no doubt that but for that consideration, I should have used his sign. But for that I would go back to it now. But the sign [fig]copula sign[/fig] is a sign of algebra generally, and not of logic exclusively; and therefore it needs to be one which will go along with existing algebraical signs. Besides, I wish to say, not [u]a propos[/u] of <, but [del]a propos[/del] [ins]a propos[/ins] of your [del]argumentum[/del] [ins]argumentum[/ins] ad hominem, that I was formerly not sufficiently awake myself to the moral aspect of the matter; & it is a bad excuse for sin that the preacher sins.

I don't think I can have said that Aristotle does not say that not (a universal proposition) is the contradictory of it. On the contrary, he usually does. In his Analytics he does not usually if ever write οὐ πᾶς ἄνθρωπος ἐστι λευκός. But I have forgotten what remark of mine you allude to.

very faithfully C. S. Peirce
