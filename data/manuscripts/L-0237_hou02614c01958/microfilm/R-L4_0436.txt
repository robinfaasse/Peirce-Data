My dear Mrs. Franklin

You ask whether Logical Graphs have any
bearing on Non-Relative Logic. Not much, except
in one highly important particular, that they supply an
entirely new system of fundamental assumptions to
logical algebra.

Graphs are of two different systems, Entitative and Existential
Entitative Graphs are described Monist VII pp. 161-187. I will
mail you the number.

An existential graph is a figure which if it stood alone
on a blank sheet, called the sheet of the graph, would assert,
or be pretended to assert, (or say would have the form of a
proposition) according to the following seven rules:

I. Any unenclosed ([ins]a lightly drawn[/ins] [del]inter[/del] oval) graph, whether it be the whole
graph on the sheet or a part of it, can be erased without asserting
anything more.

II. Graphs separately scriptible for [del]different sheets[/del] [ins]referring to the same universe[/ins], are scriptible
[del]together[/del] on the same sheet, [del]at once[/del] side by side, without asserting
[del]anything[/del] additional.

III. If there is a heavy line (it signifies [ins]see next rule[/ins] the identity of points on it)
it can be [del]joined[/del] [ins]continued[/ins] so as to join some index, as soon as
all less enclosed [ins]in their outermost parts[/ins] heavy lines have been so joined. There must
be some such index, but not necessarily of a signate individual.

IV. Mutually transferable indices can always be joined by
a heavy line.

V. Indices joined by a heavy line [ins]which is[/ins] unenclosed and uncrossed
(by a light line of an oval) are mutually transferable
