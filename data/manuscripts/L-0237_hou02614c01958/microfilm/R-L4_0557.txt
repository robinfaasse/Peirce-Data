Milford Pa
[date]1902 Jan 21[/date]

My dear Mrs. Franklin:

I have pretty much decided to adopt myself the bulk of the signs resulting from the diagram
[fig]cross-shaped diagram with x, y, non-x, non-y[/fig]
Note that [u]x[/u] asserts x and [u]x̄[/u] denies it.

Some of the modifications are rather neat & I may charge them

$x \times y$ Empty proposition
$x \circ y$ absurdity
$x \circ y$ I think I shall write $x \circ y$
$x \vee y$ constant, along with $x \circ y$ and $x \circ y$

$x \supset y$ is perhaps better than $x \supset y$
$x > y$ to stand
$x \subset y$ becomes decidedly $x \subset y$
$x < y$ to stand
$x \curlywedge y$ to become $x \curlywedge y$
$x \wedge y$ is perhaps better than $x \curlywedge y$

$x \circ \circ y$ I think must be $x = y$
$x \circ y$ will then become $x \parallel y$

The four degenerate propositions are occasionally wanted

$x \Delta y$ i.e. y is V or [del]y[/del] [ins]y[/ins]
$x \nabla y$ y is f or [del]y[/del] [ins]ȳ[/ins]
$x \Delta y$ x is V or [del]x[/del] [ins]x[/ins]
$x \nabla y$ x is f or [del]x[/del] [ins]x̄[/ins]

[marginalia]and [fig]diamond symbol[/fig] though this maybe so written so may turn of the [u]six[/u][/marginalia]

This seems to me to do full justice to the merits of your idea, even to extend it; and still it does not do too much violence to history.

All the signs except the last six are made with one stroke of the pen.

very faithfully
C. S. Peirce
