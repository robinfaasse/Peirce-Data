Some of your words seem to me contrary to the rules of ter-
minology. I don't see why you use [u]application[/u], which you
acknowledge to be bad, in place of my [u]result[/u], from scholastic
[u]resultus[/u], which is not liable to any confusion, & was proposed
in 1867. You speak of [u]is[/u] as expressing a "copulative con-
nection". This is entirely inadmissible on principles of
priority. Copulative has been used in the sense of such a com-
bination that the truth of the whole requires the truth of every
part, since Aulus Gellius at least. As he says "si unum est mendacium
etiam si cetera vera sunt, totum esse mendacium dicitur." It is true
that Gellius does not happen to use the adjective [u]copulativus[/u]; but if his
[u]copulatum[/u] and [u]disjunctum[/u], used in one passage, are compared with his
[u]disjunctum[/u] and [u]disjunctivus[/u], in another, (the passages are XVI viii and V xi 8)
it will be seen that it is a mere accident that [u]copulativus[/u] does not occur. It is
used by Marcianus Capella, and has been so used ever since, & is in full use
to day. [u]Copula[/u], on the other hand, was never thought of before the Nominalist Realist
Controversy. Priscian called is the [u]verbum substantivum[/u] translating the Greek
[greek]ὑπαρκτικὸν ῥῆμα[/greek]. Abelard is the first author who speaks of the [u]copula[/u]. So if
these two terms jostle, it is copula that must give way.

Your "special kind of syllogism"
All A is B
A is existent
$\therefore$ B is existent,
overlooks the proof by Kant and Scotus that existence is not a predicate. To
regard it so leads to all manner of absurdity. Your syllogism put into cor-
rect form becomes
All A is B
Something is A
$\therefore$ Something is B
It is a common syllogism in [u]Darii[/u].
