The next thing to be set forth is what used to be called the
principle of identity (a very ambiguous name) that things which
are the same as a third are the same as one another. I am
rather ignorant myself about the history of this. I know that
Aquinas refers to it in his Summa theol. part I qu. xxviii art. 3 and
Scotus In I Anal. pr. Q. ix. where he calls it regula ista, quaecumque
uni et eidem sunt eadem illa inter se sunt eadem. Several lo-
gicians of [del]the[/del] about 1600 make it the first rule of reasoning. What
you want to look up is expository syllogism. Note too that it was
supposed to be opposed to the doctrine of the Trinity.
