To say that any secure inference can be drawn from
English grammar to general psychology seems absurd
when we see for instance that in the majority of
languages no [u]verbum substantivum[/u] is required & that
some languages put a relative pronoun in place of it.
A variety of languages, among them Gaelic & Old Irish
do not commonly put the subject in the nominative
but treat it as [del]of[/del] on a par with the rest of the objects
spoken of. 'He loves her' being 'loves his her' or
something like that. In Gaelic it is the genitive, in other
languages locative ablative, etc.

To say that a tendency to thinking is a scientific [del]logical[/del]
argument in logic is simply to destroy the science of logic
and is, therefore, most inconsistent with the general position
of an exact logician. If an Sigwart says (a Sigwart has
the German obtuseness in a high degree) [ins]logical validity[/ins] logic consists in
an inclination to think in certain ways, which he supports
by an argument which is a good example of a formal fallacy,
why argue at all? Why not simply believe what we are
inclined to believe?
