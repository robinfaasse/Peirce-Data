I. Space is divided into four regions; $\alpha$ and $\beta$ intersect in a finite point.

[fig]Two intersecting lines with arrows labeled A, B, A', B'[/fig] There coexist $\left[ \begin{aligned} A &+ B \\ A &+ \bar{B} \\ \bar{A} &+ B \\ \bar{A} &+ \bar{B} \end{aligned} \right]$ (1)

II. Space is divided into three regions, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are parallel. There are four cases.

The directions are the same and $\beta$ is on the

(a) right of $\alpha$.

[fig]Two parallel vertical lines with arrows pointing up, labeled A and B[/fig] There coexist $\left[ \begin{aligned} A &+ B \\ A &+ \bar{B} \\ \bar{A} &+ \bar{B} \end{aligned} \right]$ only. (2)

(b) left of $\alpha$.

[fig]Two parallel vertical lines with arrows pointing up, labeled B and A[/fig] There coexist $\left[ \begin{aligned} A &+ B \\ \bar{A} &+ B \\ \bar{A} &+ \bar{B} \end{aligned} \right]$ only. (3)
