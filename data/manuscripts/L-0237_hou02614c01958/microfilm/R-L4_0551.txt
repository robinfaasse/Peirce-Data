Select a row and then a column of that row or first a [del]row[/del] [ins]column[/ins] and then a row of that column. But you cannot select a row and then a column from different rows; for if you could, your row would not have been already selected. There is no more difference then between relative and non-relative multiplication than merely between $(x + \text{etc})(y + \text{etc})$ and [del]$(x \cdot y + \text{etc})$[/del] $(x \cdot y + \text{etc})$. It is $\Sigma\Pi$ or $\Pi\Sigma$. It is from not having this doctrine of universes clear that Sigwart can say that a problematic judgment is no judgment. Every judgment is no-doubt assertory as to the Truth, which is [ins]singularly[/ins] [del]definite[/del]. But it is indefinite in reference to a subsidiary propositional universe. P is possibly true and Q is possibly true. That is, as I would formulate it, beneath the truth there is a universe of universes of possibilities. In that universe of universes of possibilities, there is a universe of possibilities containing the possibility of P and the possibility Q. But if I identify not merely the universes of possibilities, but the possibilities themselves, I assert that P and Q are possibly both true. Of course, I do not say that other formulations are not possible. With a narrow field of view another formulation might be preferable. Narrow fields of view are the most common cause of bad logical doctrines. To consider a subject as indefinite is plainly to take it disjunctively; as long as it remains indefinite it is meaningless to talk of composition; the components must be definite in so far as they are compounded. To consider a subject as universal is to take it copulatively; and subjects aggregated must in [ins]that[/ins] respect in which they are aggregated be individual. Since then we seem forced to define aggregation and composition as applicable to subjects, in order to avoid defining them twice over, to regard them as only derivatively affecting predicates.
