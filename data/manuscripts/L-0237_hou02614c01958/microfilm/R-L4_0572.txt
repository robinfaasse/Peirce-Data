Milford, Pa. [date]1904, July 27[/date]

My dear Mrs. Franklin:

I am much interested in the announcements of courses [del]th[/del] that you send. I fully agree with you about the Peanists. Indeed, a year has passed since I agreed to notice Russell's Principles of Mathematics Vol. I. and I feel its pretentiousness so strongly that I cannot well fail to express it in a notice. Yet it is a disagreeable sort of thing to say, and people may ask themselves whether it is not simply the resentment of the old man[del]d[/del] who is getting laid upon the shelf. Perhaps I might require strict searchings to be sure myself that that element was not present. The result is that I have never written the notice yet.

I have the same opinion about Whitehead.

I winked at it in the Nation by making a few commonplace remarks about the Hon. Russell's book and then turning to Lady Welby's [del]xx[/del] "What is Meaning" and saying "This is a book as opposite to the other as possible. It is entirely free from pretentiousness and from pedantry", etc. I can't give my exact words, not having kept a copy; but it must have appeared Oct. 15 last.

Lady Welby's idea is not quite definite enough. But she is [del]not[/del] on [del]it[/del] the trail of the truth in saying that there are three different things that "Meaning" can mean. So I said in my pragmatism paper.
