My dear Mrs. Franklin:

I write to correct an important error which I committed in my letter to you of the other day.

The subject of a proposition is, in my terminology, [u]individual[/u] if it allows the interpreter of the proposition no latitude of choice in finding an instance [del]for[/del] [ins]in[/ins] subsumption; otherwise, it is [del]general[/del] [ins]universal[/ins]. The subject of a proposition is [u]definite[/u] if it allows the utterer of the proposition no latitude of choice in finding an instance to support it; otherwise it is [u]particular[/u].

A subject may be both individual and definite. In that case, [del]it[/del] [ins]I[/ins] call it [u]singular[/u]. It must be either individual or definite, for if either the utterer or interpreter has a latitude of choice as to an instance to represent a given subject, the other party is thereby deprived of all choice in the matter.

To an individual subject the principle of excluded middle applies: to a [del]general[/del] [ins]universal[/ins] subject not. That is, it need not be that either 'Any S is P' or that 'Any S is not P'. To a definite subject the principle of contradiction applies: to a particular subject not. That is, it may at once be true that 'Some S is P' and 'Some S is not P'.

A universe is either [u]singular[/u], giving no latitude of choice to either party, or it is [u]general[/u], giving a latitude of choice to one or the other, according as the proposition is universal or particular. If the universe is general, the subject cannot be singular. For there is no sense in referring a singular subject to any universe but itself. A universe is nothing but the range of choice of instances [del]for a subject[/del].

A proposition may [ins]refer to[/ins] [del]have[/del] as many universes as it has subjects. The subjects are taken in a definite order (at any rate when their quantities are unlike). But preceding all the other [del]subjects[/del] [ins]subjects[/ins] is the singular subject, the Truth, to which [del]both[/del] the Princip[del]o[/del]al of Contradiction and the Principle of Excluded Middle apply.
