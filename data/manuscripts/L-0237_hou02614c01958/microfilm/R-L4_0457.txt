My dear Mrs. Franklin:

My "Virgo" were better called Taurus, since it was a decided "bull." There seems to be no need of numbering the indices to indicate the order of specification in verification. Let

$\overline{\bigvee}_{pi}$ mean [del]that[/del] [ins]either[/ins] the proposition $P$ (involving $i$ and perhaps other indices) would not be true, however the subjects replacing those indices [del]were[/del] [ins]individual[/ins] chosen, or else that $i$ is such [del]a subject[/del] [ins]an individual[/ins] as to permit that proposition to be true, provided the other subjects are rightly chosen individuals.

$\overline{\bigvee}_{(Pi)i} \cdot P_i$ states this, and also that $P_i$ is true. But the general understanding is that the indices may denote any subjects/individuals at pleasure. Therefore this means Everything is $P$.

$\overline{\bigvee}_{(Pi)i} \overline{\bigvee}_{Pi}$ Means that either the proposition $P_i$ would be true if a suitable subject were put for $i$, but the subject actually chosen does not permit it, or else that $P_i$ is true. That is, Something is $P$.

Then will see that

$\overline{\bigvee}_{(Pij)i} \prec \overline{\bigvee}_{(Pij)j}$
