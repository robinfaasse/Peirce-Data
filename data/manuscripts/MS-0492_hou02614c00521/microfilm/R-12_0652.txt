All rules admitted into this section will be demonstrated from a single general principle, as follows:

If a graph, A, would be transformable into a graph, B, in case it were the entire graph, standing alone upon the sheet, then, if A is an unenclosed [del]or evenly enclosed[/del] partial graph [ins]or is evenly enclosed[/ins] (whether it be alone in its enclosure or not) it may still be transformed into B, retaining, as far as possible, every connection [ins]by a heavy line[/ins] with the rest of what is written in the same enclosure[del], but without the addition of any new connections,[/del] while B, when oddly enclosed (whether alone in its enclosure or not) [del]can[/del] [ins]may[/ins] be transformed into A without any new connections with other partial graphs in the same enclosure, but retaining all the connections with those other partial graphs that it had before the transformation, or their equivalents.
