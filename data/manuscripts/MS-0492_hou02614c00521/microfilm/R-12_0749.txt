<u>Subsection 3</u>

The question may properly be asked whether Fig. 29 means that something N is loved by all saints or whether it means that each saint loves something. This question may be resolved with certainty in various ways.

[fig]Fig. 29: M is a saint, M loves N[/fig]
Fig. 29.

One way is to compare two graphs like Figs 30 and 31.

[fig]Fig. 30: It storms, It thunders, It lightens[/fig]
Fig. 30.

[fig]Fig. 31: It thunders, It lightens, It storms[/fig]
Fig. 31.

Fig 30 asserts that it storms and in addition, that it either does not thunder or else it [del]does not[/del] [ins]fails to[/ins] lighten[del]s[/del]. That is, it [ins]either[/ins] storms and does not thunder or else it storms and lightens. Fig. 31 means that it either does not thunder or else it both storms and lightens. It is plain that the former [del]shall[/del] asserts all that the latter does and more besides; namely, that if it does not storm and lightens both, it still storms, which Fig 30 asserts while Fig 31 does not assert it.
