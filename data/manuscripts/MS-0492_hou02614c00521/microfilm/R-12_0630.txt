[u]Subsection 3. Of Abstraction, Entia Rationis, and allied matters.[/u]

[u]Abstraction[/u] is a mode of [ins]illative[/ins] transformation of a proposition such that the conclusion has a subject that was [del]not mentioned[/del] referred to [ins]in[/ins] the premises, as
Opium makes whoever takes it sleep;
Hence, Opium has a [u]dormitive virtue[/u].

A subject which can only be known by abstraction is said to denote an [u]ens rationis[/u]. [del]What[/del] Every symbol is an [u]ens rationis[/u]. On every page of English there are something like a dozen [u]the[/u]s. All those [u]the[/u]s are one and the same word. What is that word? What is its mode of being but that of a habit, of a regularity? Now a regularity consists in the fact that something [u]will[/u] happen, and not merely [u]has[/u] happened. Therefore the being of a word consists in the [u]esse in futuro[/u] of something entirely different [del]and independent of that word.[/del]
