A subject is either general or singular. A general is
"quod aptum natum est dici de multis"; that is that
which of its nature might be applicable to more than
one real thing.

A singular subject is a subject both individual
and definite.

An individual subject is a subject, [ins]S,[/ins] such that,
whatever monad rhema P may be, either it is either
true that S is P or that S is not P. [del]A subject not[/del]
individual is universal. For example, it may be
false that [del]every[/del] [ins]any[/ins] man is rich and [del]also[/del] [ins]generally[/ins] false that
[u]any[/u] man [u]is not[/u] rich.

A definite subject is a subject, S, such that whatever
monad rhema P may be, it is either false that S is P
or else is false that S is not P. [del]A subject not[/del]
definite is indefinite. For example, it may be true at once
that [u]Some[/u] man [u]is[/u] rich and also that [u]Some[/u] man [u]is not[/u] rich.
