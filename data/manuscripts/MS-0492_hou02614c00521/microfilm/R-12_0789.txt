If one or more subjects be [del]removed[/del] [ins]erased[/ins] from a proposition so as to leave their places blank (or an analogous operation be performed in case the proposition is not written), the resulting blank form is called a [u]rhema[/u].

For example, the following are rhemata:
— sells — to the daughter of — something N for the value of N
Everyman gives something to —
— loves —
Brutus killed —

Rhemata, according as the [ins]number of[/ins] [del]the number of[/del] their blanks, are 0, 1, 2, 3, etc. are called [u]medads[/u], [u]monads[/u], [u]dyads[/u], [u]triads[/u], etc. If a rhema has more than two blanks, then, and then only, it is to be called a [u]polyad[/u].
