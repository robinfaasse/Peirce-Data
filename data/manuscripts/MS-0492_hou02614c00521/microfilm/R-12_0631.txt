There are certain rhemata which though predicated
of [del]co[/del] existing individuals and though [ins]helping to[/ins] conveying positive
information concerning those existents, nevertheless [del]only[/del]
signify what they signify [del]can only[/del] concerning signs. A
striking example is the line of identity, which is a dyad
rhema without which or some equivalent for it it is
impossible to convey any information concerning exis-
tents. Yet it signifies only identity, which is plainly a
relation between two signs of the same thing, not a
factual relation between things. These rhemata are
called [u]rhemata of second intention[/u]. They are the special
concern of [u]logic[/u]. Avicenna, and after him many
scholastics, [ins]as Raymond Lully,[/ins] [del]of[/del] Scotus, Burleigh, and Armandus de Bello Visu,
make logic to be the science of second intentions; while
Durandus a Sancto Porciano, followed by Gratiadeus
Esculanus makes it relate to entia rationis. All
the rhemata of second intention ought to be provided
with special signs in this system.
