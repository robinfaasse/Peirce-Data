[u]Subsection 3. Of Abstraction, Entia Rationis, and allied matters.[/u]

[u]Abstraction[/u] is a mode of [ins]illative[/ins] transformation of a proposition such that the conclusion has a subject that was not [del]mentioned[/del] referred to the premises, as Opium makes whoever takes it sleep;
Hence, Opium has a dormitive virtue.
A subject which can only be known by abstraction is said to denote an [u]ens rationis[/u]. [del]What[/del] Every symbol is an [u]ens rationis[/u]. On every page of English there are something like a dozen [u]thes[/u]. All those [u]thes[/u] are one and the same word. What is that word? What is its mode of being but that of a habit, of a regularity? Now a regularity consists in the fact that something [u]will[/u] happen, and not merely [u]has[/u] happened. Therefore the being of a word consists in the [u]esse in futuro[/u] of something entirely different and independent of that word.
