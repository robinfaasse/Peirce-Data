A [u]subject[/u] of a proposition is any part of it for
which a proper name of a known existing individual
may be substituted without otherwise modifying
the meaning.*

* It will be observed that this makes what modern
grammars call the direct and indirect objects to be subjects.
The word subject is a term of logic, and the exigencies of logic
must determine the meaning that is to be given to it. It
was not used by ancient or medieval grammarians. Even the
Port Royal Greek Grammar does not contain it. The subject
nominative is not the only kind of subject. The word first
occurs in Boethius who defines it thus: Subjectum est id de quo
dicitur id quod praedicatur. Now [del]the[/del] any rhema in a proposition may
be regarded as its predicate and therefore my definition of subject is correct.
