All rules admitted into this section will be de-
monstrated from a single general principle, as
follows:
If a graph, A, would be transformable into a
graph, B, in case it were the entire graph, standing
alone upon the sheet, then, if A is an unenclosed
[del]or evenly enclosed[/del] [ins]partial graph or is evenly enclosed[/ins] (whether it be alone
in its enclosure or not) it may still be transformed
into B, retaining, as far as possible, every connection
[ins]by a heavy line[/ins] with the rest of what is written in the same enclosure;
[ins]but without the addition of any new connections,[/ins]
while B, when oddly enclosed (whether alone in its
enclosure or not) [del]can[/del] [ins]may[/ins] be transformed into A with-
out any new connections with other partial graphs
in the same enclosure, but retaining all the
connections with those other partial graphs that
it had before the transformation, or their equi-
valents.
