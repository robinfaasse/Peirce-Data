The universe of discourse, — at any rate, the highest
universe, to which all propositions alike refer, that of
'the Truth,' or the aggregate of all reals, — is singular.
If, therefore, a general subject is used, it must be under-
stood that it is capable of replacement by a singular.
The proposition may intend to leave the choice of this
singular instance to the interpreter, which is the case
when the subject is universal; as in 'Any man is a
sinner;' or the proposition may intend to reserve this
choice of the singular instance for further informa-
tion to disclose, which is the case when the subject is
indefinite; as in 'Some king of Egypt made the Sphinx.'
Evidently, the specification of the singular instance cannot
be at once reserved and, [ins]be[/ins] left to the interpreter, and there-
fore no subject can be at once universal and inde-
finite, although it may be neither.
