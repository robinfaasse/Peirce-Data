[ured]I Tr II[/ured]
[ured]94[/ured]	Transformations to be taken as basic ought to
consist of operations as simple as possible. Since,
therefore any transformation other than an
insertion or an erasure may be conceived to
result from an insertion followed by an erasure,
operations of these two sorts shall alone be ex-
plicitly permitted by rules admitted to this section,
[del]entitled to rank as basic.[/del] [ins]of a basic character and be ranked as basic.[/ins]
This restriction will not prevent the permission of
any illative transformation. For if [del]A[/del] [ins]any graph, x,[/ins] can be illa-
tively transformed into any other, y, it can be
transformed into the graph x y which merely
asserts the truth of both, and this having been
done, it will be permitted to erase the x; so that
the transformation into y will be effected in two
steps.
