There is thus no doubt the totient of zero is
zero.
But the totient of $a^{\alpha}\cdot b^{\beta}\cdot c^{\gamma}$ etc where $a, b,$
$c,$ etc are the prime factors of $a^{\alpha}\cdot b^{\beta}\cdot c^{\gamma}$ is
$a^{\alpha-1}(a-1)\cdot b^{\beta-1}(b-1)\cdot c^{\gamma-1}(c-1)$ etc. Since
the only factor of $0$ is $0,$ if its exponent is $1$
the totient is $0^{0}(1-1) = 0$ & if the exponent
is $N,$ the totient is $0^{N}(N-1) = 0.$ In any case
the totient of $0$ is $0.$

We must therefore make $0$ a prime factor
of itself and [del]therefore[/del] since
[u]A prime factor of a cardinal number is
a prime number that is a factor of it,[/u]
one must define a prime number thus
[u]A prime number is a number to which
every number but its own multiples is
prime.[/u]

It is commonly said that the number of
divisors of a number is the product of the
exponents of its prime factors after each
exponent has been increased by $1.$ Thus
$12 = 2^{2}\cdot 3^{1}\cdot 5^{0}\cdot 7^{0}\cdot$ etc. and $(2+1)(1+1)(0+1)\text{etc} = 6$
and the $6$ divisors of $12$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$
So $1 = 2^{0}\cdot 3^{0}\cdot 5^{0}\cdot 7^{0}$ and $(0+1)^{0+1}\text{etc} = 1$ and
$1$ is the only divisor of $1.$ But to make this hold
for $0,$ we must either regard zero as $0^{\infty}$ or else
as $2^{-\infty}\cdot 3^{-\infty}\cdot 5^{-\infty}\cdot 7^{-\infty},$ etc. Either view would

make the totient of zero, zero. Either view
would require the wording of the present
rule to be slightly changed in one or another
way.

It is commonly said that $(N-1)! + 1$ is divi-
sible by $N$ if $N$ is prime, but otherwise is not.
Thus $6! = 720$ and $721$ is divisible by $7.$
But $5! = 120$ and $121$ is not divisible by $6.$
$0! = 1$ and $2$ is divisible by $1;$ so that this
makes $1$ prime. But $(-1)! = \infty$ and
$0$ is not a divisor of $\infty.$
