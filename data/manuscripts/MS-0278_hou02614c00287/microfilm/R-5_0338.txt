Thus,

$$A = B \times x$$

will express that the assertion $A$ [del]with something[/del] [del]he[/del] includes the assertion $B$, and

$$A = B + x$$

will express that the assertion $A$ is included by the assertion $B$. So that

$$\{ A = B \times x \} = \{ A \prec B \} = \{ B = A + y \}.$$

My main reason for preferring this new view is that the algebra founded upon it is simpler than one founded on the adoption of an unbalanced copula.

The algebra of the copula, as given in my first paper, is adequate to every problem of non-relative logic, and it [del]contains[/del] [ins]makes use of[/ins] but two operational signs, the copula $\prec$ and the sign of negation.
