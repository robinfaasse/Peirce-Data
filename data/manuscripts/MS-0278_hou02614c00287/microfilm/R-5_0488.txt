There [del]is another[/del] [ins]are other[/ins] methods of attaining the principal formula for [del]log (1+x)[/del], one of which, being fundamental, may here be given. We have when N = ∞

$$e^x = (1 + \frac{x}{N})^N$$

Put $x = \log y$. This gives
$$1 + y = (1 + \frac{\log y}{N})^N$$
$$\log y = N ((1+y)^{\frac{1}{N}} - 1)$$

Developing $(1+y)^{\frac{1}{N}}$ by the binomial theorem, we have

$$\log (1+y) = N \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{N} y - \frac{1}{2} (\frac{1}{N} - \frac{1}{N^2}) y^2 + (\frac{1}{3N} - \frac{1}{4N^2} + \frac{1}{6N^3}) y^3 - \text{etc} - 1 \right]$$
$$= y - \frac{1}{2} y^2 + \frac{1}{3} y^3 - \text{etc} + \frac{1}{N} (\frac{1}{2} y^2 - \frac{1}{4} y^3 + \frac{1}{6N} y^3) - \text{etc}$$

When N = ∞ this becomes
$$\log (1+y) = y - \frac{1}{2} y^2 + \frac{1}{3} y^3 - \text{etc}$$

Another formula for $\log x$ is easily derived from this
$$\log \frac{1+x}{1-x} = \log (1+x) - \log (1-x) = 2 (x + \frac{1}{3} x^3 + \frac{1}{5} x^5 + \frac{1}{7} x^7 + \text{etc})$$

Put $\frac{1+x}{1-x} = y \quad 1+x = y(1-x) \quad x+xy = y-1 \quad x = \frac{y-1}{y+1}$
$$\log y = 2 \left[ \frac{y-1}{y+1} + \frac{1}{3} (\frac{y-1}{y+1})^3 + \frac{1}{5} (\frac{y-1}{y+1})^5 + \text{etc} \right]$$

[ins]This holds for all values of y[/ins]
