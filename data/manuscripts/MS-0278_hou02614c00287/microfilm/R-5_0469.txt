[u]Definition 95[/u]. A [del]polyhamma[/del] [ins]hamma, or polyhamma[/ins] (plural, polyhammata) is a point specially marked on a line and the extremity of every sketch arriving at it. According as 2, 3, 4, 5, etc sketches extend from it, it is called a [u]dihamma[/u], [u]trihamma[/u], [u]tetrahamma[/u], [u]pentahamma[/u], etc. [del]The[/del] [ins]The[/ins] number of radiating sketches [ins]diminished by one[/ins] is called the valency of the polyhamma.

[u]Definition 96[/u]. The station-number, or connective valency, of a line, or linear configuration, is the number of [ins]its[/ins] separate pieces less the number of times it can be cut (through?) without increasing the number of separate pieces. When we speak of the station-number of a line we [ins]usually[/ins] mean that a space [ins]or surface[/ins] must exist between the pieces; but when we speak of the station-number of a tree, or figure composed of lines and points, a polyhamma is regarded as a sufficient interruption.
