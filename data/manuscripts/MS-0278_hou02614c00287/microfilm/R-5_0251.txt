We think in signs; and indeed meditation takes the form of a dialogue in
which one makes constant appeal to [del]the[/del] [ins]his[/ins] self of a subsequent moment for rati-
fication of his meaning in respect to [del]his[/del] [ins]thought-[/ins]signs really representing the objects
they profess to represent. Logic therefore is almost a branch of ethics, being
the theory of the control of signs in respect to their relation to their objects.
My idea of a sign has been so generalized that I have at length despaired of
making anybody comprehend it, so that for the sake of being understood, I
now limit it, so as to define a sign as anything which is on the one hand so
determined (or specializes) by an object and [del]so[/del] on the other hand so determines
the mind of an interpreter of it that the latter is thereby determined mediately,
or indirectly, by that real object that determines the sign. Even this may well be
thought an excessively generalized definition. The determination of the Interpre-
ter's mind I term the [u]Interpretant[/u] of the sign. But it is necessary to distinguish
the [u]Immediate Object[/u] of a sign [ins]which is the Object as it is represented in the sign[/ins] from the real thing which is its [u]Dynamical Object[/u]
in its own Being. And in like manner it is requisite to distinguish the [u]Immediate[/u]
[u]Interpretant[/u], or the Interpretant as the Sign itself represents it, the [u]Dynamical Inter-[/u]
[u]pretant[/u], which is the [del]effect[/del] [ins]effectual Interpretations,[/ins] the sign does as a matter of fact produce in the mind of
the Interpreter, and the [u]Rational Interpretant[/u], which is the effect it ought to
have upon the mind of the Interpreter, as such. I divide Signs by ten trichotomies
governed by the 3 categories of Valency in respect 1[u]st[/u] to the Nature of the Sign itself as
being either a possible Quality, or an Occurrence (i.e. either an Existent thing or an

actual fact), or a general form, such as the word 'the' which is one and the same word
every time it is written, printed, spoken, or telegraphed; 2[u]nd[/u], according to the Nature of
their Immediate Objects, 3[u]rd[/u] according to the nature of their Dynamical Objects,
4[u]th[/u] according to the relation of the Sign to its Dynamical Object. (And since this
is the trichotomy I have most studied, and consequently most frequently mention,
I will here say that the division is into, 1[u]st[/u], [u]Icons[/u], which represent their Objects by
virtue of resembling them, as a geometrical figure in a geometry-book, or as any
Diagram, or Array of letters in algebra, where the resemblance is not sensual
but is intellectual; 2[u]nd[/u] into [u]Indices[/u], which represent their Objects by virtue of
being in fact modified by them, as a clinical thermometer may represent fever,
or a letter attached to a [del]f[/del] figure of a triangle may from its position represent an
angle of the triangle; and 3[u]rd[/u] into [u]Symbols[/u], which represent their Objects by virtue
merely of the certainty (or probability) that they will be so interpreted; as any [del]word[/del] [ins]noun[/ins]
represents the thing for which it stands. I fear this definition may be open to criti-
cism. Symbols are either conventional signs or they are proper names (or involve
[ins]in meaning[/ins] proper names.) We have to distinguish Symbols, which are not themselves exis-
tent things from [u]Instances[/u] of them, which are Icons of them. Just as if the word
'the' occurs 20 times on my copy of a certain page of a certain book, there are
20 "Instances" of a single Symbol. In the 5[u]th[/u] to 10[u]th[/u] places I divide Signs ac-
cording to their Interpretants.
