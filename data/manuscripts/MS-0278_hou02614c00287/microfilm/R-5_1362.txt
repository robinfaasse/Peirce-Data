Definitions. A sphere is a surface all the points of which are at the same distance from one point, called the centre. The distance is called the radius. Any line from the centre to a point of the sphere is also called a radius.

A circle is a [del]line[/del] [ins]curve[/ins] in a plane all the points of which are at the same distance from one point. We speak of the centre and radius of a circle in the same senses in which we speak of the centre and radius of a sphere.

In the ancient elementary geometry, [ins]as well as in some modern geometrical books,[/ins] [del]it is[/del] the solid [del]space[/del] [ins]region[/ins] contained [del]in a sphere, that[/del] [ins]by the surface[/ins] is called the sphere, [del]and[/del] [ins]while[/ins] the surface itself is called the surface of the sphere. In like manner, the ancient [del]elementary[/del] geometry calls the region of the plane enclosed in [del]the circle[/del] [ins]a curve[/ins] the circle, and the line itself the circumference. But modern usage as well as that of higher geometry, influenced [del]by[/del] substantially the same motives, have transferred the names [ins]sphere[/ins] and circle to the boundaries of those regions. [ins]In this book,[/ins] [del]therefore,[/del] without discarding the convenient word circumference, [del]we shall[/del] speak of the region enclosed within the sphere-surface as a [del]ball[/del], and that enclosed within the circle-line as a disk.
