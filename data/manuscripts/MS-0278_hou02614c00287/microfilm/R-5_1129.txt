Binary Arithmetic

All primes end with 1 and when this one is docked and the remainder separated into periods of 2, the periods [ins]are[/ins] added and this repeated as often as possible
give [del]00 or[/del] 11 (first class) or 10 (second class).

A number of the first class consisting of an odd number of figures or one of
the second class consisting of an even number when docked of the first
figure gives a number divisible by 3, otherwise a number of the other class.

To find whether such a number is divisible, separate it into periods of not more than two
less than its number of figures. Add the first figures of the periods and then all the
other figures. And the sum of the latter sum must be divisible by the former, the quotient
being less than expressible in a less number of figures than the period
