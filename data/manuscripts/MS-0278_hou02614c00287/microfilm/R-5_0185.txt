[u]Definition [del]104[/del] [ins]93[/ins][/u]. An [u]oval[/u] is an unbroken line returning [del]to[/del] into itself without furcations or cusps.

[fig]Three drawings of ovals[/fig]
Ovals.

[The word [u]oval[/u] is used in different senses by different geometers. It is one of the few words which mathematicians give over to individual caprice or convenience. The [del]sense[/del] [ins]meaning[/ins] here given to it is its commonest meaning. Of course, oval means etymologically "egg-shaped;" but there are many kinds of eggs in nature and still more in the [ins]fecund[/ins] imaginations of geometers. One celebrated geometer used to dream of eggs every night. Moreover, as he usually forgot to go to his meals, he used to carry eggs in his coat tail pockets to eat when he was hungry. But as he always forgot that circumstance, too, except when reminded of it by sitting on them, his ovals were not always definable as unbroken lines.]
