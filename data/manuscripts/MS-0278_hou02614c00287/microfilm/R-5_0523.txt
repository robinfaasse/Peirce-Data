[u]Axiom[/u] . Nothing follows after itself.

[u]Scholium[/u] . If we were to define what we mean by following after this axiom could be proved. But taking for granted that the pupil knows what following after means, we take for granted that he knows the axiom is true.

[u]Definition 16[/u] . The objects of a collection are said to be arranged in a [u]sequence[/u] when of any two different ones among them one follows after the other. If there are any two of them neither of which comes after the other, they are not in sequence.

[u]Theorem 1[/u] . [u]In any sequence there can be but one object that does not follow after other of the sequence[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u] . Imagine a sequence, and let any two objects in it be designated by A and B. Then I say that it cannot be [del]true[/del] [ins]denied[/ins] of both of these that it follows after some other of the sequence.

For, by the definition of a sequence, [ins]taking the pair A, B,[/ins] either A follows after B or B follows after A. Q.E.D.

[u]Theorem 2[/u] . [u]In any sequence there can be but one object that no other of the sequence follows after[/u].

[u]Exercise[/u] . Demonstrate this theorem.
