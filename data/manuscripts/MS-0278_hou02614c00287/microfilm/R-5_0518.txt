[u]Scholium[/u]. The above definitions are carefully
drawn so as to suit the exigencies of arithmetic and
enable us to express its propositions without exceptions and
in the simplest manner. It is sometimes desirable to class
0, or 1, or both along with prime numbers. In those cases, we
can use the following descriptions.

The primes together with 0 (but without 1) may be described as
numbers [del]independent of[/del] [ins]"unaffected by"[/ins] their factors, that is to say, [ins]they are[/ins] numbers
which [del]can[/del] [ins]have factors and[/ins] be multiplied or divided by [del]their own factors[/del] [ins]any of them[/ins]
without alteration. Or any such number may be said to be
a product of two factors of which it must itself be one.

The primes [del]and[/del] [ins]together[/ins] with 1 (but without 0) may be described
as the numbers without prime factors, or as the numbers that
are prime to themselves, or which have not two factors, or to
which 0 is prime, or [del]as prime factors of 0[/del] [ins]which have no factor unless it be 1[/ins].

The primes together with 1 and 0, are the numbers not
composite.
