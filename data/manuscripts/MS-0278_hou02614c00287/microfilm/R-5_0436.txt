the definition of a character, when every
character is spoken of, we may substitute
any statement we like concerning the subject
of that character. This is no more than is
done in ordinary syllogism. But as Aris-
totle [ins]\*[/ins] recognized that the search for middle
terms calls for the exercise of an aiming
faculty, εὐστοχία, or sagacity, higher and
less mechanical than that which draws con-
clusions, so here, to select a statement to
be substituted for $\beta$ which shall yield a conclu-
sion not too obvious nor trivial, calls for an
act of internal observation. But this only
furnishes a suitable premise; it has
nothing to do with the passage from
the premises to the conclusion.
