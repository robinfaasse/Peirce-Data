[u]Definition 41[/u]. A space which contains no boundless surfaces but

[u]Scholium 16[/u]. It is [del]very[/del] easy to imagine space to be either
artiad or perissid. In fact, it is conceived differently, in this respect,
in different branches of geometry. Which it really is, is doubtful.

[del]Modern[/del] Geometers always conceive [ins]unlimited[/ins] straight lines to return into
themselves. Why they do so, and what facts there are in favor of
that view, we shall see in the next Article. But, [del]be ye[/del] right or
wrong, the assumption will always be made in geometry on account
of its great convenience.

[del]How[/del] Hence, a plane, or flat surface, upon which unlimited straight
lines can move about in various ways, must be regarded as returning
into itself. The question is whether two unlimited straight lines in
one plane cut one another in one point or in two.
