Binary. Numerical Notation.

This is a system of written signs by which any rational
number can be denoted to the exclusion of all others, the
essential characteristics of the system being these four.

[u]1[sup]st[/sup][/u], The number denoted is represented as a sum of different
integral powers of two (though it may degenerate to a single such
power.) [del]To avoid confusing repetitions of the phrase 'power of 2'[/del]
[del]it will be convenient to substitute for that the expression '2-power.'[/del]

[u]2[sup]nd[/sup][/u], Only two incomplex numerical legisigns, or sign-types,
are employed, one of which signifies the presence, the other the
absence, in that sum, of that particular 2-power to which any
individual character of the one or other type refers.

[u]3[sup]rd[/sup][/u], In order that the sign which according to this method denotes
any particular number may show what are the 2-powers which
are to be summed to get that number, this sign is made to consist
of a row of individual numerical characters each of one or other
of the two types mentioned (though this row may degenerate to a
single character). In this row, every character except 2 (at most,
[ins]and 1 at least[/ins]) has two other characters reciprocally next to it; and there must
be some [del]generalized[/del] [ins]regularly[/ins] employed indication distinguishing these
two from each other, the one as the first character of the row and
the other as the last (or if there be but 1 such exceptional cha-
racter, and it be [del]not[/del] the only [del]one[/del] character in the whole sign, there
must be something to show whether it is the first or the last of the row).
