Imagine an axis of rotation [del]through[/del] [ins]conjugate[/ins] [O] perpendicular to the plane (ABC). Then, the [del]axis[/del] of translation will have its every point distant half a circuit from [O], and will be in the plane (ABC). It will, therefore, pass through the points [I], [J], [K]; so that these points lie on one ray.

Hence,
$$\angle IJK = \angle JKI = \angle KIJ = \mathcal{O},$$
$$\angle IJK + \angle JKI + \angle KIJ = 3\mathcal{O}.$$

(Many [del]students[/del] will stumble here, because to conceive of three points on one ray as forming a triangle, though it perfectly in accordance with the definition, requires a certain [del]interfere[/del] generalization of their habits of thinking. Such power of generalization is what chiefly constitutes a mathematician; and the student must be exercised in it. He should resist the instinct of narrow minds to become angry when [del]their habi[/del] called upon to broaden their ways of thinking.)

The triangle IJK is dissected by the three lines IBC, JCA, KAB, into four triangles ICJ, JAK, KBI, ABC and by the three lines IO, JO, KO, into three triangles IOJ, JOK, KOI.

The angles of the four triangles are
$$\angle ICJ, \angle CJI, \angle JIC,$$
$$\angle JAK, \angle AKJ, \angle KJA,$$
$$\angle KBI, \angle BIK, \angle IKB,$$
$$\angle ABC, \angle BCA, \angle CAB.$$
