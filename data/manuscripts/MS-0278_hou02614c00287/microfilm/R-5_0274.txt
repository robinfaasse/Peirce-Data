The fraction 7/4 expresses the idea that if between every two integers, 3 new numbers are interposed, this is the 7th from the beginning. The fraction 8/5 expresses the idea that if between every two integers, 4 new numbers are interposed, this is the 8th from the beginning. The new numbers are interposed so that according to some consistent way of viewing them they are equidistant. Quantity and magnitude are words generally applied to things which can be directly perceived, or about which we are supposed to be able to learn; while number, value, and measure are applied to the words of our system of designations for discriminating between things as greater, less, etc. All these words may be used either in the concrete to denote objects or in the abstract to denote characters. Magnitude is more particularly [del]applied[/del] [ins]appropriated[/ins] to things divisible into parts which can be taken by themselves and ascertained to be one greater, less, or equal to another; and in the use of it all qualities foreign to measurement are left out of account. We often speak of a quantity as varying, that is, changing its value, and this change does not make it a different quantity, since the quantity is identified by its relations to other quantities.
