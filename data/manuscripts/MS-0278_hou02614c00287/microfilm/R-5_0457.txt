A fraction is an expression involving two numbers one called its numerator and the other its denominator and of such a nature that if the denominator is one, the fraction equals the numerator, and if numerator and denominator are multiplied by the same number the value is not changed, but of two fractions having the same denominator the one with the greater numerator is the greater.

A divisor of a cardinal number, N, is any cardinal number the product of which by [ins]a[/ins] [del]another[/del] cardinal number equals N.

When one number is divided by another leaving a remainder, every common divisor of the divisor and dividend is a divisor of the remainder. Hence, every number is a divisor of 0, and [del]every[/del] 1 is a divisor of every number, and every number is a divisor of itself.

A part, or aliquot part, of a cardinal number, N, is a divisor of N other than N itself. A perfect number is the sum of its parts.

A factor of a cardinal number, N, is any number which multiplied by an aliquot part of N gives N as the product.

Thus, 1 has neither part nor factor, and is a factor of no number, but it is a part of every number except itself. Every [ins]other[/ins] number is a part of zero, but zero is its own sole factor. Zero is a part of no number, and is a factor only of itself.

A cardinal number, [ins]M,[/ins] is prime to a cardinal number, N, when no divisor of M is a factor of N.

A prime number is a cardinal number prime to every number except its own multiples.
