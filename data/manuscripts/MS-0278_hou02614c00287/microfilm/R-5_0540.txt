[u]Definition[/u]. A spherical triangle is a figure formed by any three planes, being one of four regions of rays, called its [u]enclosure[/u], into which the three planes separate the thistle of rays round their common point, called the [u]centre[/u].

The [u]exclosure[/u] of a spherical triangle is the multitude of planes [del]containing its centre which do not pass[/del] [ins]contain[/ins] any ray of its enclosure.

The [u]vertices[/u] of a spherical triangle are the three rays common to pairs of its 3 planes.

The [u]external angles[/u] of a spherical triangle are those angles [ins]round[/ins] at its vertices between pairs of its planes, which do not sweep over any ray of its enclosure.

The [u]exterior sides[/u] of a spherical triangle are those spherical lengths [del]along[/del] [ins]in[/ins] its planes between pairs of its vertices which do not pass across any plane of its exclosure.

The [u]internal angles[/u] of a spherical triangle are those angles round its vertices between pairs of its planes, which sweep across all the [del]pl[/del] rays of its enclosure.

The [u]interior angles[/u] of a spherical triangle are those spherical lengths in its planes between pairs of its vertices which pass across every plane of its exclosure.
