representing relations. As the indices are sufficiently
distinguished by being in different alphabets from the
relative terms, they may as well be written on the line.
Let, for instance
$$\Gamma b A$$
mean $A$ has the dual quality $b$. What I mean by a [u]dual[/u]
[u]quality[/u] will appear presently.

$$\Pi A \Pi B \ \Sigma c \ \ \ \Gamma c A \cdot \Gamma c B$$
That is, take any two points $A$ and $B$, there is a
dual quality, $c$, such that $A$ [ins]and[/ins] $B$ both possess it.

$$\Pi A \Pi B \ \Pi c \ \Pi d \ \Pi e \ \ \ \overline{\Gamma c A} + \overline{\Gamma c B} + \overline{\Gamma d A} + \overline{\Gamma d B}$$
$$+ \ \overline{\Gamma e A} + \Gamma e b$$

That is, take any two points $A$ and $B$ (not necessarily different
points, they may be one and the same), [del]then[/del] [ins]and[/ins] take any three
dual qualities $c$, $d$, [del]ep[/del] [ins]e[/ins] [del]&[/del], then, [ins]if[/ins] $A$ has all three qualities
and $B$ two of them, it also has the third. It is on this
account particularly I call them dual properties.

$$\Pi A \ \Pi B \ \Sigma c \ \ \ \Gamma c A \cdot \overline{\Gamma c B} + 1 A B$$
That is take any two points $A$ and $B$, either you can
find a dual quality that $A$ has and $B$ has not or
$A$ and $B$ are identical.
