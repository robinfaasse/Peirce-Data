Your simple propositions become complex when the terms are defined, so that the distinction is merely verbal.

How does some A is B imply the separate existence of A and B? I should think it only implies the existence of the one something that is spoken of. So thus. Some murderer [del]are[/del] [ins]is a[/ins] kind husband means

Something which unlawfully kills a human being is also married to a woman to which it is kind

This implies no doubt that there is some human being killed and also a wife but it does not seem to me to imply any separate existence of the murderer and the kind husband.

You say that No a is b "makes an implication" of existence of something, unless the speaker denies that something exists. There seems to me to be a difference between denying and not asserting. Does No a is b "make an implication" of Fermat's Theorem unless the speaker is "prepared to deny that theorem"? It seems to me one thing is as relevant as the other.

It is not necessary to regard "All but a is b" as asserting the existence of anything. There is no absurdity in supposing that it refrains from passing any judgment upon that point just as it does about the binomial theorem.
