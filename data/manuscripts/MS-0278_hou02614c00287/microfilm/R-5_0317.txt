A=0 & E=0 \\

Another equation $F=0$ will give a second tetrahedron. The plane, $A=0$ $E=0$, of the first tetrahedron will cut the corresponding plane of the second tetrahedron, $A=0$ $F=0$, in the line $A=0$ $E=0$ $F=0$; and all the four [del]intersecting[/del] lines of intersection so formed lie in the plane $E=0$ $F=0$. Here, then we have come, like rolling down hill, upon a quite difficult theorem, that if 4 lines meet in one point, and two tetrahedra are formed with their vertices one by one on these lines, [ins]the[/ins] corresponding planes of these tetrahedra intersect in 4 complanar lines.
