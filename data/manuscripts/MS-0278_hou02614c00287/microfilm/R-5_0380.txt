$$\frac{d^2s}{dt^2} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{d}{ds} \left(\frac{ds}{dt}\right)^2$$ [del]Hence[/del] $\int F ds = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{ds}{dt}\right)^2 + C$ and the negative of this constant is what is called the energy. How very odd an idea to suppose that such a constant represents a substance! There are lots of such constants that do not vary with the time. Such is the total rotation-area. Such is the centre of gravity of the universe. Why are not these, just as much as the total capacity for work, made substances? Most of them are more like substances in that the [u]zero[/u] means something. Foucault's pendulum experiment for example shows that the rotation-area of the system of fixed stars must be roughly zero. Whether one supposes space an "entity" as Newton did, or whether one supposes a "body alpha" or (very absurdly) makes the fixed stars or the total of the universe that body, in any case, the rotation-area is a [u]dynamical fact[/u]. In that respect, it is fully as much like a substance as energy & is more like it in that its complete absence would mean something.
