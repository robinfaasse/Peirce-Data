[u]Definition[/u]. If all the stages of a simple motion are numbered consecutively, so that differences of [del]one[/del] [ins]determinate[/ins] unit represent the [del]amount[/del] [ins]determinate[/ins] [del]of motion[/del] [ins]displacement[/ins] required to [del]carry[/del] [ins]bring[/ins] one [del]particle[/del] [ins]point of a fixed ray[/ins] [del]A[/del] from the point [A] to the point [B] occupied by another [del]fixed[/del] particle B, or, what amounts to the same thing, the [del]motion[/del] [ins]displacement of a fixed ray[/ins] required to carry one fixed flat lamina [del]$\alpha$[/del] [ins]from[/ins] [del]to[/del] the plane ($\alpha$) to the plane ($\beta$) occupied by another fixed flat lamina, then these numbers may be called the stage-numbers [ins]or longitudes[/ins] of the particular motion.

When there is no limit to the motion, $\Theta$ will be the natural unit of measurement. When there are two distinct limits, a partially natural unit may be found. When, as in ordinary long measure, there is but one limit, the unit must be entirely arbitrary.

The particular stage of displacement [del]assumed[/del] called zero is in any case arbitrary, and also the sense of the motion. For a simple motion being a singly-continuous multitude of positions, is odd-dimensional and has a sense. Although after one circuit, $\Theta$, the positions of the [del]points[/del] [ins]particles[/ins] on the axis of translation, [ins]and of straight fibres through both axes[/ins] and of flat laminae round the axis of rotation, are restored, yet [del]they[/del] [ins]other points and[/ins] [del]are not[/del] particles, [ins]and[/ins] flat laminae, [del]as well as[/del] [ins]and[/ins] all fibres [ins]are[/ins] except not restored, and therefore the axes considered as paths of motion are double, and the [del]measurement[/del] [ins]numbering[/ins] is carried up to $2\Theta$ as a limit. For most purposes, $2\Theta$ is considered as equal to zero, and is added or subtracted, as convenience may dictate.
