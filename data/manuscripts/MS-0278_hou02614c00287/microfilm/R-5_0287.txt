Every perspective representation of a [del]straight[/del] [ins]straight[/ins] [del]line is a straight line[/del] [ins]line[/ins]. For let AB be the natural line; then the perspective representation of it is the intersection of the plane SAB, with the plane of delineation.

Consequently, as seen in perspective, all parallel lines will intersect [del]on[/del] [ins]in[/ins] the vanishing line of their common plane.

Thus, [del]all[/del] [ins]every pair of[/ins] straight lines in a plane either intersect in that plane or their perspective representations have a point of intersection.

But a perspective representation of a point is a point. To make this hold without exception, we speak of the [u]line at infinity[/u], meaning the points at infinity which in a perspective representation appear as a straight line.
