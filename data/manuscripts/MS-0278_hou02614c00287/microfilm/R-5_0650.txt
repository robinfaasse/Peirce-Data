16 [u]Listing's numbers[/u] are numbers [del]one[/del] more numerous by one [del]than[/del] [ins]X[/ins] [del]the dimensionality of space[/del] which [del]are[/del] characterize the shape of a figure apart from its singularities. For three dimensional space, they are the [u]choresis[/u], [u]cyclosis[/u], [u]periphraxis[/u], and [u]apeiry[/u] of the figure in question.

A figure has [u]choresis[/u] one if and only if, a particle can [del](whether in time finite or infinite is of no consequence)[/del] move within the figure to every point of it. It has choresis zero only if it does not exist. The number of separate [del]part[/del] bodies of choresis one that must occupy parts of a figure in order to reduce the unoccupied place to choresis zero is the choresis of the figure. This form of statement shows the relation of choresis to the other Listing numbers. It is more familiar expressed by saying that the choresis is the number of parts of the figure that are such that a particle cannot move from one to another within the figure.

A figure has [u]cyclosis[/u] zero if, and only if, it cannot contain a stress in the line of which a particle might move so as to return to its first point unless there is room in the figure for this stress to move by a non-strict mere motion so as to become a particle. The cyclosis of a figure is the number of parts in it that must be occupied, [del]that[/del] each of them having choresis one and cyclosis zero, and being of dimensionality one
