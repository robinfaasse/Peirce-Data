A little training in the use of this system, will quickly
develope mathematical distinctness of thought. Let
us suppose we are considering values of $x$ and of $y$
which satisfy certain conditions, — say which satisfy the
equations $\Phi(x,z)=0$ $\Psi(y,z)=0$ for real
values of $z$. As we are thinking of no other values, $x$ and $y$
may be used to mean such values only. Let us write $fxy$
to mean $F(x,y)=0$, $rx$ to mean that $x$ is real, and
$my$ to mean that the modulus of $y$ is less than $1$. Now,
your pupils will learn very quickly to apprehend instantly such
distinctions as those between
$$!x . ?y \ \ rx . fxy + \overline{my}$$
which means that if any value of $y$ satisfying $\Psi(y,z)=0$ with
a real value of $z$, has its modulus equal to or greater than $1$,
then there is a [ins]real[/ins] value of $x$ satisfying $\Phi(x,z)=0$ with [del]a[/del] [ins]real[/ins] $z$,
which [del]is[/del] satisfies the equation $F(x,y)=0$ independently of the
value of $y$.
$$!x \ ?y \ \ rx (fxy + my)$$
means the same except that it positively asserts that there is a
real value of $x$ satisfying $\Phi(x,z)=0$ with a real $z$.
$$?y \ !x \ \ rx (fxy + my)$$
means that such a real value of $x$ exists and that it satisfies
$F(x,y)=0$ for every value of $y$ there may be (if any) that with
a modulus greater than or equal to $1$ satisfies $\Psi(y,z)=0$ for a
real $z$.
