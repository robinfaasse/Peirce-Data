It is mathematically demonstrable that this is substantially
that mode of combining propositions which is used in the
hypothetical proposition, "If A is true, B is true." Only, this
must be interpreted, not according to the customary usage
of language, but according to a strict generality.

It is next requisite to introduce the idea of a general
statement. A certain proposition has, as it were, a blank
left in it, with liberty to fill that up no matter how,
and yet, it is asserted, the proposition will be true.
That is a general proposition.

From the three fundamental conceptions of (1) assertion,
(2) illation, (3) generalization, all other conceptions
of logic are compounded.

There can be no dispute about necessary inferences,
when they are rightly understood. But the logical analysis
and classification of them appears under a new light
when relative terms are taken into account. In fact,
most of the traditional doctrines of logic are [del]reversed[/del]
overthrown. All of the ideas upon which Kant's table
of categories is based are refuted. Still, curiously enough,
the triadic form is better established than ever.

All inference can be reduced to this form:
A is true,
and If B is true, C is true;
Hence, If from A. [del]follows[/del] [ins]follows[/ins] B, C is true.
