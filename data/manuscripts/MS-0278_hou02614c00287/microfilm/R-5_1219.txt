To find the Day of the Week on which any Date
of the Christian Era falls.

I. Reckon each year as beginning on the 1st day of March, referring all
previous dates to the preceding year.

II. Four numbers, or indices, are necessary for our purpose; namely:-
the Century Index, the Year Index, the Month Index, and the Day Index.

III. The Century index is 0 (zero) for any date of the Old Style.
For dates of New Style, the Century Index is as follows:-
A.D. 1582 - 1699 inclusive, 4,
A.D. 1700 - 1799 " 3,
A.D. 1800 - 1899 " 2,
A.D. 1900 - 2099 " 1; and so on.

IV. [u]To find the Year Index, add to the number of the year its fourth part[/u],
omitting fractions. [u]This result may be reduced by dividing it by 7 and keeping
only the remainder.[/u]
[u]Example.[/u] To find the Year Index of 1894; we have
1894 + 473 = 2367 = Year Index; for which (dividing by 7) we
may substitute the remainder, 1. Thus 1 = the Year Index of 1894.

[u]Note.[/u] Before dividing the number of the year by 4, we may shorten the work
by subtracting from it any convenient multiple of 28; such as 1400, 1540, 1680,
1820, 1960. Thus, 1894 - 1820 = 74; 74 + 18 = 92 = 7 X 13 + 1.
Hence, 1 = the Year Index of 1894.
The sum of the Century Index and the Year Index may be called the Corrected Year
Index. This is, therefore, equal to 3 for 1894; 4 for 1895; 6 for 1896; 0 for
1897; 1 for 1898; 2 for 1899; 3 for 1900; etc.

V. [u]The Month Indices are as follows:-[/u]
March 0 September 2
April 3 October 4
May 5 November 0
June 1 December 2
July 3 January 5
August 6 February 1
These numbers should be carefully committed to memory.

VI. [u]The Day Index is the same as the number of the day of the month;[/u] but
from this any multiple of 7 may be deducted.
