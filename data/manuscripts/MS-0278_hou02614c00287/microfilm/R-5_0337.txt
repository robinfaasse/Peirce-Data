Criterion is taken in two senses; for the rule is called the criterion in one sense, and the
object whose determination is evidence is called a criterion in another sense.
The Oxf. Dict. says it is a test, principle, rule, canon, or standard. Now test, principle,
rule and canon seem to be all one. But the standard seems to be the object determined.
Fuller says "the moving [del]of a statue[/del] was made the criterion of women's chastity." (But whether
its moving or not, or its moving one way or another is the criterion is not said.) In any case, it
[del]seems to[/del] He seems to mean that the principle is the Criterion, and yet he does not say so. For is it
he says [del]the[/del] state of the statue is to motion that is the criterion. This is the object determined.
But Blackstone says "some probations or trial, which the law of the country has ordained for a
criterion of truth and falsehood" Here the trial is the object determined. But whether the mode of trial
is so, or not, is another question. That mode remains the same whichever way the trial issues. A mode
of trial, being ordained, would seem to be the rule.
Mrs. Hughes says "regular uniformity and the straight line were the criterions of taste and
beauty" She probably means that the rule that where there is regular uniformity [del]and straight lines[/del]
there there is taste or beauty. That would make the rule the criterion; but she expresses herself
so inaccurately that it is hard to say.
Another quotation (from [del]Tale of[/del] Sedley) says "Lord Stokerland the criterion of gallantry and politeness"
This is the object determined.
Froude says "we have no criterion by which, in these matters, degrees of good and evil admit
of being measured" What this means depends on whether "by which" means by the observation of which
or whether it means "according to which"
The only time Aristotle uses the word it means the object that will be determined, and I think that
is the preferable meaning. This object may be a single object but is generally a kind of object.
