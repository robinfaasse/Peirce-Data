A plane is a surface of which every part is like
every other part, and of which the back and
front sides are alike. To form a plane, two
[ins]rigid things,[/ins] [del]bodies[/del] A and B, are ground down against
one another, until they have a common
surface; and, then one is moved over the
other until this surface is [del]made[/del] [ins]ground down to be[/ins] everywhere
alike, so that the two things fit together in any
position so [del]long[/del] [ins]far[/ins] as certain portions of their sur-
faces are concerned. Then, a third thing, C, is
fitted to A in the same manner. But A may
fit both B and C without these two fitting
one another. When, however, AB, AC, and BC
all fit, the fitting surfaces are called planes.
