Definitions.
Of antecedent and subsequent connection.

<u>Clause 1</u>. If any one integer, C, is in antecedent connection with any integer, M, and if any integer, N, is S to M, then C will be in antecedent connection with N.

<u>Clause 2</u>. If any integer, C, is [ins]not[/ins] in antecedent connection with an integer, N, then C is not N and is not in antecedent connection with any integer, K, that is in antecedent connection with N.

<u>Clause 3</u>. To say that one integer is in antecedent connexion with another is the same as to say that the latter is in [del]antecedent[/del] [ins]subsequent[/ins] connexion with the former.

<u>Corollary</u>. By clause 2, every integer is in antecedent (and hence by clause 3, also in subsequent) connexion with itself.
