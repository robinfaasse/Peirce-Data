§1. Suppose a person says that if one thing, A, is true, then another thing, B, is true. Then, if what he says is true, and if the antecedent A is true, the consequent, B is also true; is it not?

§2. This granted, I inform you that a person does say: "If what I am saying is true, black is white." (I know, for I am the party.) Then, by the principle of §1 just admitted, if what he says is true (and if the antecedent is true, but that is the same thing), the consequent is true, and black is white.

§3. But that is all he says, namely, that if what he says is true, black is white. So what he says precisely accords with the fact and is true.

§4. But we have seen [ins]in §2[/ins] that if what he says is true, black is white. Since then, by §3, what he says is true, it follows that black is white, though you would hardly think it without this clear demonstration.
