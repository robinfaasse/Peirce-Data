[u]Lesson III.[/u]

[u]On Projection[/u]

What the drawing masters call "perspective pictures" mathematicians call "projections." Only the [del]mathematicians[/del] [ins]latter[/ins] add something to the picture which we will now proceed to explain.

Suppose you take a thin board and cut a groove or socket for the nose so as to hold the eyes perfectly steady. Then if you place yourself in position and put up a flat pane of glass between you and any scene [ins]or object[/ins] you may wish to represent in perspective, and draw on that glass with talc or any sticky ink you will trace out [del]on the pane[/del] [ins]on the pane[/ins] a perspective picture. And the properties of this picture will be found to be that the place on the glass where any object in the [del]picture[/del] [ins]scene[/ins] will fall on the picture is fixed & determined by being in the same line between the real object and the point at which the eye is situated.
