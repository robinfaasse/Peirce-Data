$\frac{1}{e-1}$ where $e$ is a number greater than 1. Namely,

<u>Art. 5</u> We may however examine the $\Sigma$ of a few simple
functions, where $x$ is a whole number.
$$\Sigma x = (x-1) + (x-2) + (x-3) + \dots + 1 + 0 + C$$
$$= \frac{1}{2} x(x-1) + C$$
$$\Sigma x(x-1) = (x-1)(x-2) + (x-2)(x-3) + \dots 2 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 0 + 0(-1) + C$$
$$= \frac{1}{3} x(x-1)(x-2)$$
For $\Delta \frac{1}{3} x(x-1)(x-2) = \frac{1}{3}(x+1)x(x-1) - \frac{1}{3}x(x-1)(x-2)$
$$= \frac{1}{3}(x-1) \begin{pmatrix} x^2 + x \\ -x^2 + 2x \end{pmatrix}$$
$$= (x-1)x$$
$$\Sigma x(x-1) \dots (x-n) = \frac{1}{n+2} x(x-1) \dots (x-n)(x-n-1)$$
For $\Delta x(x-1) \dots (x-n)(x-n-1) = (x+1)x(x-1) \dots (x-n)$
$$\phantom{\text{For } \Delta x(x-1) \dots (x-n)(x-n-1)} - x(x-1) \dots (x-n)(x-n-1)$$
$$= [(x+1) - (x-n-1)] x(x-1) \dots (x-n)$$
$$= (n+2) x(x-1) \dots (x-n)$$

<u>Continued products of this kind are called factorials</u>
