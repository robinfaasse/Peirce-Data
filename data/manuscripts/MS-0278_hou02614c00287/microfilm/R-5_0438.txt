advocate thought not. However, said he, it makes no
difference. Leaving the question open what Dick said,
Tom's opinion was certainly correct. For suppose
it were not. Then, Harry's testimony would be
reduced to the statement that eggs are eggs, and
would be true. But if Harry's testimony is thus true,

and so would be [del]true[/del] [ins]correct[/ins], and thus the supposition it

Tom's opinion is therefore absolutely correct; and
according to it either Dick did not tell the truth
or Harry did. Supposing then that Dick said
that Harry would lie, if Dick was lying when
he said so, Harry must have told the truth.
According to Tom's correct opinion, then, Harry
told the truth any way. Now since Harry told
the truth and Tom's opinion was correct we
must accept the consequence of Harry's
testimony that what Susan said was true.
