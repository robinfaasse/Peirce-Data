A second corollary is that every member of a class for which the Fermatian inference holds good, except only $V$, is the sole [ins]member[/ins] $Q$ of some member. For [del]let[/del] suppose $B$ is a member of the class, [del]other than $V$,[/del] for which this is not true; and let the character $\beta$ be that of being either $V$ or not $B$. Then since $B$ is not the sole $Q$ of anything, but everything has a $Q$, it follows that every member has something not $B$ that is $Q$ of it. Thus, the premises of the Fermatian inference holds and the conclusion is that every thing is either $V$ or not-$B$, which contradicts the hypothesis.

[del]A third corollary is that no member of a dinnumerable but innumerable class is $Q$ of itself.[/del]
