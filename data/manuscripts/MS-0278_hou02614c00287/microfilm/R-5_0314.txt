In like manner, an equation may be found for any 4 points (and generally only one) since they lie in one 3 dimensional space. Let E and F, respectively, be the equations for the [del]two[/del] tetrahedra. That is, the planes of the first tetrahedron will be represented each by E [ins]together with[/ins] [del]and[/del] one of the four equations A, B, C, D; and those of the second tetrahedron each by F and one of A, B, C, D. The corresponding planes will therefore intersect, each in lines represented by the two equations E and F, together with one of the four A, B, C, D. Hence these lines all lie in the plane represented by E = 0, F = 0.
