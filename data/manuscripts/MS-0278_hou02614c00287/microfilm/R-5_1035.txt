Hyperbolic

The calculation of [ins]hyperbolic[/ins] logarithms of numbers near to unity is effected by the use of the formula
$$\log (1+x) = x - \frac{1}{2}x^2 + \frac{1}{3}x^3 - \frac{1}{4}x^4 + \text{etc.}$$

Thus the logarithm of $1.1$ is calculated as follows:

$$\begin{array}{r|r}
x = 0.100000 & \frac{1}{2}x^2 = .005000 \\
\frac{1}{3}x^3 = 0.000333 & \frac{1}{4}x^4 = .000025 \\
\frac{1}{5}x^5 = 0.000002 & \\
\hline
0.100335 & .005025 \\
0.005025 & \\
\hline
\log 1.1 = \phantom{0.0}0.095310 &
\end{array}$$

The logarithms of higher numbers are obtained by multiplications, and involutions, and finally by interpolations. Thus, the $7^{\text{th}}$ power of $1.1$ is about $1.94872$ and consequently, the logarithm of this number is $7$ times $0.095310$ or $0.667170$; while the $8^{\text{th}}$ power of $1.1$ is about $2.14359$, so that the logarithm of this [ins]number[/ins] is about $0.762480$. Then, as the difference of these [del]logarithms[/del] [ins]numbers[/ins] is to the difference of the logarithms, so approximately is the [del]difference[/del] [ins]excess of 2[/ins] over the first number to the excess of the logarithm of 2 over the logarithm of the first number. Accordingly, the logarithm of 2 is $0.69$, as given by this rule, accurately to 2 places. [del]Vulgar loga[/del] The vulgar logarithm of a number is the natural number multiplied by such a constant as to make the logarithm of 10 equal to 1. This constant, called the modulus of the system of vulgar logarithms is $0.4342944819$. Every system of logarithms has its modulus in the same way.
