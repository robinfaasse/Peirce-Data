[u]Art. . Definitions[/u] . A part of a whole number, $N$, is a whole number, [ins]$\wedge$[/ins] unequal to that number, which, multiplied by a suitable whole number, called [del]a factor[/del] [ins]a divisor[/ins], of the first number, $N$, gives that number as the product. Thus, the parts of $6$ are $3, 2, 1$; its factors, or divisors, $2, 3, 6$.

A [u]prime number[/u] is a number with a single part.

A [u]prime factor[/u] of a whole number is a prime number that is a factor of that number.

A [u]homogeneous[/u] number is a whole number with a single prime factor. Thus, $81 = 3.3.3.3$ is a homogeneous number. The [u]multiplicity[/u] of the factor is the number of times it has to be used as such to give the whole product.

A [u]heterogeneous[/u] number is a whole number having more than one prime factor.

A [u]composite[/u] number is a whole number which is the product two or more homogeneous parts.

Two whole numbers are said to be [u]prime to[/u] one another when they have no common divisor.

[u]Scholium[/u] . The smallest [del]variations[/del] [ins]departures[/ins] from the above definitions will lead to difficulties; so that they should be learned by heart.
