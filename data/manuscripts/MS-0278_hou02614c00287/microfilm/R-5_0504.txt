[u]§ . Definitions[/u] . A factor of a whole
number is a whole number unequal to that number
but which multiplied by a suitable whole number
gives that number as the product.

A [u]prime[/u] number is a whole number with
just one factor.

A [u]prime factor[/u] of a whole number is a [del]prime[/del] [ins]prime[/ins]
number which is a factor of it.

A [ins]whole[/ins] number is said to be [u]prime to[/u] a whole
number when they have no prime factors in
common, [ins]and neither is a prime factor of the other.[/ins]

[del]A composite number is a whole number with more than[/del] [ins]which is a product of prime[/ins]
[del]factors each repeated raised to a power equal to[/del] [ins]whose[/ins] [del]index is one or more.[/del]

[u]Corollaries[/u] . No number is a factor of itself.

1 is a factor of every number [ins]except itself.[/ins] Every number except
0 is a factor of 0.

The one factor of every prime number is 1.

1 is not a [del]prime[/del] number, because it has no factor,
at all.

0 is not a prime number because every prime
number is a factor of it.

1 is the only number [del]prime to itself.[/del]

0 and 1 are the only numbers neither prime
nor composite.
