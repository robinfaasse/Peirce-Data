It being agreed that every angle and arc shall be measured
in the way in which it is $< 180^{\circ}$;
call an arc "acute" if, and only if, it is $< 90^{\circ}$ if greater "obtuse"
call two angular quantities "alike" if and only if both are
acute or both obtuse, if one is obtuse and the other acute
call them "unlike".

Then by the two formulae
$\cos c = \cos a \cos b + \sin a \sin b \cos C$
$\cos C + \cos A \cos B = \sin A \sin B \cos c$
it follows that
if the number of acute angles is odd
or if the number of obtuse sides is odd
every side and its opposite angle must be alike

If, however the [ins]number of[/ins] acute angles and the [del]number of[/del] obtuse sides are both [del]even[/del]

adjacent angles remain constant any infinitesimal variation of the third

But a triangle may have all its sides and angles equal to $90^{\circ}$. Hence
we may suppose
$\begin{array}{cc}
A = 90^{\circ} + \delta A & a = 90^{\circ} + \delta a \\
B = 90^{\circ} + \delta B & b = 90^{\circ} + \delta b \\
C = 90^{\circ} + \delta C & c = 90^{\circ} + \delta c
\end{array}$
Where of $\delta A, \delta B, \delta C$ two are negative or all positive
and of $\delta a, \delta b, \delta c$, one or three are negative.
We now have $\begin{array}{ccc}
\cos A = - \delta A & \cos a = - \delta a & \sin A = \sin a = 1 \\
\cos B = - \delta B & \cos b = - \delta b & \sin B = \sin b = 1 \\
\cos C = - \delta C & \cos c = - \delta c & \sin C = \sin c = 1
\end{array}$
and the two formulae become
$\delta A = \delta a + \delta b . \delta c = \delta a + \delta B . \delta C$ so that $\delta b . \delta c = \delta B . \delta C$
terms of the third degree being neglected.
