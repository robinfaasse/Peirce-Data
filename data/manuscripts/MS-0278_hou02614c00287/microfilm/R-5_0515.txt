§ . Definitions . A factor of a [ins]whole[/ins] number, N,
is a whole number [del]less than[/del] [ins]whose double is not greater than[/ins] N, [del]which[/del] [ins]and[/ins] multiplied by a
[ins]suitable[/ins] whole number gives N as the product.

[del]A number is prime to every number with which

A [u]prime number[/u] is a [del]num[/del] whole number with just
one factor.

A [u]prime factor[/u] [del]of a f[/del] [ins]of a number[/ins] is a prime number which
is factor of the other number.

[del]A[/del] [ins]A[/ins] number is [u]prime to[/u] another if they have no prime

[u]Corollary[/u] . 1 is a factor of every number higher
than 1 but not of 1 nor 0.

0 is not a factor of any number, except itself.

1 and 0 are not prime numbers, because they have
no factors at all.

1 and 0 are prime to all numbers.
