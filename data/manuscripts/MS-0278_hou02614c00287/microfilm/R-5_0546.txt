[u]Art. 23.[/u] [ins]^[/ins] The most important of the problems of topical geometry, after the fundamental propositions given in [del]Arts.[/del] [ins]Art.[/ins] 12-22 have been settled, is the [u]Census Problem[/u]. The question here is to determine the connection between the numbers of marked points, lines, surfaces, and spaces in any configuration. Assuming that every singularity is marked, [ins]and that every [del]marked place stops at a marked limit[/del] [ins]called the Census Theorem, or Listing's Theorem[/ins] [del]thereof[/del][/ins] the rule is that the sum of the virtual numbers of points and surfaces equals that of the lines and spaces. The virtual number of points is the actual number of different points. The virtual number of [ins]marked[/ins] lines is the actual number of totally different [ins]marked[/ins] lines, diminished by [del]the[/del] [ins][Def. 42.][/ins] their total cyclosis. [del]The[/del] [ins]The[/ins] cyclosis [ins]of a place[/ins] is the number of [del]closed[/del] self-returning filaments which can [del]be[/del] exist in the place none of which can by any motion in the place either shrink to nothing or [del]come[/del] [ins]be brought[/ins] into coincidence with any [del]of[/del] other of those filaments. The virtual number of marked surfaces is the actual number of totally different marked surfaces less their total Cyclosis and increased by their total Periphraxis. [del][Def. 43.][/del] [ins][Def. 43.][/ins] The periphraxis of a place is the number of sacs, or closed films, which can exist in the place and can [del]neither shrink to[/del] [ins]by no motion in the[/ins] place either shrink to nothing or come into coincidence with another. The virtual number of marked spaces is the actual number of totally different marked spaces less their total cyclosis increased by [del]their[/del] total periphraxis and diminished by their total Immen-sity. [del][Def. 44.][/del] [ins][Def. 44.][/ins] The immensity of a place is the number of unlimited solids which [del]it contains[/del] [ins]can exist in[/ins] which by no motion in the place can either shrink to nothing or [del]come[/del] [ins]be[/ins] brought into coincidence with any other.
