P.S. The solution given in my above letter does not take into
account the action of bending the rod that comes down
from the engine. That ought to be proved by mathematical
calculation to be too slight to be dangerous, or else ought to
be allowed for. But for that purpose I should have to know the
weight of the rails etc, and have an exact drawing &
scale of the whole thing. If, however, the piece coming down
from the engine remains in contact with the guide after the
switch is turned long enough for all vibration to subside, there
could be no danger.

Of course, the longer the guides are made, the less
danger there will be from the terrific violence of the
action.

Ought not the piece running down from the engine to
[del]run[/del] [ins]move[/ins] between two guides to avoid the switch being thrown
over with such violence as to break something? Or
it could clutch the guide on both sides, two little wheels in
the piece attached to the engine being pressed against the
two sides of the guide by springs.
