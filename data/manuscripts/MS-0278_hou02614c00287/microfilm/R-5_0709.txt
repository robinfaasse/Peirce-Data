DR. ABBOT'S SUPPRESSED REPLY.

TO THE EDITOR OF THE NATION:

SIR: Since Mr. Peirce has thought fit to
bring this subject before your readers, and to
comment on Prof. Royce's conduct, as charged
by Dr. Abbot in connection with his reply to
a threat of legal proceedings, I feel compelled
to ask you to publish the evidence on that
point in full.

Dr. Abbot bases his charge upon a letter
written to him by me, as Prof. Royce's coun-
sel. In a pamphlet addressed to the govern-
ing boards of Harvard College (but widely cir-
culated and put on private sale), Dr. Abbot
characterizes that letter as an attempt on
Prof. Royce's part, "to gag the man he had
injured," and formally sums up his accusation
by asserting that "Prof. Royce has sought,
with incredible cowardice and meanness, to
deprive me of all opportunity of being heard
in self-defence."

I now give the letter (of which Dr. Abbot
publishes only an extract of a few lines
of formal warning) which implies Dr. Abbot's reply.
I should premise that I knew nothing of the
controversy until Prof. Royce sought my ad-
vice in consequence of threats of a law-suit
from Dr. Abbot. At that time Dr. Abbot's
reply had been set up in type by the [u]Journal[/u]
[u]of International Ethics[/u] in the expectation
of publishing that, together with a re-
joinder by Prof. Royce, and a final
reply which Dr. Abbot was to write,
all in the July number. This plan had
broken off, as stated by Dr. Adler in his
pamphlet to Dr. Abbot, because he could not agree
with Dr. Adler as to the tone which he should
adopt in his final reply; Dr. Adler requiring
a parliamentary tone, while Dr. Abbot de-
manded a freedom which he described as the
freedom of the "woods." Dr. Abbot's
main reply, already in type, which is referred
to in my letter. Dr. Adler and Prof. Royce
are both editors of the Journal.

[date]June 9, 1891[/date].

LARCH STREET, CAMBRIDGE, MASS.,
[date]June 9, 1891[/date].

[u]Dr. Francis E. Abbot, Cambridge, Mass.:[/u]

DEAR SIR: Dr. Adler has sent me your article entitled
"Dr. Royce's 'Professional Warning'" has been
submitted to me as a part of the case upon
which my professional advice has been sought. I
must call your attention to some passages in
it, in the hope that you will think it well, upon
deliberation, to revise.

I will say at the outset that, considering the
severity of Dr. Royce's criticism, I can, on my
own part, that you are justified in replying
with warmth, and that you should properly be
allowed more warmth than the ordinary dis-
cussion of such subjects calls for. Of that Dr.
Royce is aware, and he is willing that in
the heat of your reply you have in some places
used language which I think you will, on re-
vision, with cooler judgment, to allow to re-
main to lower the tone of your argument.

But there are other passages in which you are
right in thinking that Dr. Royce has trans-
gressed the limits of courteous controversy. I
must say that your article, in some places,
goes far beyond anything that he has said.

By the publication of it, in its present form,
he protests against the publication, or any
other publication of it, in that you are
must point out to you that it may, if circu-
lated, entail a serious legal responsibility.

Many of your charges are of Dr. Royce being
guilty of "a slanderous attack" and of "li-
bel," and that he has "publicly slandered
for" you seek to belittle and injure him in
his profession and business as a teacher in
Harvard College; you charge him with "a policy
of wilful misrepresentation; you seek to bring
him into contempt by charging him with having
made a wanton and injurious attack upon your
professional reputation, compromised his acade-
mical position, compromised the dignity of
Harvard College, of which he is a pro-
fessor, publicly traduced and libelled a fel-
low-citizen, and finally you pronounce him
guilty of "a slanderous attack."

Such language, even though used in con-
troversy, and in replying to criticism, goes
so far exceeds the proper limits that in my
judgment you cannot indulge in it without
danger of legal liability.

Permit me, too, as a cool spectator of the
controversy, to say that your use of it really
weakens and lowers a very forcible argument,
and must have the effect of distracting atten-
tion from the points you wish to make, and
stamping the whole discussion as a strangely
unbefitting a journal of ethical science.
And aside from the effect of such an article
upon yourself, let me call to your attention
the scandal which is brought upon Harvard
College by such a public wrangle between two
of her instructors.

I have not read carefully the whole of Dr.
Royce's article, but I have read the parts
which must be most offensive to you, and
while I do not defend, in all respects, the tone
of his review, I think that you have greatly
exaggerated and misinterpreted it. As I said
to you on Tuesday, Dr. Royce disclaims, and
disclaims in the strongest way, any intention to
wound you, or to reflect in any way upon your
personal character; and it is only by a misinter-
pretation to insist upon putting the worst
and most offensive construction upon what he
says, omitting the qualifications which go far
to soften his hostile expressions?

Is it necessary for you to elsewhere calls
you an impostor, as you repeatedly charge,
or does it assist in making your point? If
the Journal of Ethics should publish
of it, it could not do so with self-respect. The
editors are, however, very willing to publish
it if it is revised, as I have suggested,
if you will leave out those passages which are
not necessary to your argument, and for the
ance of tameness. If these passages are omit-
ted, or so changed as to be free from objec-
tion, it can still appear in the July
number, and without any other comment than
a brief note that a reply will appear in the
October issue.

I hope that you will adopt my suggestions
and make the changes, which I believe will
strengthen the article in the minds of those
whose opinion you value, and that you will
not overlook the great advantage it will be to
you to have your reply published in the same
Journal which originally published the review,
and that you will be willing to conform to the
vious requirements which the Journal must
demand. Very sincerely yours,
J. B. WARNER.

P. S.—Please let me know your decision as
soon as possible, as the Journal must be made
up. Will you kindly return my copy of your
article?
J. B. W.
