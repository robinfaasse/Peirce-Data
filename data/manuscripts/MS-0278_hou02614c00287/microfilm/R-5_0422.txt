In the calculus of logic, a proposition is separated
by its copula, $\prec$, into two parts, as $A \prec B$. But these
parts may again be separated in like manner, as
$(A \prec B) \prec C$ and $A \prec (B \prec C)$, and so on indefinitely.
It becomes interesting to inquire how many such
propositional forms with a given number of copu-
las there are; and the same problem may arise
in general algebra where $\prec$ is any nonassociative
sign of operation. We may also consider these
forms under the aspect of forking trees, thus,
