A FIRST KEY,

showing for each integral value of the exponent, [del]e[/del] [ins]e[/ins], up to 64 (inclusively),
the application of Euler's extension of Fermat's Theorem, to the fact that the numbers m corresponding to each e are the
only integers to each of which just e integers are prime without exceeding it.
The Theorem is that if b is any integer prime to many one of the m's corresponding to any e, then b^e is one more than
an integer number of times that same m. [del]Examples[/del] For e = 10, the only m's are 11 and 22, that means that one less
than the 10th power of any number prime to 11 is divisible by 11, and that one less
than the 10th power of a number prime to 22 is divisible by 22. Thus, 2 being
prime to 11, 2^10 - 1, which is 1023, is divisible by 11; and 3 being prime to 22, 3^10 - 1,
which is 3^10 - 1 = 59048, is divisible by 22.
