If when we regard objects in
the same way, they appear
different, they must really
be different; but if they ap-
pear alike, it does not follow
that they are really alike, but
merely that whatever difference
there may be does not concern
us for the moment.

If our momentary purpose would
not be subserved by regarding any
given experience as resulting
from different moments, elements,
or parts, we should not so regard
it; since that will not deny that
it may really be so composed
