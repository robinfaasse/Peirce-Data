This gives us an entirely new proposition, the truth of which it would be hard to recognize in any other way. Let us first draw a line representing the "line at infinity," and cross it by the projections of the four lines (or two pairs of lines) A, B, C, D. Draw a diagonal from the intersection of A with C, through the like crossing of B with D and prolong it to infinity. This diagonal we will call E. Then let the line F be drawn through the intersection of A with D and parallel to E — that is meeting it [del]at[/del] [ins]at[/ins] the line at infinity. Also a line G, through the intersection of C with F and parallel to A, and a line H, through the crossing of B and F parallel to D. In the picture these [ins]parallel[/ins] lines all meet at infinity.
