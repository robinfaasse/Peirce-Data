[u]Theorem[/u]. Of [del]two[/del] any two rational
fractions [u]$\frac{M}{N}$[/u] and [u]$\frac{R}{S}$[/u] one is as small
and the other is as great as [u]$\frac{M+R}{N+S}$[/u] and
if either is both as small and as great
so also is the other.

[u]Proof[/u]. [del]If[/del] Either of the [del]two[/del] fractions, say
$\frac{M}{N}$ is as small [ins](or as great)[/ins] as $\frac{M+R}{N+S}$ or not according
as $MN+MS$ is as small [ins](or as great)[/ins] as $MN+NR$
or not, That is, according as $MS$ is as
small [ins](or as great)[/ins] as $NR$, and [del]its[/del] according as
$MS+RS$ is as small [ins](or as great)[/ins] as $NR+RS$. But
$\frac{M+R}{N+S}$ is as small [ins](or as great)[/ins] as $\frac{R}{S}$ according as
$MS+RS$ is as small [ins](or as great)[/ins] as [del]$R$[/del] [ins]$NR$[/ins]$+RS$.

But $\frac{M}{N}$ is either as small or as great as $\frac{M+R}{N+S}$
and therefore of the two fractions $\frac{M}{N}$ and $\frac{R}{S}$ one is as
small and the other as great as [del]$\frac{M+R}{N+S}$[/del] [ins]$\frac{M+R}{N+S}$[/ins] and if either
is both as small and as great as [del]$\frac{M+R}{N+S}$[/del] [ins]$\frac{M+R}{N+S}$[/ins] so likewise is the other.
