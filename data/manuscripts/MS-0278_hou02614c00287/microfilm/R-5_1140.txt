Examples of the $n$ school-girls' problem, [del]which[/del] solves
cyclically by B. P.

A number ($m$) of school girls take $n$ successive
walks in ranks of which the number in each rank is $p$, and
[del]no[/del] [ins]no[/ins] two girls are to be in same rank upon different walks.

In general
$$m = pq$$
$$q = (p-1)n + 1$$
$$n = \frac{m-1}{p-1}$$

The following examples have been solved by an exhaustive analytic research.

$$m = 4, \quad p = 2, \quad n = 3$$

of the [del]subscript[/del] indices in all the examples. This
solution is geometrically [del]represented[/del] [ins]expressed[/ins] as [del]$\sqrt[3]{1}$ with[/del] [ins]$\sqrt[3]{1}$ with[/ins] [del]reference to[/del]
