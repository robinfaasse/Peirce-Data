[pn]C[/pn] It is to be observed that there are [del]two grades[/del] [ins]varieties[/ins] of doubt, a more acute definite doubt whether a certain proposition be [ins]commonly[/ins] true or not, and a milder indefinite doubt as to what is the character of a given subject of speculation.

Besides belief and doubt, there is a third attitude of mind, that of calm ignorance, whether conscious or unconscious, — the latter the birth-stage of thought, the former its death.
