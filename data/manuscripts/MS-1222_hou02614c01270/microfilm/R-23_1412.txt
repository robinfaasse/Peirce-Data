These three sounds, which in Mulcaster's phrase 'entermedle
with each other' so much, will be conveniently considered
under one head. In fact, in the year 1600 and thereabout,
they were all pronounced alike. The sound 'daunt' only
existed in such words as [del]car[/del] [ins]are[/ins] mentioned above for [u]father[/u],
[u]rather[/u], [u]water[/u] (in the last century 'wahter,') took the sound of
long [u]a[/u], and [u]aunt[/u], [u]daunt[/u], &c were pronounced like other
words in [u]au[/u]. Words now sounded [u]dance[/u], and some others,
