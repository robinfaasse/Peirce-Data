[del]¶[/del] [ins]¶[/ins] But a knowledge of the old pronunciation is not merely
a curious thing, it leads to other knowledge highly important.
By shewing, in many places, puns hitherto unnoticed, it gives us an
understanding of lines which have been unintelligible, hitherto.
It suggests, too, many corrections of the text and renders many
precious emendations far less credible. Besides this, it helps us
in discovering the derivations of words; and finally it renders
clear and indisputable the fact that the writers of the Elizabethan
epoch had quite as definite though not so constrained a sys-
tem of orthography as our own; and that the arbitrary system of
pronunciation, which is especially characteristic of the English tongue,
is in many important respects of a distinctly modern origin.

Such being the results which were to follow an investigation
of this subject,
