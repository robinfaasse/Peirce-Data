It will be seen from these select quotations that for a period of
two centuries ending at or shortly after the year 1800, the sound of
[u]a[/u] long in French is made equivalent to the English diphthong [u]aw[/u].
Many of the authors we have cited were distinguished in their day for
accurate and varied scholarship, and their uncontradicted testimony
is strengthened by many contemporary grammatical works both English
& foreign. Percival, it is true, in his Spanish Grammar edited by
Minsheu in 1623, asserts that "[u]A[/u] is sounded plainely, with open-
ing the mouth, as in the Latine, French, and Italian, as in English
[u]man[/u], [u]can[/u], so in Spanish [u]manada[/u], [u]ensalada[/u]". But he had
reference probably to the sound of [u]a[/u] short in French, which as Bo-
yer and other writers tell was pronounced as at the present day.
