4. 'dance', 'daunt', 'dawn'.

These three sounds which, in Miltonic phrase, intermeddle with each other so much, will be conveniently considered under one head. There is no reason to suspect that, with the exception of a slight sinking of the humbler sound of [u]r[/u], words like 'car' have undergone any change of sound. 'Are' however was also pronounced 'air', an enunciation which is far from being eradicated even now, although it has failed to receive the sanction of any respectable authority. 'Father', and 'water' were respectively pronounced 'farther' and 'waiter'. [u]a[/u] following [u]w[/u], [u]wh[/u], and [u]qu[/u], and preceding [u]r[/u], was not pronounced broadly as at the present day, as in 'war', 'wharf', and 'quart', but with the force of 'ah'.* Accordingly 'war' and 'car' were perfect rhymes. On the other hand the terminations 'alm' and 'alf' were sounded 'awlm' and 'awlf'. This pronunciation was general, and is given by Gil, [del]Coote[/del], Butler & Jonson. In the words of the Elementarie: "We entertaine [u]l[/u], generallie before most of our consonants. For we write calm, balm, talk, walk, chalk, calf, calves, salves: yet we pronounce them so as if we had no, l, but melie the duble, w, or single, cawm, bawm, taulk, waulk, chauk, caulf, cauves, sauves."†
