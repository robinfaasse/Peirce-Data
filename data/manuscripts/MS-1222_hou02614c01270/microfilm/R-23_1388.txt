1. 'ooze'

This sound we derived from the Saxons, and it has consequent-
ly been in the language at all periods. Wallis describes it accu-
rately, and Baret remarks as follows: [u]O[/u] is also one of the vow-
elles, because it yieldeth a full and plaine voice, audible
from the breast to the eare, without any stop of the teeth, [del]or[/del]
But that which we call double o, (oo) I thinke in English is much
mistaken, and abused. For how can [u]oo[/u] have the name of [u]o[/u],
when it chaungeth his sound (which is as it were his name)
and doth degenerate into the nature and name of another
vowell? hop. hope, hoop. A diphthong, I thinke, it can not
be: for that no vowell can be compounded in a diphthong with
itselfe [del]+++[/del] Some thinke such wordes should be rather written
