<u>Th.</u>

Several mares' nests in this article.
The 'demonstration' which is so much
superior to 'assertion' really demonstrates
only that several words not now spelt with
<u>th</u>, as murder & anatomize, were formerly
aspirated by most people.

<u>Th</u> was doubtless a more prevalent sound
formerly [del]than[/del] [ins]than[/ins] at present. Exceptions of course
as sixt for sixth, fift for fifth & London
sound.

All R. G. W's 'demonstrations' prove nothing
but his astounding arrogance. He leaves nothing
settled except perhaps the pronunciation of <u>oi</u>.
The pronunciation of almost every [del]consonant[/del]
vowel or diphthong is left more unsettled
than ever. 'Conjecture', 'guesswork', 'mares nests'
'in the mire' is all that can justly be
said of his researches. He lacks the power of
generalization of the facts, ie, the forms
which come under his notice. Consequently
they lead him into deeper ignorance.

[del]The direct testimony of the most exact writers
goes for nothing when placed beside the reckless
chance misspelling of ignorant type-setters
or a far-fetched & miserable pun, the deepest
& strained allusion of a disordered fancy.[/del]
A tithe of the ingenuity displayed in the intro-
duction to Much Ado, the notes upon 'pierce',
the pronunciation of <u>th</u>, <u>oa</u>, & <u>ie</u>, rightly
directed, would have given us facts instead
of theory, a correct & suggestive article respecting
the changes in English Orthoepy in the place of
the heap of obscurity, inaccuracy & misleading
rubbish which forms the baseless fabric of
the Memorandums.
