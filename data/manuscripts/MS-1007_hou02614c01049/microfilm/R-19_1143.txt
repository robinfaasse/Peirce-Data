Second Book of the Transcendental Analytic.
The [del]analytic[/del] [ins]Analytic[/ins] of Principles.

Universal Logic is erected upon a ground plan which coincides quite strictly with the division of the higher faculties of cognition. These are: Understanding, Judgment, and Reason. That doctrine treats therefore in its analytic of conceptions, judgments, and [del]syllogisms[/del] [ins]conclusions[/ins], in direct conformity with the functions and order of that power of the mind which are comprehended under the more extensive appellation of the understanding in general.

The aforesaid merely formal logic abstracts from all content of the cognition (whether it is pure or empirical) and concerns itself merely with the form of thought (of discursive cognition) in general; so it can touch in its analytical part also upon the canon [del]of[/del] [ins]for[/ins] the reason too, whose form has its sure prescription which without considering the special nature of the cognition used therein, can be perfected a priori by mere analysis of the operations of the reason into their moments.

Transcendental logic, which is limited to a determined content, namely merely to pure cognitions a priori, cannot imitate it in this division.
