1.
<u>Of the Synthesis of Apprehension in the Intuition.</u>

Let our representations arise whence they may, whether
they [ins]be[/ins] [del]are[/del] produced by the influence of outward things or by inward
causes, be they originated as phenomena a priori or empirically,
they yet belong as modifications of the mind to the inner sense, and
as such all our cognitions are subject to the formal condition of the
inner sense, which is Time, as that in which they must be [del]ordered[/del] [ins]arranged[/ins]
together, conjoined and brought into relation. This is a general remark
which one must constantly bear in mind through out what follows.

Every intuition contains a manifold in itself which yet would not
be represented as such, did not the mind difference the time in
flow of the impressions on one another: for as contained in an instant,
can no representation be anything but an absolute unit. Now in
order that there should come out of this manifold the unity of intu-
ition (as something in the representation of space,) there is necessary
firstly the running through of the manifold and then the connection
of it, which operation I call the <u>Synthesis of Apprehension</u>, since it
