§ 2 Of Language

How shall an abstraction or meaning emerge in a
modification of consciousness? By the law of the preservation
of meaning forms only by being before connected with a
thing-or-things. Then it appears in a modification of con-
sciousness by means of abstraction. In every such abstraction
there are two [del]things[/del] [ins]objects[/ins] which differ in certain respects & are alike in
others. The [ins]proper[/ins] meaning of either object is its peculiar form, that
which it has in common with others is only a condition of
the appearance of the former. I may generalize the word
Language to express this common form, for that is a com-
mon form established in order to enable peculiar & meaning
forms to appear. By language, therefor I mean, [del]the[/del] com-
mon expressing form [del]regarded as an abstraction[/del]. By meaning,
I [del]refer to[/del] [ins]designate[/ins] the peculiar form [del]regarded in the same way[/del].
These being both abstractions have neither a real existence
among finite beings. What exists is an expression.
Were an expression [del]is not[/del] to partake of its own nature
not at all it would be mere language, were it so much
an expression as to be its own language, it would
amount to meaning. We have then the triad
