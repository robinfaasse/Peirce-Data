My Dear Professor Peirce:

Your favor of the 4th inst. is at hand & carefully noted.

You are perfectly right in being "sure I could not be offended at your proposal."

The facts are that even Mr. Stewart does not know of [del]not[/del] [ins]our[/ins] "bias" or are at it, but of this thing I am confident, that is that he will respond to us even to liberality.

Count d'A. has shown himself very human & as matters have shaped themselves — through his fight against you — towards a very successful end, he has tried to put the armor on & demanded an interest which he could not get, but in entering him, even more than fairly, Mr. S.'s reply was ever protecting "factions here" as part of the cable game. Then he told me that, as he had had all his plans made in accord with the original proposition to d'A. that this would leave him but about $100,000 of the stock for Mr. Y. & myself.

Now yesterday & today there has been very active cabling between the Trust Co in London — thro. their lawyers, Seward Guthrie, Morawetz & Steele, here — & Stewart & Co. Exactly how the deal will culminate no one can say but I don't think it will very materially alter our allotment of stock which is for us the [del]cable[/del] [ins]sole[/ins] $3.333.666 portion. I write in haste to catch train.

Yours faithfully, [u]W. J. Taylor[/u].
