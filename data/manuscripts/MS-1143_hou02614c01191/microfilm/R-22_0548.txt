Abbess is the female superior of a convent.
Abbey is a monastery, but not any monastery.
Abbot is the man who is the head of an abbey,
or holds some other [ins]ecclesiastical[/ins] position which gives him
this title. Since we have no abbots in this country
I cannot define the title more precisely.

[u]To abbreviate[/u] is to shorten, almost always the
way of writing a word or phrase. The result
is called an abbreviation. Thus Esq. is the
common abbreviation for Esquire, Dr. for Doctor,
Hon. for Honorable, Mr. for Master, pronounced
mister, etc. for et cetera meaning "and the rest,"
A.D. for anno domini, that is, in the year of Our
Lord, B.C. for before Christ, i.e. for id est,
meaning "that is", e.g. for exempla gratia, meaning
"for example," and hundreds of others.

[u]To abdicate[/u] means to surrender ones sovereign

Abdomen is a Latin word, [del]employed[/del] [ins]applied[/ins] by medical
[del]men[/del] to mean the human belly, that is to
that part of the trunk below the midriff or
diaphragm. Also the hind segment of an
insect, spider, or crab is so called. The
adjective is abdominal.

[u]To abduct[/u] is Victorian word, replacing the older
abduce. It means to carry away (a person) wrong-
fully. One of the earliest instances of it was Car-
lyle's saying in his French Revolution, [date]A.D. 1837[/date]
"His Majesty has been abducted, or spirited away."
