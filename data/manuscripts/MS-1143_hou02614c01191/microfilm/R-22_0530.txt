[u]A Little Dictionary of Particularly
Good English Words[/u]

[u]aback[/u]. 1. Backwards. The great poet
Coleridge says "to shrink aback"
Carlyle says "Alas, all went aback
from the time [del]a[/del] [ins]of[/ins] Belisles accident."
2. Behind the front. "Keep yourself
in the love of Christ and stand far aback
from the pollutions of the world." So
we say "to hold [del]x[/del] aback," that is to
prevent from going forward.
This word is especially used by
sailors. A ship is "taken aback" when
the sails are blown back against the
masts, which is a dangerous and
sudden accident. So we say a
person is taken aback when he sud-
denly meets with a wholly unanticipated
check to his purpose.
[u]abaft[/u] Behind [del]in[/del] [ins]in[/ins] the hinder part. Only
used in speaking of a ship.
[u]abb[/u]. The woof of any woven think. But
it is so little used except by people
in that business that others hardly
understand what it means.
