The first great division of Science is according to its
fundamental purpose; which is as for the [u]Theoretical[/u]
Sciences, simply and solely knowledge of God's truth; for
the [u]Practical[/u] Sciences, the uses of life. [del]Louis Agassiz[/del]
[del]has called branches[/del] those divisions of the animal
kingdom in which he thought he saw different plans. I will,
somewhat in imitation of him, call that first great division
of Science, according to purpose, a Division into two [u]Branches[/u].

I shall undertake only the classification of the Theoretical
Sciences. [ins]Bacon,[/ins] I shall divide the first Branch into two Subbranches.
[ins]For the present, I shall speak only of the first Sub Branch, the Theoretical

Of the Theoretical Sciences, in respect to their relation
to observation, the [del]sources[/del] [ins]mother[/ins] of all knowledge, we must dis-
tinguish three Classes.* The first is [u]Mathematics[/u], which
does not undertake to ascertain any facts whatsoever, but
merely posits Hypotheses, and traces out their consequences.

The Second Class is [u]Philosophy[/u], which deals with positive
Truth, or fact, but contents itself with such observations
