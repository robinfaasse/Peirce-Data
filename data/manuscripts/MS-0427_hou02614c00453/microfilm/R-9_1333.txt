Although there seems to be no independent instinctive sense of logicality, it is impossible to deny that there are instinctive ways of forming opinions. It is hard to say how far in following those methods we depend upon instinct proper, [del]or[/del] [ins]or[/ins] inherited impulse, and how far upon tradition or even upon habits formed in mature life, — what is called the rule of thumb. I shall not attempt to draw any such distinction. By instinct I shall here mean any way of attaining a belief of which we can give no thoroughly rational account. A little reasoning there may be; but it is based upon some axiom which we adopt uncritically, or at least without being able to perceive that it is rational.
