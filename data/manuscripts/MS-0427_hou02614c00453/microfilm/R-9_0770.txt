Now, then, Dynamics considers bodies only under that
aspect of them under which they are nothing more than
the sums of their parts; and it also considers their motions
under that aspect of them under which they are simply the
results of each moment's contribution. Dynamics, so re-
garding bodies and their motions, searches out whatever re-
gularities there may be in the motions under these aspects.
[del]Recognizing that bodies may be related to one another, especially in position, as to be in certain
laws, and that they accelerate, but not inquiring what those relating laws are.[/del]

Dynamics, then, in so far as it deals with laws of motion,
deals with ideas; and that the motions of bodies conform
to those laws, — not merely historically have done so, but
are doing so, and will do so, — is a result of final causa-
tion. But as for the actions which Dynamics studies, it
