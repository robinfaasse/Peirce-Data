Excluding Crystallography, then, from Nomological
Physics, as is the usual custom, the other sciences of this
stage [del]of[/del] are distinguished mainly by the nature of the first
appearances of the phenomena they study. Accordingly, if we
turn back to any treatise on physics written when this
stage was the most living of the stages, we find that the
classification used to be, Acoustics, Optics, Heat, corre-
sponding to three senses, and then Magnetism and Electricity
(which [del]were[/del] [ins]have always been[/ins] connected [ins]together[/ins] [del]for[/del] since Gilbert's De Magnete of 1600).
No other classification appears in this stage.

Let us now pass to the Quantitatively Nomological
Stage. Here, studies are no longer governed by the first
appearances, but by conceptions; and the division into
molar, molecular, and etherial physics is clear. By molar
physics is not to be understood merely the motions of
masses of sensible size; but the motions of portions
of matter to which we attribute the same properties
