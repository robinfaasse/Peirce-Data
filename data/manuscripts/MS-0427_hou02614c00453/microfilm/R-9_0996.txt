Ancient Egyptian, getting some lessons in the former from my friend E. H. Palmer. I have constantly used Latin and Greek; and have paid some attention to comparative philology, so-called. Of [del]most of[/del] [ins]the commoner of[/ins] the modern European languages, I have a decent [del]knowledge[/del] [ins]acquaintance[/ins], including English. I have [del]not[/del] quite no natural talent in this direction, nor shall a good memory. Most of what I once knew, I have lost all ready hold of. I am [del]not[/del] infrequently at a loss even for [del]an[/del] English or French word, while of the languages I am least ignorant of.

I have always taken the deepest interest in archeology, although my sole attempt at original study is too slight to be mentioned. The methods of the science, have been a matter of careful study with me. I have carefully studied ancient metrology. [del]But my days of thirty years ago in documents, my knowledge is slender.[/del]
