Agglutination, in particular, has less significance than
other structural features because it does not of itself deter-
mine how the sentence shall be built. For what is ag-
glutination? If is commonly defined as the compounding
of words. That is what any language must be driven to
when it finds itself confronted with a great swarm of new
conceptions for which it has made no provision. In that
sense, scientific men are becoming great agglutinists;
as when they talk of the "Indo-European" languages. The
chemists speak of "carbamide" and of "pseudoitaconanilic
acid", better called "gammaanilidopyrotartrolactamic acid".
