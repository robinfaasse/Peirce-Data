[u]Psychical Sciences[/u] do or do not, stand in need of much help
from the [u]Physical Sciences[/u]. Such need and help there
certainly is. It is much more evident than any help re-
ceived by the Physical from the Psychical sciences. Still, it
is, after all, not of a commanding kind, nor at all to
be compared to the influence of mathematics and philo-
sophy upon Physics or even upon Psychics. The histo-
rian could hardly get along without a knowledge of geo-
graphy. If acoustics has hitherto afforded little aid to Linguis-
tics, that undoubtedly is partly to be accounted for by the utter
inadequacy of acoustical instruments. But even when that
fault is remedied, it will be probably be long before the
acoustician will be able to discover what [ins]physical[/ins] [del]physical[/del]
resemblance there is between sounds which habit causes us
to be unable to distinguish or what points of physical difference
there is [del]from[/del] between sounds which we are in the habit of
accurately distinguishing even when they are muttered words.
