$$(x+1) X_1 + 2(x+1) U_1^{-1} - x X_0$$
while reckoned in another way, its census number is
$$X_1 + (x+2) U_1^{-1} + U_1^x$$
Hence
$$U_1^x = x X_1 + x U_1^{-1} - x X_0 = -x U_1^{-1}$$
But there is no reason for supposing that this formula holds
for $x$ [del]=[/del] negative, except that it identically holds for $x = -1$.
If a simple line passes through the outlying point of another
line figure, the census value calculated in one way, is (if we
assume that the outlying point adds one to the chorisy)
$$3 X_1 + 4 U_1^{-1} + U_1^{-2} - X_0$$
while, calculated in another way, it is
$$2 X_1 + 4 U_1^{-1}$$
Hence $U_1^{-2} = X_0$ [del]If then we had $U_1^{-2} = -(-2) U_1^{-1}$ we should

which would not be convenient.[/del]

[del]If[/del] A simple self-returning line of cyclosis 1 and non-singular
may be conceived as consisting of any number, $y$, of simple
terminated lines, joined at $y$ [del]po[/del] extremities. Hence we have
$$X_0 + K_1 = y X_1 + 2 y U_1^{-1} - y X_0 = 0 \text{ or } K_1 = -X_1$$
If joining two ordinary points of a line figure by a simple
line increases the cyclosis by $z$ while it adds two singularities
of the first order, we have
$$z K_1 + 2 U_1^1 = X_1 + 2 U_1^{-1} - 2 X_0$$
whence $z = 1$.

The added line, instead of increasing the cyclosis, may diminish
the chorisy [del]to[/del] by $z$. Then $-z X_1 + 2 U_1^1 = X_1 + 2 U_1^{-1} - 2 X_0$ or $z = 1$.
