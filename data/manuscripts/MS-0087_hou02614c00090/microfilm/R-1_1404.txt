have an instant [u]in[/u] it, this does not mean that there actually
is an instant between its limits but merely that it is logically
possible that there should be such an instant. Any two instants
whatever are the limits of a lapse. In any lapse any multitude of
instants whatsoever may be determined.

Possible states of things which are such that if they are realized
at all they are realized in respect to determinations of time, are
of the following classes.

1[u]st[/u], such a state of things may be [u]momentary[/u], that is, to say, if
it is realized, there is an instant, [u]t'[/u], in respect to which it is realized,
while whatever instants [u]t[/u] and [u]t"[/u] may be either [u]t'[/u] is not between
[u]t[/u] and [u]t"[/u] or else there is an instant in the lapse from whose limits
are [u]t[/u] and [u]t'[/u] in respect to which the state of things is not realized,
and consequently there is an instant in the lapse whose limits are
[u]t'[/u] and [u]t"[/u] in respect to which the state of things is not realized;

2[u]nd[/u], such a state of things may be [u]prolonged[/u], that is to say, it
is realized [del]at[/del] [ins]in respect to[/ins] any instant there may be between two limiting instants,
while whatever instant be taken [del]between[/del] such that one of the
limiting instants is between it and the other limiting instant,
there is an instant in the lapse between the former [ins]limiting[/ins] instant
and the third instant [del]at[/del] [ins]in respect to[/ins] which it is not realized. [del]As to[/del] [ins]In respect to[/ins] the
limiting instants themselves, the prolonged state of things may be
said to be [u]half-realized[/u], that is to say, it may be regarded arbitrarily as
realized or unrealized, without altering the facts.

3[u]rd[/u], such a state of things may be [u]gradual[/u], that is to say, it is
not realized [del]at[/del] [ins]in respect to[/ins] any instant, but is realized in respect to a definite
lapse.
