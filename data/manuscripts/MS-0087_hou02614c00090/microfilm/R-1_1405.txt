Time being thus analyzed, so far as is necessary for our purpose, we [del]can[/del] recur to space. Space, like time, is a [del]A point is an indivis[/del] general respect [ins]to[/ins] whose determinations objects refer. But instead of these objects being states of things, they are subjects of force. A determination of space [ins](collectively considered)[/ins] is called a place.

A complete individual determination of space, or an indivisible place, is called a point. A thing which at any one instant is realized in a single point is called a particle.

A particle may move. [del]The[/del] [ins]A[/ins] motion is a prolonged state of things [del]at the beg[/del] occupying a lapse at the commencement of which a thing occupies one place and at the completion of which it occupies a place, and at any two instants between the limits of the lapse [del]there will[/del] the place the object occupies will be different, with the possible exception of [del]a[/del] finite multitudes of pairs of instants. Moreover, any displacement, or alteration of place, which takes place [del]in one[/del] by a motion in one lapse might take place by a motion in any other lapse, and if in one of these motions the instant at which the thing is in one place is between the instants at which it is at two other [ins]given[/ins] places, it will be the same in the other motion.
