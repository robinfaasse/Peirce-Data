Any thing at any instant occupies a place, and of course,
one place only; and no place can at any one instant be
occupied by two different things. At different instants the
same thing can occupy different places; but it can only do
so under certain restrictions which I shall develope. Such
change of place under [del]these[/del] [ins]the[/ins] restrictions is called [u]motion[/u], if
it is a state of change, and [u]movement[/u] if it is an entire change.
Under still further conditions it is called [u]mere* motion[/u]; but if these
conditions are violated, it is called [u]disruptive motion[/u].

An individual determination of space is called a [u]point[/u]. A
thing which [ins]at an instant[/ins] occupies a point is called a [u]particle[/u]. In a place
occupied by a thing each point is occupied by a [del]particle[/del] [ins]particle[/ins], and
such particle is called a particle [u]of[/u], or [u]belonging to[/u], or a [u]part of[/u],
the thing occupying the place in which the point lies.
