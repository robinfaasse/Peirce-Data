The [u]apeiry[/u], or solid separateness, is [u]zero[/u] only for a place in which there is no room for a body geometrically incapable of shrinking to a species by a mere motion within the place. It is [u]one[/u] for a place in which there is room for such a body but in which such a body can come by mere movement to occupy [del]every[/del] [ins]any[/ins] place which might be occupied by such a body. In general, the value of the apeiry is the least number of things dimensionally less by three than that of the place whose apeiry is in question, each of these being such that a particle could not move [del]from every[/del] in their neighborhood and in the place in question from every point in that neighborhood to [del]any[/del] [ins]every[/ins] point in that neighborhood, but the place of the thing being of the simplest form compatible with the fulfillment of this condition; these things leaving the unoccupied remainder of the place of zero apeiry.
