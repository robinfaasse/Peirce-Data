Each [u]fornix[/u] adds two to the cyclosis. For consider a ball with a fornix, making it topically of the same shape as a torus. It is plain that a stress round the bar of the bridge or round the tube of the tunnel could not shrink in the surface to nothing, nor could one such stress prevent others parallel to it. But if one were placed [ins]round the bridge[/ins] [del]and then the other[/del] and then one round the tunnel, with the necessary interruption where it meets the other, [del]the[/del] there would be room for no other unshrinkable stress in the unoccupied part of the surface. It is plain that the effect would be the same upon any other non-singular surface. An annular surface, as on one side of a piece of paper with a hole in it would have cyclosis one, since one stress across its bar would [del]for[/del] leave no room for [del]another[/del] an unshrinkable stress. Of course, the topical geometry of solids is sadly shorn of its characters by limitation to three dimensions.
