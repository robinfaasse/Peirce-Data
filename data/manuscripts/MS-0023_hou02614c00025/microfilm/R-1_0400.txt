[u]3rd. To prove the distributive principle, first clause.[/u]
The distributive principle consists of two propositions:
[u]1st.[/u] [u](x+y)z=xz+yz[/u]
[u]2nd.[/u] [u]x(y+z)=xy+xz.[/u]
We now undertake to prove the first of these. First, it
is true for x=1. For
(1+y)z
=z+yz by the definition of multiplication;
=1.z+yz by the definition of multiplication.
Second, if true for x=n it is true for x=1+n; that is, if
(n+y)z=nz+yz, then ((1+n)+y)z=(1+n)z+yz. For
((1+n)+y)z
=(1+(n+y))z by the definition of addition;
=z+(n+y)z by the definition of multiplication;
=z+(nz+yz) by hypothesis;
=(z+nz)+yz by the associative principle of addition;
=(1+n)z+yz by the definition of multiplication.

[u]4. To prove the second proposition of the distribu-[/u]
[u]tive principle, that[/u]
[u]x(y+z)=xy+xz.[/u]
First, it is true for x=1; for
1(y+z)
=y+z by the definition of multiplication;
=1y+1z by the definition of multiplication.
