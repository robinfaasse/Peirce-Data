[u]Limited Discrete Simple Quantity.[/u]

Let such a relative term [del]c[/del] [ins]e[/ins], that whatever is a e of anything is the only e of that thing and is a e of that thing only, be called a [u]relative of simple correspond-[/u]
[u]ence.[/u]

If every object, s, of a class is in any such relation c, with a number of a semilimited discrete single system, and if further every number smaller than a number c'd by an s is itself c'd by an s, then the numbers c'd by the s's are said to count them, and the system of correspondence is called a count. If in any count there is a maximum count-ing number, the count is said to be finite, and that number is called the number of the count.

The relative "identical with" satisfies the definition of a relative of simple correspondence, and the definition of acount is satisfied by putting "identical with" for c, and "as small as [del]y[/del] [ins]x[/ins]" for s. In this mode of counting the number of numbers as small as x is x.

Suppose that in any count the number of numbers as small as the minimum number, one, is found to be n. Then by the definition of a count, every number as small as n counts a number as small as one. But, by the definition of one there is only one number as small as one. Hence, by the definition of single correspondence, no other number than one counts one. Hence, by the definition of one, no other number than one counts any number as small as one. Hence, by the definition of the count, one is, in every count, the number of numbers as small as one.

If the number of numbers as small as x is in some count y, then the number of numbers as small as y is in some count x. For if the definition of a count is satisfied by the relative c, it is equally satisfied by the relative c'd by.

Hence, since the number of numbers as small as one cannot in any count be [del]as[/del] greater than one, it follows that the number of numbers as small as any number greater than one cannot in any count be one.

Suppose that there is [ins]a[/ins] count in which the number of numbers as small as I [ins]m[/ins] is found to be I+n, since we have just seen that it cannot be I. In this count, let m' be the number which is c of [del]m[/del] [ins]I+n[/ins], and let n' be the number which is c'd by I+m. Let us now consider a relative, e, which differs from c only in excluding the relation of m' to I+n as well as the relation of I+m to n' and in including the relation of m' to n'. Then e will be a relative of single correspondence, for c is so [ins]and no exclusion of relations from a single correspondence affects this character,[/ins] while the inclusion of the relation of m' to n' leaves m' the only e of n' and an e of n' only.
