To show that these two things are analogous, write x as the sign of multiplication and let the inverted [del]y[/del] dagger † be so used that
[u]u[/u] † [u]v[/u]
shall mean [u]u[/u] + [u]v[/u] + [u]u[/u][u]v[/u].
Then the two formulae in question are
([u]x[/u] † [u]y[/u]) x [u]z[/u] ≡ ([u]x[/u] x [u]z[/u]) † ([u]y[/u] x [u]z[/u])
([u]x[/u] x [u]y[/u]) † [u]z[/u] ≡ ([u]x[/u] † [u]z[/u]) x ([u]y[/u] † [u]z[/u]).

In short, this dagger-sign, which may be read "or", stands upon an equal footing with the sign of multiplication. While the sign of addition is of little use, except to add 1, that is to pass from one of the two values to the other. Now, it's very artificial to look upon such a reversal as addition. The truth is that genuine addition implies a series, and cannot exist where there are only two values. It will, therefore, be better, instead of writing [u]a[/u]+1 to simply draw a line [del]of[/del] over the [u]a[/u], thus: [u]a[/u], and read it "other than [u]a[/u]" [ins]or "not [u]a[/u]"[/ins], because it has the value [u]a[/u] has not.
