[u]Theory of Luminosity[/u]

Fechners law would
lead us to suppose that
the equation of a surface
of constant luminosity
would be
$xyz = C$
or $\log x + \log y + \log z = \log C$
a sort of hyperbola of
the third order. In that case
the mixture of any two colors
of the same luminosity should
have a [del]higher[/del] [ins]higher[/ins] luminosity.

Let $x+dx$ $y+dy$ $z+dz$ be values
for one color
$x-dx$ $y-dy$ $z-dz$ be values
for another color. Their mixture
in equal parts is $x$, $y$, $z$.

Lumin of $1^{\text{st}}$
$xyz + dx yz + dy xz + dz.xy + dx dy z + \text{etc}$
Lumin of $2^{\text{nd}}$
$xyz - dx yz - dy xz - dz xy + dx dy z + \text{etc}$
if these are equal $dx dy z + \text{etc} = 0$
and lum of these exceeds that
of mixture, if $dx, dy, dz$ have all
same sign but this cannot be.
