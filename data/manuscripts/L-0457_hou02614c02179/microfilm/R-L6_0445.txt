To Charles Sanders Peirce,
Milford, Pike Co, Pennsylvania.

My dear Doctor Peirce

An Englishwoman who
has written several books under the
nom de plume of "Valerana," begs
the sincere, great, and affectionate
honour of thanking you for your
profound, timely, and vastly important
article in the Hibbert Journal on the
"Reality of God". I have read it over
twice very, very carefully. It has arous-
-ed my philosophical and psycholo-
-gical hopes. In fact I see in
the article — or think I do —

[pn]4[/pn]
"Transcendental Accusation"
to account for the facts. In
my attempt [del]to[/del] [ins]of[/ins] account for
some of the "miracles" of Jesus,
I have assumed that his relig-
-ious sensibility and that of
his patients was — dynam-
-ical, — due to an actual
impact on his sensory nerves
of some Transcendental Sub-
-stance, intelligently directed,
in its operations, by his own
Soul? Is it any use for me
to spend three months of my
time this winter in an attempt
to prove the "Reality of God"
along this broader scope of
the laws of Transcendental
Accusation? Your views, more
