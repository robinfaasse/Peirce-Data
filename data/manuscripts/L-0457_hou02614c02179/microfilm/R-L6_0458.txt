New York, Philadelphia & Reading R.R.
Langhorne, Bucks Co, Pa.
[date]March 4, 1914[/date]

Mr + Mrs Charles Sanders Peirce
Milford Pike Co, Pa.

My dear Friends: —

As in your last letter you said Mrs Peirce wished to make my acquaintance therefore I address this letter to you both. To understand a correspondent one has to understand his "emotional" nature as per the "Wisdom of Passion"; hence I first beg your pardon for "explanationing" myself as being a man of the most extraordinary emotional nature, [ins]ten women's souls in one man's body,[/ins] this hereditary fault I have never, - never - been able to overcome and at middle life am beyond redemption. ; Bless your dear dear hearts Mr Peirce I have often often thought of you in the most affectionate sort of way. Well, as to my [del]friend[/del] Gifford Pinchot. I hope to do some stumping for him for State Senator, and, if I lived in Milford Pa, and knew Gifford's mother [ins]heart throbbing[/ins] I should certainly ask [del]to[/del] [ins]her[/ins] to write Gifford a line [ins]through a Progressive[/ins] in my behalf. We got our Progressives to fuse with the Democrats in Bucks County last fall. Well, it was sweetly kind - sweetly kind of you to say in your last letter that you have been led to attach much more value to the line of thought in your "Wisdom of Passion" than I had done before! Now weigh well what a pathologically disposed man of extraordinary emotional susceptibility has to reply. Of course - of course - all this sort of language as coming from a woman would be all right, but coming from me it of course is positively, unpardonably pathological. Nevertheless - if I am able to rationally understand the nature of my extraordinary emotional soul - I beg to say, that if Charles Sanders Peirce had said that and if he had been in my house, or I had been in his, I should have followed the bent of my extraordinary emotional nature and fell on his neck [del]and smother-[/del] [del]-ered[/del] [ins]and covered[/ins] his face with kisses of affection. And it would have done you no harm to prove that I loved you.
