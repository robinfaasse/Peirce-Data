"Dear Peirce:"
I have been reading over once more your article in the Hibbert Journal, Cousin's phil-
-osophy of "Spontaneous Reason" complements your "Introduction to Metaphysics"
-used in this article on "Metaphysics" in the Ency. Britt. Of course to myself who have been in the habit of defining Reason as the effect of many experiences and concepts, the "a priori" definitions and "instinctive reason" of the hyphened terms is, of itself, contradictory. And the old problem remains as to how we are to reconcile the organic unity of Reason and Will with the "spontaneous conjectures of Cousin and your own "instinctive reason."
Only in its higher, evolved stage — in my opinion — can Reason be defined as spontaneous and instinctive. Of course it is comparatively easy to come to some conclusion, if we can set up some — by accepted law, such definitions of such terms as "Reason," "limit identities" with the syllogistic process, and the "Muse-
-remain," whether any other form of "veri-
-ment" than that which is born of veri-
-fied deviations, percepts, and concepts, could prove an unerring guide to our "Movements" of Retrodiction?
Then again, if your word "Move" has any meaning in relation to "thought" and "affinity," according to any view of "rationalising" feeling per se, and odd as it may sound (defining sentiment as a feeling formed by a judgment) what words but the two words "ed-
-ucated-sentiment" can explain to us this [del]psychic[/del] rationalising of our [del]go[/del] per se, considered distinct from the objective — more objective — problem of the relationship of our thought, concepts, beliefs, and reasons.
Of course, subjectively in the Soul's Experience, both Reason and Feel-
-ing are constantly transforming each other in their relation to the objects of experience. Anyway, Truth, like Sunlight, is a pluralistic fact, for its identities are plural, and I am glad to know that the "pragmatic" is "pluralistic logic" — in its subjective psychological series — may supersede the physicalistic, static-logic of merely material identities. If we follow [del]the[/del] [ins]your[/ins] method of inverse proportion, I believe we shall find that the most "vocal identities" are plural, and that each element of such identities may be "the other" of the "others," as with colors in the formation of white sunlight.

Kindly & Truly Yours
R. Sylvester Clifton of Penn'a
