[del]East and West[/del] [ins]E and N S[/ins] scales to be equal

I begin by making the simplest [del]projection[/del] [ins]representation[/ins] that will just cover [del]an[/del] [ins]the[/ins] unlimited plane

This will be one in which all the circles of constant latitude are represented by concentric circles

Then let $\rho$ and $\theta$ be the polar coördinates of any point on the map

The E W scale will be $\frac{\rho}{r}$

The N S scale will be $\frac{d\rho}{\sqrt{(dr)^2 + (dz)^2}}$

To make the map conformal, then, it will suffice to put

$$ \frac{\rho}{r} = \frac{d\rho}{\sqrt{(dr)^2 + (dz)^2}} $$

multiplying by [del]$\frac{\sqrt{(dr)^2 + (dz)^2}}{\rho}$[/del] we get $\frac{d\rho}{\rho} = \frac{[del]\sqrt{(dr)^2 + (dz)^2}[/del] \sqrt{1 + (dz/dr)^2} \cdot dr}{r}$ and [del]taking[/del] [ins]separating[/ins] $dr$ as a factor

Differentiating the equation of the earth's surface

$$ \frac{r dr}{R^2} + \frac{z dz}{Z^2} = 0 $$

so that $\frac{dz}{dr} = - \frac{Z^2}{R^2} \frac{r}{z}$

$$ \left(\frac{dz}{dr}\right)^2 = \frac{Z^4}{R^4} \frac{r^2}{z^2} = \frac{Z^4}{R^2} \frac{1 - (z/Z)^2}{z^2} = \frac{Z^2}{R^4} \frac{r^2}{1 - (r/R)^2} $$

$$ = \frac{Z^2}{R^2} \frac{r^2}{R^2 - r^2} $$

[marginalia]
$x = \frac{r}{R}$
$dr = R dx$
[/marginalia]

$$ 1 + \left(\frac{dz}{dr}\right)^2 = \frac{R^4 - (R^2 - Z^2) r^2}{R^4 - R^2 r^2} $$

$$ = 1 $$
