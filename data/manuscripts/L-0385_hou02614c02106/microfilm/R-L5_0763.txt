[pn]5[/pn] [ins]5th[/ins] Some friends of mine have subscribed
some money to enable me to get started on
my book on Logic by devoting some months
exclusively to it. I am now sending to J.M.B.
typewritten copies of my first chapter and also
of a long statement which I made as the
basis of some of the methodistic chapters,
fearing I might die without having recorded
ideas I think useful to science generally.

I should feel myself greatly obliged to you
if you could find time to look over them, in
order to feel that you knew what I was driving
at, even if it did not lead, as it might, to some
pertinent objections of value to me. In hopes
you may get time to look at them, I will just
say here what you may find in each of
possible interest.

Chapter I has two parts. Part I very briefly
states my reasons for disapproving (or approving)
13 different foundations upon which different
writers wholly or partially found the science of
Logic.

Part II is a prospectus of the contents of
the book and enters considerably into some
points that could not otherwise be made
to convey much idea.

The other paper is called The Logic of
Ancient History. It begins by considering
the merits & demerits of the method of
balancing probabilities, [del]as[/del] [ins]e.g.[/ins] by Hume.
