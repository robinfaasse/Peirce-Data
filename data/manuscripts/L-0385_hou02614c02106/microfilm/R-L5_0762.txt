[del]W[/del] When I gave my lectures in Cambridge,
I was wishing to advance certain ideas that
were then in embryo, and upon which I have
been since much engaged. In the development
of these I have undoubtedly been influenced
by talks in Cambridge, and by your book
and reflections upon it, and by James's views.
I now see more clearly the fundamental de-
pendence of logic upon ethics, and also
the indispensibility of the active element
in thought. To be sure there was some-
thing of this way back in my articles for
the Jour. of Spec. Phil. If I were a psycho-
logical logician, it might not mean much
to say that I found logic on ethics. But con-
sidering that my logic is quite divorced
from psychology or how we think consi-
dered as an operation of the soul, I think
you will see that my thoughts in this
direction are likely to interest you.
