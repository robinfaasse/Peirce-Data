I should think myself much obliged
if you could find time to look over
these. I will now state briefly what their
contents are.

Chapter I has two parts. The first men-
tions 13 different views which are taken
of the foundations of logic & very briefly
states my objections to these I do object to.

The second part is a sort of table of
contents of the whole book, explaining
some points almost in full, and hardly
noticing others of a more special kind, &
giving an outline of the general design.

The other paper is called "On the Logic
of Ancient History." It attacks the method of
balancing probabilities, as a general method;
and shows how I would proceed.

For this purpose, the various kinds of
