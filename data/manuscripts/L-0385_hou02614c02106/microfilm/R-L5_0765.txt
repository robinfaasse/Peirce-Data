get done at all. To finish all my work
on which considerable progress has been
made is perhaps more than I can now
do under the most favorable circum-
stances. But there is no doubt that if
speedy aid comes, the Logic, at any rate,
may be completed, — unless I meet
with some accident of which there is
no probability. Should aid not come, I
am already principal in a way such as
do people have no conception of — cannot
possibly imagine, — & I shall cannot for-
ever endure the temptation to take a
step which would prevent my coming back
to this work. Were my mind not kept in
constant training my powers both of
thought & of work would soon begin to
deteriorate. There are 23 chapters to
my Logic. They are very long and re-
