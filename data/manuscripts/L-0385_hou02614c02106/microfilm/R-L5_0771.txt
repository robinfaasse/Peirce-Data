My dear Professor Royce:

I have several very particular things to say to you.

1[sup]st[/sup], Please know that I never received from your publisher Vol I of your [u]World & Individual[/u]. Otherwise, I should have written to thank you. And now I am going to send you Vol II to have you write my name in it; and it can come back with some books I am begging the loan of from the library.

2[sup]nd[/sup], I have been much touched by your kind references to me, especially considering how seldom one knows, for the most part, where one gets hints. Witness the conclusion of the Sophistical Elenchi, which no doubt Aristotle believed fully represented the truth, yet it is very difficult to believe it really did.

3[sup]rd[/sup], I transmitted to Baldwin your request about the extra copies of my article on Symbolic Logic, and reinforced it by a personal request on my own part, although the latter was hard to bring myself to make. They may accede to your request. If so, I should be much obliged for one copy for myself, if you would kindly send it.

I gave up a dictionary of my own to help Baldwin with his Second Volume, under a promise that I should have entire charge of the logic and receive the magnificent sum of $150 for four or five months of solid work, out of which I had to pay for books & the freight expressage on borrowed books, paper, ink, pens, & postage, reducing the sum to about half.
