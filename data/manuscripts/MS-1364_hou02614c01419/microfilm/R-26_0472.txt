The ROTATIONAL DISPLACEMENTS of
crystallography may conveniently be symbolized
by a notation of Leibniz independently invented and improved
by me in my memoir of 1870, and applied to the expression
of dyadic relatives in their relation to their elements, or sin-
gular relatives. Capitals being used to denote single objects
$(A:B)$ is such a relative that $(A:B)B = A$ and $(A:B)C = 0$ unless
$C = B$; and $(A:B)(B:C) = A:C$ but $(A:B)(C:D) = 0$ unless $C = B$. Such
relatives as $A:B$ can be added as well as non-commutatively
multiplied.

I shall further employ the letter $A$ to represent a rotational
displacement about an axis not distinctly denoted through $180^\circ$
in a sense not specified. Exponents will be attached to $A$ to mul-
tiply the angle of displacement; as in $A^{\frac{2}{5}}$ which denotes a rotational
[del]about the axis of $A$[/del] through $\frac{2}{5} 180^\circ = 36^\circ$. An obelus or horizontal line
above any symbol representing anything $s$, to make it repre-
sent $1-s$. Thus $A^{\overline{x}} = A^{\frac{2}{5}}$. I will, in the first example, take $0, 1,$
$2, 3, 4$ or $0, 1, 2, \overline{2}, \overline{1}$, to denote the five rays of a pentaster,
to whose plane, and at the centre of the pentaster, the axis of $A$ is
normal. [del]Subjacent[/del] Symbols of lines subjacent to $A$ show other
axes of rotational displacement than that of $A$. Thus, whether
the [del]axes[/del] rays denoted by $0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ be arranged [ins]$X_1$ or $X_1^{-1}$[/ins], we
shall have $A_0^2 = \sum_a (a+1):a = 1:0 + 2:1 + 3:2 + 4:3 + 0:4$
and $A_0 = \sum_n (0+n):(0+n) = 0:0 + 4:1 + 3:2 + 2:3 + 1:4 [del]- \sum_n (0+n)[/del]$
$A_1 = \sum_n (1-n):(1+n) = 2:0 + 1:1 + 0:2 + 4:3 + 3:4$
$A_2 = \sum_n (2-n):(2+n) = 4:0 + 3:1 + 2:2 + 1:3 + 0:4$
$A_3 = \sum_n (3-n):(3+n) = 1:0 + 0:1 + 4:2 + 3:3 + 2:4$
$A_4 = \sum_n (4-n):(4+n) = 3:0 + 2:1 + 1:2 + 0:3 + 4:4$

$A^2 = 0:0 + 1:1 + 2:2 + 3:3 + 4:4 = 1$
$A_m A_n = A_{m-2} A_n = 1:0 + 2:1 + 3:2 + 4:3 + 0:4 = i = \sum_n (n+1):n$
$A_m A_n = A_{m-4} A_n = 2:0 + 3:1 + 4:2 + 0:3 + 1:4 = i^2 = \sum_n (n+2):n$
$A_m A_n = A_{m+4} A_n = 3:0 + 4:1 + 0:2 + 1:3 + 2:4 = i^3 = \sum_n (n+3):n$
$A_{m+2} A_n = A_{m-3} A_n = 4:0 + 0:1 + 1:2 + 2:3 + 3:4 = i^4 = \sum_n (n+4):n$

$A_m A_n = i^{2(m-n)} = i^{3(n-m)}$
$1:A_r = A_r^{-1} = A_r$
$A_n A_m = A_m A_n^{-1}$
$i A_q = A_{q+3} = A_{q-2}$
$A_p i = A_{p+2} = A_{p-3}$
