The Rotational Displacements of
crystallography may with great advantage be discus-
sed in the notation [del]I[/del] published in [date]1870[/date] for the [del]dis-[/del]
treatment of relations in general. A non-commutative
multiplication is used. The capital letters representing
individual objects, $A:B$ denotes the relation of $A$ to $B$,
[del]the[/del] and no other relation whatever. Then $(A:B)B = A$
[del]and[/del] $(A:B)(B:C) = A:C$ [del]but[/del] $(A:B)X = 0$ unless $X = B$ or con-
tains $B$. [del]Thus[/del] $(A:B)(B+C) = A$.

I shall further use the letter $A$ to denote a rotational dis-
placement round an axis no distinctly represented and through
$180^\circ$, clockwise to an eye looking in a way not specified. To this
[del]letter $A$ exponents[/del] will be attached to multiply the angle of dis-
placement. Thus, $A^{\frac{1}{5}}$ will be a displacement about the same
axis through $\frac{1}{5}(180^\circ) = 36^\circ$. [del]An[/del] An obelus or horizontal line
over any symbol, say $S$, to make it denote $1-S$. Thus $\bar{A^{\frac{1}{5}}}$ will
denote a rotation in the counterclockwise sense of $A^{\frac{1}{5}}$. I shall denote by
$0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ the five rays of a pentaster to which the axis of $A$ is
perpendicular, these being numbered phyllotactically, the phyllotactic
ordinals of a pentaster being double the ordinals proceeding from each
ray to the next. These numbers attached to $A$ below, as $A_1$, $A_2$, etc.
[del]shall[/del] express rotational displacements round these axes. [fig]pentaster diagram[/fig]

$A_0 = 0:0 + 4:1 + 3:2 + 2:3 + 1:4$
$A_1 = 2:0 + 1:1 + 0:2 + 4:3 + 3:4$
$A_2 = 4:0 + 3:1 + 2:2 + 1:3 + 0:4$
$A_3 = 1:0 + 0:1 + 4:2 + 3:3 + 2:4$
$A_4 = 3:0 + 2:1 + 1:2 + 0:3 + 4:4$

$A_0^2 = A_1^2 = A_2^2 = A_3^2 = A_4^2 = A^0 = 1$
$A_0 A_1 = 3:0 + 4:1 + 0:2 + 1:3 + 2:4 = A_1 A_2 = A_2 A_3 = A_3 A_4 = A_4 A_0$
$A_0 A_2 = 2:0 + 3:1 + 4:2 + 0:3 + 1:4 = A_2 A_4 = A_4 A_1 = A_1 A_3 = A_3 A_0$
$A_0 A_3 = 1:0 + 2:1 + 3:2 + 4:3 + 0:4 = A_1 A_4 = A_2 A_0 = A_3 A_1 = A_4 A_2$
$A_0 A_4 = 4:0 + 0:1 + 1:2 + 2:3 + 3:4 = A_1 A_0 = A_2 A_1 = A_3 A_2 = A_4 A_3$

Calling $A_n A_{n+2} = i$, we have $i^2 = A_n A_{n-1}$, $i^3 = A_n A_{n+1}$, $i^4 = A_n A_{n-2}$
$i^5 = 1$
