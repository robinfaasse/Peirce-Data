**HENRY B. KINGHORN,**
COUNSELOR AT LAW,
189 BROADWAY,

New York, [date]January 15th, 1894.[/date]

Mr. C. S. Peirce, "Arisbe," Milford, Penn.

Dear Sir; I have received your circular announcing the publication of some XII Vols. on kindred subjects at $2.50 each.

I should be willing to accept these as a return for the $30.00 I paid you some years ago and for which i never had any equivalent. You advertised to give instruction in the "Art of Reasoning" for which I paid you $30.00. You sent me a few conundrums which I have since asked a lot of College Men to solve and have not so far [ins]found[/ins] one who could do anything with them. They were of no practical use to me, nor to any one else for that matter, except, that you got the money from me. I have no criticism to offer; but I do consider that it [del]it[/del] is the duty of every man, and specially of one who offers to instruct, to see that he meets the mind of his pupil and gives him something as an equivalent for his money. I do not believe you did this in my case.

Yours &c,

[fig]signature of Henry B. Kinghorn[/fig]
