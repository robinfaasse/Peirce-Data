LITERATURE.
NOTES.

A WORK in twelve volumes, each distinct, as contemplated (and partly executed) by Mr. Charles S. Peirce, member of the National Academy of Sciences. Its full title reads: 'The Principles of Philosophy; or, Logic, Physics, and Psychics, considered as a Unity, in the light of the Nineteenth Century.' The first volume, which is ready for the press, will be 'A Review of the Leading Ideas of the Nineteenth Century.' Mr. Peirce also issues a prospectus of a limited edition, now in course of printing, in two colors on hand-made paper, at the De Vinne Press, of 'The Epistle of Pierre Pelerin de Maricourt to Sygur de Foucaucourt, Soldier, On the Lodestone.' The original treatise dates from 1269, and "occupies a unique position in the history of the human mind, being without exception the earliest work of experimental science that has come down to us." The transcript of Peter Peregrinus's text has been made afresh from a contemporary MS. in the Paris Library, and is reproduced in black-letter together with a translation and notes. Subscriptions for either work should be addressed to Mr. Peirce at Milford, Pa.
