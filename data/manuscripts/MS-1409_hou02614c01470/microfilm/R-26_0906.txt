This writer undertakes to prove that the Hittites were an "Altaic",
or Turco-Tartar, people, closely allied to the Accadian Chaldees, — a
proposition which will find ready acceptance. He further professes
to [del]read[/del] [ins]decipher[/ins] the Hittite syllabary of over 150 "emblems" by comparing
them with [del]those[/del] of the "Linear Babylonian" script upon the one hand, and
with the [ins]characters of the[/ins] Cypriote syllabary and the Lydian and Carian alphabets on the
other; and though he has no doubt committed some mistakes (as where
for No. 133, he takes a manifest hare with stumpy tail for an animal
which he calls a monster, but which is [del]unquestion[/del] demonstrably an ass),
yet for the majority of the "emblems", and those the most important, he
cannot well be wrong. Nor does he stop [del]here[/del] at this, but goes on
to translate all the texts, thirty-seven inscriptions on slabs and rocks,
and thirty-two on seals. Nothing like this has been attempted hitherto, and
it is an important contribution to historical science, — especially
since, if the translations are correct, it would appear that the kings
of the second Babylonian dynasty, from [date]1950 to 1600 B.C.[/date], were Hittites.
Throughout that period, the Hyksos kings of Egypt, who were either
Hittites or some closely related folk, were in their glory. That
people seem to have been in their prime at that time.
