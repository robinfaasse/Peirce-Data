In point of fact, however, Col. Conder does not make nearly so
good a showing as de Rougé. Only two or three letters are quite
satisfactorily accounted for, and four or five more tolerably. The
rest either utterly fail to represent, either the name, the shape, or
the sound. How utterly can only be conceived by looking at the
table. For the present, therefore, this attempt of Col. Conder rather
strengthens the theory of de Rougé. It will be observed that this theory,
[ins]also too,[/ins] is open to the objection that since only half the letters can be
explained, even in Col. Conder's fashion, by the Cypriote syllabary,
but resort has to be had to the very archaic Hittite emblems, we
must repeat the inquiry, where did this writing hide itself from
during half a dozen centuries? Moreover, there is a much more
weighty difficulty in supposing a merely consonantal alphabet
to have been derived from a syllabary which not only scrupulously
distinguished the vowels but even had five characters for A, E, I, O, U.
