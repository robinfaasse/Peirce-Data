Col. Conder is a bold theorizer. There is no harm in this, so
long as he theorizes about the Hittites with a complete mastery of
all the known facts. On the contrary, conjecture in such an
inquiry is, in the earlier stages of it, indispensible to progress.
Even when he carries this tendency, in heightened degree, into Egyptology,
where he is by no means so thoroughly at home, no great harm
will be done by his theories, unless it be to his own reputation, since
no well-informed person will linger long to consider them. The author
apparently imagines that Egyptian chronology still remains in the
unsettled condition of fifteen years ago; for he proposes to revive
the exploded idea that all the dynasties [del]of the old and mid[/del] before the
Hyksos are to be doubled up into two contemporaneous series, the one,
of kings of Upper, the other of Lower Egypt. In order to make both series
reach an end at about the same time, he has to treat the kings of
the V[sup]th[/sup] dynasty as belonging to Upper Egypt, although their
