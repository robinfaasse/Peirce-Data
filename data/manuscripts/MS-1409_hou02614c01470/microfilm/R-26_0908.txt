associated with their fathers on the throne without the chroniclers

on the only pertinent historical tablet, where the records of
some eighteen reigns are broken away. It is quite possible,
therefore, that the death of Burnaburias has been put twenty-
five or even fifty years too early. Col. Conder does not remark
the still worse discrepancy in the circumstance that the
Babylonian king Calimmasin, who, according to him, reigned
from 1490 to 1480 B.C. was the brother-in-law and correspondent
of Amenhotep [u]III[/u], who, according to Petrie, reigned from 1414 to 1379 B.C.
