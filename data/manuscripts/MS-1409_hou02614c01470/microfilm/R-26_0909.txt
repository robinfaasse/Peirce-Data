Scientific Egyptian chronology began in 1884 with von Oppolzer's
determination of the beginnings of the Sothic periods in 4236, 2776,
and 1318 B.C., and A.D. 139. Dr. Mahler, a fellow-townsman of von
Oppolzer in Vienna, subsequently using the records of the heliacal
risings of Sirius, i.e. its rising an hour before the Sun, in order to
furnish approximate dates, made them precise by means of records
of new moons, which, of course, are not repeated on the same days of
the year for a long time. The luni-solar tables of von Oppolzer
rendered this easy. He thus found that Tahutmes III reigned from
1503 to 1449 B.C., — that is, till long past Conder's date for the
death of Calimmasin, although after Tahutmes III, certainly came
Amenhotep II and Tahutmes IV, reigning 26 and 9 years respectively,
before the accession of that Babylonian king's correspondent, Amen-
hotep III. Col. Conder seeks to support his rejection of Mahler's
date by the authority of Brugsch's 'History,' a work written before
Mahler's discovery, although Brugsch in his last book, 'Aegyptologie,'
1891, unreservedly accepted not only the date of Mahler for
Tahutmes III but also a less certain one for Ramessu II. Later
after Mahler by the same method had determined a third date, Eisenlohr
