CALCULUS OF FINITE DIFFERENCES.

[u]Art 1.[/u] The calculus of finite differences is an algebraical method
for the study of the relations of [del]num[/del] [ins]a series of[/ins] numbers denoted by
the same letter with different whole numbers as subscript indices.

No arithmetical law necessarily connects the value of the index
with the value of the number. Thus, [ins]$u_1, u_2, u_3,$ etc. might denote[/ins]
the number of persons living in No 1, No 2, No 3 etc of a certain
street.

At the same time, unless there were some general relation
[del]between the, and[/del] [ins]two sets of numbers, the calculus of finite differences would
be of no use. It suffices, however, that there should be a general relation
sully related to[/ins] [del]any[/del] [ins]intelligent law[/ins], etc without either $u$ or $v$ being gene-
rally related to [ins]1, 2, 3, etc.[/ins]

[u]Art. 2.[/u] The [del]greatest[/del] [ins]ordinary[/ins] tool of this calculus is the symbol, E, of the
operation of increasing the index by 1. If there can be any doubt
as to which index is referred to, it is subjoined to the E. Thus
$E u_x = u_{x+1}$
$E_x u_x v_y = u_{x+1} v_y$

The effect of the E is supposed to extend to the end of the term. Thus,
$E u_x v_x = u_{x+1} v_{x+1}$

If this is not intended, one or more lines should be drawn under some
of the indices and the E should receive a similar index, [del]thus[/del]:
$_x E_x u_x E_y v_y w_z = u_{x+1} v_{y+2} w_{z+1}$

With this understanding the character E can be treated, if it denoted
a quantity, according to the ordinary rules of algebra except so far
as these are modified by the circumstance that $E u_x$ is not equal to $u_x E$.
