In framing definitions no guide is so treacherous
as etymology. Science is usually defined as systematic
knowledge. But if we are to put together what belongs
together, we must not insist upon knowledge as
essential to science. "Systematic knowledge" better
describes a positive philosophy. It is not knowledge
but research (which implies a defect of knowledge)
that is the vital element of science; so that the
derivations of the words would be better fitted to
their meanings if science had received [del]the[/del] [ins]the[/ins] name
of philosophy, and vice versa. Nobody will contend
that a history of science could ignore the Ptolemaic
system and the corpuscular theory of light because
they [del]are[/del] [ins]were[/ins] not true knowledge.

But not all investigations are scientific. A
practising physician whose only object is to cure one
patient after another, pursues an art, not a science.
[del]No doubt, there is a science which searches out the
theory of any art;[/del] and the inquiry for it is science.
