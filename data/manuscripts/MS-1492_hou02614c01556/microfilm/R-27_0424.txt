The doctrine of the phase rule decidedly resembles topical geometry in its modes of ratiocination. It deals with [del]a space filled with[/del] [ins]a num-[/ins]ber of substances, each in contact with every one of the others. [ins](The whole is called the system)[/ins] The parts of the space [del]which[/del] [ins]up to[/ins] are separated from one another by distinct bounding surfaces [del]as the two sides or each of which the matter is either chemically or physically markedly different are called phases.[/del] The chemical constituents which just suffice to distinguish the different phases [del]by[/del] [ins]each[/ins] being present in some phases and absent from others are called components.
