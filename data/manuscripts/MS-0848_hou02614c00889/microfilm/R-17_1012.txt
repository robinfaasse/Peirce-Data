took up the C.d.r.V. — as I always write
"Die Critik der reinen Vernunft," and I
spell "Critik" with a C, partly because
Kant himself did so (at any rate in the
first edition) and partly because the word
was borrowed from [del]the Eng[/del] Locke's English
word "critic," meaning the science of
criticism; and it should be remembered
that Kant is emphatic in declaring that
he does not want it confused with
the word we spell "critique." The title
of the book ought to be translated,
"Critic of the Pure Reason," and by no
means as "The Critique of Pure Reason,"
since, in the first place, we do not in
English put an article before names
of sciences, such as, Logic, Rhetoric, etc.
and since, in the second place, it
would be ridiculous to criticize reason,
the whole object of criticism being to
subject reasonings, or in Kant's case
the human reason, to reason. [ins]to the[/ins]
