Doubt and belief are two states of mind which feel different. We can tell by our immediate sensation almost always when we doubt & when we [del]believe[/del] [ins]are convinced[/ins]. This is such as [ins]as there is[/ins] difference [del]between[/del] red & [del]blue[/del] [ins]yellow[/ins], pleasure & pain. Were this the whole distinction it would be almost without significance. But in point of fact the sensible distinguishability is attended with an important practical difference. When we believe there is a proposition in our mind which determines our conduct according to rule, so that our belief being known the way in which we shall behave may be surely deduced. In the case of doubt we have such a proposition in our mind but do not act from it, or at most it [del]has[/del] [ins]exerts[/ins] only a [del]small[/del] [ins]limited[/ins] [del]influence[/del] [ins]force[/ins] upon our action. [del]Thus[/del] If we [del]do[/del] not so much as conceive the proposition to be believed, there are [del]two[/del] [ins]three[/ins] cases, either our actions are entirely unconcerned with the matter or we act as if we had some belief, or it is mere chance how we act. The last two are practically belief & doubt respectively.

But this is not all belief is satisfactory; doubt is unsatisfactory. It is the wavering of doubt which is unsatisfactory.
