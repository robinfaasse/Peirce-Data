We must not lose sight of the double gearing, or mu-
tual helpfulness existing between the different Orders both
of physiognosy and of psychognosy. By the side of the
truth that Classificatory Science must draw upon the
results of Nomological Science for its major premisses and
in turn, along with Nomology, furnishes major premisses to
the Descriptive Sciences in their business of explanation,
stands the other truth that the minor premisses, the data, of
Classificatory Science are, in their first state, Descriptive of
single objects, and that, in like manner, the data of Nomo-
logical science [ins]must[/ins] have passed through the descriptive stage and
have become facts about classes before Nomology can
make use of them. Accordingly, history informs us that several
