Now then, Dynamics considers bodies only under that
aspect of them under which they are nothing more than
the sums of their parts; and it also considers their motions
under that aspect of them under which they are simply the
results of each moment's contribution. Dynamics, so re-
garding bodies and their motions, searches out whatever re-
gularities there may be in the motions under those aspects,
[ins]recognizing that bodies may be so related to one another, especially in position, as to be by certain
laws relatively accelerated, but not inquiring what those relations and laws are.[/ins]
