<u>A PROBLEM IN TESTIMONY.</u>

The probability that [ins]$A$[/ins] speaks the truth is [del]$A$[/del] [ins]$p$[/ins]. The probability that [ins]$B$[/ins] speaks the truth is [del]$B$[/del] [ins]$q$[/ins]. What is the probability that if they agree their statement is true?

This problem is stated and solved in Boole's "Laws of Thought" [del]pag[/del] [ins]p.[/ins] 279.

The solution here given is in effect the same as Boole's, though expressed in terms of descriptions.

The data here consists of the following propositions and probabilities represented by the symbols indicated:

| Propositions | Symbols | Probabilities |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| [del]$A = At$[/del] | $x$ | $p$ |
| [del]$B = Bt$[/del] | $y$ | $q$ |
| [del]$AB = AB(At.Bt + \bar{At}.\bar{Bt})$[/del] | $x y + \bar{x}\bar{y}$ | $r$ |

The above propositions are as follows:

$A$ speaks the truth. The probability of this proposition is [del]$A$[/del] [ins]$p$[/ins]

$B$ speaks the truth. The probability of this proposition is [del]$B$[/del] [ins]$q$[/ins]

Either $A$ speaks the truth and $B$ does not or $B$ speaks the truth and $A$ does not. The probability of this proposition is $r$.

The scheme of the possible gives the equation:

$x y + x \bar{y} + \bar{x} y + \bar{x} \bar{y} = 1$

Thus the required conditional probability may be expressed thus:

$\frac{(x y + \bar{x}\bar{y}) x y}{x y + \bar{x}\bar{y}} = \frac{x y}{x y + \bar{x}\bar{y}} = \frac{x y}{1 - r}$
