In composition before c, f, g, l, n, p, r, s, t the d is changed to that following
consonant, (or disappears before g and s), and before q is changed to c
But before b, d, h, m, v, it is not changed. [u]All this is merely the Latin of[/u]
[del]the Middle Ages or later[/del]; [ins]it is[/ins] Unchanged before b d f h j m n q v [ins]but a before ph.[/ins]
becomes ac before c and sometimes before q, becomes ag or ad before g; al or
ad before l, sometimes an before n, ap or ad before p, ad or ar before r,
at (sometimes ad) before t, as [del]ad[/del] or ad before s, but a before sc, sp, st as
well as before gn.

<u>In composition it generally means</u>
1. "to" or "toward" as ăffērrĕ, "to [del]bear[/del] [ins]take[/ins] [ins]convey[/ins] to" (fērrĕ "to bear)
2. "At" as ădēssĕ "to be present" (ēssĕ to be).
3. "On," "against" as ăttĕrĕrĕ "to rub against," "wear away," "impair"
(tĕrĕrĕ, "to rub")
