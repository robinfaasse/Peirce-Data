[u]Pa Pa[/u]; child's word for father.
This very first word is highly instructive. In the
first place, it convinces me that the very first question
in the classification of a symbol is what sort of an utterer
directly uses it (or perhaps will be understood to use it)
By "directly uses it" I mean uses it as his own utterance
not as a quotation from [del]another[/del] real or fictitious being.

My reflections on Cenoscopy shall be written, or at any
rate begun, in blue ink.

What cenoscopy is. This Bentham's word. I have forgotten
his definition of it; but it is formed from κοινός [u]common[/u]
σκοπία a [u]watch-tower[/u]. Cenoscopy is that great division
of [del]theoretical or Heuretic Science[/del] [ins]science of positive or observ-[/ins] which makes no special
observations but merely of Positive Science, i.e. Science
of the Real, (opposed to Mathematics which is the science of
the consequences of [del]by[/del] arbitrarily hypothetical states of things)
which makes no special observations but merely [del]does[/del]
occupies itself with making inferences from common
observation. Thus, heuretic science, or the science which
occupies itself with discovering truths has three grand
divisions: Mathematics, Cenoscopy, & Idioscopy, this
last being the science which specially occupies itself with
making [del]new[/del] observations of new truth. This might
be called Cenoscopy if ceno were derived from καινός, new.
