From this chapter, all doctrines upon which I would insist shall be carefully excluded, while I endeavor to explain the significations I propose to attach to the three words, First, Second, Third. I know not how to define these meanings in their generality; but can only put different

inadequate illustrations side by side, and let the reader gather what I cannot state abstractly. If he will only lend himself to this, postponing dissent until I insist upon some opinion, I think I shall be able to put him into a condition to pass judgment on the arguments which I intend to bring forward in the following chapters to show that there are realities corresponding to the conceptions described in this.

In place of the words, first, second, third, I might almost as well have used, beginning, end, and middle, --the word middle corresponding to third not to second. The First is that which has its being within itself. The Second, is that which is what it is by virtue of something else. The Third is that which is as it is by virtue of two other things between which it mediates.

Thus by first I simply mean what presents itself as original, immediate, fresh, unsubjected to anything before or behind, and therefore spontaneous and free. Of these words, fresh, perhaps, nearest expresses the idea in its virgin purity. Some of the others seem to imply a series of which the first is the beginning, or otherwise to refer to something not first. They seem to attain the notion by a circumlocution, as what is not second, instead of apprehending First in its original positiveness. The instant that freshness is classified, it has lost its innocence. The idea of prime precedes all assertion, all differentiation. There is no synthetical unity in it, no consistency; it is the sheer wonder and manifold of first impressions. In itself however, it is not manifold; it has no parts; but, because it has no wholeness nor consistency, the understanding analyzes it into an infinitely varied manifold. Though Kant always speaks of the manifold as if it were given in the sense, yet he says that the intellect evolves from it lies involved in it. I insert these remarks for whoever can accept them, but I need not quarrel with him who cannot.
