DeMorgan [del]called[/del] [ins]termed[/ins] these "syllogisms of transposed quantity," because they transfer the lexis from one term to another. His point of view was this: Take Baroko.

All M is P,
Some S is not P;
[three dots in a triangular pattern] Some S is not M.

The converse,

Some M is not S,

does not follow; but if there are as many Ms as there are Ss, then this does follow. "For if Ms, as many as there are Ss, be among the Ps, and some of the Ss be not among the Ps, though all the rest were, there would not be enough to match all the Ms, or some Ms are not Ss."
