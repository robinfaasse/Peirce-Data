[u]3[ins]rd[/ins] Case.[/u]  $iji = j$. Then
$$ijij = jj = -1$$
$$jiji = jj = -1$$
and $ij$ and $ji$ are also square roots of negative unity.
$$iji.i = -ij = ji.$$
But $(ij)i = j$
$i(ij) = -j$,
equations that do not hold for $ij = i$ nor for $ij = j$. [del]Let us[/del]
[del]then assume[/del] Nor can we put
$$ij = \sin \theta . i + \cos \theta . j$$
For then $i.ij = -\sin \theta + \cos \theta . ij = -\sin \theta + \cos \theta \sin \theta . i + \cos^2 \theta . j$
and we should have
$$\cos \theta = \sqrt{-1}.$$

Let us then write
$$ij = k$$
$$ji = -k$$
Then
$$ki = iji = j$$
$$ik = iij = -j$$
$$kj = ijj = -i$$
$$jk = -jji = i$$
This is the algebra of quaternions.
