[del]Th[/del] [del]the[/del] The former equation makes

Let us next consider such an expression as
$ai + bj = S + V$
where $S$ and $V$ are [del]the[/del] scalar and vector of the
first member. Squaring the vector, we get

$V^2 = -N = (ai + bj - S)^2 = -a^2 - b^2 + S^2 + abs + abs' - 2aSi - 2bSj$
$+ ab(1 - \frac{s'}{s})v$

or
$ab(1 - \frac{s'}{s})v = -N + a^2 + b^2 - S^2 - abs - abs' + 2aSi + 2bSj$.

But since $v$ is the vector of $ij$ it must, as we have seen
be linearly independent of unity, $i$, and $j$. Hence the
first member must vanish. But if $v = 0$, $ij = s$, whence
$-i = sj$, contrary to hypothesis. Hence
$1 - \frac{s'}{s} = 0$
or $s' = s$
whence $s'' = 1 - s^2$
