De Morgan; [del]and one of his[/del] [ins]the relative is his "for every,"[/ins] and one of his three terms is superfluous. Thus he [del]states[/del] [ins]puts[/ins] the argument about the checks into form as follows:

"For every memorandum of a purchase a countercheque is a transaction involving the drawing of a cheque;
"Some [del]purchas[/del] are not transactions involving the drawing of checks;
Therefore some countercheques are not memoranda of purchases."

But I should put it in form as follows:

Some payment for purchase was not a check;
Every payment by purchase is told off against a check;
No two payments by purchase are told off against the same check;
$\therefore$ Some checks are not payments by purchase.

That is, we prove the checks are not a part of the payments by purchase, because they are not less than the payments by purchase; and it is assumed they were enumerable. If they ran on endlessly, each payment by purchase might be told off against a check of [del]the previous[/del] [ins]a subsequent[/ins] day, the purchases increasing in number day by day.
