The above gives some idea what the further [del]study[/del] [ins]doctrine[/ins] of intransitive relations not including identity would be like. It is evidently more interesting to consider further the study of relatives of the form $(l + \breve{\bar{l}})$ and others allied to them.

The [del]conjugate[/del] [ins]converse[/ins] of $l \dagger \breve{\bar{l}}$ is $\bar{l} \dagger \breve{l}$, which is, of course, also transitive. The negative is $\bar{l}\breve{l}$, which is not transitive, but which has the property,

$$\bar{l}\breve{l} \prec \bar{l}\breve{l} \dagger \bar{l}\breve{l}.$$

For $\bar{l}\breve{l} = \bar{l} 1 \breve{l} \prec \bar{l} (\breve{l} \dagger \bar{l}) \breve{l} \prec \bar{l}\breve{l} \dagger \bar{l}\breve{l}$.

This is a property allied to transitiveness.

If $A$ is a lover of something not loved by $B$, which is, in its turn, a lover of something not loved by $C$, the conclusion is, that $A$ is a lover of something different from something not loved by $C$. That is,

$$(l\breve{\bar{l}})(l\breve{\bar{l}}) \prec l \top \breve{\bar{l}}.$$
