[ins]My[/ins] Let us resort to our algebra of relatives. Denote [del]the[/del] unlimited straight lines by lower case [ins]italic[/ins] letters. Let capitals denote points. Let Greek minuscules denote certain marks of lines. All these letters are treated as indices; but they will be written on the line. [del]Let[/del]

Let $aB$ (or any similar pair of letters) mean that the line $a$ is considered as having the point $B$, which lies on it. If the point $B$ is not on the line $a$, then $\overline{aB}$; but even if [del]B[/del] [ins]$B$[/ins] be on $a$, it does not necessarily follow that $B$ is regarded as belonging to the line $a$, and if not, again $\overline{aB}$. A point may belong to two lines, at once.

Let $\alpha b$ (or any similar pair of letters) signify that the line $b$ has the mark $\alpha$, the nature [ins]of[/ins] which will appear in the sequel.

Let $\alpha B$ etc signify that the point $B$ belongs to [ins]some[/ins] [del]a[/del] line that has the mark $\alpha$.
