[marginalia]§ 10 [u]Algebra of Real Quaternions[/u][/marginalia]

[del]Sect.[/del] I now turn back to square-roots of negative unity, not
supposing multiplication to be commutative. That is, we
do not generally have $xy = yx$. Suppose we have two
quantities $i$ and $j$, such that
$$i^2 + 1 = 0$$
$$j^2 + 1 = 0$$
Then it is plain that
$$(iji)(iji) = (ij)ii(ji) = -(ij)(ji) = -i.jj.i = ii = -1,$$
so that $iji$ [del]is also[/del] and $jij$ are also square roots of
negative unity.

Five cases may be studied
[ins]1[u]st[/u][/ins] $iji = i$
2[u]nd[/u] $iji = -i$
3[u]rd[/u] $iji = j$
[del]4[u]th[/u][/del] $iji = -j$
5[u]th[/u] $iji = k$ (a third unit.)

[u]1[u]st[/u] Case[/u]. $iji = i$. Then,
$$iiji = [del]i[/del] ji = ii$$
$$-j = i.$$

[u]2[u]nd[/u] Case[/u] $iji = -i$. Then,
$$i.iji = -ji = -ii$$
$$j = i.$$
