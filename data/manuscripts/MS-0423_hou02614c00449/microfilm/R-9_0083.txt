Let $G$ be [del]another[/del] [ins]a[/ins] letter of the first class which is a
non-leos of itself; then
$$G \overline{\Omega} G,$$
and the first class may be subdivided into three with
reference to $G$, [ins]just[/ins] as all were divided relatively to $I$. So,
if $R$ be a letter of the second class which is non-leos of
itself, or
$$R \overline{\Omega} R.$$

We can then divide all possible individuals other than
$G$, $I$, and $R$ into [del]eight[/del] [ins]ten[/ins] classes, viz: —

$1^{\text{st}}$, those which, as [del]$B$[/del] [ins]$B$, etc[/ins], are leos of $G$; as $B \Omega G$;

$2^{\text{nd}}$, those which [ins]as $\Gamma$[/ins] are neither leos of nor leod by $G$, but are leos of $I$;
as $\Gamma \bar{\Omega} G$, $G \bar{\Omega} \Gamma$, $\Gamma \Omega I$;

$3^{\text{rd}}$, those which, as $H, K$, etc, are leod by $G$ and are leos of $I$; as
$G \Omega K$, $K \Omega I$;

[del]$4^{\text{th}}$, those which, as $\Delta$, as not leod by $G$, nor leos of $I$, but are leos of $R$; as $G \bar{\Omega} \Delta$, $\Delta \bar{\Omega} I$, $\Delta \Omega R$[/del]

$5^{\text{th}}$, those which as $\Theta$, are leod by $G$, are neither leos of nor leod by $I$,
and are leos of $R$; as
$$G \Omega \Theta, \quad I \bar{\Omega} \Theta, \quad \Theta \bar{\Omega} I, \quad \Theta \Omega R;$$

$6^{\text{th}}$, those which [ins]as $P, Q$, etc[/ins] are leod by $I$ and are leos of $R$; as
$$I \Omega P \quad P \Omega R;$$

$7^{\text{th}}$ those which [ins]as $\Xi$[/ins] are not leod by $G$ nor are leos of $R$; as
$$G \bar{\Omega} \Xi, \quad \Xi \bar{\Omega} R;$$

$8^{\text{th}}$ those which [ins]as $\Pi$[/ins] are leod by $G$, are not leod by $I$, and are not leos of $R$;
$$G \Omega \Pi, \quad [del]H[/del] I \bar{\Omega} \Pi, \quad \Pi \bar{\Omega} R;$$
