§2. I will now notice the logical aspects of some of the signs of algebra. The sign of equality, [u]a = b[/u], is only applicable when we can show that two magnitudes can be so placed, with or without dissection, that each is covered by the other, that is, that a < b and b < a. The algebraists have seen fit to use the signs a < b and b > a, which exclude equality; but they might equally well have used the others. These others as being of greater logical breadth have less logical depth, and in that sense are of simpler signification.

Addition is what aggregation, signified in this book by +, becomes, when the two aggregants are mutually exclusive, or, when their common extent is counted in twice over.
