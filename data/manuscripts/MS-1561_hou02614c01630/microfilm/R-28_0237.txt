Again, they walked together in silence, Thodores trying to swallow his disgust. They came out upon a road. After a while Thodores stopped under an oak and said, "On this spot, I slew last year, Nikephoro, bishop of Petra."

"Mercy! How did that happen?"

"Perikles and I were further up the road when we got information that the good bishop was coming with some money for the monastery in Skala. We laid in wait for him, as he came up, jumped out upon him, and took the money. We had let him go and he had turned back and was out of sight when it suddenly occurred to us that he would go straight back to his cathedral and excommunicate us. It made my blood run cold. We ran after him, and caught up with him just here, and I shot him through the head."

"In America," said Karolo, "young men do not [del]assas-[/del] [ins]blow clergymen's[/ins] [del]sinate, at any rate, not[/del] [ins]brains out[/ins] from fear of excommunication."

They turned back toward the house. Thodores explained that the Turks would now be in a more active state of rage than usual, owing to the abduction, and that the klephts must be constantly on their guard. For that reason, he would not accompany Karolo to the shore in the morning, especially as he would only put him into danger by doing so. What Karolo had to do was to go down under the guidance of one klepht and get off in the sloop as quietly as possible.
