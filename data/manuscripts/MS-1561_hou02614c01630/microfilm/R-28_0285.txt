This Thessalonian epistle, our excursionist, — mere
excursionist now, never again to be a Klepht, — carried
to the boatman to be sent up by Lykophron to Mavrokordato;
and taking his cloak, his wood carvings, and his Klephtic
magnificence, the only vestiges of his past state of existence,
he boarded the steamer just as she was weighing anchor, and
thus found himself once again among the consumers of
soap. —

But very soon he went to his berth more sick than ever
he had been at sea. It was fever, as well as seasickness,
and complicated with bottomless despair; and as he looked out
at his porthole and saw, for the last time, the majestic and
sacred mountain that had become so dear to him, his frame
was shaken with grief, and he cried like a child. When the ship
reached Volo he was far too ill to rise, but he managed to
write a note and to send it to Psallopoulos to thank him
