The name of the village which the newspapers print
Belestino, but which [ins]<sub>*</sub>[/ins] we believe is Belestrina, — there
is a famous fountain there, — begins with precisely the
same sound as the name Volo. The same sound also occurs in Salembhria
[del]— another name for the river Peneios, which is pronounced Peneeo. This river is about 100 feet
wide at Larissa.[/del]

The only genuine sound of B in modern Greek is given
to a [u]p[/u] following an [u]m[/u]. Thus, Mount Olympus, which
[ins]in modern and ancient times[/ins] always began with an
E for the dwellers upon it and in the Thessalian plain,
is called E'limbo, strongly accented on the first syllable.
The remarkable cut called [del]the[/del] the vale of Tempe is pronounced
Tembe. The consequence is that when the Greeks write
a foreign name containing B in their own alphabet they
substitute for it mp, [u]mp[/u]. Thus, the village near the
tomb of Ali Baba, a Turkish saint, at the entrance of
Tempe, is spelled in Greek Mpampa, as the [del]ir[/del] nearest
approximation to the sound "Baba".

While we are upon the subject we may mention that Larissa
is accented strongly on the first syllable, while the s is prolonged.
Trikhala is likewise accented on the first syllable.

The letter y in the transcriptions of such names as Delyannis
represents gamma iota. It is spelled Delgiannis, and is pronounced
Dhelyanni. The newspapers fall between these two stools.
