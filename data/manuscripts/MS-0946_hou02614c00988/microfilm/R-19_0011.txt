The word [u]synechism[/u] is the English form of the Greek [u]συνεχισμός[/u], from [u]συνεχής[/u], continuous. For the last two centuries we have freely affixed [u]=ist[/u] and [u]=ism[/u] to words, to signify the sect that exalts the importance of the attributes such words imply. Thus, [u]materialism[/u] is the doctrine that matter is everything, [u]idealism[/u] the doctrine that ideas are everything, [u]dualism[/u] the kind of philosophy which splits everything in two. In like manner [u]synechism[/u] is the philosophy which regards the conception of continuity of prime importance. My own thorough-going [u]synechism[/u] is the doctrine that continuity rules the whole domain of experience, so that every proposition which does not relate to the infinitely distant, or [ins]to some other phase of[/ins] the unattainable limit of experience, is to be taken with an indefinite qualification.
