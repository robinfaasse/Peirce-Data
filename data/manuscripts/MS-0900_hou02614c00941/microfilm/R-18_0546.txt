The verb (and as such [del]being of a monadic nature[/del] [ins]tending to produce[/ins]). Thus, in the syllogism each term
is a medium [del]toward[/del] the union of the other two. These three [gap] are of different
characters. A reason is something which of its nature can be and is both subject
and predicate; but there is only one of the three which formally manifests this nature.

In the degenerate dyad, there is a proposition; but it is a proposition
of which the subjects are mere monads. It so lies in the essence of the
monads that such a logician as Renouvier, for instance, would call it
a tautology, and hardly admit is was a proposition, though this is a wrong
view. In the first degenerate triad, there is a syllogism; but it is a syllogism
of which the three reasons are mere monads. Considered as a reasoning it is
a mere reasoning from definition. In the second degenerate triad, there is syllogism, but it is
a syllogism whose three reasons are mere dyads.
