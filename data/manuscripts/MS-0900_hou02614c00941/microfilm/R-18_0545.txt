We now come to thoroughly genuine triads, the third [del]division[/del] [ins]class[/ins] of the third class of triads. At this [del]pa[/del] stage of the inquiry it is necessary to recall just where we are. The monad has only its suchness, which appears in logic as the material signification of the verb. It is that which the peripatetic logicians called the form. It is already in the logical term. The dyad introduced a wholly different sort of element, the subject, which appears first in logic in the proposition. The triad introduces the medium, or reason, which appears first in the inference. The degenerate was a proposition about monads, the monads being subjects, [del]and[/del] no peculiar subject appearing. The degenerate triad ought to have no reason of the peculiar [del]m[/del] kind. The first or monadic triad should be merely an inference from monads. There is no reason, or thought, except what lies in the monads themselves.
