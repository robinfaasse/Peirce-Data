[u]Art[/u] . [u]Theorem[/u] . [[u]Matthew Stewart's Theorem[/u].] If the points [A], [B], [C] are coradial [ins]and lie in that order[/ins], then, no matter where [P] may be,

[del]BC.AP² +[/del]
AB.CP² + BC.AP² + CA.BP² + AB.BC.CA = 0.

[u]Demonstration[/u]. For, by Theorem 1
[fig]Triangle with vertices A, B, C and point P above, connected to A, B, C[/fig] AP² = AB² + BP² - 2AB.BP.cos ABP
PC² = BC² + BP² - 2BC.BP.cos CBP

But, by Corollary , cos CBP = -cos ABP
We multiply the first equation by BC, the second by AB, and add their products; and thus find
[del]BC[/del] AB.PC² + BC.AP² = AB.BC² + BC.AB² + AB.BP² + BC.BP²
= AB.BC(AB+BC) + (AB+BC)BP²
= AB.BC.AC + AC.BP²

or AB.PC² + BC.AP² - AC.BP² - AB.BC.AC = 0. Q.E.D.
