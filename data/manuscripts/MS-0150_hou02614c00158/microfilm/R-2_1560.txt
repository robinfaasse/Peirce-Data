The edges of surfaces, and lines along which a surface splits into three or more, and points at which different surfaces are tacked together are singularities.

[u]Definition[/u] . A topical denumerative peculiarity of a place is character, not a singularity, which makes the place count for more or less than it otherwise would in reckoning the number of places connected with it.

[u]Division[/u] . There are four [ins]kinds of[/ins] topical denumerative peculiarities, namely,
A. The chorisis (pronounced kos' risis)
B. The cyclosis (pronounced sik' los' sis)
C. The periphraxis (pronounced perifraksis
D. The immensity.
