. Hyperbolical measurement is somewhat rare. Or, to speak accurately, it is not really so rare; but examples which are manifestly of this kind are rare.

Upon a Mercator's Projection, measurement along a meridian is of this kind, the north pole being infinitely high, the south pole infinitely low, and the other side of the circle beyond all measurement.

. Parabolical measurement is the simplest and commonest of all.

[fig]Line segment with point P above it[/fig]

Take a ray [u]{a}[/u] and a point [P] [ins]above it[/ins]. Draw a ray from [P] in the plane [u](Pa)[/u] leaning down ever so little to the right, and according to our ideas of the movements of [del]rigid bodies[/del] it will cut [u]{a}[/u] at some finite distance to the right. But let it tilt ever so little to the left, and it will cut [u]{a}[/u] at some finite distance to the left. Only when it is exactly even will it not cut at any finite distance right or left. We then say it cuts [u]{a}[/u] at infinity.
