[u]Art. . Illustration[/u] . Imagine a bucket of water. Imagine a lump of ice to be formed in the midst of the water. The whole solid space at first filled with water is now filled with water and with ice. Between these bodies is a surface, [del]or more[/del] that is a place where water and ice come together. It is the water-ice surface. Between the water and the wood of the bucket is a water-wood surface. Between the [del]water[/del] [ins]water[/ins] and the air is a water-air surface. Between the wood and the air is a wood-air surface.

Suppose the ice instead of being formed in the midst of the water forms a lump against the side of the bucket, and suppose it reaches up out of that water.

[fig]Diagram of a bucket containing water, ice, and air[/fig] Then, the whole surface of the water is occupied by the water-air film, the water-ice film, and the water-wood film. Between the water-air film and the water-ice film is a line where water, ice, and air come together. It is the water-ice-air line. There is also a water-ice-wood line, a water-air-wood line, and an [del]or[/del] ice-air-wood line. Each of these is occupied by a filament. [del]To[/del] Between the water-ice-air filament and the water-ice-wood filament, there are two points where water, ice, air, and wood come together. The other two lines contain these same points.
