Section . Metrical propositions independent of the [del]system[/del] [ins]type[/ins] of measurement.

[u]Scholium[/u] . Some of the propositions in this section may appear to be false, unless particular attention is paid to their exact wording. Even when they are seen to be true, they may be deemed very trivial by those who do not reflect that propositions may have other uses than their direct application. Some of these which relate to rays are of little practical importance in what they teach about rays alone. But it will be found later that they lead [del]others[/del] by a very speedy path to propositions highly practical about lines upon a globe. Moreover, we get a firmer mastery of metrical geometry by studying first so much as is true independently of the peculiar manner in which rigid bodies move.

We have seen that in graphical geometry there is a principle of duality by virtue of which the ideas of [u]point[/u] and [u]plane[/u], [u]containing[/u] and [u]being contained in[/u] can always be interchanged throughout a proposition without affecting its truth. Thus, we [del]have[/del] the following pairs of propositions.

| | |
|---|---|
| Through every pair of points there is a ray. | Every pair of planes have a common ray. |
| Every pair of rays in one plane have a common point | Every pair of rays through one point lie in one plane. |
| [del]In no case are[/del] [ins]common to[/ins] two different points [del]both[/del] two different rays. | In no case [del]do[/del] [ins]in common[/ins] two different planes [del]both[/del] contain[del]ing[/del] two different rays. |
