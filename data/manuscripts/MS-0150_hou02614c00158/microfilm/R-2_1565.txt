[u]Art. 16[/u]. [u]Definition 29[/u]. An [u]ordinary[/u] point of a line is a point from which a particle can move along the line two ways. A [u]singular[/u] point of a line is point from which a particle can move along the line [del]either[/del] less or more than two ways.

[u]Definition 30[/u]. A [u]cusp[/u] is point of a line not an extremity of it from which a particle cannot only move one way. See [fig]diagram of two cusps[/fig] Fig.

An [u]ordinary[/u] line of a surface is a line from [ins]every point of[/ins] which a filament [ins]occupying the line[/ins] can move in the surface [del]two[/del] ways. A [u]line of singularity[/u] is a line from every point of which a filament [del]of[/del] occupying the line can move in [ins]less[/ins] [del]more[/del] or more than two ways.

[u]Definition 33[/u]. A [u]cuspidal edge[/u] is a line of a surface [ins]not a limit of it[/ins] from [del]every[/del] [ins]each[/ins] point of which a filament occupying the line can move but one way.
