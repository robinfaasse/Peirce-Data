Every three points lie in one plane.

Three different points are never common to two different planes, unless they lie in one ray.

Every three planes have a common point.

Three different planes never have two different points in common, unless they all pass through one ray.

This dualism comes to an end in metrical geometry, for the reason that the measurement of the displacements of a plane film round a ray is [del]is[/del] elliptic, while the measurement of the displacements of a particle along a ray is assumed to be parabolic. But in that part of metrics which is considered in this section, which is independent of the type of measurement, the [del]dualism holds[/del] [ins]principle of duality continues[/ins] to hold.
