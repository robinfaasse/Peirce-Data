<u>Scholium</u>. One type of measurement is generally more appropriate to a given state of facts than another. That is, it puts into a stronger light, and in a simpler form, a given law. But anything which is measurable in any of the three ways is measurable in either of the other ways, without any falsity whatever.

For instance, sailors always use the Mercators projection, because it puts certain truths in a very simple form, which is convenient. Other truths about the earth, it expresses, but expresses them in a very complicated and inconvenient way. It is not, in the least, false. What is called its falsity [del]is[/del] consists merely in the falsity of a misapprehension of the [del]repre[/del] [ins]mode[/ins] of representation.
