Construct a circle, knowing two tangents and a point of it.

[u]Problem[/u]. From a given point draw a tangent to a given circle.

[u]Solution[/u]. From the point draw two lines cutting the circle, one preferably nearly a tangent, the other far from tangency. Join the four points of intersection by rays in four more ways, getting two new intersections. The points where the ray through these [del]new[/del] [ins]new[/ins] points cuts the circle are the points [del]of tangency[/del].

This applies, not only to a circle, but also to any ellipse, hyperbola, or parabola.

The proper proof of this belongs to higher geometry
