The words [u]ellipse[/u], [u]hyperbola[/u], [u]parabola[/u] are
ancient Greek and refer to a particular way of
drawing the curves, which we shall not here describe.
[u]Ellipse[/u] means literally, "falling short," because in that
mode of drawing a certain line falls short of reaching a
certain point. Language which is cut short is called "elliptical."
[u]Hyperbola[/u], in ordinary Greek "hyperbole", means "going
beyond," because the same line that, in drawing the ellipse,
falls short, in drawing the hyperbola, goes beyond. Language
which goes beyond the facts is called "hyperbolical." [u]Parabola[/u],
the same word as [u]parable[/u], means falling pat; because the
line that in the ellipse falls short and in the hyperbola
goes beyond, in the parabola does neither but falls pat on
the point. A parable is a story which hits pat upon a
great truth.
