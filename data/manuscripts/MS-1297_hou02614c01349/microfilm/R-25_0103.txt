great engineers, they were not scientific. We hear
vaguely of their astronomical observations; but
in the Almagest, it is always Greek or Babylonian
observations which are cited, — nor Egyptian
ones, except Ptolemy's own. There never was a
people so slavishly bound down to tradition, a
sure sign of defective powers of observation.

The unquailingly laborious perfection of
all they did is a character of primitive peoples.
Iron was used by the pyramid-builders; but they
were near the stone age, when lives were spent
in finishing single knives, and generations in
building single boats.

Like the negroes they were very religious; but
unlike the negroes, their religion was a very
moral one.
