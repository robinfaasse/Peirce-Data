[ins]necessary[/ins] consequence of the state of things supposed, any graph
[u]P[/u] [del]within[/del] [ins]in[/ins] N-1 enclosures is transformable into a
graph, Q. Then, let there be another oval within the
N-1 ovals, this inner oval enclosing Q and let
the rest of what it encloses be written in the form
[u]R[/u]. We have, then, within the N-1 enclosures
[fig]oval containing Q and R[/fig]

Consequently, as above, within [ins]N-1[/ins] enclosures Q is
transformable in [u]R[/u] and therefore [u]P[/u] is transformable
into R. That is, we may write within the N-1 enclo-
sures
[fig]oval containing P and R[/fig]

In other words, Q is transformable into P within N
enclosures. Thus if the rule holds for N-1 enclosures
it holds also for N enclosures. It therefore holds for
any finite number of enclosures.

The same thing may be proved in another way.
