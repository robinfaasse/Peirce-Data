[u]Scholium[/u]. There is a common expression, "That is as false as anything can be." This is just the effect of the oval. The graph

[pn]A[/pn]

does not quite say express that A is unscriptible, but only that if anything is unscriptible, A is so. This Hegelian sort of denial is the only one our system expresses. It will practically answer the purpose of flat denial. [ins]As such, it is a sign of denial if there can be any[/ins] [del]totally identical with the such sign, and is identical with[/del] the obelus, or black horizontal spit written over a word or sentence to deny it; a sign which Isidorus Hispalensis about A.D. 600 refers to as an old sign. It has been used ever since, giving rise to the use of the same mark to indicate an omitted [u]n[/u] ([u]non[/u]) or [u]m[/u]. It is used by writers on probability and by Boole in the same sense. We may use it as an alternative sign equivalent to the oval.
