If a person says, "Somebody was a Harlequin-Plato", he conveys pretty nearly the same idea to a person who does not know Galiani as if he had said "The abbé Galiani was a harlequin-Plato." "Something" is an individual whose proper name is withheld, thus giving the [del]utterer[/del] [ins]liberty[/ins] to justify his assertion by a choice of instances. On the other hand, it may be said that the very sole-feather of the mathematician, — the implement he uses at every step, — is the invention of a proper designation for a something.
