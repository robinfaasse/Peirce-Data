Rule V is evident as long as the line of identity is [del]un[/del]enclosed. When we invent a name for some thing we create
a fact, and are justified in asserting the fact so created.
Only we must take care that the "something" to which the
name is given is individual, that is, is of such a nature
that the principle of excluded middle is applicable to it.
A mere ideal thing is [del]of[/del] not of that nature; but if it is to
be realized, it will become of that nature. I may, for example,
wish for an automobile; and then by a dilemma prove
that no particular kind of automobile would please me.
This will prove that there is no automobile that I wish for
but not that I do not wish for an automobile. For
wishing does not consist in a relation to any existing thing.
