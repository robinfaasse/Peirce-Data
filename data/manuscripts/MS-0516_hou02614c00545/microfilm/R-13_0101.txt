There is another logical relation the expression of which
at once constitutes the logic of relatives. This relation
is that of identity. It resembles the relation of cosubsti-
bility in being [ins]affirmatively[/ins] transitive and [del]truly[/del] [ins]and only if[/ins] continuous. I call a rela-
tion affirmatively transitive if the dictum de omni applies
to it; by which I mean that to say that A is in the given
relation to B signifies that whatever is in the given
relation to A is in the given relation to B; just as to say
that A is, all of it, B, is to say that whatever is, all of it, A
is, all of it, B. I call a relation [u]truly continuous[/u], if, and
