It is now time to consider indices of definite individuals.
As already stated, an individual term is one to which the
principle of excluded third applies. A definite term is one to
which the principle of contradiction applies. For example,
'Something' is indefinite because it may be true that
Something is good,
and Something is bad.
But we no sooner have defined something as good than
we have made it in so far definite, that [u]that[/u] something
cannot be bad; although
Something good may be edible,
and Something good may be inedible.
[del]Nothing can[/del] [ins]Everything must[/ins] be, in each respect, either definite or individual.
