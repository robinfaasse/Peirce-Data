[u]Scholium[/u]. There is a common expression, "That is as false as
anything can be." This is just the effect of the oval. The
graph

[fig]figure[/fig]

does not quite [del]say[/del] express that A. is unscriptible, but only that
if anything is unscriptible, A. is so. This Hegelian sort of
denial is the only one our system expresses. It will practi-
cally answer the purpose of flat denial. As such, it is [del]prac-
tically identical with the such sign[/del] [ins]a sign of denial if there can be any[/ins], and is identical with
the [u]obelus[/u], or black horizontal spit written over a word or
sentence to deny it; a sign which Isodorus Hispalensis about
A.D. 600 refers to as an old sign. It has been used ever since,
giving rise to the use of the same mark to indicate an omitted
[u]n[/u] ([u]non[/u]) or [u]m[/u]. It is used by writers on probability and by Boole
in the same sense. We may use it as an alternative sign
equivalent to the oval.
