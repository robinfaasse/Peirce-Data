Let us apply this principle to the case in hand. Boole, immortal, if for nothing else, for the invention of logical algebra, used [del]the[/del] signs of algebra in their strict algebraic senses; and it was merely a consequence of a certain convention adopted by him that they became, not logical signs, but algebraic equivalents of logical signs. In particular, + as used by him was not an equivalent for [del]the[/del] a sign of aggregation; and there is a positive scientific disadvantage in so using it. It was Jevons who first introduced [ins]into logical algebra[/ins] a sign for [del]logic[/del] aggregation; and the sign which he decided upon as appropriate was '.', which sufficiently resembles + to mark the resemblance of the operations. This sign is objectionable because it requires the pen to be put down three times, and also (what is more important) because it is liable to be mistaken for three signs, and would inevitably be so mistaken.
