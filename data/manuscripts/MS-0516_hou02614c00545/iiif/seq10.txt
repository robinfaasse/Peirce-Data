I proceed to show that both clauses [ins]are corollaries[/ins] [del]follow[/del] from Rules I and II. I shall refer to them as Corollaries 1 and 2. To prove the first, suppose [u]x[/u] to be written, and that, in consequence of its being written, [u]a[/u] and [u]b[/u] are separately scriptible. But by Rule II whatever graphs are separately scriptible are scriptible together, detached. Hence, [u]ab[/u] or [u]a b[/u] [del]is[/del] is scriptible. Q.E.D.

To prove Corollary 2., I remark that if [u]a[/u] were written, it could, by Rule I, be erased; whence, by Rule II, if [u]a b[/u], that is, [u]a[/u] [u]b[/u], were written [u]a[/u] could be erased. Whence, if, in consequence of [u]x[/u] being written, [u]ab[/u] should become scriptible, this could be transformed into [u]b[/u], so that [u]x[/u] could be transformed into [u]b[/u]; and the same reasoning applies to the transformation into [u]a[/u].
