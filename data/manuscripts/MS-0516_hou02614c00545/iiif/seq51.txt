Since the whole line of identity denotes the same individual, a line of identity cannot be considered as enclosed in any oval in which it is not entirely enclosed.

We now see the reason for the restriction upon Rule V. For consider the graph
[fig]a diagram showing two nested ovals, with lines connecting the text "is a man", "loves", and "is a woman"[/fig]

This reads "Every man loves some woman". But if we were to attach a proper name, [ins]say P,[/ins] to the inner line of identity, without attaching one to the outer, the meaning would be "Every man loves some [del]name[/del] [ins]individual[/ins] woman, P," which [del]since it would[/del] [ins]would amount[/ins] name that line before the other would be equivalent to
[fig]a diagram with three nested ovals and labels P, Q, "is a man", "loves", and "is a woman", and a second diagram with two nested ovals and a T-shaped line connecting "is a man", "loves", and "is a woman"[/fig]
that is, "Some woman is loved by all men."
