If a person says, "Somebody was a Harlequin Plato", he 
conveys pretty nearly the same idea to a person who does not 
know Galiani as if he had said "The abbé Galiani was a 
Harlequin Plato." "Somebody" is an individual whose 
proper name is withheld, thus giving the utterer [ins]liberty[/ins] to justify 
his assertion by a choice of instances. On the other 
hand, it may be said that the very sole-leather of the 
mathematician, — the implement he uses at every step, — 
is the invention of a proper designation for a something.
