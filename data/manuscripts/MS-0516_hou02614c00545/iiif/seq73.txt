There is, however, no doubt that it is extremely convenient
in logical algebra to have a special sign for aggregation.
This is sufficiently proved by the fact that De Morgan, in 1858,
having given appropriate signs to [del]these[/del] two operations of com-
position [del]and[/del] (or copulation) and aggregation, without em-
ploying the Booleian algebra was followed in this by the
Booleians, Jevons, in 1864, Peirce in 1867, Robert Grassmann
in 1872, Schröder in 1877, and McColl in 1877, each being
unaware of the works of his predecessors (and the last three,
doubtless, unaware also of the work of De Morgan.) In short,
all the [del]Bo[/del] followers of Boole, with the distinguished exception
of Mr. Venn, have given up the use of the operation of addition
and have substituted for it that of aggregation. If we repre-
sent addition by + and aggregation by [u]ψ[/u], the relation of
the two is
