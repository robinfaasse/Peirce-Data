§3. Since we have to frame a system for symbolizing propositions, it is plain that we shall have to analyze the nature of a proposition. But before doing so, we can begin by dealing with propositions as units.

We are going to use visible marks upon a surface for our symbols. We will provide ourselves, then, with a surface which we will call the [del]sheet of assertion[/del], and will pretend to hold ourselves responsible for, or in other words to assert, the [del]truth[/del] [ins]that is, the conformity to a certain purpose[/ins] of whatever proposition we write upon it. [ins]Let us call a proposition expressed in the system we are to develop a graph.[/ins] As long as it remains blank we are irresponsible. Having once written something upon it, we assume that to be [del]true[/del] [ins]conformed to our purpose[/ins]. We will abstract from tense; and then we do not render it [del]false[/del] [ins]inconsistent with our purpose in[/ins] by allowing it to remain.
