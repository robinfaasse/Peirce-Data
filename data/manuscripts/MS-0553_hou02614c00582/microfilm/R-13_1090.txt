The two arises from the difference in the order of selection. I am
accustomed to use a sort of algebra in which $\Sigma$ signifies
a suitable selection and $\Pi$ taking no matter what one.
With this convention, I should write the first proposition
thus,
$$\Sigma_{\underline{w}} \Pi_{\underline{m}} \Sigma_{\underline{l}} (\underline{m} \underline{l} \underline{w});$$
and the second thus,
$$\Sigma_{\underline{l}} \Pi_{\underline{m}} \Sigma_{\underline{w}} (\underline{m} \underline{l} \underline{w}).$$
In such a formula, the $\Sigma$'s and $\Pi$'s with their affixed letters are [ins]collectively[/ins] called the quantifiers[del].[/del]
