Every relative term has a converse, produced by
reversing the order of the members of the pair. Thus, the
converse of "lover" is "loved". The converse may be
represented by drawing a brachys, or mark of a short
vowel, over the letter. It is defined by the equation
$$\breve{l}_{ij} = l_{ji}$$
The converse has some analogy with the negative.
Thus
$$\bar{\bar{l}} = l \quad \breve{\breve{l}} = l$$
$$\bar{\breve{l}} = \breve{\bar{l}}$$

$$\overline{l \cdot b} = \bar{l} \dagger \bar{b} \qquad \breve{l \cdot b} = \breve{b} \cdot \breve{l}$$
$$\overline{l \dagger b} = \bar{l} \cdot \bar{b} \qquad \breve{l \dagger b} = \breve{b} \dagger \breve{l}$$
The converse [del]changes[/del] [ins]reverses[/ins] the order, while the negative
reverses the mode of combination.
