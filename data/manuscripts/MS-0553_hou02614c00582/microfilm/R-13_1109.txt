If only one operation is to be used, it cannot be
symmetrical; and it will be proved that it is such
that, if the sign $\prec$ is used to signify it,
$$\text{true} \prec \text{true}\quad\text{is}\quad\text{true},$$
$$\text{false} \prec \text{false}\quad\text{is}\quad\text{true},$$
$$\text{false} \prec \text{true}\quad\text{is}\quad\text{true},$$
$$\text{true} \prec \text{false}\quad\text{is}\quad\text{false}.$$
That is to say, $A \prec B$ has very nearly the meaning 'If $A$ be
true, then $B$ is true'; It has more precisely the meaning
'Either $A$ is false or $B$ is true.' This operation is not
associative.
