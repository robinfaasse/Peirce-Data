That every relation of linear quantity has a minimum is plain from this that its converse has a maximum; and the maximum of the converse is the minimum of the first relation.

The chief characteristic of enumerable collections is that an enumerable part is less than its whole. [del]If the Ms[/del] [ins]To say that[/ins] the Ms are a part of the Ns is to say that every M is an N, but some N is not an M. To say that the Ms are less than the Ns is to say that in every way in which an M can be set off against an N, there remain Ns against which no M is set off. It is very easy to see that it is not generally true that a part is less than its whole. Thus, the even whole numbers form a part of the whole numbers; for every even number is a whole number, but some whole numbers are not even numbers. But every number has a double; and that double is an even number; and thus for every number there is an even number.
