§12. The above method is suited to the most difficult cases. But for a large proportion of those which occur the following [del]may[/del] [ins]will[/ins] suffice, and will be simpler.

Namely we write
$$\begin{array}{rcl}
a & \text{for} & \Pi_i\ a_{ii} \\
ae & \text{for} & \Pi_i\ \Sigma_j\ a_{ij} \cdot e_{ji} \\
\breve{a}e & \text{for} & \Pi_i\ \Sigma_j\ a_{ji} \cdot e_{ji} \\
a \dagger e & \text{for} & \Pi_i\ \Pi_j\ a_{ij} \dagger e_{ji}
\end{array}$$

To express
$$\Pi_i\ \Pi_j\ l_{ij}$$
we put it in the form
$$\Pi_i\ \Pi_j\ l_{ij} \dagger 0_{ji}$$
and write
$$l \dagger 0.$$

To express [del]$\Pi_k\ \Sigma_i\ \Pi_j\ l_{ij}$[/del], we put it in the form
$$\Pi_k\ \Sigma_i\ \Pi_j\ \infty_{kj} \cdot (l_{ij} \dagger 0_{jk})$$
and write
$$\infty (l \dagger 0).$$

To express $\Pi_j\ \Sigma_i\ l_{ij}$, we put it in the form
$$0_{kj} \dagger l_{ij} \cdot \infty_{ki}$$
and write
$$0 \dagger \breve{l} \infty.$$
or
$$\infty \breve{l} \dagger 0$$
