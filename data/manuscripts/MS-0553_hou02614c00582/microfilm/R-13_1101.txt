§6. Among relations of superiority, particularly important
are those in reference to which everything either contains or
is contained by everything else [ins]whatever A and B may be[/ins] (within the special universe of discourse)
In that case, [del]f[/del] either A is above B, or B is above A, or
A and B coincide.

Such relations of superiority are termed relations of [del]successi[/del]
and we say that A is in some sense [u]after[/u] B.

The converse of a relation of superiority is itself a
relation of superiority.

[ins]A special variety of [del]relation[/del] succession is where no two different things coincide,
so that A is after B or B after A or A and B are identical. Such relations are termed relations of linear
quantity, and we say the A is in some sense [u]more than[/u] B.[/ins]

§7. Another important relation of superiority is illustrated
in the following examples:

A can lift [del]some[/del][ins]everything[/ins] B can lift, and something B cannot
lift, but cannot lift anything that cannot be lifted by
everybody who can lift anything that B cannot lift.

That is, taking any two weights U and V, no matter what, and
any third person C, no matter who, either A cannot lift U, or
C can lift U, or C cannot lift V, or B can lift V.

Here is another example:

The number 9 is reached in counting any collection in the counting
of which 10 is reached, and is reached in counting some collections
in the counting of which 10 is not reached; but [ins]it is not true that[/ins] 9 is [del]not[/del] reached
in counting a collection in [del]the[/del] counting of which a number is not
reached, that is [del]not[/del] reached in the counting of a collection [del]in the[/del]
counting of which 10 is not reached.
