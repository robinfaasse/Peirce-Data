I first give an account of the doctrine of inclusion, using the notation and method of De Morgan (Syllabus of Logic. [date]1860[/date].)

[del]A spicula is a single[/del]

The letters denote [ins]terms, which are[/ins] propositions (or classes, which may be regarded as propositions.) A parenthesis mark used as a logical icon is called a spicula. A spicula attached to a letter on the concave side of it, shows that every possible state of things which includes the truth of the term; but if the convex side is toward the letter, it shows that only some state of things in which the term is true is considered.
