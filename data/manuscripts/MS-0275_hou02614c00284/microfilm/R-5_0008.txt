III. It [del]will[/del] [ins]may[/ins] be useful to investigate the application of the
above nomenclature and notation, as well as of the general theory
which has suggested this nomenclature and notation, to the case
of those simple, finite, discrete manifolds, which are analogous,
in regard to the relations of their units, to the regular solids.
I mean such manifolds as have a set of relations among their
units analogous to those neighborhood relations which exist among
the faces of the several regular solids.

Thus there is the discrete manifold consisting of four
units related inter se in the same way that the faces of a tetra-
hedron are related inter se; in that case, taking any arbitrarily
selected unit as a starting point, it is obvious that there are
three, and only three, immediate neighbors of that unit, and that
there is no neighbor at one remove.

There is also the discrete manifold the units of which
are related inter se in the same way as the faces of a cube. In
that case any arbitrarily selected unit has four, and only four,
immediate neighbors, one, and only one, neighbor at one remove,
and no neighbors at two removes.
