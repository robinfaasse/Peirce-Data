[u]II. Beta Part.[/u]

8. The beta part adds [del]certain[/del] [ins]to the alpha part,[/ins] signs to which new permissions are attached, while retaining all the alpha signs with the permissions attaching to them.

9. [del]A[/del] [ins]The[/ins] [u]line of identity[/u] [ins](also called a line of identity)[/ins] is a Graph [del]whose[/del] [ins]any[/ins] replica of which [del]is[/del] is a heavy line with two ends and without other topical singularity (such as a point of branching or a node), [del]that[/del] not in contact with any other sign except at its extremities. Otherwise, its shape and length are matters of indifference. All lines of identity are replicas of the same graph.

10. A [u]spot[/u] is a graph [del]whose[/del] any replica of which occupies a simple bounded portion of a surface, which portion has qualities distinguishing it from the replica of any other spot; and upon the boundary of [del]this portion[/del] surface occupied by the spot are certain points, called the [u]hooks[/u] of the spot, to each of which, if permitted, one extremity of one line of identity can be attached. Two lines [ins]of identity[/ins] cannot be attached to the same hook; nor can both ends of the same line.

11. Any indefinitely small dot may be a spot [ins]replica[/ins], called a spot [ins]in[/ins] of [u]identity[/u], and [del]several[/del] [ins]three[/ins] lines of identity may be attached to such a spot. Two lines of identity one outside a cut and the other on the area of the same cut may [del]have[/del] each [del]an[/del] extremity at the same point on the cut. The totality of all the lines of identity that join one another is termed a [u]ligature[/u]. A ligature is not generally a graph, since it may be part in one area and part in another. It is said to lie within any cut which it is wholly within.
