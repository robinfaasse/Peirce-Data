[u]Existential Graphs.[/u]
[u]Rules of Transformation.[/u]

[u]Pure Mathematical Definition of Existential
Graphs, regardless of their Interpretation.[/u]

[u]I, Alpha Part.[/u]

1. [u]The System of Existential Graphs[/u] is a certain class of diagrams
upon which it is permitted to operate certain transformations.

2. There is required a certain surface upon which it is prac-
ticable to scribe the diagrams and from which they can
be erased in whole or in part.

3. The whole of this surface except certain parts which may
be severed from it by "cuts" is termed [u]the sheet of assertion[/u].

4. A [u]graph[/u] is a [del]say[/del] legisign (i.e. a sign which is of the
nature of a general type) which is one of a certain class
of signs used in this system. A [u]graph-replica[/u] is any
individual instance of a graph. The sheet of assertion
itself is a graph-replica; [ins]and so is any part of it, being called the blank.[/ins] Other graph-replicas can be
scribed on the sheet of assertion, and when this is done the
graphs of which [del]these[/del] [ins]those[/ins] graph-replicas are [del]the[/del] instances
is said to be "scribed on the sheet of assertion"; [ins]and when a graph-replica is erased, the graph is said to be erased.[/ins] Two graphs
scribed on the sheet of assertion constitute one graph, of
which they are said to be [u]partial graphs[/u]. All that is at any
time scribed on the sheet of assertion is called the [del]entire[/del]
[del]scribed graph.[/del]
