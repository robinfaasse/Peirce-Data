four parts as follows:

1[ins]st[/ins], the Napoleonic generation, embracing all the men whose
greatness was developed between the beginning of the French
Republic and the Battle of Waterloo, [date]June 18, 1815[/date], — an age when
men had constantly before them new examples of momentous
and hasty decisions, leading to such half-cooked schemes as
the philosophies of Hegel, Fourier, etc.

2[ins]nd[/ins], the Metternich generation, embracing all the men whose
greatness was brought out between the Battle of Waterloo and the
Revolution of [date]Feb. 24, 1848[/date], — an age when laying hands upon
the established condition of things was so reprobated that men
either wrote novels or poetry, or devoted themselves to the purest of
pure science, or if they aimed at reforms, it was in some distant and
abstracted way, studying theories of government in general, or
political economy, or utilitarianism, or education, or at any
rate kept well within the bounds of legality or else plotted
secretly, but never spoke the language of Henry George or
the modern reformer, if they valued their lives.
