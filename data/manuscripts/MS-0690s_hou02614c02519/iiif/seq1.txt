Logic of Ancient History Note on Aristotle's Writings ([ins]except the [del]Athenian[/del] Constitution[/ins])

Since writing this paper, I have reëxamined all the works of Aristotle so far as
I could in a month, with a view to forming a general opinion, which I proceed to state,
without argument, as follows:
1[u]st[/u], the fragments are, generally speaking, genuine works of our Aristo-
tle, mostly published works.
2[u]nd[/u], all our works (exclusive of the Athenian Constitution, which I have not
read,) are those same [u]Opera Inedita[/u] published by Andronicus, and substantially
in his recension. The Politics is in an advanced preparation for publication; so are some
[ins]minor works.[/ins]
3[u]rd[/u], these works are mainly Aristotle's [u]ipsissima verba[/u], but with inser-
tion of many references, and injured in various ways.
4[u]th[/u], Of these works, some are note-books, of facts, of ideas, etc. A few,
like the Perihermeneias are early attempts before his doctrine was settled. Others
are abstracts of his lectures, put into brief syllabus statements, & published only
in the sense of being placed at the disposition of his scholars, who were not
to copy them, but could, and did, make abstracts of them. Some, while they
served this purpose, were written with the idea that they might be published
as books, after being revised. Generally, the works we have were put together by Andronicus.
Even the Politics is not a unit. Of course, the titles are by Andronicus.
5[u]th[/u], Aristotle was much given to restating his views, and in doing so, usu-
ally quoted his own definitions almost or quite verbatim, and varied the
manner of presentation more than the doctrine, which is almost perfectly
consistent, throughout, and shows that an immense amount of work had been
done before any of the philosophical writings, with a few exceptions, that
we have, were written.
6[u]th[/u], I am rather disposed to retract my idea of many transpositions
of sheets; because, the numerous cross-references, occasionally not very
intelligent, seem to have been inserted by his scholars & those of Theophrastus;
and therefore, if the leaves were not pasted together, they would, at least, be
numbered. Larger parts are manifestly often disordered. But they might be
separate rolls. I rather favor the loose sheet theory, however, for most of
them.
7[u]th[/u], I have pretty strong reasons for these opinions; but I am quite
sure that a minute study in the light of such ideas as those of this paper
would disclose and demonstrate much more.
