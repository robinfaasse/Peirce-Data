[u]4[sup]th[/sup][/u] Modal consequence or inference from a necessary proposition to
a contingent one. [u]5[sup]th[/sup][/u] Opposition or inference from the falsity of one pro-
position to the truth of its contradiction, & conversely. [u]6[sup]th[/sup][/u] Conversion or inference
from a proposition whose subject is A & predicate is B to another whose
subject is B and predicate is A. [u]7[sup]th[/sup][/u] Contraposition or the same change with the
predicate [del]in a negative form[/del] [ins]negated[/ins]. [u]8[sup]th[/sup][/u] Partial conversion or conversion of part
of the subject with the predicate or the reverse. Let us [del]examine[/del] [ins]analyze[/ins] each of
these so-called inferences.

[u]1. Subalternation[/u]. In this way, we infer from All A is B to some A is B.
But it seems to me that All A is B already declares that some A is B and
in the same way the specifying proposition always expresses a particular case,
Hence, I think, subalternation is [del]partial repetition[/del] [ins]restriction[/ins] but not inference.

[u]2. Æquipollence[/u]. In this way, we [del]infer[/del] [ins]conclude[/ins] from All A is B to No A is non-B.
This depends upon the fact that Non-non B is B and B is non-non B. These
are cases of the principle of identity, and [del]consequently[/del] [ins]otherwise[/ins] are the same as the original.
[del]Hence[/del] [ins]Therefore[/ins] Æquipollence is [del]not[/del] [ins]some[/ins] [del]inference[/del] [ins]sort of[/ins] [del]but[/del] [ins]repetition[/ins] [del]repetition[/del] [ins]but not inference[/ins].

[u]3. Opposition[/u]. In this way, we infer from the falsity of All A is B
to the truth of Some A is not B. This is a real inference. The premise is
that All A is B is false. This is equivalent to saying that the relation
expressed by All A is B does not exist. This relation is that there is no
A which is not B. To say this does not exist is to say there is some A
which is not B. This is a real inference.

[u]4. Modal consequence[/u]. In this way, we infer from A must be B to A
is B. I may be said that the necessary proposition contains the contingent one.
