In addition there are various books of exercises, more or less trivial, [del]and more or less calculated to inculcate machine made discussions, and consequently more or less dangerous to really sound reasoning.[/del] [ins]the ready logic of the English leader-writers, a vicious habit fatal to all living and healthy[/ins] I will not say, however, that a moderate use of such books is altogether useless. I would not absolutely condemn even Jevon's [u]Studies in Deductive Logic[/u] although the author shows in his own person how very unadroit a reasoner a man of real ability may be.

But neither reading books nor working exercises, — whether they be trivial or serious, — will suffice to develope the reasoning powers. An analytical method of proceedure is requisite which shall perfect one by one our performance of the different mental operations that enter into the business of inquiring. Now the mental operations concerned in reasoning are three. The first is [u]Observation[/u]; the second is [u]Experimentation[/u]; and the third is [u]Habituation[/u].
