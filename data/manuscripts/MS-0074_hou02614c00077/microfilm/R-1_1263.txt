[del]On the Number of Dichotomous Divisions; a problem
in permutations. By C. S. Peirce.[/del]

In the calculus of logic, a proposition is separated
by its copula, $\prec$, into two parts, as $A \prec B$. But these
parts may again be separated in like manner,
as $(A \prec B) \prec C$ and $A \prec (B \prec C)$, and so on indefinitely.
It becomes pertinent to inquire how many such
propositional forms with a given number of
copulas there are. The same problem [ins]presents itself[/ins]
in general algebra, where $\prec$ is replaced by
any non-associative sign of operation; and,
indeed, the question not infrequently arises;
but I do not know that the solution has been
given.
