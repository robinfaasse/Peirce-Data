two parts by a punctuation mark, and each part
into two parts by slighter [ins](or subordinate)[/ins] punctuation marks, and
so on, until the letters are all separated. Then,
if [u]n[/u] is the number of punctuations (or [u]n[/u] + 1 the
number of letters) the question is how many dis-
tinct forms of separation there are.

fection. 1[ins]st[/ins], we may obtain a rule for calculating

an equation of finite differences. 2[ins]nd[/ins], we
may obtain a general formula, which we
may be morally certain satisfies that equation.

between the former and the latter, so as to demon-
strate the formula.
