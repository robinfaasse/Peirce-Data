[u]Chapter II[/u] [u]Extension, Comprehension, Implication.[/u]

Logic is solely occupied with the reference of symbols [ins]in general[/ins] to their objects; but as these objects have three elements, matter, form, and accidents; logic must concern itself with the reference of the symbols to each of these.

To connote, [ins]for a symbol[/ins] is to refer to the [del]form[/del] [ins]form[/ins] of its object. To denote is to refer to the matter of its object. The quantity of the difference of a symbol from some other in [del]its connotation[/del] [ins]respect to what it connotes[/ins] is called its comprehension; the quantity of the difference of a symbol from some other in respect to what it denotes is called its extension. But extension & comprehension may be taken in five different senses.

Sense No 1. One symbol is more extensive than another when [del]it is true of more things[/del] [ins]the [del]qualities[/del] [ins]attributes[/ins] it connotes reside in[/ins]. One symbol is more comprehensive than another [del]when[/del] the things it denotes [del]have more qualities[/del] [ins]attributes[/ins] in common.
