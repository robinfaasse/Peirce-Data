[u]Phyllotactic Numbers[/u]

Write $\phi_m$ for the $m^{\text{th}}$ Phyllotactic number, and read "Phyl $m$."

(1) $\phi_0 = 0$

(2) $\phi_1 = 1$

(3) $\phi_{m+1} = \phi_m + \phi_{m-1}$

[u]Theorem[/u]. If it is possible to find any number $a$ such that

$\sum_i (-)^{A_i + a A} C_i \phi_{B_i + a} = 0$

and $\sum_i (-)^{A_i + (a+1)A} C_i \phi_{B_i + (a+1)} = 0$

then we shall have for every number $m$.

(4) $\sum_i (-)^{A_i + m A} C_i \phi_{B_i + m} = 0$

For successive additions and subtractions of the two given equations will, by (3), always yield (4).

Every proposition concerning phyllotactic numbers is a corollary from this theorem. For it is plain that this contains everything which can be deduced from (3).

[u]Corollary 1[/u]. From (1) we have
$\phi_0 + (-)^0 \phi_0 = 0$

Putting $m=1$ in (2) we have
$\phi_1 + (-)^1 \phi_{-1} = 0$

Whence
$\phi_m + (-)^m \phi_{-m} = 0$
