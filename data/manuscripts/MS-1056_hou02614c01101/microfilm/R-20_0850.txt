I propose now to prove that

(16) $v_{m+q} = v_{q+1} v_m + v_q v_{m-1}$

First if this holds for two successive values of $q$ it
holds for all values of $q$. For suppose the above holds
and also
$v_{m+q+1} = v_{q+2} v_m + v_{q+1} v_{m-1}$

Then the sum of these reduces to
$v_{m+q+2} = v_{q+3} v_m + v_{q+2} v_{m-1}$

But when $q=0$ and $q=1$ (16) becomes
$v_m = 1 \cdot v_m + 0 \cdot v_{m-1}$
$v_{m+1} = 1 \cdot v_m + 1 \cdot v_{m-1}$

both of which hold and therefore (16) always holds.

Let us now put $q=-p$ in (16) transpose and divide
by $v_m$
$\frac{v_{m-p} - v_{1-p} v_m}{v_m} = v_{-p} \frac{v_{m-1}}{v_m}$

This reduces to
(17) $\delta_m^p = v_{1-p} + v_{-p} \delta_m$

[marginalia]
$v_p = v_q v_r + R v_{p-1}$
$v_{p+1} = v_{q+1} v_r + R v_{p-1}$
[/marginalia]
