And if, not only,
$$\sigma_i = (-)^{i+1} \sigma_{-i}$$
but also
$$\sigma_{i-1} = (-)^i \sigma_{1-i}$$
Then the equation just got reduces to
$$\sigma_{i-2} = (-)^{i-1} \sigma_{2-i}$$
Consequently if [del]an[/del] equations of this form hold for two consecutive values of $i$ there is a similar equation for every value of $i$ further removed from zero. But from (1)
$$\sigma_0 = (-)^1 \sigma_0$$
and putting $m=0$ in (3b)
$$\sigma_1 = (-)^2 \sigma_{-1}$$
So that we have in general
$$(4) \quad \sigma_m + (-)^m \sigma_{-m} = 0$$

We may write (3b) in the form
$$\Delta \sigma_m = \sigma_{m-1}$$
$$(5) \quad \Delta^p \sigma_m = \sigma_{m-p}$$

Thence by (3b) we have
$$3^{\frac{d}{dm}} \sigma_m = (1+\Delta) \sigma_m = \sigma_{m+1} = \Delta^{-1} \sigma_m$$
$$(6) \quad (1+\Delta)^p \sigma_m = \Delta^{-p} \sigma_m$$
