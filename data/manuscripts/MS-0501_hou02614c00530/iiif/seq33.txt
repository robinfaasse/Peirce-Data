The term “non-scriptible,” which the notation had not
afforded us any means of writing, is of such a charac-
ter that as soon as it is admitted among the number
of signs there [del]is[/del] [ins]comes to be[/ins] something non-scriptible. Before that
was done, [del]it[/del] [ins]the axiom[/ins] was not demonstrable. It therefore involves
a new premiss, which is, strictly speaking that it is
possible to invent a non-scriptible sign. Giving that
its objective expression it becomes “Something is
non-scriptible,” which ought therefore to be accepted
as an axiom, the “proof” of which, though formally
necessary, involves a [u]petitio principii[/u].
