Axiom. Something is scriptible.
Scholium. Consider the following argument: By
Corollary 1, $a \widehat{C} a$ is scriptible. Ergo, Something is scriptible.
This is unexceptionable reasoning. Yet since Corollary I
is based exclusively upon a definition, it must not be
understood to assert, or imply, the existence of anything;
while the Axiom does distinctly assert existence.
Corollary I does assert, or imply, that $a \widehat{C} a$ is scriptible.
It simply writes $a \widehat{C} a$; and then the reasoner [u]observes[/u]
that $a \widehat{C} a$ [u]is[/u] written. When the argument says, 'By
Corollary 1, $a \widehat{C} a$ is scriptible,' this is true in the sense
of 'By [del]the[/del] observation of Corollary 1, etc.' This does not
affect the validity of the argument. For if a reasoner
