Every axiom, therefore, is essentially capable of proof, provided anybody can be found who does not already see the truth of it. But it is quite true that there can be no real proof of what is not doubted; and therefore, so far as an axiom is self-evident, Aristotle is quite right in calling it indemonstrable. But it is possible that a man should be in a state of mind in which he cannot apprehend the meaning of an axiom; and for him it may be proved.
