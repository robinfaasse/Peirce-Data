Since it asserts existence, cannot be implied in any
definition. It is not asserted or implied in the defini-
tion, or in Corollary I, if this is properly understood. It
is true, that we cannot [del]make[/del] [ins]assert[/ins] Corollary I without asserting,
or implying, that a Cia is scriptible. This, however, is
not the meaning, or any part of the meaning, of Corollary
I. So any clause of a definition, being observed, is
seen to be asserted to be true; yet the existence of the
definition is no part of what the definition means.
That something is scriptible is [del]derived from[/del] [ins]an[/ins] observation; but it
is so evident that it requires a somewhat subtle thought
to perceive that it is a distinct observation. It is therefore
eminently fit to be called an axiom.
