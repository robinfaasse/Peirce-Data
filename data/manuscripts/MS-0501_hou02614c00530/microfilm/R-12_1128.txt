[u]Corollary 8[/u]. If [u]i is then e[/u] = [u]i Ĉ e[/u]. For assume, first
that i and e are any such things that if i is then e. Then, [ins]since[/ins]
by [u]Clause 2 of the Definition of Ĉ[/u], Either i or i Ĉ e, it
follows that either e or i Ĉ e. But, by [u]Clause 1 of the same[/u]
[u]Definition[/u], if e then i Ĉ e. Hence e. Thus it is proved that
(No 1) Supposing that if i is then e, it follows that i Ĉ e.

Secondly, assume that i and e are any such things that
i Ĉ e. Then, by [u]Clause 3 of the Definition of Ĉ[/u], it follows
that if i, then e. Thus it is proved that, whatever i and e may be
(No 2) Supposing that i Ĉ e, it follows that if i, then e.

From (No 1) and (No 2), taken together with [u]Clause 3 of the[/u]
[u]Definition of =[/u], the corollary follows.
