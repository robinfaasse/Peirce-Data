[pn]7[/pn] [u]Theorem I.[/u] If, whatever $x$ may be, $x \hat{C} e$, then $e$.
[u]Proof.[/u] For if, whatever $x$ may be, $x \hat{C} e$, then, putting $a \hat{C} a$ for $x$, $(a \hat{C} a) \hat{C} e$ will be scriptible. Whence, by Clause 3, putting $b \hat{C} a$ for $i$,
$b \hat{C} a$ and $(a \hat{C} a) \hat{C} e$, then $e$.
Whence, by Corollary 1, $e$ will be scriptible. Q.E.D.

[pn]4[/pn] [del]Corollary 1.[/del] [ins]Corollary 4.[/ins] By Corollary 1, something is scriptible. Indeed, either clause of the definition, since it holds good, is scriptible.

[u]Definition of $\gamma$.[/u]

[u]Scholium.[/u] It can neither be proved nor disproved that anything is not scriptible. The following sign therefore can never be written by itself on the sheet of assertion.

[u]Definition of $\bigcirc$.[/u]
Clause 1. Whatever $a$ may be
$\bigcirc \hat{C} a$
Clause 2. If, whatever $x$ may be, $x$ is scriptible, $\bigcirc$ is scriptible.
