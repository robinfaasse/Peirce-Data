[u]Logic — Chapter 2.[/u]
[u]Of Inquiry.[/u]

The irritation of doubt causes a struggle to attain a state of belief. This struggle I shall term [u]inquiry[/u], though it must be admitted that [del]it[/del] [ins]this[/ins] is sometimes not a very apt designation.

The irritation of doubt is the only immediate motive for the struggle to attain belief. It is certainly best, [ins]for us,[/ins] that our beliefs should be such as may truly guide our actions so as to satisfy our desires; and this reflection will make us reject any belief which does not seem [del]to[/del] to have been so formed as to insure this result. But it will only do so by creating a doubt in place of that belief. With the doubt, therefore, the struggle begins and with the cessation of doubt it ends.
