Let $A, B, C,$ etc denote all the individual
things in the universe. Let $m$ be a general
term, as 'man'. Then $m$ is an aggregate
of some of the letters $A, B, C,$ etc. This [ins]each[/ins] may
be represented by writing
$m = (m)_a A + (m)_b B + (m)_c C + \text{etc} = \Sigma_i (m)_i I$
Here, $(m)_a$ etc are numerical coefficients which
are [del]each of them[/del] [ins]each of them[/ins] [del]either[/del] zero or unity, according as the individu-
al which [del]they[/del] it multiplies is excluded from
or included under the class $m$.

For ordinary [ins](nonquantitative)[/ins] problems of logic, it is
unnecessary and inconvenient to [del]did[/del] insist
upon the coefficient which is the sign of ex-
istence being exactly unity. It is better to allow
it to take any value greater than zero; and
all values greater than zero are to be consi-
dered as equivalent, so that to say that
two coefficients are equal shall only mean
that if either vanishes the other does likewise.
