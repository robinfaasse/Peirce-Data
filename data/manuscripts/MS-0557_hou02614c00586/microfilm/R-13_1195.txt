The [u]converse[/u] of a relative may be defined as follows:—
$(\breve{l})_{ij} = (l)_{ji}$
From this it obviously follows that
$\breve{l\,s} = \breve{s}\,\breve{l}$
$\breve{l\dagger s} = \breve{s}\dagger\breve{l}$.

The [u]negative[/u] may be defined as follows:—
$(\overline{l})_{ij} = 0^{(l)_{ji}}$
it being understood that $0^0 = 1$. From this we easily get
$\overline{l\,s} = \overline{l}\dagger\overline{s}$
$\overline{l\dagger s} = \overline{l}\,\overline{s}$.

The two operations combined give
$\overline{\breve{l\,s}} = \overline{\breve{s}}\dagger\overline{\breve{l}}$
$\overline{\breve{l\dagger s}} = \overline{\breve{s}}\,\overline{\breve{l}}$.

Suppose we have the proposition that every lover of a servant of is [del]a[/del] [ins]merely[/ins] benefactor of or $l\,s \prec b$. This implies [del]that[/del] if $(b)_{ik}$ vanishes then $(l\,s)_{ik}$ vanishes, and consequently that for every individual, $j$,
either $(l)_{ij} = 0$ or $(s)_{jk} = 0$.
