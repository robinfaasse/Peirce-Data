Relative terms are divisible into such as contain no individual [del]relatives[/del] [ins]relations[/ins] of the form A:A, such as contain some such individuals and exclude some, and such as exclude all such individuals. [del]The first class[/del] They are also divisible into such as contain no individual [del]relatives[/del] [ins]relations[/ins] of the form A:B, such as contain some such individuals and exclude some, and such as exclude no such individuals. There are thus nine classes. But four of these consist of only one relative each. Namely, the relative 0 is the only one which excludes all individuals of both forms, and $\infty$ is the only one which includes all of both forms. The relative 'identical with' — [u]1[/u], is the only one which includes all of the form A:A and excludes all of the form A:B; and 'not' or 'other than' — [u]n[/u], is the only one which excludes all of the form A:A and includes all of the form A:B.
