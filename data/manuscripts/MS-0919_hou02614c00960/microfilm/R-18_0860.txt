[del]13.[/del] Before passing to [del]$\theta$[/del]. Let us consider the above 11 in-
stances. $\epsilon$, $\eta$, $\vartheta$, [del]$\iota$[/del], $\kappa$, $\lambda$ are artificial. $\iota$ is historical
$\beta$, $\gamma$ are subjective $\alpha$, $\delta$, $\zeta$ are natural.

Of the artificial subjects, the idea of $\epsilon$ and $\eta$, was simply
the final cause, expressed [del]as[/del] a predicate of these subjects.
If anything was added thereto, it was owing to the fact that music
may be made without sonorous vibrations in the instrument it-
self; and if this be so, then the idea was stated too narrowly.

The idea of $\vartheta$, was stated as merely the final cause, without
being expressed as a predicate [del]to[/del] thereof. This seems to be a
defect in the statement, but I feared to attempt to improve
it in this respect for fear of limiting the statement to some
particular kind of computing scale.

Why did I say sonorous vibrations neither embracing the whole scale
of vibrations [del]nor[/del] limiting myself to stringed or discrete instru-
ments and why did I limit myself to measurements of numbers
and yet not to logarithmic measurements?

To the first of these questions the answer is perhaps not difficult.

nature of man & thus to improve the condition of the universe. But
