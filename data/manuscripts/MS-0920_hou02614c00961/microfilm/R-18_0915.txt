[u]§ 3. On Faith.[/u] — A. Need of It.

I shall [del]now[/del] show

[u]α[/u]. that the Transcendentalists conclude with a return to Faith.
[u]β[/u]. that the use they make of it in their own procedure is the source of all that is valuable in their investigations.
[u]γ[/u]. that while their own faith is necessarily blind, their reasoning is not so close as to leave no room for demonstrably trust-worthy faith.

[u]I. Kant's Work[/u].

An inference is involved in every cognition

[u]Proof[/u]. Relative cognition is the recognition of our relations to things.

All cognition of objects is relative, that is we know things only in their relation to us.

Every cognition must have an object (the subject of the proposition)

The faculties whereby we become conscious of our relations to things are known as perceptions or sense

∴ Every cognition contains a sensual element.

Now the information of mere sensation is a chaotic manifold, while every cognition must be brought into the unity of one thought.
