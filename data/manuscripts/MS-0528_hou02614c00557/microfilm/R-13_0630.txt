Of formulae of four terms without a parenthesis in $v$ and
$f$ the only one that is false is $v -: v -: v -: f$. This follows
so obviously from the falsity of $v -: v -: f$ that it leads
to nothing worth mention, except that in every formula
without parenthesis having any number of terms all the
terms except the last can be transposed at will. Of for-
mulae of the form $a -: (b -: c) -: d$ there are three that are
false, viz:-
$$v -: (f -: f) -: f \quad v -: (f -: v) -: f \quad v -: (v -: v) -: f.$$
We at once see that the formula
$$a -: (a -: b) -: b$$
is necessarily true. Of formulae of the form $((a -: b) -: c) -: d$,
in $v$ and $f$, the following are false:
$$((v -: f) -: f) -: f \quad ((v -: f -: [del]v[/del][ins]f[/ins]) -: f \quad (f -: f -: [del]v[/del][ins]f[/ins]) -: f \quad ((f -: v) -: v) -: f$$
$$((v -: v) -: v) -: f.$$
These yield the necessary formula
$$((a -: b) -: a) -: a.$$
