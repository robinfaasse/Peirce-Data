The first system of relationship that logic studies
is that of an indefinite collection of disconnected [del]objects[/del] [ins]units[/ins]. It may be represented by this schema.

[fig]Row of ten dots[/fig]

These objects constitute the universe. To them are attached
in various combinations conventional signs, for which let-
ters may be used. Thus, using the schema, under some of
the dots the letter a may be written, under some (whether
the same or others) the letter b, and so forth in any arrange-
ment whatever. Speaking now of a single individual, we
write a to signify that it has the letter a written under
it, or to use a briefer locution, that it is a. In order
to signify that if it is a it is b, we write a -: b. We have
now to study the algebra of this symbol -:, which I term
the copula of inclusion, or more briefly the copula. For
