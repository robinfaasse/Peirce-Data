His definition of it rests upon the definition of a [u]Salt[/u], as a kind of body which cannot conduct electricity in the least degree without being decomposed into a [del]cathode[/del] [ins]cation[/ins] element, which is either a metal or hydrogen, and an anion residue, which may be a haloid element, i.e. either F, Cl, Br, or I. In two salts which have for one ion exactly the same chemical species, the amounts of the other ion which are combined with any given amount of the like ion are said to be [u]equivalent[/u]; and there are several other ways of determining chemically equivalent quantities in full accordance with this method of electrolysis. There are besides ways of ascertaining what proportions of different elements contain equal multitudes of atoms: such proportions are precisely what we mean by "Atomic Weights."
