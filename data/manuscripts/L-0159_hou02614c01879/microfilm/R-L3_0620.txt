The Nation, 208 Broadway (P. O. Box 794),

New York, [date]Nov. 10. 1902[/date]

My dear Peirce:

In a well-ordered newspaper
there should undoubtedly be a con-
tinuity in reporting, but your last
report of the Nat. Academy was
an innovation, & might or might
not set a precedent. However, the
Post deemed this time, as I have
wired you, content with [del]Associated[/del]
Press dispatch, far below yours as
it must be.

As view of the coal famine, I
confess I am glad to think of you
& Mrs. Peirce as not passing a
shivering winter where you are
now. But whither?
