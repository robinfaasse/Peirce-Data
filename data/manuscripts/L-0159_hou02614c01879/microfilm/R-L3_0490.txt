My dear Garrison

I wonder whether the [u]Nation[/u] ever noticed a little book published last year called [u]Verbes français demandant des prépositions[/u], par Mme. Darr. I came across it yesterday & looking over it found what was in it excellent French, but without Dictionary or Grammar I could pick up about four omissions per page. With Littré no doubt I could multiply the omissions many times. I will just try my hand on a few pages now, taking the letter C, which I have it examined. It fills 6 pages, so I shall try to find two dozen omissions.

1. [u]Céder[/u]. The verb is omitted, and consequently [u]Céder le pas à quelqu'un[/u]

2. [u]Changer[/u]. The authoress only gives "[u]Il a changé tout cela[/u]" and "[u]Il a changé de vêtements[/u]." She omits "[u]L'eau fut changée en vin[/u]."

3. [u]Charger[/u]. She only gives: "[u]Chargez ce cheval[/u]," "[u]Le ciel est chargé de nuages[/u]," "[u]Je me charge de cette affaire[/u]." She omits "[u]Je me charge de faire tout cela[/u]," and "[u]Le roi m'a chargé de le faire[/u]."

4. [u]Chercher[/u]. She omits the verb entirely & consequently "[u]Chercher à ne rien omettre[/u]"

5. [u]Choisir[/u]. She omits the verb which takes [u]de[/u] with infinitive.

6. [u]Commander[/u]. She omits the verb. "[u]Commander à quelqu'un de faire quelque chose[/u]"

7. [u]Commencer[/u]. She only gives "J'ai commencé mes leçons" and "j'ai commencé à acheter mon trousseau." She omits "[u]Louis XIV commença de regner en l'an 1643[/u]" Since there is a difference of meaning the omission is more important.

8 [u]Comparer[/u]. She only gives "[u]L'expert a comparé les deux écritures[/u]" and "[u]Il se compare à Napoléon[/u]." She omits [u]comparer à[/u] without the reflexive and also [u]comparer avec[/u].
