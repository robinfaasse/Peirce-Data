[u]To the Editor of the Nation[/u]:

[u]Sir[/u]: Professor Newcomb is, as usual, ready with his impressions of the definitions in the [u]Century Dictionary[/u]. As four of these which he blames are within my department of the work, I beg leave to show how such definitions were prepared. I cannot reply [del]to his criticisms[/del] in detail, [ins]1[u]st[/u],[/ins] because, except in one instance, he does not specify his objections, and 2[u]nd[/u], because I have received no copy of the published part and am unable to refer to the text of the definitions. I will take up the four words and phrases in alphabetical order.

1. [u]Law of action and reaction[/u]. I never needlessly depended on a secondary authority. Consequently, this definition was [del]drawn[/del] [ins]deduced[/ins] from [ins]the well-known passage in[/ins] Newton's [u]Principia[/u]; but as the precise meaning [del]is under[/del] has been the subject of recent discussions, these were carefully considered.
