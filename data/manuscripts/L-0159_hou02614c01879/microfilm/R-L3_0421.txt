I have observed if you would
account largely for your failure
to make a permanent place for
yourself. It appears in your
preference of a dozen whims
to your profession, and in your
late advertisement for private pupils
when you are in no condition to
carry out your offers. Far be
it from me to reproach you for
what you were born with, but
if you could become conscious of
it, the world would perhaps
not seem so entirely to merit your
reproaches.

You appear to have communicated
to me your intention, in a certain
contingency, to make away with your-
self. Such confidences belong to
your kindred, if you have any. No
incident has been known
to go cheerfully to prison. Two of
my ancestors lost their lives in
and are well remembered for it.

I wish you better times
with better health.

Sincerely yours,
Wendell P. Garrison
