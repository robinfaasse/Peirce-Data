Then, your tremendous praise of
the Germans arouses my incredulity
because Leland is not a first-
rate man in anything, not even
as a prose writer; he covers too
many fields to be profound;
thorough in any; he has been too
much of a rover to establish a
solid reputation. For all this, I
admit, he may be very entertain-
ing, but I cannot believe that
his work deserves to be mentioned in
the same breath with Rousseau or
Coleridge, because the prose itself is
so much inferior.
