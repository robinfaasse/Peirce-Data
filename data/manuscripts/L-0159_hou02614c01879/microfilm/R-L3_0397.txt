The Nation, 208 Broadway (P. O. Box 794),

New York, Aug. 20. [date]1894[/date]

My dear Peirce:

You ask for more books, but you are putting it out of my power to send you any more. It is not possible for me to keep along with a contributor who accompanies every notice and every proof with a controversy involving my editorial — I will not say capacity (for I never boast of that) but prerogative. This and reviews are wanting for that. I do not sit in judgment, with my blue pencil, on anything

[page break]

here indeed [del]me[/del] to give you the work I have done. Whether the Nation is all wrong in drawing the line as it does, or whether you lack the power to adjust yourself to its limitations, others may decide. My books show that I have printed a large amount of matter from your pen during the past year or two, and much of it with very little alteration. You accuse me of having uniformly suppressed your best. If this be so, then I [del]re[/del] [ins]re[/ins]gret your best is not for me.

Yours faithfully
W. P. Garrison
