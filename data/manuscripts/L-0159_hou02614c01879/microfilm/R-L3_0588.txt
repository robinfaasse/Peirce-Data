To the Editor of The Nation,

Sir: Is the phrase "psychological moment," in an edito-
rial article on page 478 of your issue of June 19, used
advisedly? The phrase is a [del]common one[/del], but is it in your
opinion a good one? Psychology is the science of the mind.
That name can never signify the mind itself, because the
termination is from [greek]λόγος[/greek], word or discourse. "[del]Psycholog-[/del]
ical," for the same reason, should never mean, pertaining to
the mind, but always, pertaining to the science of the mind.
The moment when men's minds are prepared to be easily influ-
enced in a desired way is not psychological. Why not call it
the psychical moment?

Respectfully yours,
J. C. Allen.

Yarmouth, Maine, [date]June 22, 1902[/date].
