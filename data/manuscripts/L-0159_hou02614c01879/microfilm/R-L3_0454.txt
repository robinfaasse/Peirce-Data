Holman says he is also willing "to submit to scientific arbitration" his views about the non-Euclidean geometry, [ins]—[/ins] that is, "relative and absolute views of motion, etc." in opposition to mine. As before, I think his best way is to let the terrestrial ball jog on without disturbing it. Probably the world will either accept his views, which are widely current in Germany, or else will accept mine, which are necessarily those of the student of the foundations of the physical sciences. All he has got to do is to present his views with energy & leave me to fulfil my duty as I see it. At the same time, I do not think that if there were any sense [del]I am going[/del] before a scientific jury with scientific questions, that his ideas would be likely to prevail after my arguments had been heard.

K. Holman [del]and also[/del] says he "sedulously avoids" "metaphysical inquiry." So he does, and with the usual result of swallowing un-masticated a great quantity of crude metaphysical doctrine that he does not recognize as such.

L. Holman says [del]I[/del] [ins]I[/ins] "make it appear" that "the effort of the book" is "to discuss terms in themselves." I deny that make that appear. I make it appear the effort of the book is mainly to revise & improve the conceptions of physics, which in my opinion has been done stupidly and without sufficient discrimination between questions of words & questions of fact.

I have not said there was no [ins]a[/ins] room for difference of opinion and for scientific discussion" about mass and weight, goodness knows! It is the [del]the[/del] favorite pastime of cloudy headed physicists to talk about those things.
