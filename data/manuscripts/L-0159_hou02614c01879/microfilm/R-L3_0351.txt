The Nation, 208 Broadway (P. O. Box 794),

New York, [date]Jan. 30 1894[/date]

My dear Peirce:

I never wrote that you were not trying to reduce your indebtedness, but that it was not being reduced; & while I am not your keeper, it seemed to me that you were needlessly incurring fresh charges, as this was to be done through me I had, of course, some right to counsel with you.

As regards the review of Funk, I never was so presumptuous as to question your ability to judge, renew knowing that you had the apparatus for comparison at hand. In this light you were an useful man de Milford who would have been the fittest man in N. Y.

I solve the difficulty by lending you our Debts, & you can return it to us at our expense. & with no charge against you.

Be assured that your straits are never out of mind, & that I regret my own closeness of living as inca-pacitating me from rendering any personal assistance.

As fixing plagiarism from the Century upon Funk, you should not forget that Dr. Murray was very indignant with the Century for appro-priating his labors. Sincerely Yours,
W. P. G.
