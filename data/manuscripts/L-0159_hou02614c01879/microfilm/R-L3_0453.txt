Let him say so.

G. Holman says I have mistaken the "purpose" of the Le Sage theory of gravitation as well as that of the Vortex atom theory. I say, on the contrary, that I am backed up by history in my idea that both are due to a desire to get rid of action at a distance. But I will wait till he offers some proof of his assertion before I go into the evidences.

H. Holman says I have given an incorrect impression of the influence of these theories in the book. They occupy rather more than a third of the volume, and I do not remember that I said anything to imply they were very prominent.

I. Holman says he is willing to submit his opinion of action at a distance and its relation to energy to "scientific arbitration." The only scientific arbitration concerning scientific views which I am disposed to recognize is the survival of the fittest. But I think I can state the matter of the relation between action at a distance and the doctrine of energy so that every competent person will admit it would have satisfied Helmholtz who was the chief exponent, if not discoverer, of the doctrine of the conservation of energy. In point of fact Helmholtz's great memoir on the subject is nothing but the demonstration that that great law is nothing but [del]the impression of[/del] another expression of the proposition that all forces are attractions & repulsions. Look at Lagrange's [u]Mecanique analitique[/u] which is based on virtual velocities, which is substantially the law of energy. Everything is done by continuous variations of the energy, in that book. Where there is no continuity you can do nothing with the principle of energy other it loses all real significance. Now if it is only when forces act at a distance, that continuous variations with the distance are possible.

However, I will not [ins]now[/ins] pursue this discussion, in which I have a great deal more to say.
