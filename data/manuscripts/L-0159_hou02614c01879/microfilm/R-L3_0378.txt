This is why the violin is so rich, and the

Now it is a most curious fact that of all the
elements the spectrum of hydrogen alone shows
its lines in the relation of exact har-
monies, one to the other, carried far be-
yond the limit of vision, and doubtless far
below its red line, into the dark.

So when a current of electricity, with its
own vibratory index strikes a column
of hydrogen, as narrated in the water-pail
experiment, in my book, under compression,
it throws this gas into harmonic vibrations
coincident therewith, and acts as a true
electrical transformer, but instead of
merely doubling the vibrations, it runs them
up into heat and light. A solution of
sulphate of quinine, I have shown, will
run them down in the same way.
