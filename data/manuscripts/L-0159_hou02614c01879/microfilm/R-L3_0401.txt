Your account I will try to send
you in the course of the week, I
have but one [del]obet.[/del] [ins]obit.[/ins] of yours remaining.
If that turns out well, it will be
so much to the fore, & I hope it will.

My long silence was not inten-
tional in the beginning, but it came
about naturally, & I concluded that
the practical thing to do was to
print your store of notices as rap-
idly as I could, & then make a new
footing. The more you write (so long
as I advance anything) the deeper
we seem to get into the mire.

I am, as always, extremely sorry
for your straits, but I have stretched
my privilege to an extreme in striving
to lend you a helping hand. It may
be we can arrange a system of
half pay, or there can be some
obituaries to prepare.

There is no sign of Funk's Diction-
ary, but I had not expected it pub-
lished yet (Vol. 2).

Yours faithfully,
W. P. Garrison
