My dear Garrison:

I hasten to reply to Holman's communication which has this moment reached me. I have lettered his points in his margin & reply to them hastily herein in the order he has selected. If any notice is to be taken of his communication, you will please return his paper to me.

A. Of the eleven physicists named, I only know what one has [del]really[/del] [ins]really[/ins] said. That was Mendenhall.

Some of them I know are not very clear about these matters. Mendenhall's stupidity is gross. Some others I have found tripping. About none of the others do I positively know they are good authorities on such points, though there are several that I should suppose would be.

As many of them as knew about Holman's state of health would make their criticisms as tender as possible, as I myself should have done had I known. But I am glad I did not know.

I am particularly competent to speak of such matters & of course, no amount of authority could alter my opinion.

B. The idea of my leaving an erroneous impression by design is absurd. I never heard of the man, & all I knew of him I learned from his book.

If I have left an erroneous impression about his use of the word gravity, it is only by not noticing his inconsistencies for the cases in which his expressions give rise to suspicion that he blunders about the facts. I will return to this under M.
