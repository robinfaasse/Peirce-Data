German notions of reasoning are
for the most part very crude. They
seem to think, for example; that it
is a mark of superior logicality
to make a great and signal
distinction in every case between
what is positively proved and
what is not so, even as regards
questions of experience. In
fact, such a habit, — as I need
scarcely say, — [del]as I need not[/del]
[del]say,[/del] but that some Readers
may be ill taught, — is of course
on the contrary a mark of
inexactitude of thought,
since in point of fact there
is no such hard and fast
lines between what is known
