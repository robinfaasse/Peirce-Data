Dear Sir:

I venture to suggest an addition for a future edition of Illustrations of Logic.

The denials of equivalent propositions are equivalent.

The propositions ‘A is true’ and ‘This [ins]very[/ins] writing asserts that A is true’ are equivalent.

[fig]three dots in a triangle[/fig] The propositions ‘A is false’ and ‘This writing does not assert that A is true’ are equivalent.

The propositions ‘A is true’ and ‘A is true and I say so’ are [del]true[/del] equivalent.

[fig]three dots in a triangle[/fig] The propositions ‘A is false’ and ‘Either A is false or I do not say it is true’ are equivalent.

The propositions ‘A is true’ and ‘I know that A is true’ are equivalent.

[fig]three dots in a triangle[/fig] The propositions ‘A is false’ and ‘I do not know that A is true’ are equivalent.

The propositions [del]that[/del] ‘I know that A is true’ and ‘Every man who knows all I do and much more [del]would[/del] knows that A is true’ are equivalent.

[fig]three dots in a triangle[/fig] The propositions ‘I do not know that A is true’ and ‘Some man who knows much more than I does not that A is true’ are equivalent.

The propositions [del]‘I know that lead can be transmuted to gold’ and ‘the chemist knows lead can be transmuted to gold’ are equivalent[/del] [ins]‘I know that lead can be transmuted to gold’ and ‘the perfect chemist knows lead can be transmuted to gold’ are equivalent[/ins] [fig]three dots in a triangle[/fig] the prop. ‘I do not know’ and ‘the perfect chemist does not know’ are equivalent.

very truly C. S. Peirce
