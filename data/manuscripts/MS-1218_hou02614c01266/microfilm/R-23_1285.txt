But in 4 cases there
should be no final comma.
1[sup]st[/sup], when and, or, or nor precedes
the last term of the series,
2[sup]d[/sup], when the comma would
separate the last term from
the word which it qualifies.
3[sup]d[/sup] When in a series of governing
words a conjunction precedes
the last of them.
4[sup]th[/sup] When a series of governing
words governs a monosyllable
only.

There is also a rule about
two or more phrases each
having two [del]coupled[/del] words
coupled in one and the same
way. Namely
1[sup]st[/sup], when these stand in the
same relation of signification
to the rest of the sentence, a
comma should follow each
couple,
2[sup]d[/sup], when the last pair has
a complement not belonging
to the others, no comma must
separate it from its complement.
