That is, if $x$ is true (of the individual spoken of)
$y$ is true. So that
$$\begin{array}{c}
x \prec y \\
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{is true} & \text{is false} \\
\text{when} & \text{when} \\
x = \mathfrak{f}\ \ y = \mathfrak{f}; & x = \mathfrak{v}\ \ y = \mathfrak{f}. \\
x = \mathfrak{f}\ \ y = \mathfrak{v}; & \\
x = \mathfrak{v}\ \ y = \mathfrak{v}. & 
\end{array}
\end{array}$$
Hence the formula $x \prec x$ is necessarily true.
In writing formulae containing the copula $\prec$ several
times, let it be understood that parentheses ex-
tending to the end of the formula or to the end
of an enclosing parenthesis are to be omitted.
Thus,
$$x \prec y \prec z = x \prec (y \prec z)$$
$$(x \prec y \prec z) \prec w = (x \prec (y \prec z)) \prec w.$$

It is plain that $x \prec y \prec z$ is false only if $x = \mathfrak{v}$
$(y \prec z) = \mathfrak{f}$, that is only if $x = \mathfrak{v}$, $y = \mathfrak{v}$, $z = \mathfrak{f}$;
and so for any formula written without
parentheses. Hence, the truth of such a for-
mula is not affected by the transposition
of any letters except the last. Also, any
such formula containing an $\mathfrak{f}$ in any place but
the last is true.
