My dear William:

I thank you greatly for your lot of articles. I have read the French one, which is perfectly clear. When you write in English I am seldom sure of understanding you. But even an approximate adherence to the rules of French rhetoric compels a absolute perspicuity. I agree to every word of the article excepting only your thinking that it is a novel doctrine. It is nothing in the world but the doctrine of [u]Immediate Perception[/u] held not only by Reid but also by Kant in his refutation of Berkeley. You will find if you look over my articles that I never talk about "egotistic" or "subjective" in [del]vow[/del]

P.S. I am very unhappy today for various reasons; but largely because I am not to have the opportunity of presenting my Logic in outline at that summer school. My only comfort is in religion. I am a theist, — not a pagan like you. It is a religion, organism [del]This is[/del] to my mind little better than none. This finite god of yours must have rivals no doubt [ins]Exist only consist in reaction[/ins] It must be a whole genus. And they have, I suppose, no religion at all. They are matched atheists with no ideals. The true theist's God is a being whom we can only vaguely know; that His essence is reality. The Ideal, — the [u]Is[/u]. When we attempt to think out what He is, we fall into contradictions because He is above human comprehension. But nevertheless we can in some [del]what[/del] approach a comprehension. That is the God that we can love and adore, and this loving adoration [ins]which is not useless but creation[/ins] is the best proof of His Being. How finite gods may be very proper [fig]circle[/fig] to fly but they are creatures to criticize. The true Ideal is the living [del]Powers[/del] [ins]loving[/ins].

To think of the true theist's God is real balm to the heart. It comforts one for one's own shortcomings.
