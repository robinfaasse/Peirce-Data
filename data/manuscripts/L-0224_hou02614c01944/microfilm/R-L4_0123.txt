P.O. Milford Pa [date]1904 Sep 29[/date]

My dear William:

What I have to communicate in this letter, will have not the slightest interest from any point of view. Skip to the interesting part that is the signature. I am dead with overwork. My wife is lying down, done up. I tried to read a book but found I could not grace myself to the exertion requisite for Edmund Lear's Poems. I might read Paracelsus because I know that Browning always wrote what would sound well and that there is no use trying to scan him. Louis Stevenson, the immortal Samoan, tells us that the whole art of writing is in knowing what to omit. That puts him in harmony with his reader because the whole art of reading consists in skipping what the author has omitted to omit. I have often taken to heart the many admonitions that I have received from my dearest friends that what I write is perfectly lovely, but that nobody can understand a word of it. Such in particular from the rescuer of psychology from Wundtian supersensationalism. Now of late I have had occasion to dip into some books of psychology. Höffding whom I am persuaded is great is not in my library(!) Nor Wundt. Nor Dewey. But I have been reading Sully, Hamilton, Stout, W. D. Wilson, Cornelius, Bascom, Volkmann von Volkmar, Lotze, Murray, Rogers, Maher, Külpe, Ribot, Bain, Stratton, Spencer, Scripture, Hartley, Calkins, Baldwin, Gay, Granger, J. Mill, and others of that ilk. I pick one up. I read what he says on a given topic. Nothing
