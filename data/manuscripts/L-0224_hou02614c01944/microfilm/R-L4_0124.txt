I have also been straining my intellects trying to learn something about physical science from the great writers on the subject, — Dolbear, Holman, Hoffman, etc. I remark that these physicists, — with K. Pearson in their wake, — have discovered that if you analyze physical phenomena to their smallest parts, you discover there nothing but Matter & Motion, no matter to how great a degree of minuteness they go. They seem to me sceptical in the extreme. At least they did until I found the answer to my [del]why[/del] question What about adaptations, what about triadic truths generally; do you deny them? I found they did not deny them, but that a minute analysis of physical phenomena proved that all these things were psychical, true, but psychical. That carried me to the psychologists. Psychology I thought was the science of mind. It is so I believe. But it is just the same thing to say that it is the science of feeling. I was rather appalled at this at first. What I said Do you make of triadic truths? Oh, they are all right only they are the cerebral connections, purely physical. The minutest analysis of psychical phenomena puts this beyond all doubt. I always thought that if consciousness was anything more than an epiphenomenon, a sort of reporter for the Home Journal, it was in that it helped in selfcontrol. Now the principal subjects of control are thoughts. But it turns out that thought is altogether a matter of cerebral connections, a physical affair purely. The physicists however by a still more trustworthy analysis of physical phenomena find that thought if anything at all is purely a psychical affair.
