P.O. Milford Pa
[date]1904 September 29[/date]

My dear William:

There will be nothing of the slightest interest in this letter from any point of view, and if you skip from here to the interesting signature you will do well. I am simply done up with over work. My wife is lying down exhausted or I might play a game of backgammon. I cannot make the exertion necessary to read Edmund Lear's poems, and therefore I will simply waste a sheet of paper in making-believe to write a letter which I trust I may not be such a fool as to send. I was duly impressed by your many admonitions that what I write is very obscure. I flatter myself the obscurity does not lie in the writing but in the vast thoughts that cannot be compassed at once. I know very well that I have a mental left handedness which makes it unnatural for me to express myself as others do, but I have conquered that in a measure. I have even learned some bit of that which the immortal Louis Stevenson declared to be the whole art of writing, the art of omission. But I am not going to exercise it in this letter, this imaginary epistle. What I was [del]about to say[/del] was that I have had occasion lately to read in a good many psychological books. Not Höffding
