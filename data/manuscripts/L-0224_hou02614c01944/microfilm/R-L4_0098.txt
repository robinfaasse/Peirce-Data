Note to p. 62. [u]Impenetrability[/u] is not essential to matter. Matter is that which
$1^{\text{st}}$, occupies space in the sense of being connected with 3 dimensional continuous
portions of space, $2^{\text{nd}}$, every part of it however small, preserves a self identity
and otherness from the others. This is true not only of 3 dimensional parts but
of parts of lower dimensions, $3^{\text{rd}}$, its place varies continuously with the time,
$4^{\text{th}}$, it is [del]absolutely[/del] [ins]or[/ins] [del]or[/del] nearly, permanent in all its parts, and they all pre-
serve their dimensionalities. Impenetrability maybe a property of matter, but if so, it
amounts only to a repulsion. Attraction is the more general law; repulsion, if it exists
at all, which is not absolutely certain, is a special phenomenon.
