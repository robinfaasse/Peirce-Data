My dear William:

Since writing to you I have devoured your book with immense pleasure. But I see, I cannot have the pleasure of altogether agreeing with you. I particularly marvel at your seeming to think that religious people are no better for their religion. I have often heard that said; but I certainly never expected to hear it from an observer, to say nothing of a keen observer.

I think you will do well, regardless of the religious question, to consider my spiral. It proves [del]an[/del] [ins]a[/ins] proposition in logic which is important for psychology. Since writing, I chanced to come across a dynamical illustration of it. I suppose you have enough mathematics to follow it. Suppose a particle to be attracted to a point by a force inversely as the cube of the distance. Let $r$ be the distance. Let the attraction then be $\frac{k}{r^3}$. If, then, the particle were to be thrown directly away from the point, its motion away would be retarded so that $\frac{d^2r}{dt^2} = -\frac{k}{r^3}$. Since we know the common formula $\frac{d^2r}{dt^2} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{d}{dr} (\frac{dr}{dt})^2$, the first integral of the equation is $\frac{1}{2} (\frac{dr}{dt})^2 = \frac{k}{2r^2} - C$, where $C$ is an arbitrary constant, which I will assume to be [del]sh[/del] positive so that the violence of the throw may not be sufficient to carry the particle to an infinite distance.
