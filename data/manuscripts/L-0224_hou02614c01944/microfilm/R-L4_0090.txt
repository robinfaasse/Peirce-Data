That every $A$ has an $A$ as its $r$ is written thus:
$$\Pi_{5}\Sigma_{4}\ \ r_{45}$$

That there is an $A$ not $r$ of any $A$ is written thus:
$$\Sigma_{6}\Pi_{7}\ \ \bar{r}_{67}\ .$$

Now, if there is any contradiction involved, when we multiply these [del]premises[/del] [ins]propositions[/ins] into one another a sufficient number of times and suitably identify the indices, then the principle of contradiction, $x\bar{x} \prec 0$, must reduce the product to zero. That is,

$$\Pi_{5}\Sigma_{4}\ \Pi_{5'}\Sigma_{4'}\ \Pi_{5''}\Sigma_{4''}\ \text{etc}\ \Sigma_{6}\Pi_{7}\ \Sigma_{6'}\Pi_{7'}\ \text{etc}\ \Pi_{5'''}\Sigma_{4'''}\ \Pi_{5^{IV}}\Sigma_{4^{IV}}\ \text{etc}\ \Pi_{1}\Pi_{2}\Pi_{3}\ \Pi_{1'}\Pi_{2'}\Pi_{3'}\ \text{etc}$$

must vanish on suitable identification of the numbers. But no $4$ can be identified with a $6$, because the quantification of both is particular (that is both are attached to $\Sigma$ in the [u]Peircian[/u]). Neither can one $4$ be identified with another $4$ [ins](because the $\Sigma$ follows the $\Pi$, the only other quantifier)[/ins], and therefore both cannot at once be identified with a $1$. Hence, there is no way at all in which the expression can vanish. In other words, we have proved that the hypothesis of an innumerable collection involves no contradiction.
