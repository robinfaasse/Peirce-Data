I [del]beg you[/del] particularly to call your attention to my use of the word Multitude. I never use this word to mean a [u]Collection[/u] or [u]Plural[/u]. I always use it as an abstract noun denoting that character of a Collection which makes it greater than some collections and less than others. Thus, twoness, tenness, three-hundred-and-sixty-five-ness, are [ins]individual[/ins] Multitudes, as I use the word.

At the very outset of the study of the logic of mathematics our attention is forced to the subject of Multitude. Everybody knows that there is a Mathematics of Multitude; and I am going [del]this[/del] evening to tell you some [ins]elementary[/ins] things about it that you probably do not know.
