The richer assemblages are called sometimes "continuous in a more general sense" or "absolutely continuous".

You doubtless do not agree with me in thinking that I acted wisely in restricting myself to a definition of continuous assemblage in the ordinary sense, but I had in mind the space at my disposal & the fact that the readers were presumably not to be mathematicians. And it seemed to me quite enough if I should succeed in making that one point — the only point of practical importance — clear. My principal regret is that I did not say explicitly that I was not defining continuum and that I was not attempting to identify the line with the assemblage of the points upon it; for since you have misunderstood my meaning, I cannot but expect others to do the same. At the same time the word number-continuum is applied almost universally to the ordinal number assemblage which I describe.
