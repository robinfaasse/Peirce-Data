28 any indefinite individual we may confer a proper name,
[ins]or special designation,[/ins]
as soon as this individual can be identified in two presen-
tations. It is true that a proper name is definite; and as
long as an individual is utterly indefinite, merely 'some-
thing', though you may say you give it a proper name, yet
this name will not function as such, until two presenta-
tions can be identified. As soon as this is the case, it ceases
to be utterly indefinite. The principle of contradiction begins
to apply to it.
