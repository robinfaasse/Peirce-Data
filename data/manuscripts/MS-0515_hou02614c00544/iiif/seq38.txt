§7. There is a certain logical relation of the utmost importance.
We may denote it, for the moment by [u]I[/u]. It is not only transitive, so
that [u]If X is I to Y,[/u]
[u]and Y is I to Z,[/u]
[u]then X is I to Z;[/u]
but it is also continuous, so that
[u]If A is I to Z,[/u]
then A is I to something (say B) that is I to something (say C) — —
— — that is I to something (say X) that is I to something (say Y) that is I to Z;
and in short, [u]any multitude whatsoever of intermediaries might[/u]
[u]truly be inserted.[/u] This relation, which is that of absolute identity, so
that each individual is so related to itself alone, is most appropriately
represented by a continuous heavy line which may be ever so long
or may be shortened to a hyphen. Thus A—B will mean
A is B, C— that C is something, [fig]three lines radiating from a point[/fig] that some three
things (or designations) are identical, —is a mother of a son that is—,
—is a black thing that is—, having obvious interpretations.
