§ 7. There is a certain logical relation of the utmost importance. We will denote it [ins]for the moment[/ins] by I. It is not only transitive, so that
If X is I to Y
and Y is I to Z,
then X is I to Z;
but it is also continuous so that
If A is I to Z
then A is I to B, B is I to C, - - - - X is I to Y, Y is I to Z,
and, in short, any [u]multitude[/u] whatsoever of intermediaries exist.
This relation, which is that of [ins]absolute[/ins] identity, is most appropriately [del]represented[/del] represented by a continuous heavy line between its relate and correlate; thus:
