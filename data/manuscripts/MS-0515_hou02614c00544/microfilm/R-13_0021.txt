[u]§7[/u]. A relation [u]r[/u] is said to be transitive if the relates of the relate are [del]included[/del] [ins]among[/ins] the relates of the correlate, so that

If M is [u]r[/u] to P,
and S is [u]r[/u] to M,
then S is [u]r[/u] to P.

Thus, transitiveness depends on a relation of inclusion. Taking any relation, say 'loves,'

If M is loved by every lover of P,
and S is loved by [del]every[/del] lover of M,
then S is loved by every lover of P.

Such a transitive relation has the special character that

X is loved by every lover of X,

for [del]that[/del] which, at present, there is no better designation than an anti-alio-relation. Not all transitive relations have this character. Some are not merely anti-self relations, but are alio-relations, or such in which nothing stands to itself.
