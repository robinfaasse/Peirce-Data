[pn]4/[/pn] [del]be tried[/del] [ins]to be evident[/ins]. Being evident, it is not [del]doubted[/del]. Not being doubted, it cannot really be criticized. For a doubt is a real state of dissatis-faction; and the common practice of making believe to doubt, and then offering considerations to appease that make-believe doubt, is a foolish waste of time, since the man who does not doubt can [del]understand[/del] [ins]realize[/ins] the state of mind of the man who does, only to a very limited extent, and he thus quite fails in all diffi-cult cases to appease [del]any[/del] real doubt that may exist, or appeases it quite otherwise than by his attempted reasonings. Another man's doubt will usually set up a doubt in my mind which I can handle intelligently; but when his doubt excites no doubt in my mind, it is either that I know by my own experience where his mind has probably stumbled (and if I do he can be set right in a moment) or it is that he has thoughts that I have never had, & prob-ably understand the matter better than I. But what appears to be evident is past all possible genuine criticism, until it ceases to [del]be evi[/del] appear evident.
