Gallon
Scotland: 8 pints = 840 cubic inches. According to 5 Ann. 12
make 34 English ale gallons, which should therefore be
296 1/2 cubic inches; but this was probably only intended as
an approximation.

Ireland: 217 6/10 cubic inches, for all purposes. 9 G. 2. 26 G. 3.
Irish acts.

Garb, - of blocks or steel, 30 pieces. 31 Edw. 1.

Gaun or Gawn: Shropshire and Wales; a corruption of gallon, applied
to butter, containing 12 pounds.

Bridgnorth: of butter, 16 pounds.

Gill - a quarter of a pint, whether of wine or of ale measure.

Scotland: 1/16 of a pint, [ins]6 1/2[/ins] cubic inches; about an Eng. gill.

Banffshire: 1/2 a pint English.

Glean or Glen, of teazles, Essex and Gloucestershire; a bunch. See
Bunch.

Glene - of herrings, 25. 31 Edw. 1.

Goad of cloth: Seems to be 4 feet. 28 Ja. 1. "15d. the yard or 20
the goad."
of land: Dorsetshire, 15 feet 1 inch; called also lug.

Grain - [ins]1/7000[/ins] part of a pound avoirdupois. Three wheat corns weigh,
on an average, 2 grains in Leicestershire.

Great Hundred. See Hundred.

Groce or Gross: Commonly 12 dozen.
of bracelets of necklaces, 12 dickers or bundles of 10,
make a small gross. 12 G. 2.
of pill boxes, 12 dozen nests of 4 boxes each. 11 G. 1.

Gunny, of cinnamon, 3/4 cwt.
of saltpetre, 3/4 cwt.

Gwaith Gwr: N. Wales, of peat, 150 square feet, or 50 yards in length,
3 bricks or spits in breadth.

Handbreadth: In Wales, formerly, 3 inches.
