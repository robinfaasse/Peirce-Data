Pint, $\frac{1}{8}$ gallon = $\frac{1}{2}$ a quart = 28$\frac{7}{8}$ cubic inches, wine measure; 35$\frac{1}{4}$ customary ale measure, 35$\frac{9}{10}$ Winchester measure.

Scotland; 2 choppins, about 105 cubic inches, 3 ale pints. But the standard jug, which was entrusted in 1621 to the care of the magistrates of Stirling, appears to contain only 103$\frac{4}{10}$ cubic inches, or 103$\frac{7}{10}$. The standard at Aberdeen contains 105$\frac{1}{10}$ cubic inches. The ale pint of Stirling is $\frac{1}{10}$ above the standard.

Argyleshire: The customary pint contains 109.87 cubic inches.

Caithness: The standard pint contains 18 gills, for ale, or above the regular standard; but it has a pin within, marking 16 gills, which is used for measuring spirits.
