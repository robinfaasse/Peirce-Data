[u]Rules for Determining the Spelling by the Sound.[/u]

[u]Of the Vowels.[/u]

1. Y is always a vowel except at the beginning of a word or of [del]a part of[/del] a constituent of a compound word, [ins]where[/ins] it is always a consonant.

[u]Exceptions to the former statement.[/u]

[u]Exceptions to the latter:[/u] Yttrium and its derivatives, Ygdrasil, Ypres-Lace, y-clepped and other such words.

2. W, although never a vowel by itself, is to be regarded as a vowel when it follows a vowel in the same syllable, as in the words flaw, flew, flow, now, new, mow.

3. Diphthongs and double vowels are usually long.

[del]A[/del] [ins]An accented[/ins] single vowel ending a syllable is long. A single vowel [del]not[/del] ending [ins]only[/ins] a syllable is short.

For the purposes of this rule, [ins]only[/ins] a single consonant, or a combination of consonants that could be sounded
