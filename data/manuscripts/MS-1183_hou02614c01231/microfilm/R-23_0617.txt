Spelling
[/marginalia]

There is considerable economy both of time and of
mental effort in a [del]perfect[/del] uniform system of spelling.
It also involves great economy in printing. The compositor
reads a few words and sets them up in type. If he had
to copy the author's spelling, a vast amount of time [del]was[/del]
would be lost. [del]He cannot[/del] [ins]The only practical way is for him to[/ins] read the words and then spell
them right. But what is right? The proof-reader of the
printing-office must be the ultimate judge of that, and
his decisions will be cited by the compositors as precedents.
In that way, it is inevitable that a standard system of
spelling should grow up in a printing-office.
