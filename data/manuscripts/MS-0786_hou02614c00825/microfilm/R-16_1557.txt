[u]Syllogism[/u]

Eliminative reasoning is, strictly speaking, reasoning
from a single premise. (I should prefer to speak of
inference here. Reasoning is too integrated.) I do not
think it is properly definable as "saying" anything. But it
is a process of thought the essence and purpose of
which is to abstract from all consideration of
something uninteresting [del]at the[/del] on that occasion. It is
not the question whether any positive information
about the eliminated term is there or not. The point
is that we want to free our mind from an idle intruder.

All men are mortal
Enoch is a man
$\therefore$ Enoch is a mortal man, if there [ins]as a mortal man[/ins] [del]any such thing[/del]

Here all the information that is dropped in the
ordinary conclusion is already dropped. Yet so far
there is no elimination. When we pass from this
to
Enoch is [del]a man[/del] [ins]mortal[/ins]
we have dropped some information about Enoch
but no other information about men (which is not
already dropped in the previous conclusion.)

You will perceive that the definition of eliminative
reasoning given errs very badly in implying that
there must be two premisses. On the contrary, if
the steps are fully separated [del]in the analysis[/del], there
never is but one premiss in such [del]reason[/del] [ins]inference[/ins].
