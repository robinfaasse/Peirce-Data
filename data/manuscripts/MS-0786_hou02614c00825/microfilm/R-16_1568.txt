As to your boast about what Symbol Logic
can do, I think it most extravagant. Suppose,
since Kempe's proof of the map-coloring problem
turns out to be fallacious, you justify your faith
in symbolic logic by just solving that problem
by it.

In my opinion the logic of relatives is the only
part of symbolic logic having any use at all as a
calculus, & that nothing by any means marvellous.
It is good for what it was cut out for, as an in-
strument of logical analysis.

Instead of saying modern logic begins with De Morgan
[del]as a whole[/del], why not say "modern exact logic"
Mill's logic is modern logic, and there are
several other kinds.

What is the "common definition" of syllogism
that you speak of. I think you ought in this
article to tell us this. I know no common
definition but Aristotelic.
