<u>Syllogism</u>

In fact, every elimination in any kind of algebra
depends upon the fact that for some particular form of term
a general value can be substituted; although in ordinary
algebra this is masked by the peculiarity that division is
there determinative. However, to stick to the matter in
hand, in logic elimination can only take place
because [del]the[/del] [ins]some[/ins] particular form of term [del]some second[/del]
[del]intentional term[/del] is predicable.

All men are mortal animals
$\therefore$ All men are mortal
because for animals we may substitute [del]eings[/del]

$m \prec d \cdot a$
$a \prec \infty$
$m \prec d \cdot \infty$
$m \prec d$

Now, if it is not a simple term but a [del]form[/del] complex form
of term [del]for which a second[/del] intentional term can be
predicated, then in every case we may regard the
elimination as arising from $x \cdot \bar{x} \prec 0$. For example

Every mother loves every lover of her son
Every mother loves her son
$\therefore$ Every mother loves herself -

$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Pi_k \bar{m}_{ij} \dagger \bar{l}_{kj} \dagger l_{ik}$
$\Pi_u \Pi_v \bar{m}_{uv} \dagger l_{uv}$
$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Pi_k \Pi_u \Pi_v (\bar{m}_{ij} \dagger \bar{l}_{kj} \dagger l_{ik}) \cdot (\bar{m}_{uv} \dagger l_{uv})$
$\Pi_i \Pi_j [del]\Pi_k[/del] (\bar{m}_{ij} \dagger \bar{l}_{ij} \dagger l_{ii}) \cdot (\bar{m}_{ij} \dagger l_{ij})$
$\Pi_i \Pi_j \bar{m}_{ij} \dagger \bar{l}_{ij} \cdot l_{ij} \dagger l_{ii} \qquad \therefore \Pi_i \Pi_j \bar{m}_{ij} \dagger l_{ii}$
