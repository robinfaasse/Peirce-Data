Syllogism
Indeed, we may go a step further (see my [u]Classifi-[/u]
[u]cation of Arguments[/u] [date]1867 Apr 9[/date]) and say that if [del]all[/del]
valid arguments are equivalent if both are necessary
and both proceed from the same premises to the
same conclusion, then all necessary inference is
syllogistic in the sense of consisting of the two essen-
tial ingredients of syllogism, 1[ins]st[/ins] the principle of
copulation, &
[del]A[/del] S is P
S is Q
[triple_dot] S is both P and Q

and 2[ins]nd[/ins] the principle of elimination [u]by the aid of[/u]
[u]empty forms of propositions[/u]. Thus

[u]By Darapti[/u]
All A is B
All A is A
[triple_dot] Some A is B

[u]By Cesare[/u]
No A is B
All B is B
[triple_dot] No B is A

All S is P
[del]All T is T[/del]
[del]All[/del] S that is T is [del]B[/del] [ins]P[/ins]
[triple_dot] All S that is T is P

[u]By Camestres[/u]
All A is B
[del]All[/del] non-B is [del]not-[/del] B
[triple_dot] [del]All[/del] non-B is not A

All S is P
All P is either P or Q
[triple_dot] All S is either P or Q
