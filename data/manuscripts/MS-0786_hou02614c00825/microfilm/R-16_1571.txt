[pn]327[/pn] I cannot admit that [u]deductive reasoning[/u], or any reasoning, consists in stating explicitly anything. (The word [u]explicitly[/u] is redundant, because a statement is essentially an explicit, or even a formal, assertion.) I think reasoning is an operation of the intellect and statement is an outward act. It is true that I belong to that school of logicians who hold that products of the understanding are of the nature of signs and that logic equally applies to mental signs and material signs of certain kinds. But when I speak of a proposition, rather than a [u]judgment[/u], as being more general, I also speak of [u]argument[/u] as occupying a similar relation to reasoning. I think those opposed to my views in respect to logic dealing with outward signs would say with some justice that it leads to such a monstrous statement as that 'reasoning is stating.'

But that is not my only objection. I do not think that reasoning is judging, or that an argument is a proposition. I think reasoning is an operation whereby a judgment is produced, - not [u]any[/u] such operation, but a particular sort of such operation...
