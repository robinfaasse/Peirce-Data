Since Aristotle was the inventor of the syllogism and the
introducer of the word, as he himself expressly claims,
this, according to any decent ethics of terminology, ought
to settle the question of its meaning. At any rate, of this
you may be sure, that to advocate any other use of the
term is simply to work for anarchy in logical language,
because it puts irrational prepossession above principle.

I, at once, said your (m) was an admirable example
of a syllogism, before I dreamed that you would say that
"nobody would consider it to be a syllogism." I have no
doubt I could bring some excellent authorities to support
my calling it a syllogism. But even if not, Aristotle's
dictum should settle the question & must if it is to be settled at all.
