[u]Notes on Mrs. Franklin's Article Syllogism[/u]

[pn]|[/pn] I cannot admit that deductive reasoning, or any reasoning, consists in [del]stating[/del] [ins]“stating[/ins] explicitly” anything. (The word [del]explicitly[/del] is redundant, because a statement is essentially an explicit, or even a formal, assertion.) I think reasoning is an operation of the [del]mind[/del] [ins]intellect[/ins] and statement is an outward act. It is true that I belong to that school of logicians who hold that products of the understanding are of the nature of signs & that logic equally applies to mental signs and material signs of certain kinds. But when I speak of a proposition, rather than a judgment, as being more general, I also speak of argument as occupying a similar relation to reasoning. I think those opposed to my views in respect to logic dealing with outward signs would say with some justice that it leads to such a monstrous statement as that “reasoning is stating.”

But that is not my only objection. I do not think that reasoning is judging, or that an argument is a proposition. I think reasoning is an operation whereby a judgment is produced, — [del]not any such operation,[/del] but a particular [del]sort[/del] of such operations. [pn]|[/pn]

Would not “implicitly asserted” be better English than “implicitly stated”?
