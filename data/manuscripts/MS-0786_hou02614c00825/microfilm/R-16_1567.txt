[u]Syllogism[/u]

I don't know what you mean by the "axiom of
the syllogism." Aristotle regards the [u]dictum de omni[/u]
as a definition merely of universal predication.
That is the reason it is called the [u]dictum de omni[/u].
What "axiom" besides this is required?

When you call the dictum [del]de[/del] omni the "dictum
of Aristotle", you have the air of misunderstanding
what dictum means. The name [u]dictum de omni[/u]
is like the name [u]habeas corpus[/u], that is, it is named
from that which begins the definition as you can
see by referring to the passage in Aristotle. The
dictum de omni is that to predicate anything of
a subject universally means to predicate it of
whatever that subject is predicable.

Any distinction between Aristotle's dictum de omni
and Kant's [u]nota notae[/u] is perfectly imaginary.

I don't think Lotze and Wundt are worth notice in
regard to such questions.

What you call De Morgan's & Mill's view is simply
read in Aristotle.
