[u]Syllogism[/u]

Syllogism is a form of inference a sort of ideal to which a
real inference may more or less closely, perhaps even
perfectly, conform. At any rate, I think we are at
liberty to swerve from Aristotle to that extent.

As to the opinion that syllogism consists in the deduction
of a result by applying a rule to a special case, in the
first place, I cannot see why my term [u]result[/u] is not
perfectly satisfactory. I proposed this in 1866, and I have
priority on my side. [u]Resultus[/u] and [u]resultare[/u] are good
scholastic Latin. But the word was never before applied
as a technical term before me, which is certainly a great
negative merit. The opinion itself was defended in
Kant's ridiculous tractate of the [u]Falsche Spitzfindigkeit[/u]
