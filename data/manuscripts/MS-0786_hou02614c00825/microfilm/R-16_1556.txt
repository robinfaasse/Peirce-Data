<u>Syllogism</u>

You say "in the most useful form of mediate reasoning
there is involved the dropping of [del]information[/del] [ins]into a copulative proposition[/ins]? It is
better to look upon the colligation [ins]as[/ins] one step of
the reasoning & the elimination as another. One
cannot say that the former step is less useful or
difficult than the other. Indeed, all the rules for finding
a middle term about which Aristotle expatiates are
merely apparatus for colligating premisses.

But in the example [del]m[/del], [del]of[/del] information is dropped
although the same terms appear. It is like this:

All men are mortal
Whatever lives after death has a soul
<tex>\therefore</tex> All men [del]without souls[/del] are mortals who will not live after death

Therefore the dropping of information [del]and the
elimination of terms[/del] are not the same (as is
stated, p. 2). The above conclusion has dropped
all information about men with souls. Here
is another example -

Some men are wicked
Some men are fools
<tex>\therefore</tex> Some men are either wicked or fools

Information is manifestly dropped; yet there is
[del]no elimi...[/del]

When a middle term is eliminated, information
about the relation of the extremes is always dropped,
and therefore what is said immediately after the
example ([del]r[/del] [ins]r[/ins]) needs more precise statement.
