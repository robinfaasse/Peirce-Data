Premise instead of premiss is insisted on by Baldwin; but it is gross ignorance of the English language and the history of the word. Premise & premiss have been distinct words since Chaucer. The former is a legal term. When logic fell into neglect, people seldom talked of a premiss in the singular, and premisses became confused with the commoner term premises. Hence, the latter spelling [del]became used[/del] in the plural, while premiss was retained in the singular. So Johnson in his Dictionary, though he does not condemn premisses, says premises is more usual in the plural. Later, it was chiefly Hamilton's writing in the Edinburgh Review where the proofreaders created the word premise for premiss in the logical sense.
