There is a vividness due to [del]external[/del] the actuality, or nearness of experience, which distinguishes perception from imagination, and a superposed vividness due to the mind's action in attention; and an idea [del]which[/del] which excites attention is said to be interesting. Other things being equal an intense feeling attracts more attention than a faint one, and is therefore more vivid. Vividness is suddenly altered in acts of sense and of will. When an outward [del]object[/del] affects the senses, the vividness of a cluster of feelings is suddenly raised, practically from nothing to an extreme high grade. When the will acts upon an external object, that vividness of the idea which we call desire is suddenly lowered.
