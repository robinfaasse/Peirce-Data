[u]Note 1[/u]. I will not quote Aristotle's definition of mathematics; because it could only be rendered generally intelligible to mathematicians by a disquisition on the Aristotelian philosophy. Suffice it to say that it has this merit, that it recognizes that the essence of mathematics lies in the peculiar character of the abstractness of its objects.
