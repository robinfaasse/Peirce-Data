Axioms of Number

By C. S. Peirce

The following is a complete list of the [del]axioms[/del] [ins]assumptions[/ins] of [del]arithmetic[/del] [ins](positive, discrete)[/ins]. They may be considered as constituting a definition of [del](positive, discrete)[/del] number. From them, every proposition of the theory of numbers may be deduced by formal logic.

<u>I.</u>

Whatever is greater than, is other than.

<u>II.</u>

'Greater than' is a transitive relative; that is, whatever is greater than something greater than, is greater than.

<u>III.</u>

Whatever is greater than a number is a number.

<u>IV.</u>

Number is a [ins]system of[/ins] simple quantity; that is, [ins]to say,[/ins] [del]no[/del] [ins]every[/ins] number is related to every other either as greater than [ins]or less[/ins].

<u>V.</u>

There is no maximum number; every number is less than a number.
