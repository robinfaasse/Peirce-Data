POLYGON, [u]n.[/u] In geom., a closed figure formed by the intersections of a number of straight lines,
each with two others, especially a plane figure of this sort. Skew polygon, a non-plane polygon.
Spherical polygon, a polygon whose vertices lie on a sphere; also, a similar figure formed by
arcs of great circles on a sphere. A convex polygon, a plane rectilinear figure without re-en-
trant angles, opposed to a concave polygon. Inscriptible polygon, see INSCRIPTIBLE. Regular poly-
gon, one all whose sides and angles are equal; it is further generally understood that the peri-
meter only wraps around the interior once. Stellated polygon, one which wraps its interior more th
than once. [del]S S[/del] [ins][fig]Stellated octagon[/fig][/ins] [Give figure of a stellated octagon.] Complete polygon, a plane figure formed of
three or more straight lines produced so as to show all their intersections; opposed to a simple
polygon, or polygon as above defined. Similar polygon, see SIMILAR. Polygon of forces, a diagram
used in graphical statics, depending on a theorem of the same name due to Leibnitz, a polygon
each side of which represents in magnitude and direction one of the component forces acting on a
material point; if then the polygon is closed, the forces are in equilibrium. Closed polygon,
a plane polygon [del]closed[/del] enclosing an area, according to the definition of a polygon; opposed
to open polygon, which is only a part of a plane polygon. Funicular polygon, originally, an open
polygon representing a series of connected ties, but extended to a closed polygon representing a
series of virtual ties and struts.
