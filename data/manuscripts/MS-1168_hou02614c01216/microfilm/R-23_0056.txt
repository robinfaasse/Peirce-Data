The troy pound came into use in England about [date]1400[/date]. It was so called from the standard of the city of Troyes. There is no better explanation of its origin than that its twelve ounces of 31.10 grammes were derived from the ounces of 30.59 grammes of which the French poids de marc contained 16. This mark was undoubtedly merely an enlargement of the Frankish monetary [ins]later called the livre[/ins] livre esterlin, whose 12 ounces had each the same value as that of the 16 in the poids de marc. This livre was identical with the yousdrouman pound of the Arabs, of which Haroun-al-Rashid had sent a copy to Charlemagne, and which is still current in Constantinople.
