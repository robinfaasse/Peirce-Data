The two figures of hypothesis are -
[u]X is B[/u]                      [u]X is not A[/u]
[u]All A is B[/u]                    [u]All A is B[/u]
[u]X is A[/u]                      [u]X is not B[/u].

In order to understand these, it is necessary to premise
that a Rule cannot be a mere denotative proposition [del]which[/del] [ins]the office[/ins]
of which is to [del]give a mere example of a[/del] [ins]indicate a class by an example, merely.[/ins] Hence, every
rule must have a connotative object as its predicate. In the
same way, a Subsumption cannot be a Connotative proposition which
merely analyzes its subject & does not subsume it to anything.
Hence every subsumption must have an object of denotation
as its subject. Now in these schemes, [u]B[/u] is the predicate
of a Rule & [u]X[/u] the subject of a subsumption. Hence [u]B[/u] is a [u]form[/u]
& [u]X[/u] is a [u]thing[/u]. While [u]A[/u] which may occupy any position except
these may be taken as a symbol in general. Now you
observe that it is impossible to form an induction with-
out saying [u]X is A[/u] that is a thing has a symbol & [del]hence[/del] [ins]thus[/ins]
the principle of the universal symbolizability of things [del]gives[/del] [ins]is[/ins]
the condition of Induction. In the same way, every hypothesis
must say [u]All A is B[/u] that is to say a form is embodied
in a symbol & thus the principle of the universal symbolizability
of [del]forms[/del] gives rise to hypothesis.
