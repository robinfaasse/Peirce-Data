Now as I have said every predicate is a symbol of its subject.
The first figure consists in taking as predicate, the predicate of
a predicate. This clearly depends on the fact that every symbol
of a symbol is itself a symbol. The second figure abolishes as
predicate that of which the predicate cannot be predicate. Its
rule is therefore, the symbol of that which is not a symbol [ins]of X[/ins] is
not a symbol of [del]the the[/del] [ins]X[/ins]. The third figure denies as universal
predicate [ins]of A[/ins], that which is not a [del]symbol[/del] [ins]predicate[/ins] of what A is predicate
of. Its rule is That is not a symbol of a symbol which is not
a symbol of the object.
Now these three rules are but expressions of the nature of
a symbol [del]but their of[/del]; namely that a mediate symbolization
is possible. But that a mediate symbolization is possible is
summed up in the formula Every symbol is symbolizeable.
