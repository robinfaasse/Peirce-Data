An Attempted Classification of Ends.
By C. S. Peirce.

In the [u]Popular Science Monthly[/u] for [date]January, 1901[/date] (LVIII et seq.) I enumerated a number of ethical classes of motives, meaning by a motive, not a spring of action, but an aim or end appearing ultimate to the agent. Any such classification may be rendered more minute by subdivisions, or broader by aggregations of classes. My endeavor was to make my enumeration about evenly specific throughout. Upon a reëxamination of it, it appears to me to be sufficiently complete and systematic to [del]make[/del] [ins]afford[/ins] a tolerable material to be cut up, worked over, and amplified into a satisfactory classification of ends.
