July 10, 1860, after the entry in the journal.
The sixth week commenced this morning with an auspicious
looking sky. The sun soon dissipated the mists that
floated above the mountain peaks, and it shone down
on the temperature stations & the post that we hoisted by the
tent. As the breeze blows the cloth [ins]it[/ins] shows the state of the
atmosphere for our observations. At 12 M. the air
felt cool, so that we were glad to seek the sunny side of
a tent and while there looking down the precipice
we caught the notes of several instruments coming upwards,
giving tokens of the arrival of the party from San Antonio.
And while thinking how soon they were expected they
appeared above the summit. (The [del]two[/del] [ins]other[/ins] party from
San Antonio). They came by the foot-path. [del]up[/del]
Up these mountains and it was by far the best way.
The instruments they brought were safely carried by
the men, and when I saw them in perfect order [del]o[/del] I
felt gladness. All they had to report was an accident
to the mules, two of which were left at the foot of the mountains.
