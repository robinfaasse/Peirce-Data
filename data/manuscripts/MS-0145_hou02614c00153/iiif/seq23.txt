But besides Projective Geometry, which I prefer to call, with 
Clifford, [u]Graphics[/u], there is another kind of [ins]descriptive[/ins] geometry which 
utterly ignores the essential distinctions of graphics, not 
distinguishing straight lines from curves or from crooked lines, 
nor cusps or inflexions from ordinary points, but con-
fining its attention exclusively to the connectivity of places. 
This is [u]Topics[/u]. It thinks of fixed Places, and of Objects 
called [u]Movables[/u], occupying each some fixed place at each 
instant of Time, and capable in Time of displacement 
with deformation, called [u]Motion[/u], by which in the course 
of a lapse of Time, it occupies another place, which it 
is thus said to [u]generate[/u] (and I may now adopt the word 
[u]traverse[/u], also,) it being understood that if the movable 
retraces any part of its wake, going over it in re-
versed order, it thereby undoes its work of generation
