I found myself once more in the midst of a battle
in that same wood where I had seen it before. The battle
raged all about me with the utmost fury. Men were
fighting and shooting from behind the thick trees, and
were falling in heaps. The air was filled with the
bullets, and the screams of the wounded. I saw at some
distance from me a dark mass, although I could see
nothing clearly. I rushed into the wood and found
it was a company of Russian soldiers, and in front
of them stood a young officer with a sword in his hand.
I knew him at once; he was the leader of the party.
I rushed towards him, and as I did so he turned his
head and looked at me. His eyes were wide open and
had a wild look in them. He looked at me for a few
seconds, and then he fell back. I rushed to catch him
as he fell, and as I did so the dream vanished. The next
morning I received a letter from my sister in which
she told me that the Russian officer had been killed in
a battle in the wood on the day on which I had had
the dream. She said that he had died in the same
way as I had seen him in my dream. I was told that
this young officer had often talked of me before starting
for the war, and had always said that if anything
happened to him I should be sure to know of it.
