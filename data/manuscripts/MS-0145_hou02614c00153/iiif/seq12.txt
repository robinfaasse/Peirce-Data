The first few months of our residence in
Dunedin we lived at the house of a Mr.
Black, a friend of the Campbells. It was situated
in what was then called "The Glen" – a lovely
wild wooded spot, now the site of the
Railway workshops & many streets. At that
time it was almost primitive nature. On
the edge of the bush. Near us, and higher up the
gully, lived the Revd Mr Bannerman, who with
his wife & children occupied a small two
roomed house. They had come down to
the Presbyterian settlement [del]of[/del] [ins]at[/ins] the Taieri to
which charge Mr Bannerman had been
appointed – one of the first three ministers.
They were waiting until their house was built
at the Taieri. They were friends of ours
ever after. –
Mr Black was a good, kind man. One night
one of the children [ins]having[/ins] been very ill, he
insisted on setting off at midnight
through the bush to look for a doctor.
He did find one – an eccentric old gentleman
called Dr Manning – who with some diffi-
culty was persuaded to turn out & come
to see the little one. He had to be guided
the whole way, as it was very dark & through
high ferns – [del]there[/del] [ins]there[/ins] being then no roads.
