There is another way of proving that it is indifferent to the
banker whether the player who has won goes on or not. When we
say the game is even, we do not pretend to know [del]what will happen in[/del] [ins]how[/ins]
any special series of bets will result. We only say that in a long
enough run, it would be safe to assume the gains and losses would
be insignificant, provided it were somehow arranged that not their
total amount, but the ratio of this to the whole amount risked were
the important consideration. Calculation shows that out of ten million
players, each betting a franc, the number of winners would not, in two
plays out of every three, differ from five million by as much as a
thousand, one way or the other. In a million plays, this thousand would
grow to a million francs, which might be a serious matter to the
banker. This relatively small surplus, might be added to or deducted
