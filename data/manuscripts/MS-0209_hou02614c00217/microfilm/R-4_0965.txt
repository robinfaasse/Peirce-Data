The proportion of players who have lost $b$ francs after the $m\text{th}$ bet,
is that term of the development of $(1 - \frac{p}{q}) (p+q)^m$ which has $b$
as the exponent of $q$. For example,

$(p+q)^7 = q^7 + 7pq^6 + 21p^2q^5 + 35p^3q^4 + 35p^4q^3 + \text{etc.}$
$\frac{p}{q}(p+q)^7 = \qquad\quad pq^6 + 7p^2q^5 + 21p^3q^4 + 35p^4q^3 + \text{etc.}$
$(1 - \frac{p}{q})(p+q)^7 = q^7 + 6pq^6 + 14p^2q^5 + 14p^3q^4 + 0p^4q^3 + \text{etc.}$

That is, since $2^7 = 128$, [del]the proportion of the original number of[/del]
[del]play[/del] out of every 128 players, there will be left at the end of
the $7\text{th}$ bet, [del]1[/del] [ins]one[/ins] who [del]have[/del] [ins]has[/ins] lost 7 francs, 6 who have lost 5 francs,
14 who have lost 3 francs, 14 who have lost one franc, and none
who have gained a franc (since they [del]have[/del] [ins]are truncated[/ins] left the table). This can
be verified by forming the following triangle:

After 0 bets. 1 bet. 2 bets. 3 bets. 4 bets. 5 bets. 6 bets. 7 bets.

For large values of $m$, the calculation must be made by means of the function
$\int e^{-x^2} dx$. *
