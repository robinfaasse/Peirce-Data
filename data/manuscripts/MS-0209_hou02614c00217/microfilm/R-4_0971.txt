There is another way of proving that it is indifferent to
the banker whether the player who has won goes on or not. When we
say the game is even, we do not pretend to know how any special
series of bets will result. We only say that in a long enough run,
it would be safe to assume the gains and losses would be insignificant,
provided it were somehow arranged that not their total amount, but
the ratio of this to the whole amount risked were the important con-
sideration. Calculation shows that out of ten million players, each
betting a franc, the number of winners would not, in two plays out
of every three, differ from five million by as much as a thousand,
one way or the other. In a million plays, this thousand would
grow to a million francs, which might be a serious matter to the
banker. This relatively small surplus, might be added to or
deducted from the amount paid to the winners. It would be only
about [fraction]1/100[/fraction] of one per cent of the amount paid them; and they would
hardly be aware the coins were heavy or light in this minute ratio.
