There is still another way of reaching the same conclusion.
Namely, granting that it is [u]per se[/u] a disadvantage is fully counter-
balanced by the losses of those who remain. Thus, in the long run,
out of every 1024 players who sit down to play a franc each time
at an even game, at the end of the first bet 512 will carry away
their franc each, but 512 will have lost a franc each.

At the end of the second bet 512 will have carried away a
franc, and 256 will be square, while 256 will have lost 2 francs
each, making 512 francs in all.

At the end of the third bet 512 + 128, or 640 players
will have carried away a franc each but
128 + 128 = 256 will have lost 1 franc, making 256 fr.
and 128 will have lost 3 francs, making 384 fr.
total lost 640 fr.

At the end of the fourth bet, 640 players will have carried
away 640 francs, 128 players will be square,
128 + 64 = 192 will have lost 2 francs, making 384 fr.
64 will have lost 4 francs, making 256 fr.
total lost 640 fr.

At the end of the fifth bet 640 + 64 = 704 players will
have carried away 704 francs.
64 + 96 = 160 players will have lost 1 franc, making 160 fr.
and 96 + 32 = 128 will have lost 3 francs, making 384 fr.
32 will have lost 5 francs, making 160 fr.
total lost 704 fr.
