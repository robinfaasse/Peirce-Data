It has been objected, by some by whom this project is in
other respects applauded, that the very basis of the doctrine of
chances (upon which this scheme rests) is that whatever is possible
will sometime actually occur. Now, nobody can deny that it is
logically possible that a player should, on the whole, lose more
and more, perpetually. Hence, though the numerical probability
of this case is, by calculation, zero, yet out of that infinitely
long run of cases which alone lends any meaning to numerical
probability, that case must be found to occur, at least, once.
Thus, horrible to contemplate, when finite periods of time,--years,
centuries, and eras,--had all elapsed, these out of the infinite
multitude of players for a franc, would still sit one Ahasuerus,
left behind to bear upon his shoulders losses equivalent to the
infinite sum distributed as gains among the rest, and doomed to
struggle on through all eternity in the desperate and hopeless
quest after that one accursed franc. His case, it is urged, has
