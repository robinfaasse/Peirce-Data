It makes no difference how many cards there are
in a pack, [del]name it[/del] To cut it, when arranged in its
proper order, and transpose the two parts, is to add a
constant amount to the face-value of every card. So
much for addition.

Now how shall we multiply? Suppose we have the
pack of ten in its proper order, and wish to multiply
the face value of the cards by 3. We deal out the cards
[ins]one by one[/ins] (from first to last, into 3 piles, laying them face up
upon the table. We first take up the pile the ten, [del]falls[/del] [ins]or zero,[/ins]
falls upon, then the next [del]pile[/del], last the third, putting
each pile after the first at the back of that last taken.
We now find in place 1 card 3, or 3 times 1;
in place 2 card 6, or 3 times 2;
in place 3 card 9, or 3 times 3;
in place 4 card 2, congruent to 3 times 4;
in place 5 card 5, congruent to 3 times 5;
in place 6 card 8, congruent to 3 times 6;
in place 7 card 1, congruent to 3 times 7;
in place 8 card 4, congruent to 3 times 8;
in place 9 card 7, congruent to 3 times 9;
in place 10 card 0, congruent to 3 times 10.

Take this pack and multiply again, [del]this time by 7[/del] by 3.
Multiplying by 3 twice is multiplying by 9. But 9 ≡ -1.
