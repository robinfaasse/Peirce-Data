[u]Probably beginning Octave VIII[/u]

It is not my intention at all to attempt a criticism of Hegel. I have not studied him deeply enough to do so. But certain remarks about him strike me, from time to time, and these I insert.

I am struck with the utterly feebleness of Hegel in mathematics. For example, when he says the Past, Present, & Future are the 3 dimensions of [del]being[/del] time, could anything be more infantile? Hegel taught mathematics in the university. God save the mark!

The best thing in Hegel is that he [del]never[/del] dimly sees in his way, and under his unfortunate paradox-form [del]which[/del] the only value of which is that it brought him to this idea, that he sees continuity and the logic of continuity. That distinguishes him from all the other seminary-philosophers, and is the great cause of the influence that Hegelianism has continued to exert since it was exploded.

Were I to take the categories of First, Second, and Third, and to classify in their order all [del]the forms of Secondness[/del] [ins]of each with the preceding behind it[/ins] and then all these of Thirdness [del]— a work which,[/del] [ins]by the way,[/ins] I have [del]performed[/del] [del]gone through,[/del] — I should be developing my doctrine as it seems to me very much in the sort of [del]way[/del] spirit and method in which Hegel developes his [del]logic[/del] Encyclopädie.

But having [del]perform all[/del] gone through with that work and studied its result as thoroughly as I have been able to do, I am convinced that whatever utility it may have, — it does not proceed according to the true logic. The second does not spring out the first directly [del]with[/del] [ins]but firstness looked at from[/ins] a second point of view gives [del]me transition[/del] [ins]birth[/ins] to a Thirdness and the Secondness comes out of the Thirdness. This is the true logic of events.
