The list is sufficiently ridiculous; but the statement that every predicate must refer to [u]some[/u] [u]one[/u] of these classes, and not to a class embracing several of them is at once refuted by the mere invention of the word [u]ens[/u], the conception of which is indeed used by Aristotle himself in this very statement, in speaking of "that which can be affirmed", — τῶν λεγομένων, i.e. "of the entia", or "of things affirmable". Of course, the more [ins]the thing affirmed[/ins] [del]an affirmation[/del] is complicated, the narrower and less general it becomes, as Aristotle perfectly recognizes. But it is absurd to say that that is not affirmable which he himself has just spoken.
