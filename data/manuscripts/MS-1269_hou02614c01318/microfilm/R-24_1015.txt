Very ancient papyri are preserved which relate to the two scientific arts of arithmetic and medicine. The most important of the former is the Rhind papyrus of the British Museum, translated and expounded by Dr. August Eisenlohr.* It is an early hieratic MS. in relatively good preservation. It begins as follows:

"Rules for calculating the results (?) of things, the knowledge of everything that is, of [all] obscurities, [of all mysteries], of all difficulties.

"It may be added that this papyrus was written in the 33[sup]rd[/sup] year, [del]4[sup]th[/sup][/del] [ins]4[sup]th[/sup][/ins] summer month, day -- under the king Aa-user-rā, now reigning, as a copy of ancient writings made in the time of the king Maāt-[del]en[/del]-rā; it was the scribe Aahmesu who wrote this copy." (Griffith's translation.)
