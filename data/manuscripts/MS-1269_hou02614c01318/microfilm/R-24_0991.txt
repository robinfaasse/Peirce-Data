* Prof. Eisenlohr's translation of 1877 (an early date in the [del]know[/del] [ins]Egyptian[/ins] philology)
reads as follows: “Vorschrift zu gelangen zur Kenntniss aller dunkeln
Dinge .... aller Geheimnisse, welche enthalten sind in den Dingen.
Verfasst wurde dieses Buch im Jahr 33, Mesori, Tag ... unter dem König von
Ober- und Unterägypten Ra-ā-us, Leben gebend, nach dem Vorbild von alten
Schriften, die verfertigt wurden in den Zeiten des Königs von Ober- und Unter-
ägypten [Ra-en-m]at durch den Schreiber Ahmes verfasst diese Schrift.”
Lieut. Griffith's version follows: “Rules for calculating the results (?) of things, the
knowledge of everything that is, of [all] obscurities, [of all mysteries,] of all dif-
ficulties. It may be added that this papyrus was written in the 33[ins]rd[/ins] year, 4[ins]th[/ins] summer
month, day ... under the King, Āa-user-rā, now reigning, as a copy of ancient
writings made in the time of the King Maāt[-en-rā]: it was the scribe Aahmesu
who wrote this copy.” Griffith transfers the words “m Xt from the second line to the
first, retaining Eisenlohr's translation “in things”, which I change to “in the future.” The
words at the end of the first line, yw yet q[del]r[/del][ins]t[/ins], translated by Eisenlohr, “which are contained,”
and by Griffith, “It maybe added”, I translate “though they be”, literally, “be, however, but.”
