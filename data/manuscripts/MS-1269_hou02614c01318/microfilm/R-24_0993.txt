† The complicated hypothesis of MM. Revillout that the book is a copy of the blotter of a backward school-boy under Amenemhat III, cannot be admitted. These savants profess to state only a few of many reasons in their possession for their opinion; but we can only [del]examine[/del] [ins]judge of[/ins] their unstated reasons by those which they put forward as sufficient. The first of these is that no rules are abstractly formulated. The second is that under each rule one example is worked out with explanations, while for the others only the figures of the computation are given. That first example they [del]s[/del] suppose to have been done by the master, the others by the pupil. The third reason is that the latter class of examples are sometimes worked wrongly. The fourth is that a correction of an erroneous figure is sometimes given in the margin, without this correction being carried through the following computations. Such marginal correction is supposed to be a note by the teacher.

This looks plausible at first sight; but the hypothesis is open to the [ins]general[/ins] objection that it has to be bolstered up by a mass of subsidiary hypotheses. In particular the theory is directly contrary to the only positive historical testimony we possess about the book, namely, that of its title and preface.
