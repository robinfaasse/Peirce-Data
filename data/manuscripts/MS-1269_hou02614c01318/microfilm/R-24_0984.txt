Another theory, which has had vogue enough to justify the mention of it, is that the slope of the faces of the pyramid proves that its builder was acquainted with the ratio of the circumference of a circle to its diameter. This is a prudent sort of hypothesis, because, the casing of the pyramid having been all carried away for building stones by the [del]Arabs[/del] [ins]Sultan Hasan, A.D. 1356*[/ins], except four or five stones, it is impossible to ascertain the original slope of the faces accurately enough to confront the [del]hypothe[/del] theory very closely with the facts. There is reason to believe that the Egyptians of the IVth dynasty were ignorant of any close approximation to the ratio of the circumference to the diameter; and [del]there is no reason to suppose[/del] [ins]it violates all we know of their character[/ins] that had they known [del]of[/del] such an approximation they would have done a thing so unpractical [del]and so[/del] [ins]as well as[/ins] theoretically unsuitable as to embody this ratio in the proportions of a pyramid. We shall soon see, when we come to study the [ins]Rhind[/ins] mathematical papyrus, that there is exceedingly good reason for denying to the Egyptians of the XIIth dynasty, the most brilliant [ins]later by a millenium than the Great Pyramid[/ins] of the Middle Kingdom and the most mathematical age of Egyptian civilization, any knowledge of as close an approximation to $\pi$ as $\frac{22}{7}$; and such conservatives as the Egyptians were would never have forgotten so useful a piece of knowledge had it ever have been known.
