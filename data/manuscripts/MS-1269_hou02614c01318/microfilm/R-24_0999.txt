It is evident, then, that [del]to[/del] [ins]for[/ins] the Egyptian arithmetician, multiplication
was simply such an operation that the doubling of a multiplier would

the new product. It is a significant fact, in this connection, that all
the old writers on our Vulgar Arithmetic, beginning with Muhammed ben
Musa, the Khorasmian, in the IX century A.D., treat doubling, or "duplation",
as an operation distinct from multiplication. There was no sense in
their doing so; for they doubled just as they multiplied by 3 or by 7, like
us. But the distinction was doubtless a tradition from earlier ages,
when multiplication was performed in the Egyptian manner.
