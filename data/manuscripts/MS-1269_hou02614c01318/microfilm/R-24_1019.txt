Farther evidence appears from their making an exception of the fraction ⅔, which they wrote with a single compound character, thus [fig]Egyptian hieroglyphic for two-thirds[/fig]. To a mathematical mind there are no exceptions in things mathematical. An apparent exception is, for him, but the signal for an act of generalization. The Egyptian, however, never generalized; he lacked the wings to lift him from the [u]terra firma[/u] of fact.

Of course, nothing can be put along with itself to make an aggregate. This is clearly the only way of explaining its appearing impossible to the Egyptian mind to add a fraction to itself.
