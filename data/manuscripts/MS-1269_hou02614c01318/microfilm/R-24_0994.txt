gratuitous conjecture, and that an intricate one, receiving
no support except from the four features of the work upon which
MM. Revillout lay so much stress, although we shall soon find
upon examination that they amount to nothing. This, however, is
not stating the case strongly enough. This needful accessory
hypothesis is in the light of history well nigh impossible. Although the
Hyksos were originally herdsmen, they were of an eminently civi-
lizable race, Phoenicians, according to Manetho, and when after
two centuries the great Apepa came to the throne, they had assimi-
lated all the civilization of the Egyptians. Apepa's rule extended far
south of Thebes, and the country must have been in a pretty settled
condition if there was a class of parvenus "gentlemen farmers." The
population, today many times denser than that of Belgium, could not have
been sparse; and in a dense population business and government render
arithmetic indispensible, as the history of the early Khalifs shows. It is
impossible, therefore, that the arithmetical art of the XII Dynasty should have
been forgotten under the sixth king of the XVII.
