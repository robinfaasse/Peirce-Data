On the whole, then, it would be highly illogical to accept this
finespun hypothesis. The judgments of the Revillots upon all questions
display an interesting chiaroscuro. They are far from frigid.
They seem to be of the number of those Egyptologists whose feelings
become engaged in extolling the merits, intellectual and moral,
of the people they study. They wish us to believe that this gives
no idea of the real mathematical attainments and capacity of
the Egyptians, but is merely the perfunctory exercise-book of
