Existential Graphs

The Constitutive Conventions

1. Each and every conceived fact written (unenclosed) on the sheet of the graph is thereby asserted to be realized [ins]somewhere[/ins] in the universe of actuality.

2. The expression of a conceived fact [ins]being[/ins] enclosed within a lightly drawn oval is thereby not asserted, but is looked upon as a subject of assertion. But if nothing is asserted of it, its being written enclosed on the sheet of the graph denies it.

3. Contact of individuals (unenclosed) signifies identity; and every part of a heavy line asserts the identity of its extremities.
