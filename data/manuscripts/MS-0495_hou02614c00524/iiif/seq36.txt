He rather seeks (or at least he
should seek) and at any rate it
is his only business to take such
individual parts as nature or art
gives him as symbols (alle-
gories) and represents them
so that they illustrate through
this parts, individual human
nature on every page as it is
in fact so that as Aristotle says
"poetry is more philosophical
than history" because these symbols
show clearly and definitely
relationships as they in fact are
which when we look at nature or
history are so much hidden as to be
rarely discernible. Therefore
if the poet does not use his
subjects as symbols he becomes
at once a mere historian or a
mere naturalist, which is
exactly what happens when he
becomes realistic and naturalistic.
He loses all poetic value, and
can as well be read in prose.
The end and aim of art is
reproduction of individuality.
The means is through symbolical-
ly chosen objects taken from
nature or art, so combined as
to represent the human soul
in various relationships. For the
soul is the only reality which
human nature can know and
every human activity is a
striving for the soul's freedom.
The natural world (which is our
only medium) must be used as
a medium to that end, for we
cannot say anything except through
this medium. It is an end in itself
as a medium (paradox) and
this represents a profound
philosophical fact. It represents
the fact that individuality through
its medium finds its only ex-
pression. In proportion as the medium
and soul are one, is art perfect.
This relationship of soul to its medium
is the point where all human activities
take their source. In poetry the
form of the individual (which is its soul)
shows itself through individual
objects. These objects are not used
as their realities but as sym-
bols. The same holds true of
all art. In sculpture and paint-
ing it is even more readily seen.
The medium should be ignored
as medium but used as symbols.
