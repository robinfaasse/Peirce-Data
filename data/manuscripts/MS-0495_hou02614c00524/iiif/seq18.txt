The Various Formal Rules
of Reasoning

Let [fig]figure[/fig] denote the transformation of the
premisses into the conclusion.
In syllogistic reasoning we must know which terms
are subject and which predicate and which middle.
But here let us denote the copula by any sign [fig]figure[/fig].
[u]All terms are on an equal footing[/u] as far as
formal transformations go.
Suppose we have a sentence containing only
terms A and B, which we may represent by [fig]figure[/fig].
A transformation may consist in changing
this into some other sentence containing only
terms A and B, which we may represent by [fig]figure[/fig].
This process may be called a transformation
[u]between sentences in two terms A and B.[/u]
