The sulphuric acid so formed neutralizes half the hydrate at the cathode while the hypochlorous acid is sufficient to neutralize the other half.

Although all the chlorine is thus converted into hypochlorous acid, yet in an acid solution some of it is slowly reconverted into chlorine. For though the avidity of sulphuric acid is only about half that of hydrochloric, yet it sets a certain amount of the latter free from the solution of chlorides; and this hydrochloric acid acts to convert the hypochlorous acid into chlorine.

There is no formation of chlorate in this process.

If the mixed sulphate and chloride solution is used as in Woolf's method, the operation is cyclical, and the efficiency is slightly greater than with Woolf's solution. This process should be patented.[ins]*[/ins]
