The carbonate of lime is precipitated in a
peculiar gelatinous condition, which is converted
into crystalline powder after a short time.

The magnesium salts are seldom present in
important quantities. In the absence of ammoniacal
salts, they will generally be precipitated by the
lime water. In the presence of ammoniacal salts,
the addition of sufficient sal ammoniac will hold
them in solution. It is not believed that special
treatment will ever be required. The phosphate
of soda would precipitate them.

The sulphate of soda should be precipitated
with chloride of barium.
