One part $\text{LiCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ dissolves in $\frac{1}{5}$ part water;

water. Consequently, in a quart of water one may
use $13\frac{1}{2}$ ounces $\text{Li}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ and 12 ounces
$\text{LiCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. One part of $\text{MgCl}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ dissolves
in $\frac{2}{3}$ part of water; while one part $\text{MgSO}_4 + 7\text{H}_2\text{O}$
dissolves in 1 part water, [del]and[/del] Consequently in
a quart of water one can dissolve 2 pounds
of Epsom salts and $11\frac{1}{2}$ ounces of crystallized
chloride of magnesium. These proportions
would all be more or less modified by
practice.

In the electrolysis of these mixtures, there
is a waste of from 1 to 5 per cent of the
"available chlorine" in making oxygen;
but no chlorate seems to be formed at all.
