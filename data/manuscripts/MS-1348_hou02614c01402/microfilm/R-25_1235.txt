There is evidence to show that wood-
pulp in Mr. Woolf's solution and in the
electrical circuit has its coloring-matter de-
composed by direct electrolysis. This is similar
to the well-known process of tanning hides by
electricity. But in Mr. Woolf's process of
bleaching, this part of the action is not
important. For I find that ordinary Javelle
water of such a strength as to oxydize the
same amount of Arsenious acid that
Woolf's solution will oxydize under a certain
current in five minutes, will during that
five minutes bleach considerably more per-
fectly a given quantity of pulp than will the
Woolf's process in the same time. This
proves that his process does not utilize the
direct electrolysis of the coloring-matter to
any important extent; and this indicates
the greatest defect of the process, and the
direction in which scientific research
