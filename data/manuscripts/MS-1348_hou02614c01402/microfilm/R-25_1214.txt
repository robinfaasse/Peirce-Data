In the presence of coloring-matter or other
reducing agent having an attraction for the
oxygen, this takes place rapidly. But in the
absence of such substances, the oxygen is set
free, and the action is then so slow, that only
about 7 per cent of the chlorine is lost in this
way. The hydrochloric acid so formed is neutra-
lized by the sodium hydrate. Consequently, there
is no free acid. The liquor about the anode
bleaches powerfully, but appears to be neutral.

rite will be formed; and as this will not
bleach so quickly as the chlorine, it will
accumulate until it is in excess of the
