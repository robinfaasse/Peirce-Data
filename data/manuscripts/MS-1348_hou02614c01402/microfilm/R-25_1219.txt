In a former letter, I stated that in this case, the chlorine begins by acting upon the caustic soda; but I am satisfied that it is not so.[ins]*[/ins] We may trace an analogy between the two actions by supposing that chlorine acting upon caustic soda first replaces the sodium thus
$$\begin{matrix} \text{Cl} \\ \text{Cl} \end{matrix} + \begin{matrix} \text{Na} \\ \text{H} \end{matrix}\text{O} = \begin{matrix} \text{Na} \\ \text{Cl} \end{matrix} + \begin{matrix} \text{Cl} \\ \text{H} \end{matrix}\text{O}$$
and that the hypochlorous acid so formed is at once neutralized by the excess of caustic soda; while in acting on the carbonate it first forms a chlorido-carbonate, if I may use such a word, thus:
$$\begin{matrix} \text{Cl} \\ \text{Cl} \end{matrix} + \begin{matrix} \text{Na} \\ \text{Na} \end{matrix}\text{CO}_3 = \begin{matrix} \text{Na} \\ \text{Cl} \end{matrix} + \begin{matrix} \text{Cl} \\ \text{Na} \end{matrix}\text{CO}_3.$$
Then this imaginary "chlorido-carbonate" in the presence of water may form hypochlorous acid, thus:
$$\begin{matrix} \text{Cl} \\ \text{Na} \end{matrix}\text{CO}_3 + \begin{matrix} \text{H} \\ \text{H} \end{matrix}\text{O} = \begin{matrix} \text{H} \\ \text{Na} \end{matrix}\text{CO}_3 + \begin{matrix} \text{Cl} \\ \text{H} \end{matrix}\text{O}.$$
All this is speculation, not to say metaphysics. The solid fact is that in this process only hypochlorous acid and no hypochlorite is formed, except by means of the caustic soda from the cathode. Thus, the difference between the action of caustic and of carbonated alkali is tremen-

[marginalia]* That is to say, that is not the beginning of the action. But that process undoubtedly takes place.[/marginalia]
