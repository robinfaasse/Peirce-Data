[del]Kali in a solution of sodium or potassium chloride; and in my opinion[/del] the use of this carbonated alkali is the sole item of value in Woolf's patents. Mr. Woolf seemed to have no idea whatever of the part played by the carbonic acid until I told him about it, and even now he does not understand it. The real point is that the chlorine does not act upon the carbonate to produce hypochlorite as it does upon the hydrate. The action on the hydrate follows the formula
$$\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} = \text{NaCl} + \text{NaOCl} + \text{HOH}.$$
Or it can be shown in the diagram, where it is seen three atoms are cyclically transposed.
[fig]Diagram showing cyclical transposition of atoms[/fig]
But the action on the carbonate is this:
$$2\text{Cl}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} = 2\text{NaCl} + 2\text{HOCl} + \text{CO}_2$$
