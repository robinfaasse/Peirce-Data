[u]Direct electrolysis of coloring matter[/u]. When wood-pulp in Woolf's alkaline solution of sodium chloride is put into the electrical circuit, its coloring-matter is partly decomposed by direct electrolysis. The action is similar to that which takes place in the world-renowned process of tanning hides by electricity.

I had intended to investigate this action, conceiving that to be the most important service which science could render to the art of bleaching. Mr. Woolf, however, objected to my doing so, on the ground that my contract did not call for such a research. I then made an experiment which satisfied me that he was right, because it showed that in Woolf's process [del]there is[/del] no considerable amount of aid is derived from direct electrolysis.
