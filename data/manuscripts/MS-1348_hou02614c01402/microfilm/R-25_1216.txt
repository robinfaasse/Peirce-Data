[u]Electrolysis of alkaline solutions of chlorides[/u].

When the hydrate of the same metal is added
to the chloride of sodium, potassium, calcium,
or barium (and doubtless the remark is true
of lithium, caesium, rubidium, and strontium),
the reactions are but little modified. The evo-
lution of oxygen is reduced, because the chlo-
rine is pretty promptly converted into hypo-
chlorite.

When coloring-matter is in the vessel, the
bleaching may be started by the electrolysed
hypochlorite or the hypochlorite itself un-
soused may start the process. Indeed, both
actions must concur. In this way hydrochlo-
ric acid will be formed, which will neutralize
a part of the alkali, and the bleaching will
be slow until the solution is neutral.
