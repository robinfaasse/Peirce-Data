It is a familiar law of chemistry that
when two solutions of salts are mixed and by
"double decomposition", or interchange of their
metallic elements, an insoluble salt can be
formed, it will be formed and be precipitated.

Therefore, to precipitate [u]sulphate of lime[/u] we
[del]have[/del] [ins]have[/ins] to do one of two things, viz:

either, find an [u]acid[/u] whose lime salt is in-
soluble, and introduce the right quantity of a
[del]soluble[/del] [ins]solution of a[/ins] salt of that acid

or, find a [u]base[/u] whose sulphate is insoluble,
and introduce the right quantity of a [del]soluble[/del]
solution of a salt of that base.

Of insoluble salts of lime, the only one worth
considering is the carbonate.
