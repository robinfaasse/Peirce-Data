[fig]Chemical equation showing the reaction of sodium carbonate, chlorine, and water to form hypochlorous acid, sodium chloride, and carbon dioxide.[/fig]

The carbonic acid immediately unites with the hydrate from the cathode to produce carbonate. Thus, the action is utterly different from that with caustic alkali; and hypochlorous acid is formed without any possibility of forming hypochlorite, except in the same way as when sodium chloride alone is electrolysed; while when caustic alkali is used hypochlorite alone is formed, with no possibility of hypochlorous acid, until the solution becomes [del]acid[/del] neutral.
