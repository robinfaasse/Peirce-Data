Water may contain
1. Scale forming materials.
2. Frothmaking substances.

1. The scale forming materials are particularly
Sulphate of lime $\text{Ca SO}_4$
Acid carbonate of lime; probably $\text{Ca H}_2\text{ C}_2\text{O}_6$
Salts of magnesia

The sulphate of lime is soluble in about 500
parts of water, or a gallon dissolves about 100
grains. Hence, if water contains 20 grains to
the gallon, it begins to deposit when boiled down
to 1/5 part. It is deposited in the form of
anhydrite $\text{Ca SO}_4$. But in cold water it is
converted into gypsum or plaster $\text{Ca SO}_4, 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$
with considerable swelling. When the water is boiler
this is reconverted into anhydrite, and so on, tending
to tear the boiler to pieces. But the worst is that
it is a specially bad conductor of heat, so that to heat
water through an inch of scale is like heating it
through [u]several feet[/u] thickness of iron.

The acid carbonates of the alkaline earths are
generally [del]more[/del] soluble and [del]less[/del] stable than the
[del]normal carbonates[/del], — in both respect reversing
the relations of the carbonates of the alkalies.
