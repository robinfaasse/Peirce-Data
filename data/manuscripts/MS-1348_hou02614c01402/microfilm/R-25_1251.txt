There is no waste of material except by the
slight escape of gaseous chlorine and hypo-
chlorous acid. The bleaching proceeds with
great rapidity, and the efficiency is slightly
greater than with Woolf's process. This pro-
cess should be patented.

But if the electrolytes are of wire gauze
sheets, and if the solution is allowed to flow
in between them and is drawn off outside
of them at a rate just a little exceeding that
of their diffusion into one another (which
can be observed by coloring the liquor about
the cathode), we can draw off into separate
vessels the bleaching fluid containing hypo-
chlorous acid on the one hand and a fluid
containing caustic alkali on the other
hand. Now if the hypochlorous acid be used
without delay, before it has got converted into
chlorine to any serious extent, we have a
process better than cyclical, since the caustic
alkali will be of great value in any paper
or pulp manufactory or bleachery. This pro-
cess should be patented.
