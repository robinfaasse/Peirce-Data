The [u]sulphate of[/u] [del]soda[/del] [ins]lime[/ins] may be eliminated from water by intimately mixing with it a solution of proper strength of some salt precipitating the [u]sulphuric acid[/u], or precipitating the [u]calcium[/u], or both.

The sulphuric acid is precipitated by all soluble salts of strontium, barium, and lead; such as
[u]chloride of barium[/u]
[u]acetate of barium[/u]
[u]nitrate of baryta[/u]
[u]acetate of lead[/u]
[u]nitrate of lead[/u]
[u]formiate of baryta[/u]
[u]formiate of lead[/u].

Of these salts the most suitable is [del]sulphate[/del] [ins]chloride[/ins] of barium, since its price is low, and since the precipitated sulphate of baryta has considerable value. It goes down in an excessively fine powder requiring a day or more to settle.
