$$2\text{HOCl} = 2\text{HCl} + \text{O}_2.$$
The hydrochloric acid thus liberated sets free
another equivalent amount of hypochlorous
acid. Accordingly, a hypochlorite only requires
an indefinitely small amount of acid to sour
it so that it will go on bleaching as long
as anything remains to bleach.

At the same time, another action, though
a slow one, takes place. Namely, the
hydrochloric acid acts upon the hypo-
chlorous acid to make free chlorine and
water, according to the formula
$$\text{HCl} + \text{HOCl} = \text{Cl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}.$$
The molecule of chlorine has the same bleaching
power as the molecule of hypochlorous
acid, acting according to the formula
$$2\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} = 4\text{HCl} + \text{O}_2.$$
