The same thing is still more true of the electrolysis of chloride of aluminium. But the addition of baryta water in excess checks the formation of chlorine.

In the electrolysis of the chlorides sufficient of the chlorates are formed to reduce the bleaching power by from 5 to 12 percent. The chlorates are generally very soluble and deliquescent. But the chlorate of potassa requires at ordinary temperatures about 15 parts of water for its solution, or double that in the cold. If, therefore, the chloride of potassium were used in the electrolysing solution, the chlorate would accumulate until it would pay to recover it by crystallization.
