If it were not for the liability of the chlorine
to escape as gas, and if there were nothing
present for it to act upon except the
coloring-matter, its formation would
be a matter of indifference. But the
chlorine is very violent in its action and
attacks the fibre. Now, just so far as it at-
tacks the fibre it is wasted and fails to
[del]attack the coloring-matter[/del] [ins]by destroying ditto[/ins]; not to speak of
[del]its injurious[/del] effect on the fibre.

This inconvenient production of chlorine
is due to the free acid, and is almost una-
voidable in the presence of free acid. Hence,
the Hermite process necessarily fails, be-
cause it uses salts of magnesia which are
precipitated by alkalis, and in a solution
exposed to electrolysis require free acid
to insure their remaining dissolved.
