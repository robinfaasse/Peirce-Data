It is true that the ratio C/c is [del]not exactly[/del]
known; but it cannot differ greatly from
the value assumed, since it has been ascertained
by Ostwald that the value is nearly this for
Nitric Acid and Potassium hydrate.

The chlorine, then, if not liberated too
rapidly, finds an abundance of free caustic
soda upon which it acts to make hypochlorite
[ins]and chloride[/ins] in equivalent proportions.
The free hydrochloric acid present decomposes
this and forms hypochlorous acid, which is
partially decomposed by the hydrochloric acid
with the reformation of chlorine. But as the
action of hydrochloric acid upon hypochlorous
acid to form chlorine is known to be very slow
compared with the action of chlorine upon
caustic soda to form hypochlorous acid, it
follows that but a small amount of chlorine
will be liberated.

This accords with observation; since
the color and odor of the electrolyzed
sodium chloride both show that extremely
little chlorine is present. The same is true
of the electrolysis of the chlorides of
lithium, potassium, calcium, strontium
