The carbonate of lime is insoluble in neutral
solutions but is soluble as an acid salt in
ordinary water, which contains CO₂ carbonic acid.
It is best precipitated by adding the requisite
quantity of any soluble base to neutralize the
carbonic acid. Of soluble bases, ammonia,
soda, potash, lime, baryta, etc., lime is
the most available.

The carbonate of lime goes down in a gelati-
nous condition, but quickly becomes [ins]a[/ins] crystalline
powder, settling speedily to the bottom.

To get rid of the sulphate of lime, we
apply the [del]simple[/del] familiar law that when
the solutions of two salts are mixed and by "double
decomposition," or the interchange of their metallic
or basic elements an insoluble salt would be
produced, that salt is so produced, and is precipitated.

Therefore, [del]to precipitate[/del] [ins][u]eliminate[/u][/ins] sulphate
of lime, we may do either of two things, viz:

1. We may mix with the water a solution
of a salt whose acid makes an insoluble lime
salt.

2. We may mix with the water a solution of a
salt whose base makes an insoluble sulphate.

Either of these is a familiar method of
proceeding in the laboratory.
