[u]Outline of the Idea of an Almanac.[/u]

[u]Price 75 cts.[/u]

[u]500 pages[/u] A small octavo legibly printed, bound in cloth or paper, & [u]Sewed.[/u]

Four principal parts

1. The calendar and astronomical almanac [ins]30 pages[/ins]
2. Register of the past year, political, social, etc.
3. Encyclopædia.
4. Hand book of everyday information.

The successive numbers of the almanac to be differentiated by laying the stress on different things. (This is done in the Almanac de Gotha.) But the distinctive feature of the first number to be equal balance of different parts.

My hope is that the Almanac may in a few years be changed into a quarterly; when Part 4 especially may be much improved, giving [ins](express trains only)[/ins] then Railway time-tables, and other information needing renewal oftener than once a year.

In no respect is the almanac to be a mere substitute for something better; but every information it gives to be in some respect superior to every other, and more intelligent and answering to real wants better.
