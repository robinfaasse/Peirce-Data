§6 of Chapter IV (in the separate publication in Book II,) is headed "Reduction of Hypotheticals". It begins as follows: "For this purpose we must consider every Conditional Proposition as a universal affirmative categorical Proposition, of which the Terms are entire Propositions, viz. the antecedent answering to the Subject, and the consequent to the Predicate; e.g. to say, "if Louis is a good king, France is likely to prosper", is equivalent to saying, "the case of Louis being a good king, is a case of France being likely to prosper"." For Whately, [del]like[/del] [del]most[/del] almost all logicians of his time, takes the [del]same[/del] view of categorical propositions that is iconized in Euler's diagrams, and that I formally took when I first called the ordinary copula "the copula of inclusion".
