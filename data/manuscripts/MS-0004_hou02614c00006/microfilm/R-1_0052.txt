It seems, at first sight, as if it must be easy to
distinguish between a fundamental assumption
concerning the subject of a deductive reasoning,
and a fundamental convention respecting the
general signs employed in that reasoning. But
the primary subject of a course of deduction is
itself of the nature of a general sign; since there
can be no necessary reasoning about real
things except so far as it is assumed that certain
general signs are applicable to them. It therefore
becomes a delicate matter to draw a just line
between the subject of the reasoning and the
machinery of the reasoning. The subject of geome-
