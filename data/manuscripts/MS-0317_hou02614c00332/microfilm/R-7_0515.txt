Mr. Editor:

The philosophical journals, the world over, are just now brimming over [del]as you know,[/del] with pragmatism and antipragmatism. [ins]The number of [del]Leonardo[/del] [ins]Leonardo[/ins] that reaches me this morning has an admirable piece on the subject by a writer of genius and of literary skill, Giovanni Papini. Yesterday brought news of discussions along the same line in New Zealand.[/ins] Often, however, one hears [del]glib[/del] utterances that betray complete misunderstanding of this new ingredient of the thought of our times; so that I gladly accept your invitation to explain what pragmatism really is, how it came into being, and whither it is tending. [del]Any philosophical doctrine that should be completely new could hardly fail to prove completely false; but the rivulets at the head of the river of pragmatism are easily traced back to almost any desired antiquity.[/del]
