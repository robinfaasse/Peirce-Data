The Multitudes are [del]classes, class-names[/del] characters
of [u]collections[/u]. So that each multitude is applicable
to a collection of collections, and [u]multitude[/u] itself is
applicable to a collection of collections of collections.

The different collections of each multitude are
characterized by being in the relation of the last page
to the same collections.

Given any two collections whatever one of them
must be in that relation to the other. For if not
there would be a [del]relation[/del] collection of pairs which would
not constitute the pairs of any relation, which is
absurd & contrary to the idea of a relation; for a
relation is nothing but any collection of pairs.

But it is impossible (I believe) to express this
reasoning in any logical system yet invented, for
the reason that no system of algebra or graphs
can analytically express "every possible collection
of objects formed from the objects of the collection q."
The impossibility of this will become apparent
when we come to study multitude further.
