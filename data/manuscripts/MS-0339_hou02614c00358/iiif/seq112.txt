and so
if $\Sigma A_i \equiv \Sigma (A_{i, C_{B_i}})$ [del]then[/del] $\Sigma B_i = \Sigma (B_i, C_{A_i}^{-1})$
and conversely

or if $\Sigma'$ denotes the logical sum
if $\Sigma_i A_i \equiv \Sigma_i A_{i, \Sigma'_i C_{B_i}}$ then $\Sigma_i B_i \equiv \Sigma_i B_{i, \Sigma'_i C_{A_i}^{-1}}$

Now $C$ may be so taken that
$C_{x+y} \equiv C_x [del]C_y[/del] C_y$
or so that
$C_{x+y} \equiv C_x [ins],[/ins] C_y$
