If my present view, held for four or five years, is right that
Abduction Deduction Induction or Primarian, Secundarian,
and Tertian, then there ought to be two types of Deduction
& three of Induction — & the three figures of syllogism must sink
into insignificance.
Now I don't recognize any such two types of deduction.
I have since 1882 and earlier recognized three kinds of
induction; but have never taken them to be fundamental.
They are extensive, comprehensive, & protensive. I always
considered the last to be a sort of complication of the first.
If the premiss of induction is a symbol of the conclusion,
then it should be a term, proposition, or syllogism.
If the premiss of deduction is an index of the conclusion
it should be a term or a proposition.
But if the premiss of abduction is an icon of the conclusion
it can only be a term.
A prepositional deduction should be a fact not merely indi-
cating another fact to be true but indicating what sort of a fact
it is.

considered as an argument [del]tend[/del] [ins]to[/ins] prove its conclusion but should tend
to prove something to be true of that conclusion and an argumenta-
tive induction should tend to prove [del]as[/del] reason for the conclusion
