In place of §6 on the last page but one put the following:
§6.
A general is an object which however far it may be determined
by further general definition yet leaves room for distinctions
sufficient to distinguish all the members of any multitude
whatsoever.
Or better. A general is an object such that everything of which
it can be essentially predicated leaves room for distinctions sufficient
to discriminate between all the members of any multitude whatso-
ever.
Or better: A genus is an object such that everyone of the species
which are its substantial parts leaves room etc.
N.B. If a collection of individuals forms a part of a genus, its general
character is in so far destroyed, and it is only imperfectly a genus. See
Kant on the law of affinity.
