The doctrine of substantive possibility laid down in
my article of Jan 1897 is certainly correct.
If i refers to a universe of substances, a proposition of the form
$\Sigma_{i} m_{i}$
is possible, unless its impossibility appears by some contra -
diction. [It cannot if so expressed offend against the
principle of excluded middle.]
The same is true of a proposition of the form $\Sigma_{i}\Sigma_{j} l_{ij}$
Consequently all propositions of the forms $\Pi_{i} m_{i}$ and $\Pi_{i}\Pi_{j} l_{ij}$
are impossible unless their denials involve contradiction.
But for the reason there explained propositions of the
form $\Pi_{i}\Sigma_{j} l_{ij}$ are possible unless they involve contradic-
tion, and consequently propositions of the form $\Sigma_{i}\Pi_{j} l_{ij}$
are impossible unless their denials involve contradiction.

clear. If p means [del]possessing[/del] [ins]by[/ins] as a character [del]by[/del] are we
to say that
$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{i} P_{\alpha i}$
is possible? There is no contradiction involved; yet I
am inclined to think that
$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{i} \overline{P_{\alpha i}}$
is the truth. Certainly everyone would agree that
$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{i} P_{\alpha i}$
is true and
$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{i} \overline{P_{\alpha i}}$
is quite ridiculous, although there is no contradiction
