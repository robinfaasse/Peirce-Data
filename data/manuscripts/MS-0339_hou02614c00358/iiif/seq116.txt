It would seem to follow

    since [ins]$C_{\Sigma x} \equiv \Sigma C_x$[/ins]

    that $C_{x+x+x+x} \equiv C_{n,x} \equiv n, C_x$

But it plainly does not follow. Or rather that
$n$ must be $\equiv 1$.

It is true, however, that

    $C_{n,x} \equiv v, C_{n}, C_x$

for [del]$C_{n,x+u} = C$[/del] putting $u \equiv (1-n),x$

    $C_{n,x + u} \equiv C_x \equiv C_{n,x} + C_u$

And so $C_n \equiv C_{n,x} + C_{u'}$

    $\therefore C_n, C_x \equiv C_{n,x} + C_u, C_{u'}$

Hence putting $v \equiv \text{[del]}C_{n,x}\text{[/del]} (1-C_{u,n,x}), (1-C_{(1-x),n})$

    $v, C_n, C_x \equiv C_{n,x}$
