The second class of this triplet is that of signs of actual
objects. This is the familiar class of concrete signs. Here 281r
again it would be easy to draw subtle distinctions, since
obviously no word in use is absolutely concrete. But until
we are prepared greatly to multiply our classes, this must
be taken as sufficiently [del]precise[/del] exact. In regard to
actuality, or existence, which two conceptions are the
same for present purposes, it is to be remarked that
it consists in the [del]action[/del] brute action of one fact upon
another. Take the existence of a chair or table. By
saying that it exists we mean that it is in the one
universe of existence [del]and this[/del]. Now it would not be
in the same universe with anything with which it did
not react. If A never acts on B, nor B on A, they are
not in the same universe. If this is so for five mi-
nutes, during those five minutes they are not in the
same universe. If A acts on B but B has no action on
A, they have different modes of being and therefore
do not coexist.
