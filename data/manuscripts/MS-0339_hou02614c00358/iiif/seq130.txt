If $e^x \equiv y$
one must write $x \equiv \log_e y$
But we may have also
$e^z \equiv y$ though $z \not\equiv x$ [del]$x \not\equiv z$[/del]
$\therefore z \equiv \log_e y$
$\therefore \log_e y$ must be an indeterminate expression.
$\log_e e \equiv 1$
$\log_e 1 \equiv 0$ generally
& always $\log_e 1^n \equiv 0$

Now in order to get at formula for developement
without which Boole's Calculus is nothing, let us
see if we can get at something like Taylor's Theorem.
Let $\phi x + \Delta_x \phi x \equiv \phi(x + \Delta_x x)$
where $\Delta_x x$ is whatever we please
Let us rather take
[fig]figure[/fig] $\Delta_x \phi x \equiv \phi(x + \Delta x) - \phi x$
where $\Delta x$ is anything not $\infty$
