The Fermatian inference may be extended to relations
not transitive
For suppose the relation r to be of such a character that
if anything A is r to anything B, then
either A is not r to any r to B,
or, given any character, Q, whatever, there is something,
A', [del] which [/del] resembling A in [del] that [/del] [ins] some [/ins] respect to Q and being r to some-
thing, B', which differs from A in respect to Q, while A' is not
r to any r to B', but is r to B.

Then the following reasoning is valid:
Whatever is r to X without being r to any r to X is a P;
But whatever is r to X and at the same time is r to a P
without being r to any r to that P, is a P;
$\therefore$ Whatever is r to X is a P.

For taking X as the B of the definition and an r to X [ins] that [/ins]
[del] is not an r to any r to X [/del] as A, the first alternative consequent
is excluded and as A some r to an X which is not P, if there
be any. Then, by the first premiss, A is an r to an r to X, which
excludes the first alternative consequent of the definition. Take
the being P as the character Q. Then what resembles A in this respect
is not P and what differs from A in this respect is P. Consequently
the second premiss declares that whatever is [ins] [del] r to B [/del] [/ins], and is r to any
thing that [ins] B' [/ins] differs from A in respect to Q, while itself agreeing with
A in respect to Q is not r to an r to that B'. This conflicts with the
second consequent of the definition. Hence there is not such A as
we have supposed. That is, there is not r to an X which is not
P. Q.E.D.
