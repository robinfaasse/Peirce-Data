From (15) & (17) we have

$$1 \cdot 0 \equiv 1$$

Hence

$$l_1 \equiv l \text{ to something}$$
$$l_0 \equiv 0 \times 1 \equiv \times (1 + 0) \equiv 0 \cdot l_1 \equiv 0 \cdot 0$$
or $l$ to nothing

Now if we let

$$l'x \equiv 1 - (1 - l)x$$

$$l'_1 \equiv 1 - (1 - 1) \times 1 \equiv 1 - 0 \times 1 \equiv 1 - 0 \equiv 1$$
$$l'_0 \equiv 1 - (1 - 0) \times 1 \equiv 1 - 1 \times 1 \equiv 1 - 1 \equiv 0$$
$$l'_1 \equiv 1 - (1 - l)1 \equiv 1 - \text{other than } l \text{ to something} \equiv \text{other than } l \text{ to everything} \equiv l_0$$
$$l'_0 \equiv 1 - (1 - l)0 \equiv 1 - \text{other than } l \text{ to everything} \equiv (1 - l)_1$$

Then $l'x$ might [del]be written[/del] [ins]$l^x$[/ins]

and we should have

$$1^x \equiv 1 \quad 0^x \equiv 0$$

$$l^1 \equiv l_0 \text{ which might be written } l$$

$$l^0 \equiv 1 - l_0 \equiv (1 - l)_1 \text{ which might be written } le$$
