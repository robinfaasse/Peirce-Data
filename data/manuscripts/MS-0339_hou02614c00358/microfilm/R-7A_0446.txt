The doctrine of substantive possibility laid down in
my article of [date]Jan 1897[/date] is certainly correct.

If $i$ refers to a universe of substances, a proposition of the form
$$\Sigma_i m_i$$
is possible, unless its impossibility appears by some contra-
diction. [It cannot if so expressed offend against the
principle of excluded middle.]

The same is true of a proposition of the form $\Sigma_i \Sigma_j l_{ij}$
Consequently all propositions of the forms $\Pi_i m_i$ and $\Pi_i \Pi_j l_{ij}$
are impossible unless their denials involve contradiction.

But for the reason there explained propositions of the
form $\Pi_i \Sigma_j l_{ij}$ are possible unless they involve contradic-
tion, and consequently propositions of the form $\Sigma_i \Pi_j l_{ij}$
are impossible unless their denials involve contradiction.

As to non-relative characters the matter is [del]not so[/del]
clear. If $P$ means possessing [del]as[/del] a character [del]are we[/del]
[del]to say that[/del]
$$\Sigma_\alpha \Pi_i P_{\alpha i}$$
is possible? There is no contradiction involved; yet I
am inclined to think that
$$\Pi_i \Sigma_\alpha \bar{P}_{\alpha i}$$
is the truth. Certainly everyone would agree that
$$\Pi_i \Sigma_\alpha P_{\alpha i}$$
is true and
$$\Sigma_\alpha \Pi_i \bar{P}_{\alpha i}$$
is quite ridiculous, although there is no contradiction
at all. But
$$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_\alpha P_{\alpha i} (\bar{P}_{\alpha j} + l_{ij})$$
[del]is equivalent to[/del] $\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_\alpha P_{\alpha i} \bar{P}_{\alpha j} + l_{ij}$
or even $\Pi_i \Sigma_\alpha \Pi_j P_{\alpha i} (\bar{P}_{\alpha j} + l_{ij})$ [fig]circular diagram with intersecting lines[/fig]
