One element here which is accidental is that the premises shall express a real experience.

$S'S''S'''$ owing to their genus are $\mathcal{M}$
$\therefore \mathcal{M}$ has the common characters of $S'S''S'''$

$S'S''S'''$ owing to their genus are $\mathcal{M}$
$\therefore \mathcal{M}$ (owing to its genus?) has the common character of $S'S''S'''$

$S'S''S'''$ have a common character whence it follows that they are $\mathcal{M}$
$\therefore \mathcal{M}$ has a character whence it follows that it has the common character of $S'S''S'''$

The conditions of the existence of some common character of $S'S''S'''$ involves their having the common characters of $\mathcal{M}$.
