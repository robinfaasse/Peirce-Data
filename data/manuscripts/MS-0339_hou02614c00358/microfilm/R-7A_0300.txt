[u]Existential Graphs[/u]
[u]a system of logical expression[/u]

The constitutive conventions of existential graphs.

1. A certain place [del]is[/del] [ins]maybe[/ins] called the "sheet of the graph" and what is there written may be called the graph.

We pretend that the graph is a general description of a certain recognized state of things.

We [del]pretend[/del] [ins]only[/ins] "pretend" that it is so; for our purpose is merely [del]to study[/del] formal logic, and the graph is a mere specimen of an assertion for whose matter we care nothing.

In the contents of consciousness we recognize three sorts of elements, Firstness, Secondness, Thirdness. [del]So[/del] A First is whatever it is in itself regardless of all else whether exterior to it or parts of it. It is feeling-quality. [del]A[/del] Second is [del]what it is[/del] [ins]what it is depends[/ins] partly on another regardless of any third; and thus of any reason. It is brute reaction. [del]And[/del] Third is [del]what it is depends[/del] on two other things between which it mediates. Firstness is feeling-quality; secondness is brute reaction; thirdness is mediation. Secondness is generous or degenerate. [del]A degenerate secondness is such a pairing as gives to each member of the pair only such a character as it would retain were the other nonexistent. Generous secondness imparts a character to which the existence of the other member is essential.[/del] Generous secondness is an accident of individuals. Existence is that mode of being which is constituted by generous secondness. It only belongs to individuals. An individual is an object determined
