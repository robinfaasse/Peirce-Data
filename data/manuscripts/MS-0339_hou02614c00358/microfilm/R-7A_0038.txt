Such a thing is impossible.

Let us say then that it means nothing to say that a thing has an inconceivable quality. An inconceivable quality — one inconceivable by every being & absolutely — is no quality.

That which has no qualities is nothing.

$\therefore$ That which is [del]absolutely[/del] inconceivable is nothing.

To say that a word has meaning is to say that a conception corresponds to it.

To say that we have a general conception of a triangle for instance is to say that upon the occasion of a triangle being presented to the imagination or in experience a certain feeling complicated in a certain way arises. We have no [del]feeling[/del] [ins]conception[/ins] therefore of that of which no determination can be presented in the imagination.

Consequently, though we may undoubtedly mean something by the inconceivable we can mean nothing by an absolutely [del]inconceivable[/del] [ins]unconceivable[/ins] predicate.

Hence such a predicate is no predicate.
