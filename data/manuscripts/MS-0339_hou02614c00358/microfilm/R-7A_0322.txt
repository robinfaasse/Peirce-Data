[u]The Dozen Basic Formal Rules.[/u]

I. [del]Some possible graph cannot be written unenclosed.[/del]
[del]II. Any graph once written, may remain as written.[/del]
[del]III. Any written graph may be erased.[/del]
[del]IV. Graphs that might be written separately, may be written together.[/del]
V. [del]Any graph which, standing alone, might be transformed into another, may be so transformed[/del] [ins]formed[/ins] [del]wherever[/del] [ins]it is unenclosed, and detached[/ins]
VI. Every graph is equivalent to an enclosure containing some graph.
VII. Every graph [del]may[/del] [ins]is equivalent to[/ins] [del]have[/del] two ovals drawn round it.
[del]VIII. If any partial or entire graph, A, is relatively transformable by the insertion of another graph B[/del]
VIII. If any graph is transformable [del]by insertion[/del] [ins]into another[/ins], then [del]the[/del] [ins]an enclosure containing only[/ins] the transformed graph is transformable into an enclosure containing only the untransformed graph.
IX. Any graph of the kind called an index may be written unenclosed.
X. Any two indices transformable into one another may be joined by a heavy line, such line being a graph.
XI. Every heavy line can have some index attached to it.
XII. The shape of a heavy line (especially whether a furcation occurs at an enclosed or unenclosed part is indifferent.
