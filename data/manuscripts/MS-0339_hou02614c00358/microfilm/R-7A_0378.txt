In place of §6 on the last page but one put the following:

§6.

A [u]general[/u] is an object which however far it may be determined by further general definition yet leaves room for distinctions [del]sufficient to distinguish all the members of any multitude[/del] whatsoever.

Or, better. A general is an object [del]such that everything of which[/del] [del]it can be essentially predicated leaves room for distinctions sufficient[/del] [del]to discriminate between all the members of any multitude whatso-[/del]ever.

Or, better: A [u]genus[/u] is an object such that everyone of [del]the species[/del] [ins]which are its substantive parts[/ins] leaves room etc.

N.B. If a collection of individuals forms a part of a genus, its general character is in so far destroyed, [del]and it is only imperfectly a genus.[/del] See Kant on the law of affinity.
