[date]DEC 16 1901[/date]

The algebra of the copula is deducible from the definition of the copula. This definition positively asserts that some things can be written.

First says if $q$ then $p \prec q$
Either $p$ or $p \prec q$

For $q$ put $p$, then this becomes if $p$ then $p \prec p$
[del]Either $p$ or $p \prec p$[/del]

Therefore $p \prec p$ is positively asserted to be permissible scriptible.
But the definition of the copula does not [del]positively state that anything can be written[/del] [ins]there is anything that cannot be written[/ins]. It only distinguishes between what we know to be scriptible and what we do not know to be scriptible.

The definition seems to be
(1) If $q$, then $p \prec q$
(2) Either $p$ or $p \prec q$
(3) If $p$ and $p \prec q$, then $q$
