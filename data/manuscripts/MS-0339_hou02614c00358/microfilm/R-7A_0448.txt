But in regard to relations the case seems to be other-
wise. Shall we not say
$$\Sigma_g \Pi_i \Pi_j r_{gij} \quad ?$$
No nothing but a relation of second intention.
Second intentions must be studied separately.

But are we not to say
$$\Pi_i \Sigma_g \Pi_j r_{gij} \quad ? \quad \text{Or shall we say } \Sigma_i \Pi_g \Sigma_j r_{gij}$$
Are we not to say
$$\Sigma_g \Pi_i \overline{r_{gii}} \quad ?$$
It seems ridiculous to say that this is impossible.
Yet if we admit it why does not this constitute a
character. Let the relation be that of self-generation.
Is not self-generation a character? Again if we
admit this as possible ($\text{I mean } \Sigma_g \Pi_i r_{gii}$) then we
must say that $\Pi_g \Sigma_i r_{gii}$ is impossible.

It appears that one non relative index does not
count after an index of relation.

$$\Sigma_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k \Pi_g \Sigma_\alpha \Sigma_\beta \quad p_{\alpha i} r_{gij} + p_{\beta k} \overline{r_{gjk}}$$
$$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Pi_g \Sigma_\alpha \Sigma_\beta \quad \overline{p}_{\alpha i} + r_{gij} + \overline{p}_{\beta i}$$

It is evident that the form of the Boolean has
something to do with it.
