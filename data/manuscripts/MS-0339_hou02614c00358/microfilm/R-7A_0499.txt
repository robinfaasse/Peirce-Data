[u]A. Signs are divisible according to their matter.[/u]

1. There are signs each of which has all its significance in its abstract quality, regardless
of the circumstances of its occurrence. Such a sign maybe termed a [u]Qualisign[/u].

2. There are signs each of which from its very nature can occur but once and its signifi-
cance lies in the circumstances of that one occurrence. Such a sign maybe called a
Sinsign. But [del]this[/del] it will be allowable to use this term a little loosely for a sign
which is an individual thing. Such a sign maybe termed a Sinsign [ins](excluding the first
sense, or
singill, or
simplex etc)[/ins]

3. There are signs each of which [del]is incapable in its nature of[/del] [ins]active[/ins]
existing, but [del]which is[/del] governing things which do exist and
causing their existence to signify something about the cha-
racter of what exists. Such a sign maybe called a [u]Legisign[/u].
(Is is not necessary that that which is signified should [del]have to[/del] be general?)

[u]B. Signs are divisible according to their Objects[/u]

[u]a. According to their immediate objects.[/u] The immediate object
is that object which the sign [del]of[/del] creates in representing it.

1. There are signs of each of which the immediate object is only a
possible presentment of a dynamic object, a fragment of it, the
rest being held in reserve, so that there is nothing [del]of or in the[/del]
[del]immediate object to prevent contradictory attributes being predicated[/del]
[del]of it.[/del] Thus "A certain man" [del]maybe rich[/del] [ins]turns out to be[/ins] rich. He may be poor. [del]This is owing[/del]
[del]to the reserve which makes the immediate object not any entire[/del]
[del]dynamic object but[/del] Such a sign maybe termed an [u]indefinite sign[/u].

2. There are signs of each of which the immediate object holds
nothing in reserve [ins]which the utterer can afterward specify limits to[/ins], and allows nor allows any freedom of interpretation, the imme-
diate object precisely [del]covering[/del] denoting the dynamical object.
Such a sign is called a [u]Singular Sign[/u], a term to which in the use
of which a certain latitude must be allowed, however, or else
there will be no occasion on which it can be applied.

3. There are signs of each of which the immediate object is
represented as exchangeable for any existent within
specified [del]or[/del] [ins]and[/ins] understood limits. [del]This[/del] [ins]It[/ins] maybe termed a
[u]distributive Sign[/u].
