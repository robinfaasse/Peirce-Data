<u>Combination of [del]Arithmetic[/del] [ins]arithmetic[/ins] & Boole's Calculus</u>

Given $p a , \bar{m} = 0$ where $p = \frac{1}{2}$
$q a , \bar{n} = 0$ " $q = \frac{1}{2}$
$m , n = 0$
$x , m = 0$
$x , \bar{a} = 0$

We have by ordinary processes

(1) $q a , \bar{n} + p a , n + p a , x + x , \bar{a} = 0$
[del]But for $p = q = \frac{1}{2}$[/del]

And from this

(2) $p a , q a = 0$
But [del]for $p = q = \frac{1}{2}$ $p + q = 1$[/del]
$p + q = p + q - p , q$
$\therefore p + q = p + q - p , q$
& since $p + q = 1$
$p + q = 1 - p , q$
But $p + q = 1 - \bar{p} , \bar{q}$
$= 1 - \bar{p} , \bar{q}$
Hence $p , q = \bar{p} , \bar{q}$
Hence $p a , q a = 0 = \bar{p} a , \bar{q} a$
