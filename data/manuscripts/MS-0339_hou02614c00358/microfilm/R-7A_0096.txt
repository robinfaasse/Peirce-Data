The form of a "superiority" is
$(1 + \breve{l}) \cdot l \breve{l}$

Required to find a form of "succession"
that is a superiority such that either $A$ is
superior to $B$ or $B$ to $A$.

The negative of it must implicate some
relation $b$ and also $\breve{b}$.

Then relation itself must be an
aggregate. Now how can an aggregate
be transitive? [del]And how can it be a difference?[/del]

To be a difference each aggregant must
be a difference. C'est [del]e[/del][ins]é[/ins]lementaire.

To be transitive
$(A \mathbin{\Psi} B) (A \mathbin{\Psi} B)$
$= AA \mathbin{\Psi} BA \mathbin{\Psi} AB \mathbin{\Psi} BB$

[del]Each of these four terms must be
included under $A + B$[/del]
