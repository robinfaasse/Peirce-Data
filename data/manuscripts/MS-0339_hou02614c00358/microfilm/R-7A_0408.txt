To prove that if [del]$(a \prec b) \prec c$[/del] [ins]$(a + b) \prec c$[/ins] then $(\bar{a} \prec b) \prec c$

For if $\prod_t (a \prec t)$ then $(a \prec b)$

But $b \prec b$

And therefore, by definition of Mult. (old $4$ of Class p 33),

If $\prod_t (a \prec t)$ $(a + b) \prec b$

i.e. $\bar{a} \prec (a + b) \prec b$ [fig]arrow pointing right[/fig]

$\therefore (a + b) \prec \bar{a} \prec b$

$\therefore$ By theorem $[(\bar{a} \prec b) \prec c] \prec [(a + b) \prec c]$

Another proof: (differently arranged merely)

$\bar{a} \prec \prod_t (a \prec t) \prec a \prec b$

$\therefore a \prec \bar{a} \prec b$

$\therefore [(\bar{a} \prec b) \prec c] \prec a \prec c$

$b \prec b$ is true

$\therefore \prod_t t \prec (b \prec b)$

$\therefore \bar{a} \prec b \prec b$

$\therefore b \prec \bar{a} \prec b$

$\therefore [(\bar{a} \prec b) \prec c] \prec b \prec c$

$\therefore$ By definition $[(\bar{a} \prec b) \prec c] \prec (a + b) \prec c$

[marginalia]The first is the better.[/marginalia]
