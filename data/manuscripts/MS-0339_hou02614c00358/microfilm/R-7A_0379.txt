[del]The Fermatian inference is this: any inference proceeding
upon the principle of the following[/del]

Whatever is $r$ to, but not $r$ to any $r$ to, $X$, is [del]a[/del] $P$;
But whatever, being $r$ to $X$, is $r$ to, but not $r$ to any $r$ to, $P$, is [del]a[/del] $P$;
$\therefore$ Whatever is $r$ to $X$ is [del]a[/del] $P$

To justify this inference, it [ins]seems to be[/ins] [del]necessary and[/del] sufficient that
$r$ should be such a relation that

[del]If anything, $A$, is $r$ to, but not $r$ to $r$[/del]
If anything, $A$, is $r$ to an $r$ to, and is also $r$ to, anything, $B$,
then, taking any character, $Q$, whatsoever, there is something, $A'$,
[del]which is $r$ to $B$, this[/del] [ins]resembling[/ins] $A$ in respect to the character, $Q$,
and also being $r$ to, but not $r$ to any $r$ to, something, $B'$, which
differs from $A$ in respect to the character, $Q'$.

First to show it is sufficient. If the Fermatian inference
were false, while this condition was satisfied, then because of the
first premise, there would be something both $r$ to, and $r$ to an $r$ to, $X$
which was not $P$. Identify this something with $A$ and $X$ with $B$. Let [del]being
not $P$[/del] be the character $Q$. Then the condition is that
Something [ins]that is not $P$[/ins] [del]is[/del] $r$ to $X$ and $r$ to, but not $r$ to any $r$ to, something that is $P$, [del]which
is contrary to the second premise. Thus if the conclusion is false a premise must
be false, and if the premises are true the conclusion must be true.[/del]
[del]Second to show that it is necessary. Suppose the conclusion always follows from
the premises. Then if the conclusion is false while the first premise is true, the
second premise must be false.[/del]
