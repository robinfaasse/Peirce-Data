[u]Chapter II[/u]. [u]Method[/u], continued. The law of the development
[del]of fruitful[/del] conceptions. In what manner their first
germs present themselves. The human mind has a
natural bias toward true ideas of certain orders.
How this may have come about. [del]But[/del] Yet the first
origin of ideas can only be referred to chance. They
promptly sink into oblivion if the mind is unprepared
for them. If they find other ideas suitable, a welding
process takes place. This welding of ideas is the
only law of [del]logical[/del] intellectual development. Its peculiar
essential character, a gentle force. If too strong
it would suicide. It acts two ways, causes ideas
to develop into higher ideas, and [del]to[/del] be filled out
in detail.
