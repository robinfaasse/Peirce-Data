[u]Chapter II[/u]. [u]Method, continued[/u]. The law of the devel-
opment of fruitful conceptions. [del]In what manner[/del]
their first germs present themselves. There is [del]a[/del] absolutely
no account to be given of the truly germinal ideas
in their first origin, except that they occur [ins]by[/ins]
chance. [ins][del]Although it is to be remarked that the mind has some bias toward true ideas.[/del][/ins] But if the mind is unprepared,
they immediately go into oblivion. If they find
other ideas to which they can attach themselves, a
welding process takes place. This welding is
the only law of mental development. Its peculiar
character, a gentle force. It acts in causing
the growing together of ideas to form higher ideas,
— a process roughly called generalization; and also
to assimilate one idea into another, so as to
fill out the detail of this latter.

This chapter draws upon the history of science,
upon psychology, and upon mathematics.
