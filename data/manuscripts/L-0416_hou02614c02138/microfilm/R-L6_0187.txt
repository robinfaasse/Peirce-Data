The manager. He returned all my letters, yours
included, on Saturday, & read all you have
to say! In the afternoon he brought them to me, &
explained, that he will be useless for him to
try to make a market for the St. Lawrence
[ins]floating[/ins] stock unless it is all absolutely locked up
in his keeping. That I can readily understand,
for it would not do for any manipulator to
risk the slightest chance of having anybody's
shares dumped upon him when he places a
bid in the street with one broker — buying
some other broker ready to make a sale,
& so establish quotations & trading. Yes, I too
would think it a necessity to have the
manipulator have full charge of all
stock.

But as to the chances of [del]making[/del] building
up a real credit & market for the
stock, I really dare not express an opinion.
