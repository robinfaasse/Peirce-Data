14 April 1899

My dear Peirce

In the midst of my turmoil it is a pleasure to hear from you, and to see plainly that although you have been so ill, you now, in poor Tom Hood's words, "Smell the rose above the mould."

I too have been quite ill this winter, from neurasthenia, following a fresh attack of the grippe. After my trip to Milford I rapidly improved, and things went well until Christmas, when my work and the grippe together became too much for me. I am now gaining, but must acknowledge that I begin to feel my age.

So forgive this typewritten letter, and let me say most heartily that I will not miss any chance of turning pilgrims in the direction of what my secretary — who like myself is lost in admiration of your craftily elegant circular — declares must be the New Jerusalem.

I have given one to Easton, and have told him to speed the cause. Few brokers, under the present excitement, can get away as far as Milford, but my acquaintance, as you know, is not restricted to the stock exchange.

This summer I must work steadily upon my "American Anthology," now slowly going through the press, and I cannot absent myself from my extensive collection of American [ins]verse;[/ins] [del]though[/del] most of it is of no use except to the owner. Otherwise, I surely would spend a vacation with Mrs Stedman at Arisbe Villa.

Ever sincerely yours
Edmund C Stedman

Mr C. S. Peirce
Milford, Pa.

P.S. When you visit town, look in at 16 Broad Street.
