To solve an algebraic numerical equation

[del]The equation must be known[/del]
If there are several possible solutions
all on a par, no general rule can
lead to any one solution rather than
another. Therefore the equation
[del]had better[/del] [ins]must[/ins] be put into such a form
that one solution is smaller than
any other; and that solution is sought.
For example; if the equation is $x^2 = 10$
that is if we seek the square root of 10
[del]there[/del] are two equal values, one positive
the other negative. Let us, however, seek
the positive value. It is near 3. Let
us therefore write $x = 3 + y$ $(3+y)^2 = 10$
or $y^2 + 6y + 9 = 10$ $y^2 + 6y = 1$
When this is done and the equation is
brought to the form
$Ay^n + By^{n-1} + Cy^{n-2} + \text{etc} + My + N = 0$
[del]divide[/del] by $-N$ and so bring it to the form
$1 = -\frac{A}{N}y^n - \frac{B}{N}y^{n-1} + \text{etc}$
or say $1 = ay^n + by^{n-1} + \text{etc}$
