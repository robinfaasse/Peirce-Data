My genera of Induction are, first,
a lowest, Crude Induction, which on the
basis of always having found a certain kind
of result of a certain kind of conditions
concludes that it always will be so. We can-
not avoid sometimes reasoning in this
uncritical way, and there is a sort of de-
fence of it in the fact that any error into
which it leads us will eventually get
corrected. But we [del]dont[/del] ought to avoid
as much as possible beliefs that are des-
tined one day to be corrected with a bang
or a revolution. The best genus of In-
duction is where we judge by a carefully
selected sample consisting of objects capa-
ble of being counted. If they are distinct
[ins]individual[/ins] things so much the better, other things being
equal. But that should not be insisted on.
They may very well be objects measurable
though not numerable as individuals so so
many. This I call Quantitative Induction.
It is subject to two Rules. The first is that a
sample is fairly representative of a class only if
it has been drawn from that class by a method
which would in the long run give every unit
of the class an equal chance of being drawn;
