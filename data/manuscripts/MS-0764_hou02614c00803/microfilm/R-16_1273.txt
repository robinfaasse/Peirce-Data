Of course, I utterly condemn a lot of stuff
in the books on probabilities, which I will
fully refute in an appendix to my book. What
I attack are the inverse probabilities and in
particular Laplace's procedures concerning
them, and all such statements as that if a
phenomenon has been observed on $n$ successive
occasions the probability is $\frac{n+1}{n+2}$ that it will
be observed on the $(n+1)$th occasion, and La-
place's conception of probability as the pro-
portion of knowledge in a statement (though
he does not say anything so definite, but
proceeds in vague sort of reasoning [del]about[/del]
founded on inadmissible assumption of
hypotheses being [ins]"également possibles."[/ins])
