[u]A brief Synopsis of C. S. Peirce's
Principles of Philosophy.[/u]

[u]Of[/u] whatever has to be planned, the plan is determined by the use to which the construction is to be put. Since, therefore, every book (except such as Dr. Carus writes) has to be planned, it is proper to begin by considering to what use the book is to be put. We must find out what need there is of philosophy, before one can define philosophy. To find out our needs, we have to review our situation. Hence it is, that I devote my first volume to a [del]Review of the Leading[/del] [ins]Ideas of the Nineteenth Century.[/ins]

The majority of mankind are obliged to bend all their powers to the task of supporting existence; and can think of nothing else. All who rise above this condition do so by virtue of their superior knowledge of psychology, and [del]of[/del] [ins]of[/ins] their stronger passion for cosening their fellows.
