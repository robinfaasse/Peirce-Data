We can ascertain by algebra whether the answer to any given question, as "Whether Elijah was caught up to heaven", is contained in what we already know. For that purpose, take any two numbers at will, say [u]v[/u] and [u]f[/u], to represent what is true (verum) and what is false (falsum.) Let [u]x[/u] denote the proposition[ins], or statement,[/ins] that "Elijah was caught up to heaven", and if we find [u]x[/u] = [u]v[/u], this means that the proposition is true, but if we find [u]x[/u] = [u]f[/u], this means that the proposition is false. The numbers [u]v[/u] and [u]f[/u] are different and therefore [u]x[/u] cannot be equal to both at once. This represents the principle that a proposition cannot be at once true and false, which is called the [u]Principle of Contradiction[/u]. But every proposition is either true or false: this is called the [u]Principle of Excluded Middle[/u], (there is no halfway between truth and falsity.)
