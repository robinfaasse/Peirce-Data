[u]Chapter I.[/u]
[u]Of the Classification of the Sciences.[/u]

This book makes pretension to being a scientific inquiry
in a pretty rigorous sense. But no such claim is made for
this chapter, the purpose of which is to help the reader to
seize the precise meaning of the question which the book
aims toward solving. It must fall short of the requirements
of science in two very important particulars. Namely, a
scientific statement ought to be perfectly definite. Suppose,
for example, it be said that something is "bright red". In order
that this should be a proper thing to say in a scientific descrip-
tion, it is not necessary that the writer should specify exactly
how bright. There is a certain range of color which can be
called bright, and it is understood to be probable that the
