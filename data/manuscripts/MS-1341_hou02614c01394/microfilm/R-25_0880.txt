4. The classification of Francis Bacon is chiefly interesting owing to its having been more or less followed by [del]semi-[/del] literary writers. [del]It is giv[gap][/del] [del]in the Second Book and[/del] It forms the subject of his De dignitate et Augmentis Scientiarum. He finds [ins](lib II. cap i)[/ins] the faculties of the soul to be three, Memory, Fancy, and Reason, and corresponding to these are the three principal branches of human doctrine History, Poetry (i.e. fiction), and Philosophy. History ([u]II[/u] i) is divided into Natural History and Civil History (including Ecclesiastic and Literary). Natural History ([u]II[/u] iii) is divided into the Narrative and the Inductive, and also, by a cross-classification, into the History of Generations (of free nature), of Pretergenerations (monsters in nature) and of Arts.
