EOLOTROPIC, a. (Gr. aiolos, nimble, and tropos, turn.)
Possessing different properties in different directions.
Said of an individual body and of a kind of substance.

EOLOTROPY, n. The character of being eolotropic.

EPACT, n. (Gr. epakte, epact, intercalary.) The age of the
calendar moon at the beginning of the year. The epact was
used at an early age for calculating the date of Easter.
At the time of the invention of the Gregorian calendar,
it had fallen into disuse, but was then revived by Lilius
and embodied in the new calendar, The so-called Julian
epact was invented at that time, as a means of calculating
the Gregorian epact. This has led to the erroneous statement
that Lilius invented the epact.

I never before heard of a menstrual epact, and as

the compiler has misunderstood some hypothetical account
of the origin of the epact.)

EPAGOGE. Strike out the whole article. This is simply the
Greek for induction, and there is no such word in use in
English. Writers of course have introduced the Greek word
in the course of an English composition, but not often enough
to warrant its being inserted into an English dictionary.

EPAGOGIC. For the definition, put:

Pertaining to induction, which in Greek is epagoge.

(The word is not peculiarly rhetorical.)
