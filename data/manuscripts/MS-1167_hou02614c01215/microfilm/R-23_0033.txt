ERROR. _For the definition put:_

1. _A wandering, going astray._

_(Give the examples from Spenser and Dryden.)_

2. _A discrepancy between what is thought to be true_
_and what is true: also the false proposition itself. An_
_unintentional positive falsity._

_(Give the example from Brougham, though it is not_
_particularly good considering its length._

3. _An inaccuracy, oversight, something done different_
_from what was intended, especially in speaking, writing,_
_and printing. Clerical error, an error of printing. This_
_strange expression is said to have its origin in the fact_
_that the first printing-press in England was set up by_
_Caxton in a chapel in Westminster, in consequence of which_
_a number of clerical expressions became applied to printers._

_(Refer this to the philologist.)_

4. _A wrong doing, a moral fault, a sin, genrally_
_not very heinous._

_(Give examples from the Psalms and from Tennyson.)_
