I will now run over the different grades of [ins]discrete[/ins] multitude [del]of[/del] [ins]of[/ins] collections [del]of distinct constituent individuals[/del], and point out the most remarkable properties of those multitudes.

The [del]first[/del] [ins]lowest[/ins] grade of multitude is that of a collection which does not exist, or the multitude of [u]none[/u]. A collection of this multitude has obvious logical peculiarities. Namely, nothing asserted of it can be false. For of it alone contradictory assertions are true. It is a collection and it is not a collection. [del]A[/del] Given [ins]the premises[/ins] that [del]a[/del]all the X's are black and that all the Xs are pure white, what is the conclusion? Simply that the multitude of the Xs is zero.
