[u]Art. 12[/u]. The other theorem, which gives great importance to the one just
proved is that if a collection is not too great to be discrete, that is to have
all its units individually distinct, neither is the collection of sets of units
that can be [del]drawn[/del] [ins]severally formed[/ins] from that collection too great to be discrete.

For we may suppose the units of the smaller collection to be independent
characters, and the larger collection to consist of individuals possessing
the different possible combinations of those characters. Then, any two units
of the larger collection will be distinguished by the different combinations
of characters they possess, and being so distinguished from one another
they must be distinct individuals.

On those two theorems, I build the whole [del]theory[/del] [ins]doctrine[/ins] of collections.
