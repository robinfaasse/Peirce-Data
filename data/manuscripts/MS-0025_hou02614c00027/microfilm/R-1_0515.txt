[marginalia]Multitude[/marginalia] [pn]62[/pn]

I now define the collection of the [u]$\Pi$s[/u], which entire collection it is convenient to call A.

$1^{\text{st}}$, A contains as an aggregant a certain collection [fig]square[/fig]$_0$ every unit of which is, therefore, [ins]a $\Pi$[/ins]

$2^{\text{nd}}$, A certain relation $p$ exists among the $\Pi$s such that taking any three [del]$\Pi$s[/del] [ins]whatever,[/ins] $\Pi_1$, $\Pi_2$, $\Pi_3$, if $\Pi_1$ is $p$ to $\Pi_2$ and $\Pi_2$ is $p$ to $\Pi_3$, [del]then[/del] $\Pi_1$ is $p$ to $\Pi_3$;

$3^{\text{rd}}$, Taking any two $\Pi$s whatever, say $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$, either $\Pi_2$ is not $p$ to $\Pi_1$, or or taking any aggregant [fig]square[/fig] of A, there is a unit $\Pi_0$ of [fig]square[/fig] such that $\Pi_2$ is $p$ $\Pi_0$ and $\Pi_0$ is $p$ of $\Pi_1$, or if this is not the case then taking any fourth $\Pi$ [del]whatever, say[/del] $\Pi_3$, either $\Pi_3$ is $p$ to $\Pi_2$ or $\Pi_1$ is $p$ to $\Pi_3$; ([del]This[/del] [ins]This[/ins] is only a roundabout way of saying that of any two $\Pi$s one is $p$ of the other. But I use this mode of statement in order to avoid all question as to whether I may not be requiring an impossible condition.)
