If a relation, [u]r[/u], is such that nothing is [u]r[/u] to two different things, and nothing is [u]r'd[/u] by two different things, so that some things in the universe are perhaps [u]r[/u] to nothing while [ins]all[/ins] the rest are [u]r[/u], each to its own distinct correlate, and there are some things [ins]perhaps[/ins] to which nothing is [u]r[/u], [del]while[/del] [ins]but[/ins] all the rest have each [del]a single thing[/del] [ins]then[/ins] that is [u]r[/u] to it, I call [u]r[/u], [del]a one-to-one relation.[/del] [ins]there be a one-to-one relation, [u]r[/u], such that[/ins] If [del]every object of[/del] [ins]every unit of[/ins] one collection is [del]in a one-to-one the[/del] [u]r[/u] to [del]an[/del] [ins]a unit[/ins] [del]object[/del] of a second collection, while every [del]other[/del] unit of the second collection is [u]r'd[/u] by a unit of the first collection, those two collections are commonly said to be in a one-to-one correspondence with one another.

[u]Art. 6.[/u] I borrow from Dr. Georg Cantor the two following definitions:

Two collections are equal to one another, if, and only if, there be a one-to-one relation, [u]c[/u], such that every unit of the first collection is [u]c[/u] to a unit of the second collection, while there is a one-to-one relation, [u]d[/u], such that every unit of the second collection is [u]d'd[/u] by a unit of the first collection. Of course, [u]c[/u] and [u]d[/u] may be the same one-to-one relation.

One collection, A, is greater than another, B, and B is less than A, if, and only if, there is a one-to-one [del]collection[/del] relation, [u]c[/u], such that every unit of B is [u]c[/u] to a unit of A, but whatever one-to-one relation, [u]d[/u], be taken, there is some unit of A which is not [u]d'd[/u] by any unit of B.

[u]Art. 7.[/u] I shall use the word multitude to denote that character of a collection by virtue of which it is greater than some collections and less than others, provided the [del]finite[/del] [ins]collection is discrete, that is, provided the[/ins] constituent units of the collection are or may be distinct. But when the units lose their individual identity because the collection exceeds every positive existence of the universe, the word multitude ceases to be applicable. I will take the word multiplicity to mean the greatness of any collection discrete or continuous.
