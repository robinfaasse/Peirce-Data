[u]Art. 1.[/u] Let us consider the relation of a constituent unit to the collective whole of which it forms a part. Suppose A is [ins]to be[/ins] such a unit and B is [ins]to be[/ins] such a whole. Then, [ins]in order[/ins] to avoid the circumlocution of saying that A is a constituent unit of B [del]as the collective whole of which it is a unit[/del], I shall simply say A is a unit of B, [del]or even[/del] [ins]and shall write[/ins] "[u]A is a [u]u[/u] of B[/u]"; or I may reverse the order [del]and[/del] [ins]in which[/ins] A and B are [del]mentioned[/del] [ins]writing[/ins] [del]by saying[/del] "[del]B is united by A[/del], or [u]B is [u]u'd[/u] by A[/u]".

The only logical peculiarities of this relation are as follows:

1[sup]st[/sup], whatever is [u]u[/u] of anything is [u]u'd[/u] by itself [del]and[/del] by nothing else. Hence, if anything is [u]u'd[/u] by anything not itself, it is not itself [del]u of anything[/del]; and consequently nothing that is [u]u'd[/u] by anything but itself is [u]u'd[/u] by itself.

2[sup]nd[/sup], whatever is not [u]u'd[/u] by anything does not exist.

[u]Art. 2.[/u] By a [u]collection[/u], I mean anything which is [u]u'd[/u] by whatever has a certain quality, or general description, and by nothing [del]else[/del]. That is, if C is a collection, there is some quality, [u]α[/u], such that taking anything whatever, say x, either x possesses the quality [u]α[/u] and is a unit of C, or else it neither possesses the quality [u]α[/u] nor is a unit of C. On the other hand, if C is not a collection, no matter what quality or general description, [u]β[/u], may be taken, there is either something possessing the quality [u]β[/u] without being a unit of C, or there is some unit of C which does not possess the quality, [u]β[/u].
