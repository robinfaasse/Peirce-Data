[u]Art. 21[/u]. A remarkable and important property of enumerable collections is, that every finite part is less than a whole. If the finite part is measured the [del]number[/del] [ins]multitude[/ins] of units it contains is enumerable; and if it is incommensurable with the unit, the unit can [del]be changed[/del] so as to make the finite part commensurable. Thus, to say that a finite part is less than its whole is the same as to say that an enumerable collection which is part of another is less than that other. There are two cases: 1[sup]st[/sup] when the whole is enumerable; and second, when the whole is inenumerable. Let us consider the first.

Let the Ms be contained among the Ns (which form an enumerable collection). Suppose however that the [ins]collection of[/ins] Ms is not less than that of the Ns. Then, by the definition of equality, there is such a one-to-one relation, [u]d[/u], that every N is [u]d[/u]'d by an M. Then, since the Ms are Ns, every N is [u]d[/u]'d by an N. But [u]d[/u] being a one-to-one relation, there are not two Ns that are [u]d[/u]'d by the same N. Hence, by the syllogism of transposed quantity, every N is [u]d[/u] of an N. But the Ns are, by their equality to the Ms, [u]d[/u]'d by nothing but Ms. Hence, every N is an M. That is, we have shown that if the Ns form an enumerable collection, [ins]the only[/ins] [del]any[/del] collection [ins]at once[/ins] contained in that collection and equal to that collection is the collection itself, and is not a part of that collection. That is, no part of an enumerable collection is equal to the collection. But the relation of inclusion is a one-to-one relation of every unit of the part to one of the whole. Hence, the part cannot be greater than the whole, and must be less than the whole.

We now take up the second case. But we can go further, and show that every inenumerable collection is greater than any enumerable collection. It is to be shown that it is absurd to suppose that every unit of an inenumerable collection, the Ns, is in a one-to-one relation, [u]c[/u], to a unit of any one enumerable collection, the Ms.
