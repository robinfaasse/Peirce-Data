It appears then that whatever denumerable collection the As may be and
whatever enumerable collection forming a part of it the Is may be, there
is a relation, [u]d[/u], which is, in the first place, permutational with reference to
the Is, and in the second place, if such that if it is permutational with
reference to [ins]the units of[/ins] any enumerable collection that forms a part of the As is
so likewise also with reference to the units of any [del]permutational[/del] [ins]enumerable[/ins] collection
aggregated of that and and any other collection forming part of the As and
one greater than that, [del]whence[/del] From these premises it follows that [u]d[/u] is
permutational with reference to all the As.

But it is easily shown that it is not only permutational with reference
to the As but also that it is indefinitely divident. That is to say, taking
any two As whatever, $A_1$ and $A_2$, if $A_2$ is [u]d[/u] to $A_1$, there is an $A$, say $A_3$,
such that $A_2$ is [u]d[/u] to $A_3$, and $A_3$ is [u]d[/u] to $A_1$. For whatever As $A_1$ and $A_2$
may be, they will, as has been shown, form part of some enumerable
collection with reference to [ins]the units of[/ins] which [u]d[/u] is permutational. [del]Hence[/del] Then,
calling that collection the Is, there will be [del]an equal[/del] [ins]among the As a collection [ins]itself[/ins] equal [del]greater than[/del] [ins]to[/ins] the Is, [del]by[/del][/ins]
[del]unity[/del] such that whatever two Is be taken, one of which, say $I_2$ is [u]d[/u] to the
other, say $I_1$, there will be an M, such that $I_2$ is [u]d[/u] to that M while that M
is [u]d[/u] to $I_1$.

This, then, completes the proof that there [ins]is a[/ins] [del]are[/del] vast collection of indefinitely
divident relations between the units of any denumerable collection.
