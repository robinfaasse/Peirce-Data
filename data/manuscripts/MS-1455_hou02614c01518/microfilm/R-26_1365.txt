This is a didactic, not a scientific, work. Yet it is [del]very far[/del] removed from those treatises on Logic of which the flood is perpetually passing us and losing itself in the ground [del]with[/del] at a very short distance. It is a book of original thought, although it discovers nothing, but simply teaches. Its general position is [del]not far removed from[/del] [ins]substantially that of[/ins] Mr. Venn in his work with a similar title, [u]The Principles of Empirical or Inductive Logic[/u], a book, by the way, often mistaken but always helpful to the critical reader. Only the design of [ins]Prof.[/ins] Aikins is to [del]extend[/del] the objectivistic treatment to deductive as well as to inductive Logic. On the other hand, though he does not profess such a limitation, Prof. Aikins chiefly confines his view to the every day reasonings of practical people.
