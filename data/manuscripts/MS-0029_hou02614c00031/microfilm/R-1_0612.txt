But such [del]collections[/del], though innumerable, are numerable, that
is, a whole number can be assigned to each one, though the whole
numbers are none too many for the purpose. All collections of
this description must be reckoned as equally numerous; the
disarrangement of them cannot alter their number. [del]For[/del] Consequently,
the numbers divisible by ten are just as numerous as all
the numbers. For putting a zero after every number, we get a
number divisible by ten. Thus, it is not true of innumerable parts that the
whole is greater than its part.
