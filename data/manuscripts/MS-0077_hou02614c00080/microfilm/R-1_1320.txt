[u]III[/u]
[u]On the Algebras in which division is unambiguous.[/u]

1. In the Linear Associative Algebra, the co-efficients are permitted to be imaginary. In this note they are restricted to being real. It is assumed that we have to deal with an algebra such that from $AB=AC$ we can infer that $A=0$ or $B=C$. It is required to find what forms [del]the[/del] [ins]such an[/ins] algebra may take.

2. If $AB=0$, then either $A=0$ or $B=0$. [del]For, if there be two quantities $p$ and $q$, neither zero, but such that $pq=0$, then[/del] For if not $AC = A(B+C)$, although $A$ does not vanish and $C$ is unequal to $B+C$.

3. The reasoning of $\S 40$ holds, [del]although[/del] the coëfficients are restricted to being real. It is true, then, that since there is no expression (in the algebra under consideration) whose square vanishes, there must be an expression, $i$, [del]whose[/del] such that $i^2 = i$.
