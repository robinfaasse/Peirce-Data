At a moment when several branches of philosophy
and of psychology have lately taken rank among the
progressive sciences, when others [del]have[/del] are well upon
the road to that position, and when students everywhere
are using their most strenuous endeavors to bring
the remainder of these studies to the same stage of
development, a universal agreement upon a
nomenclature consisting of well chosen terms and
of exact definitions would be comparable to the
work that Guyton de Morveau and his associates did
for chemistry, in teaching people to call Glauber's salt,
sulphate of soda, and so on, or [ins]that of[/ins] [del]Linnaeus[/del] for biology.
Philosophy is probably not yet quite ripe for such an
achievement. Students have hardly yet learned what
thorough analysis is. Too much that is crude mingles
with their work. Too many bigwigs are still given to
decorating vague, unfinished thought with phrases.
