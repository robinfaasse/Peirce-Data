The highest degree of necessity is the logical, or essential, the lowest
the substantial; while the highest, or least deep, degree of impossi-
bility is the substantial, the deepest the logical.

Having laid in this goodly supply of words, I enunciate
the last rule of the algebra, as follows:

<u>Rule 4</u>. Any antecedental, Q, of odd order, may
be omitted from a formula without loss of veritability,
provided there be a coördinate antecedental, P, such
that

<u>P < Q</u> true.

The proof is obvious. The only effect of an antece-
dental of the first order is extend the truth of the
proposition to all cases in which this antecedental
proposition is false. But if all these cases are
included [del]under[/del] among the cases in which another
antecedental is false, which is the case if from the
latter the former follows, then it has no effect whatever.
