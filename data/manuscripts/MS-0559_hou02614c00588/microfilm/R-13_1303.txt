Since if a proposition is true, its denial is false, and since no finite complex consequence can be [del]be[/del] necessarily false, it follows that the expression for a denial in terms [del]of the[/del] sign of consequence must be infinite; and several such expressions have been virtually given above. It will be convenient to use a [u]sign of inconsequence[/u], writing

$$A \ \bar{\ } \ B$$

to express the state of things in which A is true without B being true, and therefore as the negation of

$$A \ | \ B.$$

Let us term

$$A \ \bar{\ } \ B$$

an [u]inconsequence[/u], A its [u]inantecedent[/u], B its [u]inconsequent[/u], and otherwise follow the terminology of the consequence. The rules of this sign will be perfectly analogous to those of the sign of consequence. We shall have four fundamental rules, as follows:

[u]Rule 1[/u]. Every expression of the form

$$X \ \bar{\ } \ X$$

is necessarily false.

[u]Rule 2[/u]. Any inconsequential may be [del]dropped[/del] [ins]inserted[/ins] if of an odd order, [del]inserted[/del] [ins]dropped[/ins] if of an even order, without increase of [del]verilability[/del].

Thus we can infer

$$\begin{array}{c} X \text{ false} \\ \therefore \quad X \ \bar{\ } \ Y \text{ false.} \end{array}$$
