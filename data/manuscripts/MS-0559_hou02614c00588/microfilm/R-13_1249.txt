The [del]reader[/del] [ins]student[/ins] will ask if the notation is so bad why I did not select a better one. This is a sensible question; but the reply is not far to seek. This is a book to teach the art of reasoning; and for that purpose completely elaborated ideas are of comparatively little service. To learn to reason you must watch how ideas grow, and [del]that[/del] [ins]in[/ins] order that you may do so they must be first presented in an immature stage of development. There is besides in this case an additional reason. It is that though the present notation is inconvenient in practice yet [del]such[/del] inconvenience is inseparable from a notation which is to put into a clear light the elementary analysis of the conceptions of logic.

One method of improving the notation, — perhaps the best one, — would be to study it in its formulae without reference to the interpretation of it. But we should thus miss an important lesson. I therefore prefer to begin by attempting to analyze the [del]conception[/del] [ins]properties[/ins] of negation. For it is easy to see that the complexity of the formulae which have [del]just[/del] been passed in review is due to the circumstance that almost all the properties of expressions are reversed under the vincula.
