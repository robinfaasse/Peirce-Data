[u]To the printer[/u]. In the formulae, put all capital letters in Roman, all l.c. letters in Italics, except f, v, and e. If I write f, v, I mean Italics; but if F V use heavy-faced Roman. So an e made like that should be a Pica Roman l.c. e with its body cut down above [ins] & below [/ins] to make it justify and bring the base of it to the line. It will look thus: xae[ins]m[/ins]c. The capitals [greek]Π[/greek] [greek]Σ[/greek] from the Greek alphabet must be upright. You will probably have to get them cut. Some of the formulae (I try to reduce their number) are so hard to set that perhaps it would be cheaper to make wood-cuts for them.

Let v and f be the two values of the binary system of quantity. A single operational sign will suffice; and as this must be unsymmetrical (since the symmetrical can be built up out of the unsymmetrical, but not the reverse) let us denote it [del]to[/del] thus:

[fig]Logic symbol: x over y separated by a horizontal line with a vertical bar on the right[/fig]

We will call the operation [u]conditioning[/u], and the function it produces a [u]consequence[/u]. Of the two [del]letters[/del] [ins]terms[/ins] connected in a consequence, we term the [del]first, that[/del] [ins]left hand one[/ins] under the horizontal line, the [u]antecedent[/u], the other the [u]consequent[/u]. The horizontal line is to extend over the whole antecedent and serve as a vinculum. Thus [fig]Logic formula: x over y with vinculum, then vertical bar, then z[/fig] will mean ([fig]Logic formula: x over y with vinculum, then vertical bar[/fig])[fig]vertical bar[/fig]z; while [fig]Logic formula: x over y over z with vinculum, then vertical bar, then vinculum, then vertical bar[/fig] will mean [fig]Logic formula: x over y over z with vinculum, then vertical bar, then vinculum, then vertical bar[/fig].
