Here are some of his definitions

Πρότασις μὲν οὖν ἐστὶ λόγος καταφατικὸς ἢ ἀποφατικός τινος κατά τινος [u]p. 24 a 16[/u]. "A proposition is a speech affirming (saying, yes) or denying (saying, no) something of something." This is categorical.

Ὅρον δὲ καλῶ εἰς ὃν διαλύεται ἡ πρότασις, οἷον τό τε κατηγορούμενον καὶ τὸ καθ' οὗ κατηγορεῖται, ἢ προστιθεμένου ἢ διαιρουμένου τοῦ εἶναι καὶ μὴ εἶναι p. 24 b. 16. "Term I call that into which the proposition is resolved, both as predicated and that of which it is predicated, whether [del]be added[/del] being or not being be added or divided." That is, whether universals affirmative or negative, universal or particular.

That is, Aristotle uses these four propositional forms, where S is termed the subject, P the predicate. The forms are very often denoted by the vowels A, E, I, O. [To remember them, think of [u]affirmo[/u] and [u]nego[/u] from which words they were probably taken.]

A. Universal affirmative. Any S is P.
E. Universal negative. Any S is not a P.
I. Particular affirmative. Some S is P.
O. Particular negative. Some S is not P.

It is plain Aristotle conceives of the whole collection of S's being looked through to see whether all, [ins]a part,[/ins] some, or none of them have the quality P.

A, The universal affirmative is true only if all the S's are P.
I, The particular affirmative is true if all or a part of the S's are P.
O, The particular negative is true if a part or none of the S's are P.
E, The universal negative is true if only if none of the S's are P.
