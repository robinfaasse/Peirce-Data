Now the business of the logician is to distinguish [del]between the reasonings[/del] that are good and those that are bad. We, therefore, need distinctions of form in propositions sufficient to discriminate between
$$S-M \quad M-P$$
$$\therefore S-P$$
being good or bad reasoning. We must have one distinction to show whether $M$ enters into the [del]proposition[/del] [ins]premise[/ins] $M-P$ rightly, or not; and we must have a second distinction to show whether $M$ enters into the premise $S-M$ rightly or not. We must have two distinctions each with two branches, or four propositional forms. The premise
$$\text{Sinners are miserable}$$
must be a rule without exceptions. It need not assert the existence of sinners. The conclusion will follow just the same if it were here asserted only that sinners would be miserable if there were any. All the reasoning of geometry is about exact conditions that never are fulfilled as far as we know.
