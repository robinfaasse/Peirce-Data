The principle of identity shows that the sign of consequence is something more than a mere sign of inclusion.
For a relation of inclusion is any "transitive" relation,
that is, any relation such that if $X$ is so related to $Y$ and
$Y$ to $Z$, then $X$ is so related to $Z$, no matter what $X$, $Y$, and
$Z$ may be. Accordingly, the algebraic sign $<$ is a sign
of inclusion; for if
$$X < Y \quad \text{and} \quad Y < Z$$
then
$$X < Z.$$
Nevertheless, it is not true that
$$X < X.$$
Indeed, nothing is so related to itself.

antecedent and the antecedent of the consequent can be
transposed. We may term this the principle of commutatibility
of antecedents.
