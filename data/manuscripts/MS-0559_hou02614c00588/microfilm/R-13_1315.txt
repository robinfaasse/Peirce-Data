[u]Rules of Logical Aggregation and Composition[/u].

1. The signs $+$ and $\cdot$ are associative. That is
$(x + y) + z = x + (y + z)$
$(x \cdot y) \cdot z = x \cdot (y \cdot z)$.

2. [del]To a[/del] [ins]Any[/ins] aggregant may be inserted or any component dropped without decrease of veritability. Any component may be inserted or any aggregant dropped without increase of [del]veritit[/del] veritability [ins]*[/ins].

3. If any expression occurs twice in a formula, and one occurrence is [del]subordinate to the other (that is, in a parenthesis to or which the other is without,[/del] while the [del]latter[/del] is not in any parenthesis which the other is without, the latter may be [del]omitted[/del] [ins]made $0$[/ins] without loss of veritability, or the [del]former[/del] [ins]former[/ins] [del]omitted[/del] [ins]made $\infty$[/ins] without gain of veritability.

Thus these inferences hold
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
x + (x y) & & x (x + y) \\
\therefore x + 0 y & & \therefore x (1 + y)
\end{array}$$
