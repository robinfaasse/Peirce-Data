The principle of identity exhibits the sign of consequence
as something more than a mere sign of inclusion. For
by a relation of inclusion we mean nothing more nor less
than a "transitive" relation, that is, a relation such that if
$X$ is so related to $Y$, and $Y$ is related to $Z$, then $X$ is so related
to $Z$. The algebraic sign $<$ is, for example, a sign of
inclusion; for if
$$X < Y,$$
$$Y < Z,$$
then, $X < Z$.
Nevertheless, the principle of identity does not hold here.
It is not only not true that [del]$X < X$[/del], whatever $X$ may
be, $X < X$, but whatever $X$ may be $X < X$ is false.
