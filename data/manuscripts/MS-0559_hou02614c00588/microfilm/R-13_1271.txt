Thus we have justified the inference
$$\begin{array}{c}
X|Z\text{, true,} \\
\therefore\quad X|Y|Z\text{, true.}
\end{array}$$

Suppose, now, that the following inference had been justified:
$$\begin{array}{c}
X_1|X_2|\dots X_n|Z\text{, true,} \\
\therefore\quad X_1|X_2|\dots X_{n-1}|X_n|Z\text{, true.}
\end{array}$$
In point of fact, it has been justified for $\underline{n=2}$. This form
of inference holds, then, no matter what propositions $X_1$,
$X_2$, etc may be. Hence, substituting $X_2$ for $X_1$, [del]X_3[/del] [ins]$X_3$[/ins] for $X_2$,
etc., we have
$$\begin{array}{c}
X_2|X_3|\dots X_n|Z\text{, true,} \\
\therefore\quad X_2|X_3|\dots X_n|X_{n+1}|Z\text{ true.}
\end{array}$$
Suppose, then, we have given the premise,
$$X_1|X_2|X_3|\dots X_{n-1}|X_n|Z\text{ true,}$$
that is to say, from the truth of $X_1$ follows the truth of
$$X_2|X_3|\dots X_n|Z.$$
Then, [del]from[/del] [ins]by[/ins] the regula de quocunque, from the truth of $X_1$
follows the truth of
$$- X_2|X_3|\dots X_n|X_{n+1}|Z,$$
[ins]to say[/ins] which is the same as to write
$$X_1|X_2|X_3|\dots X_n|X_{n+1}|Z\text{ is true.}$$
