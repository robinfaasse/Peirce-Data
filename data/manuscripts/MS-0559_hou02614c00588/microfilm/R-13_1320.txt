Mathematicians are familiar with the device of
introducing into algebra a superfluous element for the
sake of producing balance or homogeneity. Wherever symmetry
is found, superfluity may be strongly suspected. In a system of
quantity having but two values, two operations are more than
enough. Putting v and f for the two values, let

[pn]x†y[/pn]

be such a function of x and y, that

[del]f†y = v[/del]
[del]x†v = v[/del]

x†v = v,
f†y = v,
v†f = f.

These three equations completely define the operation,
[ins]which I will term illation.[/ins]
