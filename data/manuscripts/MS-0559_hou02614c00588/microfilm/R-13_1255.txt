There are two formulae altogether similar in their
real nature to (2) and (2'), although in this
notation we use they do not appear so simple.
Namely,

(3) In [del]a[/del] $X \overline{Y|Z}$, $X$ and $Y$ may be transposed
(3') In $X \overline{Y|Z}$, $Y$ and $Z$ may be transposed.

That is, we may transpose
[marginalia]to these
lines[/marginalia]
[fig]curly bracket grouping the four lines below[/fig]
antecedent and antecedent of consequent,
inconsequent and inconsequent of inantecedent,
inantecedent and antecedent of inconsequent,
consequent and inconsequent of antecedent.

The principle is simple enough. Either member
of [del]a[/del] consequence or inconsequence can be
transposed with that part of the other member
which is [del]under the same[/del] a number of [del]vincula[/del]
which is even or odd according as its own vincula
are even or odd, and which is former or latter
according as it is itself former or latter.
