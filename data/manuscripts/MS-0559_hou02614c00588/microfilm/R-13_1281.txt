Conditioning being, then, an unsymmetrical operation,
we must assign opposite values to

$$f|v \quad \text{and} \quad v|f$$

On the other hand,

$$f|f \quad \text{and} \quad v|v$$

must have the same value. For had they severally the
same values as the first two, either

$$f|f = v|f \quad \text{and} \quad f|v = v|v,$$

when the value of [del]a[/del] [ins]every[/ins] consequence would be independent of
its antecedent, or

$$f|f = f|v \quad \text{and} \quad v|f = v|v,$$

when the value of every consequence would be inde-
pendent of its consequent.

As we have not hitherto assigned any distinguishing
characters to $v$ and $f$, we are free to assume, as
the definition of $v$

(1) $$x|x = v.$$

As we have not hitherto assigned any distinguishing
characters to antecedent and consequent, we may assume

(2) $$x|f \neq [del]x[/del] [ins]v[/ins].$$

(3) $$[del]x[/del] [ins]f[/ins]|v = v.$$
