All conclusions logically inductive are really infinite. Thus
Some despots are assassinated
All unpopular kings are despots
$\therefore$ All (or at least some) unpopular kings are assassinated.
Here the inference, so far as it is justifiable, is that there is an actual tendency
[del]for[/del] [ins]in[/ins] unpopular kings to be assassinated.

All conclusions logically à priori are really affirmative
All rotten eggs have a bad smell
No fresh laid eggs have a bad smell
$\therefore$ No fresh laid eggs are rotten
Here the inference is not that fresh laid eggs [del]have a tendency[/del] [ins]not[/ins] to rotten, but
that they actually are not so.

A proposition is [del]logically[/del] [ins]really[/ins] disjunctive when it is [del]intended[/del] [ins]intended[/ins] to hold
unless some thing not contemplated [del]presents[/del] [ins]logically[/ins] [del]itself[/del]. A proposition is [del]really[/del]
hypothetical when it holds [ins]good[/ins], provided some [del]thing[/del] [del]circumstance[/del] [ins]circumstance[/ins] which is
taken for granted, holds. A [del]really[/del] categorical proposition is one
which holds good at any rate.

Every logically à posteriori [ins]concluding[/ins] argument is really disjunctive. Thus
All men are born of women
No elf is a man
$\therefore$ No elf is born of women.
This holds unless, indeed, there are others born of women besides men.

Every logically inductive conclusion is really hypothetical. Thus

Every logically à priori conclusion is really categorical.
