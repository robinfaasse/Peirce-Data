[marginalia]Book Review[/marginalia]

George H. Ellwanger, M. A., is a man, — for certain
remarks of his about the truffle suffice to persuade
us that "Who's Who in America" errs in making him
the daughter of his parents, — is a man, then, not
destitute of wit, forks upon which he occasionally
engrafts a humor of the Charles Lamb family, not
too artificial in a book about books. For this is a book
about books, mainly, — about Cookbooks and works
on Gastronomy, and about the history of cooking and
eating. There is not very much discussion of the moot
points of the doctrine of the table, and what little
there is hardly makes us long for more. After a
