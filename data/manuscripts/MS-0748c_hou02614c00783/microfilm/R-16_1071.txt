The descriptive equation:
$$y = \mathcal{G}S$$
is thus a [ins]general[/ins] synthetic real proposition. It asserts that the things under contemplation covered by the generic description $y$ are also covered by the more specific description $\mathcal{G}S$. This will mean all $y$'s are $\mathcal{G}S$'s unless in imagination I restrict the meaning.

A synthetic real proposition is in contrast to an analytical proposition or a definition.

I may assume:
$$A = BCD$$
where the description $A$ is merely a short name for the description $BCD$ of where the description $BCD$ is a definition of the description $A$. This equality is assumed as a definition, an agreement, a convention. The equality is not proved by application to things as in real synthetic propositions.

Every real synthetic proposition is of the form:
$$y = \mathcal{G}S.$$

The proposition unrestricted asserts that all the things that fall under the description $y$ fall also under the more specific description $\mathcal{G}S$.

The above proposition may be wholly true or wholly false or partially true and partially false.

Suppose that I seek to apply the description $y$ exhaustively to the Universe of Things and in this attempt [del]discover one thousand[/del] [ins]everything there is to[/ins] individual cases to which I think the description [del]applies, but[/del] [ins]applies.[/ins] That is to say, in the Universe, to the best of my knowledge, information and belief, there are one thousand $y$'s and only 250 $\mathcal{G}S$'s. The proposition that all $y$'s are $\mathcal{G}S$'s or that the descriptions $y$ and $\mathcal{G}S$ cover the same things is to me only 25% true, under this evidence. [ins]The probability that $y$ is $\mathcal{G}S$ is 25% under this evidence[/ins]
