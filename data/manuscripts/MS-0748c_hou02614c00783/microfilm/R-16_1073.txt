Probability differs from an ordinary investigation in the fact
that in Probability I seek to ascertain the number of cases under the
generic description and the number of cases under the specific descrip-
tion and thus to obtain in figures the percentage of truth in the propo-
sition.

What is the probability of rain tomorrow? If I had a complete
description of tomorrow the description would [del]disclose[/del] [ins]determine[/ins] the fact and there
would be no Probability about it. But all I know about tomorrow is its
only description I have of tomorrow. I count the tomorrows that had yes-
terdays like to-day. I count how many [ins]such tomorrows[/ins] were rainy. These numerical fig-
ures determine the Probability based upon this evidence.

The subject or predicate or both of any proposition may split
up into compounds.

We may have, for instance, the proposition:
$y = \mathcal{G}(S_{1} + S_{2} + S_{3} + \text{\&c})$
which may be interpreted either conjunctively or disjunctively or singly
as follows:

The genus determined by the generic description $y$ in-
cludes the species $S_{1}$, $S_{2}$ and $S_{3}$ &c.

Or any $y$ that we may select is either a $S_{1}$
or a $S_{2}$ or a $S_{3}$ &c.

Or this particular $y$ under discussion is either a $S_{1}$
or a $S_{2}$ or a $S_{3}$ &c.

If the species $S_{1}, S_{2}$ and $S_{3}$ &c, make up the whole
genus then the probability that $y$ is $S_{1}$ or $S_{2}$ or $S_{3}$ &c
is unity.
