[u]I, IT, and THOU[/u]:

A Book giving Instruction in some of the Elements of Thought.

[u]Introduction[/u].

If conceptions which are incapable of definition are simple, I, It, & Thou are so. Who could define either of these words, easy as they are to understand? Who does not perceive, in fact, that neither of them can be expressed in terms of the others?

I here, for the first time, begin a developement of these conceptions hoping that this will be accompanied with a developement of the souls of those who read.

I! How simple & sublime! It! How protean & comic! Thou! How beautiful & pathetic! [fig]sketch of hands clasped in prayer[/fig]

Though they cannot be expressed in terms of each other, yet they have a relation to each other, for THOU is an IT in which there is another I. I looks in, It looks out, Thou looks through, out & in again. I outwells, It inflows, Thou commingles.
