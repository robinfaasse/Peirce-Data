It will be remarked that one or other of the two formulae
$\underline{\text{x}}\bigcirc(\underline{\text{y}}\bigcirc\underline{\text{z}}) = \underline{\text{y}}\bigcirc(\underline{\text{x}}\bigcirc\underline{\text{z}})$
$(\underline{\text{x}}\bigcirc\underline{\text{y}})\bigcirc\underline{\text{z}} = (\underline{\text{x}}\bigcirc\underline{\text{z}})\bigcirc\underline{\text{y}}$
is true of 14 out of the 16 signs, or all but [fig]figure[/fig] and [fig]figure[/fig].

Another very commonly true pair of formulae are
$(\underline{\text{x}}\bigcirc\underline{\text{y}})\bigcirc\underline{\text{x}} = (\underline{\text{y}}\bigcirc\underline{\text{y}})\bigcirc\underline{\text{x}}$
$(\underline{\text{x}}\bigcirc(\underline{\text{y}}\bigcirc\underline{\text{x}}) = \underline{\text{x}}\bigcirc(\underline{\text{y}}\bigcirc\underline{\text{y}})$.

The former of these formulae holds if, and only if, there is a side of the dexter bend of the sign on which the two quadrants are either both open or both closed; while the other formula holds if the same is true of the sinister bend.
