doubt, some reader may exclaim in dissent to this, on first hear-

of reasoning. So it is; preeminently a science that reasons.
But just as it is not necessary, in order to talk, to understand
the theory of the formation of vowel sounds, so it is not neces-
sary, in order to reason, to be in possession of the theory of
reasoning. Otherwise, plainly, the science of logic could never
be developed. The contrary objection would have more excuse,
that no science stands in need of logic, since our natural power
of reason is enough. Make of logic what the majority of treati-
ses in the past have made of it, and a very common class of English
and French books still make of it, that is to say, mainly formal
logic, and that formal logic represented as an art of reasoning,
and in my opinion this objection is more than sound; for such
logic is a great hindrance to right reasoning. It would, how-
