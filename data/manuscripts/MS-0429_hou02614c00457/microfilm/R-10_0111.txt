[pn]395[/pn] The relative multiplication must have a sign to designate it. What sign is particularly appropriate to it? I have hitherto written the operator and operand without an intervening sign, in accordance with the universal practice of mathematicians, who always write "tan x" and "log x", and usually also "F x". Schroeder uses a semicolon; thereby, exemplifying, in an unusually striking way, the known incapacity of Germans to devise characters which the rest of the world shall approve. For the connection between this sign and either of the quantities this sign connects is itself a relative multiplication; so that, instead of [u]x[/u] ; [u]y[/u], [u]x[/u] ; ; ; [u]y[/u] should be written, — or rather there should be a condensation of semicolons between the [u]x[/u] and the [u]y[/u]. For this reason, [ins]a[/ins] homogeneously continuous sign is alone appropriate. The blank fulfills this condition; but the blank is needed for another purpose; namely, to represent coexistence. For if, having written down one assertion, we desire to add another, we very properly simply write it without any sign of connection with the first assertion, with which it has nothing to do except that both are asserted. We simply write them without any sign of connection, but the blank. But two such assertions are compounded. Therefore, the blank ought to be the signs of composition. We are, then, almost driven to use a line, whether long, as a heavy dash, or short, as a hyphen, as the sign of relative multiplication. We ought then, to write 'tan-x', and 'log-x', and 'F-x'.
