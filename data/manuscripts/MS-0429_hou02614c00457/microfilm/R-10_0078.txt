It will be best to retain one or both of them. Moreover, for logical reasons, the sign [del]$\bar{\curlywedge}$[/del] [ins]$\bar{\curlyvee}$[/ins] is of the greatest importance. From a logical point of view, we may say, as we shall see, that it is the most important of any; and even from an algebraical point of view, if we retain $\bar{\curlywedge}$ and $\bar{\curlyvee}$ as our usual signs, the most important formula of the algebra, apart from those of the commutative and associative principles, requires this sign for its convenient and clear expression. Namely, this formula is

$(x \bar{\curlywedge} y \bar{\curlywedge} z) \bar{\curlyvee} (x \bar{\curlyvee} y \bar{\curlyvee} z)$
$(x \bar{\curlyvee} y \bar{\curlyvee} z) \bar{\curlywedge} (x \bar{\curlywedge} y \bar{\curlywedge} z)$

expressing a sort of associativeness involving the two signs $\bar{\curlywedge}$ and $\bar{\curlyvee}$.
