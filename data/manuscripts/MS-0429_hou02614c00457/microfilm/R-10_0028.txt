[del][u]Section 3.[/u][/del] [u]The Simplest Branch of Mathematics.[/u]

[del]250[/del] Were nothing at all supposed, mathematics would have no
ground at all to go upon. Were the hypothesis merely that
there was nothing but one unit, there would not be a possibility
of a question, since only one answer would be possible. Conse-
quently, the simplest possible hypothesis is that there are two
objects, which we may denote by [u]v[/u] and [u]f[/u]. Then the first kind of
problem of this algebra will be, given certain data concerning an
unknown object, [u]x[/u], required to know whether it is [u]v[/u] or [u]f[/u]. Or
similar problems may arise concerning several unknowns, [u]x[/u], [u]y[/u], etc.
