[del]30[/del] [ins]37[/ins] Substantially these definitions of composition and aggregation were given by me in 1880 (Am. Jour. of Math. III. 33.)* They have the effect of reducing all legitimate transformations to successive legitimate [del]insertions and[/del] omissions. The demonstration of this is very easy; but I think it will be more accurately appreciated if I postpone giving it until I have first shown what transformations are, by the above definitions, legitimated through insertions and omissions.

I. [u]Any proposition in this algebra, being written on a sheet of assertions, can, without loss of truth, receive the insertion of a vinculum below it.[/u]

[u]Demonstration.[/u] For, according to the definition of quantities, any proposition of this algebra is a quantity; and by the definition of vinculum and obelus, if written on a sheet of assertions, it must receive either a vinculum or an obelus, either of which being added to it, the result is an assertion concerning the value of the quantity.
