[u]ed, any quantity into which another could be transformed with-[/u]
[u]out loss of truth, if the latter were asserted alone, can, if it[/u]
occurs anywhere under a single obelus in [del]an[/del] [ins]a directly[/ins] asserted quantity, be [del]then[/del] [ins]there[/ins]
[u]transformed into that latter.[/u]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] represent the two quantities,
so that in the assertion that [u]x[/u] has the value [del]v[/del] [ins]v[/ins], [u]x[/u] could be
replaced by [u]y[/u], without loss of truth. Then, I say that if,
in [del]an[/del] [ins]a directly[/ins] asserted quantity, [u]y[/u] occurs anywhere under a single
obelus, it can be transformed into [u]x[/u] without loss of truth. For
let Y be the entire expression which is under the same obelus
as [u]y[/u]; and let X be what Y would become if [u]y[/u] were replaced by
[u]x[/u]. Then, by XIII, if X were asserted, it could be transformed
into Y. And consequently, by V, [u]Y[/u], if it were asserted alone
could be transformed into [u]X[/u]. Hence, by XIII, situated as [u]Y[/u]
is, not under any obelus, it can be transformed into [u]X[/u]. But
this transformation consists only in replacing [u]y[/u] by [u]x[/u] under a
single obelus.
