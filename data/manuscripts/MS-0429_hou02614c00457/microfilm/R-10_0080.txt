Adopting these signs, I proceed to state, as definitions,
those fundamental conventions from which all the properties of the
signs, and all the necessary rules and formulae of the algebra can
be deduced. In doing so, I shall, after Schröder, imitate a prac-
tice introduced into geometry, I believe, by Gergonne (about 1820),
of writing reciprocally related propositions in parallel columns.
