III.

[u]III. A quantity, written on a sheet of assertion, may, without loss
of truth, be transformed by the insertion of two obeli over it.[/u]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let [u]x[/u] represent what is written. Being written
upon the sheet of assertions, it is an assertion. It, therefore,
virtually, at least, consists in the affirmation that some proposi-
tion pertinent to the algebra is true, that is, has the value [u]v[/u].
Then, since every such proposition is [u]v[/u] or [u]f[/u], but not both, it vir-
tually implies that [u]x[/u] has the value [u]f[/u]. But [u]x[/u] merely asserts that
[u]x[/u] has the value [u]f[/u]. Hence [u]x[/u] asserts no more than is virtually im-
plied in [u]x[/u], and there will be no loss of truth in inserting the two
obeli.
