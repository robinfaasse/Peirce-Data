His five laws which are said to "define" the
"distribution" of his linear triads through the
system form a very singular sort of definition.
Namely, a definition ought to be an analysis of a
conception resulting in two universal propositions,
the one predicating the "definition", or composite term,
of the definitum, the other the definitum of the
"definition". But of the five laws, only two the
[u]III[sup]rd[/sup][/u] and [u]IV[sup]th[/sup][/u], which are quite special in their
nature, are universal propositions. The other
three are particular; and as such, one does not
see what they can have to do [ins]in[/ins] [del]with[/del] a definition.

Law V is remarkable in several respects. It runs
"No entity is absent from the system which can
consistently be present." But what has this to
do with "the distribution of linear triads through
the system", which is all these laws profess to
define?
