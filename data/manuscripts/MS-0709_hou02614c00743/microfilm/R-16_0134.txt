[u]Note on Kempe's Paper in Vol XXI of the Proc Lond Math Soc.[/u]

This paper, which displays the author's mathematical power and still more his mathematical experience, also shows his native power in the direction of logic, and his sad want of training in this branch.

Thus in the first words of §2 he distinguishes certain "entities" as [u]a[/u], [u]b[/u], [u]c[/u], [u]d[/u]... and in the same breath says they are "undistinguished". This is downright perversity; for he must have known how this would strike a logician. His only excuse for calling them undistinguished would be that they are regarded as in the same relation to the system, although in themselves they may be unlike one another. But this excuse he cuts off by saying that they "are all exactly alike."

The word "entity" is with the realists always an abstract noun; but Kempe treats it as concrete & so occupies nominalistic ground. What he means, then, we may infer, is that the objects are individual.
