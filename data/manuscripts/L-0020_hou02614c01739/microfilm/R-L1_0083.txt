Dear Sir:-

My good friend, Prof. C. F. Chandler, of Columbia College,
has suggested to me that I send you the enclosed copy of a patent issued
to me on Nov. 21st, 1905, for your consideration. As you will see,
the purpose of the invention is to increase the light gathering power
of the telescope by connecting two paraboloid mirrors, the outer one
comparatively large and the inner one comparatively small, the connec-
tion being made so that the foci and axes of the two paraboloids are co-
incident, and the smaller onethen fitted on the end of the telescope so
that the rays reach the object glass in parallel waves. Naturally,
there is no necessary relation between the size of the smaller and the
larger paraboloid, and, in fact, the only thing essential is that the
smallerparaboloid fit the particular telescope in question, and several
different size larger paraboloids could be attached to the smaller one,
according as the purse of the astronomer would permit the purchase of
a larger or smaller outer or receiving paraboloid reflector.

As I look at the matter there is no theoretical reason why
there should be any limit as to the size of the outer paraboloid, other
than the practical limit of the difficulty of making and manipulating
too large a paraboloid; but within reason, according to my theory, by
attaching a pair of paraboloids in this way to a ten inch telescope,
the receiving paraboloid having a diameter at the opening of forty
inches, one should obtain as great efficiency as with a forty inch
objective.

I should be glad if you would carefully read through this patent
and let me know what you think of the theoretical and practical possi-
bilities of the invention. I may say that I have heard no adverse crit-
icism as yet as to the theoretical merits of the invention, and as to
its practical merits the only criticism I have heard is as to the dif-
ficulty of making a very large receiving paraboloid accurately.

Very truly yours,
R. A. Anthony
