Quaternions applied to Probabilities.

Let the capital letters of the alphabet denote whatever can be
measured in its attributes, modes, or forms.

Let $A - B$ [del]be[/del] and also small greek letters, denote [ins]the measure of[/ins] the difference
between two objects.

Let $[(C-D) S \frac{A-B}{C-D}]$ denote the measure of the difference which there is be-
tween $A$ & $B$ in that respect in which $C$ & $D$ differ.

Let $[(C-D) V \frac{A-B}{C-D}]$ denote the measure of the difference which there is be-
tween $A$ & $B$ in those respects in which $C$ & $D$ do not differ.

Then as these two respects have nothing in common.
$$A-B = [(C-D) S \frac{A-B}{C-D}] + [(C-D) V \frac{A-B}{C-D}]$$

A pair of objects may be considered as differing in two or more re-
spects which [del]have[/del] [ins]relation[/ins] have something in common. Thus that $A$ & $B$ differ in
the respect in which $C$ & $D$ differ [del]will[/del] [ins]may[/ins] imply that they differ somewhat
also in the respect in which $E$ & $F$ differ, when $A$ & $B$, $C$ & $D$, $E$ & $F$ have a
mutual difference in a common respect. To denote this let
$$[(C-D) S \frac{A-B}{C-D}] = \gamma$$
then $[(E-F) S \frac{\gamma}{E-F}] = [(E-F) S \frac{[(C-D) S \frac{A-B}{C-D}]}{E-F}]$ will be the measure of the
difference between $A$ & $B$ in the respect in which $E$ & $F$ differ in consequence
of differing in the respect in which $C$ & $D$ differ.
