[pn]§ 191.[/pn]

The following examples will be sufficient [del]for the present[/del].

From the premises $\Sigma_{i} a_{i} b_{i}$ and $\Pi_{j} (\bar{b}_{j} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} c_{j})$, eliminate $b$. We first write
$$\Sigma_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i} b_{i} (\bar{b}_{j} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} c_{j}).$$

The distributive process gives
$$\Sigma_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i} (b_{i} \bar{b}_{j} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} b_{i} c_{j}).$$

But we [del]always have a right to drop a factor or[/del] [ins]can[/ins] insert an [del]additive[/del] [ins]copresent[/ins] term. We thus get
$$\Sigma_{i} \Pi_{j} a_{i} (b_{i} \bar{b}_{j} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} c_{j}).$$

By the third process, we can, if we like, insert $n_{ij}$ for $b_{i} \bar{b}_{j}$. In either case, we identify $j$ with $i$ and get the conclusion
$$\Sigma_{i} a_{i} c_{i}.$$

[pn]§ 192.[/pn]

Given the premises
$$\Sigma_{h} \Pi_{i} \Sigma_{j} \Pi_{k} (\alpha_{h i j k} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} s_{j k} \eta_{k})$$
$$\Sigma_{u} \Sigma_{v} \Pi_{w} \Pi_{y} (\epsilon_{u v y w} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{s}_{y w} \theta_{v w}).$$

Required to eliminate $s$. The combined premise is
$$\Sigma_{h} \Sigma_{u} \Sigma_{v} \Pi_{i} \Sigma_{j} \Pi_{w} \Pi_{k} \Pi_{y} (\alpha_{h i j k} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} s_{j k} \eta_{k}) (\epsilon_{u v y w} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{s}_{y w} \theta_{v w}).$$

Identify $k$ with $w$ and $y$ with $j$, and we get
$$\Sigma_{h} \Sigma_{u} \Sigma_{v} \Pi_{i} \Sigma_{j} \Pi_{w} (\alpha_{h i j w} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} s_{j w} \eta_{w}) (\epsilon_{u v j w} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{s}_{j w} \theta_{v w}).$$

The Boolian part then reduces, so that the conclusion is
$$\Sigma_{h} \Sigma_{u} \Sigma_{v} \Pi_{i} \Sigma_{j} \Pi_{w} (\alpha_{h i j w} \epsilon_{u v j w} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \alpha_{h i j w} \theta_{v w} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \Psi_{u v j w} \eta_{w}).$$
