§119. At this point the student [del]wants[/del] [ins]is ready[/ins] to devote some
time to the study of the forms of propositions involving
three terms of which one is a relative. As there [del]are 576[/del] [ins]may be expected to be[/ins]
such forms, he cannot go through them all. But he
may examine the principal genera.

$\Pi_i \Pi_j \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij} \mathbin{\Psi} \bar{g}_j$

means that taking any two objects, I and J, either I is
not a chimera, or J is not a griffin, or I loves J;
that is, whatever chimeras exist, love each of them all
the griffins that exist.

$\Sigma_i \Sigma_j \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij} \mathbin{\Psi} \bar{g}_j$

is equivalent to

$\Sigma_i \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} \Sigma_j \bar{g}_j \mathbin{\Psi} \Sigma_i \Sigma_j l_{ij}$

and means either [ins]1[sup]st[/sup],[/ins] something exists not a chimera,
or [ins]2[sup]nd[/sup],[/ins] something exists not a griffin,
or, 3[sup]d[/sup], something loves something.

$\Sigma_i \Pi_j \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij} \mathbin{\Psi} \bar{g}_j$

is equivalent to

$\Sigma_i \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} \Sigma_i \Pi_j (\bar{g}_j \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij})$,

and means either something exists not a chimera
or all griffins that may exist [ins]are loved by[/ins] some one thing.

$\Pi_j \Sigma_i \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij} \mathbin{\Psi} \bar{g}_j$

is equivalent to

$\Sigma_i \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} \Pi_j \Sigma_i (\bar{g}_j \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij})$
