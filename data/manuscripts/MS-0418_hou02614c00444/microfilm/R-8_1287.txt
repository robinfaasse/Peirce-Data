§106. After the involution has been performed, farther
identifications and diversifications of indices will
[del]be[/del] [ins]be[/ins] permitted by the following formulae

[u]For identifications[/u].
$\Pi_i \Pi_j\ l_{ij} \prec \Pi_i\ l_{ii}$,
$\Sigma_i \Pi_j\ l_{ij} \prec \Sigma_i\ l_{ii}$,

[u]For diversifications[/u].
$\Pi_i\ l_{ii} \prec \Pi_i \Sigma_j\ l_{ij}$,
$\Sigma_i\ l_{ii} \prec \Sigma_i \Sigma_j\ l_{ij}$.

The following are also occasionally useful
$\Pi_i\ m_i = \Pi_i \Pi_j\ m_i m_j$
$\Sigma_i\ m_i = \Sigma_i \Sigma_j\ (m_i + m_j)$

In order to see what the use of changing the indices
may be, let us consider the premise
$\Pi_i \Pi_j\ (m_i l_{ij} + \overline{m}_j \cdot \overline{l}_{ij})$,
from which we are to eliminate $m$. If we were just
to drop $m$, we should only get an identical [del]propo-[/del] [ins]component[/ins]
[del]sition[/del]. But we first separate the Boolean into [del]factors[/del] [ins]components[/ins],
then diversify $j$, [del]and[/del] then reunite the [del]factors[/del] [ins]components[/ins], and
then drop $m$, thus
$\Pi_i \Pi_j\ (m_i l_{ij} + \overline{m}_j \cdot \overline{l}_{ij})$
$= \Pi_i \Pi_j\ (m_i + \overline{l}_{ij}) \cdot (l_{ij} + \overline{m}_j)$
$= \Pi_i \Pi_j \Pi_k\ (m_i + \overline{l}_{ij}) \cdot (l_{ik} + \overline{m}_i)$
$= \Pi_i \Pi_j \Pi_k\ (l_{ik} + \overline{l}_{ij})$
which asserts a logically contingent proposition, namely,
that whatever is in the relation $l$ to anything is in that relation
