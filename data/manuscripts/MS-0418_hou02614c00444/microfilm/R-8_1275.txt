The following are the commonest forms:

$\Pi_i \Pi_j \ \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij} \mathbin{\Psi} \bar{g}_j$ . Any chimera loves [del]any[/del] [ins]every[/ins] griffin.
$\Sigma_i \Pi_j \ c_i (l_{ij} \mathbin{\Psi} \bar{g}_j)$ . Some chimera loves every griffin.
$\Pi_j \Sigma_i \ c_i l_{ij} \mathbin{\Psi} \bar{g}_j$ . Any griffin is loved by some chimera.
$\Sigma_j \Pi_i \ (\bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij}) g_j$ . Some griffin is loved by every chimera.
$\Pi_i \Sigma_j \ \bar{c}_i \mathbin{\Psi} l_{ij} g_j$ . Any chimera loves some griffin.
$\Sigma_i \Sigma_j \ c_i l_{ij} g_j$ . Some chimera loves some griffin.

It will, of course, be understood that $\bar{l}_{ij}$ means
$i$ does not love $j$; and so in general. Thus
$\Sigma_i \Pi_j \ c_i (\bar{l}_{ij} \mathbin{\Psi} g_j)$
means some chimera loves nothing but griffins,
or is a non-lover of anything unless it be a griffin.
