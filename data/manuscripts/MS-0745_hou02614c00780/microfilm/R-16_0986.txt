[u]Fundamental conceptions of logic proper.[/u]
The flow of time and inference. Three
parts of inference: 1, Premise, 2, Conclusion,
3, Leading principle. Propositions of two
essentially different kinds, viz:
I. Universal Propositions, affirming lead-
ing principles, and asserting non-ex-
istence.
2. Particular Propositions, denying lead-
ing principles, and asserting existence.
Definition of possibility and the limited
universe.
The distinction of hypothetical and cate-
gorical propositions not important; but
the complete and exact theory of categoricals
[del]requires[/del] [ins]depends on[/ins] the logic of relatives.
Arguments complete or incomplete. Nature
of logical principles. Syllogism and dialo-
gism.
