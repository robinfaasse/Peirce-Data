[pn]D.11[/pn] [u]Scholium.[/u] The purposes of this essay on Dichotomic Mathematics are, in the first place, to show what it is, why it is so called, and that it is the simplest possible branch of mathematics, and the foundation of all other mathematics;
2[u]d[/u], to [del]work[/del] develope its main propositions and methods;
3[u]d[/u], to exhibit, in mathematical style, the analysis of its foundations into their simplest formal elements, showing in particular that every fundamental (permissive) rule of transformation is a rule either of insertion or of omission, and to do this without any formal discussion, yet so as point to the logical doctrine with which it is connected, and to make clear the different categories of elements that [ins]have to be[/ins] introduced.
