[u]Scholium[/u]. It is impossible to prove that any transfor-
mation is bad, for the only premiss that could lead to that
conclusion is Def I ¶ 2. But this must remain useless
unless we know [del]either[/del] that there are symbols of different
quality, and there is no premiss from which this conclu-
sion could be drawn except Def I ¶ 1, and this must
remain useless for the purpose, until we know that
there is a bad transformation. This would remain true
even if we were to assume that some symbol were good.
It will therefore be unaffected by the following postulates.

[u]Postulate 4[/u]. Let it be supposed that, corresponding to
every [del]a[/del] conceivable transformation, there is a symbol,
having the same quality of being good or bad, as that
transformation.

[u]Corollary 9[/u]. Then by Cor. 7, there is a good symbol.
