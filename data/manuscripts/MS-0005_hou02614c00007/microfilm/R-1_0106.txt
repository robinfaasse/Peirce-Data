the state of things assumed throughout the theory as the
basis of the conclusions; [u]postulate[/u], in the proper sense
of the term being synonymous with (but less vague
than) [u]hypothesis[/u];

An [u]Axiom[/u] is a proposition pertinent to the theory
as a whole, which proposition is not implicitly [del]stated[/del]
asserted in the definitions and postulates, but which
becomes evident to the reader from his observation of
those propositions; and the statement of no axiom,
as Aristotle well remarks, can at all contribute to
the security of any proof, it being merely one of those
[ins]self evident[/ins] observations [ins]of[/ins] which the reader must make one or
more in order to appreciate any demonstration; so
that an axiom is mathematically a superfluity; and
the only good purpose its statement subserves is to
contribute to an understanding of what the doctrine
is built up from;
