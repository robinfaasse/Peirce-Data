A transformation is any operation upon a replica
such that at the beginning of it a replica of a symbol
exists and at the end of it a replica of a symbol exists,
and, generally, of another symbol. Still, the mere continu-
ance unchanged is to be regarded as a transformation.

In other cases, there [del]either[/del] [ins]exist[/ins] at the beginning of the
operation a replica which after it will never exist,
or else there exists at the end of the operation a repli-
ca which never before it had existed, or both. In the
first case there is a curtailment; in the second, an augmen-
tation. Every result of transformation may be brought
about by an augmentation followed by a curtailment.

If A is transformed into B, [del]every[/del] [ins]a[/ins] replica that is trans-
formed into A is transformed into B. This statement is
entitled the [u]dictum de omni[/u].

[marginalia]
and conversely, if
every replica that is
transformed into A is
transformed into B,
then A is transformed
into B.
[/marginalia]

This is to be supposed that our power of augmenting and curtailing
replicas does not go so far that we can destroy every replica and
leave none or create one where there is none. We can only
determine or render indeterminate a replica always present.
