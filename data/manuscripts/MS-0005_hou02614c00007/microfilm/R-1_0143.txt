[u]Symbol[/u].

If two symbols are mutually transformable into one
another, they are said to be equivalent.

The symbols are of three classes having different
properties, which three classes may be designated as
the singular, the vague, and the general.

Every replica of a singular or vague symbol is
capable [del](whether permissively or not)[/del] of being [u]joined[/u] to a replica so that the two
form one symbol.

The class of general symbols consists of a series
of classes called, medads, monads, dyads, triads, tetrads,
etc. together with polyads.
