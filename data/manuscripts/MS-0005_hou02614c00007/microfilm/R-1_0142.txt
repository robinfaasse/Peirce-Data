[u]symbol[/u].

If two symbols are transformable into one another, they are said to be equivalent.

The symbols are of three classes which we may designate as the singular, the vague, and the general.

Every replica of a singular symbol is capable (whether permissively or not) of being joined to a replica or to any number of replicas of any symbols so as to form one symbol. But if [del]one[/del] [ins]several[/ins] replica[ins]s[/ins] of a singular is joined to [del]replicas[/del] [ins]several[/ins], it is joined to them in the same way, so that no transposition of the latter would be significant.
