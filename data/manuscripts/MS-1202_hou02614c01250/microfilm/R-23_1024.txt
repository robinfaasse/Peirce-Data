The Anglo-Saxons had several letters which we
have lost. Finding the six vowels a, e, i, o, u, y, in-
sufficient, they added the digraph æ. They further
distinguished all seven of these as well as the diph-
thongs ea and eo by accenting [ins]them[/ins] when long. They
also used two runes, or letters of the Norse prechristian
alphabet, namely "thorn", þ, for th, and "wen" ƿ, for
w. The latter proved confusing and was replaced by
vv, which became w and W. There was also another
character for th, namely ð; but whether or not it
had a different value from þ is not known.
