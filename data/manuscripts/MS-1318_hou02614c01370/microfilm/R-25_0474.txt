[u]Rienzi, Last of the Tribunes[/u]

Four centuries and more before the French
revolution, something like it was instigated in Rome
by Cola di Rienzi. Cola is nickname for Nicholas,
Rienzo for Lorenzo, or Lawrence; hereditary sur-
name he had none. [date]1313[/date] saw his birth in a dirty
alley near the Tibur, where papa was saloon-keeper,
mamma washerwoman. No pope was then in Rome,
the see having been removed to Avignon. Emperor Henry
of Luxembourg and Robert, King of Naples and
Guelph chieftain, were warring through Italy.
Rome was sunk into deepest barbarism, justice
and order forgotten, every outrage to humanity per-
petrated by day in open streets, with no restraint but
fear of private vengeance. For safety of one's goods,
of one's handsome wife, who might be dragged from her
chamber any time by marauders, [del]one[/del] [ins]a man[/ins] must depend on
his own arm and his friends'. All this story, understand,
I am merely condensing from a contemporary chro-
nicle, with great detriment to vividness.
