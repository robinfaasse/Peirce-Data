Within a year, the barons had been put down, and in
[date]June 1354[/date], Rienzi was ordered to go and live in
Perugia. Here he made the acquaintance of Arimbaldi
and Bettrone, brothers of the robber Monreale. They sup-
plied him with some of their brother's money, with which
he raised an army, and on August 1 entered Rome
as conqueror. Here he opened a tyrannical career by
promptly killing Arimbaldi, Bettrone, and Monreale,
and confiscating the rest of their money. But on [date]October 8[/date]
he was himself assassinated in a rising of the people.

Rienzi was clearly what is called a genius, — a vi-
gorous, unsymmetrical mind. He also makes upon us
the impression of greatness; but among great men
we must reckon him as the smallest. He was not
great in action, nor in reason; but belongs to the cate-
gory of great [del]emotionists[/del] [ins]emotionalists[/ins]. Even his speeches seem now
cold and artificial. He was a more studious and
a more brilliant Robespierre, but with less
[del]factor[/del] staying power.
