a dazzlingly handsome young Roman, his face full
of meaning and fire, his voice with a sort of laugh in its tones.

Now, Rienzi had a half-brother whom he dearly loved,
and this brother was slain by a nobleman; and Rienzi
could get no redress whatever. From that time, he went
about working spirits up against the barons.

In his thirtieth year, the Guelph party were sending
a deputation to the new pope, Clement VI, patron of
learning, to whom subsequently Guelphs sold Avignon.
Rienzi succeeded in having himself added to it as
thirteenth member. [ins]On arrival,[/ins] His conversation incontinently
fascinated Clement; but when he began complaining
of crimes committed by Roman nobility, Cardinal John
de la Colonna took offence, and Rienzi quickly found
himself in disgrace. But his charm had fallen
also on poet Petrarch, a veritable power in that
polite and Frenchy court. So, by his intervention,
