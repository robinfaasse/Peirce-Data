The word "reasoning" shall, throughout this essay, be taken in a somewhat wider sense than usual, to mean any act of thought which consists in one's becoming more inclined to believe in the truth of a proposition called the conclusion of the reasoning on account of the relation of the state of things it asserts to the state of things asserted in another proposition, [ins]assumed to be true[/ins] [del]and[/del] [ins]and[/ins] usually copulative, [del]hereafter[/del] [ins]in this essay this proposition shall[/ins] be called the premiss of the reasoning, (although this term is usually applied to each member of the copulation.) Of old, an inference was hardly regarded as being, in strictness, a "reasoning" unless the reasoner thought the conclusion followed necessarily from the copulate premiss.
