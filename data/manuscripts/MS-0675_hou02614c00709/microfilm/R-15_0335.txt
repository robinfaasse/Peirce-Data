[marginalia]Sense in which "reasoning" is here used.[/marginalia]

[pn]8 1/2[/pn]

The definition and the utility of a definition require it to specify everything essential, and to omit all that is inessential, to its definition: though it may be pardoned for calling special attention to an omission in order to show that it was not inconsiderate. By "Reasoning" shall here be meant any change in thought that results in an appeal for some measure and kind of assent to the truth of a proposition called the "Conclusion" of the reasoning, as being rendered "Reasonable" by an already existing cognition (usually complex) whose propositional formulation shall be termed the "[u]Copulate Premiss[/u]" of the reasoning. The reader will remark, as the point where this definition most markedly breaks with actual usage, that it refuses the name of reasoning to the synthesis into one recognition of the major and minor premisses of a syllogism.
