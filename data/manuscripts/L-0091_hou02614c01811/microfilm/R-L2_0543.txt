SUPPLEMENTARY EXPLANATIONS CONCERNING CERTAIN SUSPENDED VOUCHERS. C.S.Peirce.

Voucher 75. Sub-voucher No. 4. This is a bill of $44.75 signed by A.W.Ames, Ann Arbor Mich., [date]July 31st, 1885[/date], for expressage on 16 packages of instruments from New York to Ann Arbor; and it is suspended for sub-vouchers to support the charge. This demand was made upon me in [date]August, 1885[/date]; and I sent to Mr. Ames for the receipt on the bill-head, which I had never taken. The American Association for the Advancement of Science was then in session, the town was overflowing, and I suppose Mr. Ames was overwhelmed with more business than he was quite prepared to attend to, for he replied that it was inconvenient for him at that time to hunt up the original entry in the books, which would be necessary before he could give me the receipt asked for. I forwarded this reply to Washington, and heard nothing more about it. It is important to note that this voucher was the first one for which a receipt on any other blank than that prescribed by the Department and issued to Assistants had ever been demanded; and that was the reason that I was unprepared for it. I have no doubt that this receipt can be obtained; but the only way to get it is for me to go and get it personally. I maintain that under the circumstances, I am entitled to have my travelling-expenses paid if this unexpected and unprecedented demand is insisted upon, and on receiving orders guaranteeing my travelling expenses, I am quite ready to get the receipt called for. I wish to call attention to the fact that there is nothing suspicious about the voucher. I was instructed to proceed from New York, to Ann Arbor, and 16 packages of instruments is not a large amount, considering the nature of my business, nor is an average of less than $3 a piece a high rate of expressage.

Voucher 75, Sub-voucher 12. t 3rd Quarter of 1885. This is a bill of Messrs, Mabie, Todd, and Bard, the well-known manufacturers of gold pens, of $6 for a stenographic pen for chronograph. An explanation of this voucher has already been furnished, to the effect the use of such a pen [del]A[/del] [ins]on the[/ins] chronograph was a new and very valuable idea, which has been highly praised by the many scientific men to whom its workings have been exhibited, and that it reduces the error of the work and saves both time and money in a manner that Mr. Chenowith can best understand after making the computations at two stations, at one of which it has been used, while at the other it has not, but that until he has done so, he ought to take my word for it that it does have this eff effect, especially as the voucher is approved by the superintendent. If this explanation is not deemed sufficient, I will publish the fact in the scientific journals, and ask scientific men to send in their opinions on the subject. Perhaps those will be found convincing by the First Auditor. Let it be fully understood that I hawe already explained this voucher at some length.
