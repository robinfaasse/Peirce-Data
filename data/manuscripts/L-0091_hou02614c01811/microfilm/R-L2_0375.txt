ever, that there are exceptions.

It is the law, we understand, that no surveyor, which name
is applied to the Assistants, can be appointed without a previous
examination. This law is, however, evaded.

The Assistants are placed in charge of parties named
after the officers.

There is a very intimate connection, we believe, with the
chief of the party, and for this opinion we have the fact that the
accounts of the party are kept with him, and most of the pub-
lic property is in his charge, or used with only his sanction.
This, we think, is as it should be. It would prevent confusion, and
is, we believe, one of the best of rules, to which addition [del]s[/del]
could be made so as to prevent abuse.
