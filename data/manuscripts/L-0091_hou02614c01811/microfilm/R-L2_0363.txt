[u]Superintendent[/u]: This is the office from which Professor J.E.Hil-
gard is at present suspended. That the professor has proved a
failure as Superintendent of the Coast and Geodetic Survey, I do not
hesitate to declare. He is dilatory and vacillating, and addicted
to drink, or some other habit, which at times much impairs his fit-
ness for duty. I believe him to be honest, but a much misguided
man - following customs that have been so long unquestioned that
they have been looked upon as laws; and that to a very great ex-
tent he alone is responsible for the humiliating condition to which
the survey is now brought. It should be borne in mind, however,
that this condition of affairs is no new thing. When I entered
the survey, fifteen years ago, it was about the same, and notwithstand-
ing the careless manner, in which the operations pertaining to the
business transactions of the survey, not of a professional nature,
have been carried on, I cannot point out an instance in which public
funds have been deliberately transferred to private use.

Assistant Andrew Braid has been on duty as executive of-
ficer to the Superintendent since Jan. 1884, aiding especially in the
preparation of instruction for field officers, and details relating
to field work.
