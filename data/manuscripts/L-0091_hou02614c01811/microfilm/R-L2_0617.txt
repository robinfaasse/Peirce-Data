The four thermometers Nos. 3, 4, 714 [del]and[/del] 715 were all of the same size and pattern - Nos. 714 and 715 were used with the Kater pendulum Nos. 4, 6 and 11 for many years and had attained great value. They were broken after they left the C. and G. Survey Office when sent to Assistant Peirce at the Stevens Institute Hoboken, N. J. [date]April 23, 1886[/date]. At the time they left the Office Assistant Braid and I know from personal inspection that they were in good order and well packed.

While the Kater pendulum apparatus was in this country it was in the hands of Assistant Peirce and myself only, after being turned over to the Survey, by Major Herschel. It was never packed or unpacked to my knowledge, at the Office except under my direction. From the statement made by Assistant Peirce to Prof. Stokes as reported to the Office, one may infer that the thermometer had been broken while in my charge.

On page 440 of the C. & G. Survey Report of 1884 is an inventory of the apparatus as it was in my charge, and a statement concerning the syphon barometer. The barometer was in good order when turned over to Assistant Peirce. The glass tube is now broken and the [del]boiler[/del] [ins]leather[/ins] case damaged.
