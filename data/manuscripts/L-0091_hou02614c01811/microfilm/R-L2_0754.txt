You will remember the proposition
which I made to you when you
were here, which seemed to me
and still seems to me the best
for all concerned. I told you
that after your connection with
the Survey as an assistant should
cease, [del]if[/del] [ins]it[/ins] was still desirable, in
my judgment, for the Survey to
avail itself of your special
knowledge of certain subjects—and
that I would willingly make
some arrangement—by means
of which this would be possible.
Appreciating your feelings in
regard to your report, I am
entirely willing that it should
remain with you a while
—that you may put it into shape
as you feel able to do so. If you
compensate you for such service. I feel sure that
we shall often have such problems on hand as
you are well able to [del]dispose[/del] [ins]dispose[/ins] of and such an
arrangement will be valuable to us. I also offered
to prepare a short notice of your withdrawal from
the Survey for "Science"—in which such an
arrangement as the above should be referred to.
You did not seem entirely favorable to this when
I spoke to you about it, and before doing it I will
await your approval. Please do me the justice to
see that in all of this matter I have been guided by
my sense of responsibility to the Government and by a feeling
of respect for your attainments, your work and your past services—as
well as personal regard and sympathy for you.
I am yours faithfully—T. C. Mendenhall.
