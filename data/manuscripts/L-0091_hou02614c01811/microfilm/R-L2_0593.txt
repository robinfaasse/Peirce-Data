Signal Office,
War Department,
Washington City, [date]April 11, 1887[/date].

The Superintendent,
The United States Coast and Geodetic Survey,
Washington, D. C.

Sir:

I have the honor to acknowledge the receipt of your letter of 9th instant, containing statement of Assistant Peirce, of your survey, with reference to an alleged loss of mass of the pendulum used by the Lady Franklin Bay Expedition at Fort Conger, Grinnell Land.

I have the honor to state that no accident of any kind or nature ever occurred to this pendulum while at Lady Franklin Bay. The pendulum was originally suspended by me, and I also personally reversed the pendulum in the entire course of observations, so that I can speak with the most positive personal assurance that the loss of mass alleged could not have occurred through any accident in handling. The pendulum was returned to Washington in the condition in which it was received originally from the Coast Survey, and the suggestion put forward by Assistant Peirce that "either the pieces of brass which were interposed between the knives and the thumb screws were lost, or the ends of these screws were broken off," is, to speak lightly, gratuitous.
