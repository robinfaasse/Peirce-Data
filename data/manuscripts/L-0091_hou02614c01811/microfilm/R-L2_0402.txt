Mr. B. A. Colonna
My dear Sir

Your idea of asking Assist. Peirce to withdraw his resignation is an excellent one in which, for the scientific reputation of the Survey, I heartily join. So far from being inimical (as he thinks) of his work I may tell you that when the question was put to me by the Commission "What do you think of Mr. P's pendulum work" I said "it is of the highest standard and its value is appreciated at home, as well as in Europe. Surely I am the last man to want the pendulum work [del]I am already too much loaded down[/del] - About that newspaper paragraph purporting to be part of Commission's report and to which Mr. P. takes 4 exceptions you know I had nothing whatever to do with, not even in the most remotest way. When I was asked officially to submit an estimate of probable expense for occupying a pendulum station I did so and am pleased that I came near the mark, tho' whether the amount was more or less than [del]what[/del] the expenses had heretofore been I know not.
