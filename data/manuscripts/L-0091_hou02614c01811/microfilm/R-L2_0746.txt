U. S. Coast and Geodetic Survey Office,

Washington, [date]Oct 1, 1891[/date].

Dear Professor Peirce:

Yours of the 29th of September reached me today. I want to thank you for having evidently accepted my previous letter in the spirit in which it was written. I should regret very much to have you think that I am influenced in this matter by any other than the most kindly feelings towards you, or that I failed to appreciate your rare abilities and long service. I shall be greatly pleased if the matter can be arranged between ourselves and all appearance of conflict or stress avoided. I think, and have all along thought that there are ways in which your services can be valuable to us, in the occasional discussion and report upon subjects in which you are a recognized authority and concerning which we require information.
