the place with great reluctance for
I am fully aware of how much
I fall short of the ideal head
of such an able and distinguished
body of men. In accepting the
responsibilities of the position, after
it had come to me without the
slightest personal solicitation, I am
moved only by an earnest desire
to do something, as much as I can,
for the conservation and advancement
of the Corps and its work. I need
hardly say that in so doing I
shall need and I trust I may
have the loyal support and assistance
of its members. I bring to the
work, I think I may say, a fair
general knowledge of the several
operations of the survey and a little
more than that of some of them.
Naturally I will do my best to
succeed but with an attitude of unfriendliness on the
part of those upon whom I must depend, success
would be extremely doubtful. With a united, harmonious and
zealous corps of Assistants, such as I am encouraged to
believe, by yourself and others, I shall find, I think
I may hope for a fair measure of its
[del]success[/del]
Yours faithfully
T. C. Mendenhall
