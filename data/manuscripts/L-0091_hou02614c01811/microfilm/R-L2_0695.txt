U. S. Coast and Geodetic Survey Office,
Washington, D. C. Aug. 23" 1889.

Mr. Chas. Peirce,
Assistant C. and G. Survey,
Milford, Pa.

Dear Sir:
It is my desire to obtain from every Assistant in the U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey a full expression of his views regarding the general and special results which ought to be reached by the organization, with any suggestions looking to the modification of its present scheme or plan to make it a fitter instrument for the accomplishment of these results.

It is also desirable to consider whether there are any lines along which the operations of the Survey can at this time be considerably extended and especially how it can be brought into a more intimate relationship with the general public.

I will be pleased to have you communicate your views in relation to these matters and I trust that you will not hesitate to respond with perfect freedom upon any or all phases of the subject, keeping in mind that the sole object of the inquiry is the increase of the efficiency and the extension of the usefulness of the Survey. I need hardly add that whatever you may have to say will be regarded as confidential.

I am
Yours respectfully,
T. C. Mendenhall
Superintendent.
