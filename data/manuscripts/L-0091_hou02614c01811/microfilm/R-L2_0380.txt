[u]Having concluded this Report, I beg your indulgence to re-[/u]
fer to the case of Assistant Boutelle, one of the employees dis-
missed by the late order of the Hon. Sec. of the Treasury, whose re-
moval, I am sure, must have been taken because no other way could
be seen of relieving the embarrassed condition of the office.
The situation of Mr. Boutelle, I am informed, is crushed by
the weight of personal affliction which has fallen upon him at
this particular time.

He is a gentleman of high character, of great industry, in-
tegrity, and capacity. The record of his whole life is
without a blemish.

He is a man of high scientific attainments, who has
never, I believe, been accused of any acts for which he is
now punished, or considered in any application he may make to be
restored to duty.

I cannot but add, that with the exception of Professor
Bache, no one's services have been more given without
regard to hours, or between whom a stronger attachment to him and
to the work existed, and I feel that Boutelle, with whom I spent
some of my youth, is now made to suffer for a condi-
tion of affairs for which Professor Peirce is, I find, responsible.
