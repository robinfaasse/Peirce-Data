[date]May 11th 1903[/date]

Dear Mr. Peirce,
I am glad to get your note of Saturday. It had occurred to me in the gallery to tell you of the curious spectacle presented by the roomful of visitors at the Monet exhibition, [del]a few years ago[/del] [ins]here, at the St. Botolph club[/ins] when I entered from the Clubrooms. They formed a ring faced towards the center of the room apparently gazing at each other, until I saw that each one was looking past his vis-a-vis at the picture on the opposite wall, thus:—

[fig]Diagram showing people in a circle looking across at opposite walls[/fig]

That power I then acquired to catch onto his view by I have since lost, but must regain.

Farm life is to me and my wife & children ideal also and in such a great good fortune that my avocation permits us to enjoy & profit by it. Here we could act in a little more physical strength to meet the needs of our independence.

Am, Signature, Cordially yours
Benjamin Ives Gilman
