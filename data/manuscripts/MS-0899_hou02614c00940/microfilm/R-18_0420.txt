No doubt, it may be quite true that one swa is not
another swa. Only, this otherness does not belong to the
swa as such; and since the swa is nothing but a such-
ness, that which does not belong to it as such does
not belong to it in its being; so that strictly speaking
it cannot be said that it is other.

[del]Those[/del] Many logicians opine that otherness is to be
defined by negation. If one thing is other than [ins]another[/ins] [del]other[/del]
thing, they hold that this otherness consists in there
being some quality, that is, an indefinite quality,
which one of the two things has while the other has
it not. But quality is essentially definite so far
as it is anything; as we have seen is true of
[del]a thing[/del]. On the contrary, two diverse things would
remain diverse, although one were to change its qualities
