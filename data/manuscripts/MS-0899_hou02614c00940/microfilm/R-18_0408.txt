§ 1. We may suppose that there are elements of the world each of which is such as it is regardless of anything else there may be other than that which constitutes its [del]being[/del] [ins]reality[/ins]. For the hypothesis expressly excludes the possibility of any contradiction in it, for anything that is self-contradictory has [del]no[/del] parts which conflict: these parts are not the whole; but the whole is such as it is by virtue of what those parts are. But what I suppose, is such as it is regardless of any parts or anything else. If there involves no contradiction, and may be supposed.
