[del]The tendency of life to increase is always in a greater ratio
than the food by which it is supported.
A struggle for existence must follow.
This struggle is between individuals of the same species
and between individuals of different species.
The result of this struggle is that the most fit
survive and multiply.
This is natural selection.
Natural selection is the preservation of favorable variations
and the rejection of injurious variations.
Variations that are neither useful nor harmful
are not affected by natural selection.
These variations may be called neutral.
A species may be modified by natural selection
so as to become better adapted to its environment.
This modification may take place in many ways.
One way is by the accumulation of small
variations.
Another way is by the preservation of large
variations.
A species may also be modified by the
direct action of the environment.
This is called use and disuse.
Use and disuse may cause a part to become
larger or smaller.
These changes may be inherited.
Natural selection and use and disuse
are the two main factors of evolution.
There are also other factors, such as
sexual selection and isolation.
Sexual selection is the selection of mates
based on certain characteristics.
Isolation is the separation of a population
into two or more groups.
This may lead to the formation of new species.
Evolution is a slow process.
It takes place over long periods of time.
The evidence for evolution is found in
the fossil record, in embryology, and in
comparative anatomy.[/del]
