The gravity at any point on the surface of the
earth or at any distance above it can be
expressed as an equation consisting of terms of
the masses of the various components or
shells of the earth. By determining gravity
at various stations and at various distances
above the earth's surface it would be
possible to find out the values of these mass
coefficients.
However, it is found that the gravity at
various stations is largely influenced by
local circumstances such as the proximity of
mountains, the density of rocks in the neighborhood
the presence of large masses of water and
these local variations are much greater than
the effect of the distribution of density in the
interior of the earth.
