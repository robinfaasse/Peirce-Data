The first year of my own serious study
of philosophy, in 1856, forty-seven years
ago was devoted to esthetics. My good
angel must have prompted me to take up first
that branch of philosophy which ought im-
mediately to follow the study of the catego-
ries, and to study it in a German book
which though it was too old to be sensibly
influenced by Hegel was nevertheless one of
those books in which the three [ins]categories,[/ins] in an almost
unrecognizable disguise, played a great part.
It was Schiller's [u]ästhetische Briefe[/u], — a very
good book for an infant philosopher.
