The relation of a number to any property or character it has. We will use "has," so that A [u]has[/u] [u]$\alpha$[/u], will mean that the number, A, has the property, $\alpha$. Then, the third proposition is:

Taking any number, A, and any number, B, and any number, C, and any property, $\alpha$, either [u]not A then C[/u], or [u]not B then C[/u], or [u]not A has $\alpha$[/u], or [u]B has $\alpha$[/u].

In ordinary language, two numbers than [ins]both of[/ins] which the same number is next greater are identical, or, no number is next greater than two different numbers. The fourth proposition is:

Taking any property, $\alpha$, [ins]there is[/ins] a number, A, such that, taking any number B, and any number C, either [u]not first has $\alpha$[/u], or [u]not A then B[/u], or [u]A has $\alpha$ but not B has $\alpha$[/u], or [u]C has $\alpha$[/u].

In ordinary language, every property which belongs to [u]first[/u] and to every ordinal number next greater than [del]a number to which it belongs[/del], belongs to every [ins]ordinal[/ins] number.
