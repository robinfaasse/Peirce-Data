necessary reasoning, we may restrict ourselves to two
"quantifications", as they are called, namely [u]any[/u] and [u]some[/u].
[u]Any[/u] signifies that the statement will be true, no matter what
individual is taken. [u]Some[/u] means that an individual can be so
selected that the statement shall be true. If two selections
are of different kinds, it makes a difference which is made
first. The earlier [u]some[/u] makes the more comprehensive state-
ment. Thus, to say that taking any man, A, a man, B, can
thereafter be so chosen that it will be true that A loves B,
is less than to say that a man B can be so chosen that, taking
thereafter any man, A, whatever, A loves B. The former states
that every man loves some man or another; the latter that
there is a man whom all men love.

In the quantifies it will be convenient to write an
upright letter, as A, to mean "Taking anything whatever, A",
and a prone letter, as [fig]prone letter A[/fig], to mean, "An individual, A, can
be found such that."
