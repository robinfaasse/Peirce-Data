The main thing is to so rationalize grammar
that its forms may be pliant to the [del]so[/del] needs of
inference, and that this process may be
exhibited in the transformations of the expressions.

We have seen in a former discussion that
demonstrative words, such as, [u]this[/u], [u]that[/u], [u]hi![/u], etc.
play an essential part in speech, that of connecting
its ideal world with the real world. Nouns are nothing
but pro-demonstratives; and verbs are little more. In
the language of necessary argument, the use of
demonstratives should be carried to its furthest
extent. Everything ought to be so indicated whose
transformations play no part in the process of
