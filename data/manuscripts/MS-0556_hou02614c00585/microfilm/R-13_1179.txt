I will now roughly sketch a Field in the domain of
logic which for some quarter of a century after DeMorgan
quit the plough, except for my good efforts, lay substan-
tially untilled. Here we produce rules for drawing and
testing necessary inferences. That ordinary syllogism
fails to do this De Morgan had fully demonstrated in 1860;
and the mental life and vigor of logical treatise-writers
has been such that at the end of thirty years, perhaps one
percent of them have waked up to the question. But a
whole gang of brawny cultivators are at work now; so that,
as some of them do not inform me of what they have done, I
may fail to give them due credit in this sketch.

The main thing is to so rationalize grammar that
its forms may be pliant to the needs of inference, and that
this process may be exhibited in the transformations of the
expressions.
