These leashes have themselves to be leashed; and
this calls for the use of a sign which, perhaps more than
any other, has contributed to confer upon algebra its
dynamic superiority over ordinary language; I mean the par-
enthesis. For we have to distinguish between (ABC)DE,
A(BCD)E, and AB(CDE), according as the first, middle, or
last member of the leash is itself a leash. This is the
punctuation of algebra.

All statements might be put into such forms that one
member of every leash should be a logical relation. For the
primary leashes, this does not seem to be convenient; but for
the others, it answers very well. Negation can be expressed
by means of a couple. The best plan is to draw a heavy line
over the statement to be denied; which is a form of coupling.
Thus if ShP means S has the character P, [u]ShP[/u] will mean that
S has [u]not[/u] the character P. The other logical relations for
