All the inferences then are obtained
by raising the Boolean to some power
and identifying.

For example
[del]$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{1} \Sigma_{\beta} \Pi_{3}$[/del] $\Sigma_{\alpha} \Sigma_{\beta} \Pi_{1} \Pi_{4} \Sigma_{\gamma} \Sigma_{\delta} \Pi_{3} \Pi_{6}$

$\overline{x}_{1} + \gamma_{\alpha 12} y_{2} (\overline{\gamma}_{\alpha 32} + I_{13}) + \overline{y}_{4} + \gamma_{\beta 45} x_{5} (\overline{\gamma}_{\beta 65} + I_{46})$

to the $n\textsuperscript{th}$ power gives

$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Sigma_{\alpha'} \text{etc} \Pi_{1} \Pi_{1'} \text{etc} \Sigma_{\gamma} \Sigma_{\gamma'} \text{etc} \Pi_{3} \Pi_{3'} \text{etc}$

$S \overline{x}_{1}^{P} \overline{y}_{4}^{Q} x_{5}^{R} y_{2}^{n-P-Q-R}$

The first power instead of yielding
$\Pi_{1} \Sigma_{2} (\overline{x}_{1} + y_{2})$ gives only
$\Pi_{1} \Pi_{4} \Sigma_{2} \Sigma_{5} (\overline{x}_{1} + x_{5} + \overline{y}_{4} + y_{2})$

The second power
$\overline{x}_{1} + \overline{x}_{1'} + I_{13} I_{1'3'} + y_{2} y_{2'} \gamma_{\alpha 12} \overline{\gamma}_{\alpha 32} \gamma_{\alpha' 1'2'} \overline{\gamma}_{\alpha' 3'2'}$
$+ \overline{y}_{4} + \overline{y}_{4'} + I_{46} I_{4'6'} + x_{5} x_{5'} \gamma_{\beta 45} \overline{\gamma}_{\beta 65} \gamma_{\beta' 4'5'} \overline{\gamma}_{\beta' 6'5'}$
$+ \text{etc}$

And so any power will yield an identical
proposition.

Ordinary reasoning can hardly
show that this proposition holds
even for ultra-infinites.
