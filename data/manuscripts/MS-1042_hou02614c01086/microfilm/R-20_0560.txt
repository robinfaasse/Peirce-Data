[pn]10[/pn] [del]refused[/del]. It must be understood that it is because he
was a very great chemist and often an almost miraculous
theorizer that it becomes worth while to examine his failings
so closely. Besides his obstinate "one-sidedness", he sometimes
jumped to conclusions in consequence of not having drawn
his logical distinctions as finely as it is requisite for any
chemist theorizer, but especially a chemist, to do. Thus, Clausius
needed no facts that were not easily accessible to Berzelius,
in order to prove that electrolytes are not decomposed by
the voltaic current; but what he lacked was the subtly per-
tinent logic of Clausius. Again, Berzelius opined for a
long time that the properties of chemical substances were
exclusively due to the material of their constituent atoms.
