[pn]4[/pn] [del]D[/del] Differences between the atomic weights of Argon and
Potassium, of Nickel and Cobalt, of Tellurium and
Iodine, [del]had[/del] were matters of grave concern to the great
Russian and his followers, while the extreme irregularities
in the differences of successive atomic weights, — [del]G-I = 2[/del]
B-C = 2, C-B = 1, N-C = 2, O-N = 2, F-O = 3, etc. — have not
troubled them in the least. Now if it be really true that the order
is the principal point and the values of the atomic weights so
secondary as they are regarded, it must be that mass
is a secondary and not a primary property of matter. This
reminds us of the extreme variety of the electron theory.
Only the modes in which the mass is secondary in the one
case and in the other appear to be quite unrelated.
