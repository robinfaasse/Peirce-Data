[u]Peripatetic Talks. No. 5.[/u]

The principle of Existential Graphs may be stated thus:
Whatever verbs A and B may be, [fig]oval containing A and B, with B enclosed in a smaller oval[/fig] asserts that of every individual [ins]object[/ins] of the universe of discourse of which A is true (if there be any such object) B is true. The heavy line [del]denotes[/del] (or the outermost part of it) denotes the individual spoken of, and the continuity of this line signifies its identity; and if there are several [del]separate[/del] [ins]disconnected[/ins] heavy lines each denotes an individual. The writing of the proposition down asserts it; and if several are written down all are asserted. An [ins]lightly drawn[/ins] oval with its contents is a proposition; and any proposition [del]included in[/del] [ins]oddly enclosed[/ins] [del]it alone[/del] is not asserted but only posited as a condition under which some proposition in the same enclosures and one besides is asserted.

This statement as useful as showing precisely what the fundamental assumptions of the system are. But in order to comprehend a given graph another form of statement is preferable as follows:

1[sup]st[/sup], every oval with its contents is a verb. -

2[sup]nd[/sup], every place of attachment of a heavy line to a verb is a subject blank of the verb.

3[sup]rd[/sup], every heavy line is the verb "- is identical with -" or "- and - are identical with -" etc according to the number of branches.

4[sup]th[/sup], the outermost point of every heavy line not attached to a designate individual index is to be considered as denoting some suitably chosen individual object of the universe of discourse

5[sup]th[/sup], every unenclosed proposition is asserted

6[sup]th[/sup], every oval with its contents is the precise denial of [del]the proposition which its contents[/del]
