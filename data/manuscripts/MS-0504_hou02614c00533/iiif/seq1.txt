The principle of Existential Graphs may be stated thus:
Whatever verbs A and B may be, [fig]figure[/fig] asserts that of every
[ins]individual[/ins] object of the universe of discourse of which A is true (if there
be any such object) B is true. The heavy line [del]denotes[/del] (or the outermost
part of it) denotes the individual spoken of, and the continuity of
this line signifies its identity; and if there are several [del]discrete[/del] separate
[ins]heavy[/ins] lines each denotes an individual. The writing of the proposition down
asserts it; and if several are written down all are asserted. An [ins]lightly drawn[/ins]
oval with its contents is a proposition; and any [ins]oddly enclosed[/ins] proposition [del]included in[/del]
[del]it alone[/del] is not asserted but only posited as a condition under which
some proposition in the same [del]enclosures[/del] and one besides is asserted.

This statement is useful as showing precisely what the fundamental
assumptions of the system are. But in order to comprehend a given
graph another form of statement is preferable as follows:
1[sup]st[/sup], every oval with its contents is a verb. -
2[sup]nd[/sup], every place of attachment of a heavy line to a verb is a subject-blank
of the verb.
3[sup]rd[/sup], every heavy line is the verb "- is identical with -" or "- and
- are identical with -" etc according to the number of branches.
4[sup]th[/sup], the outermost point of every heavy line not attached to a designate
individual index is to be considered as denoting some suitably chosen indivi-
dual object of the universe of discourse
5[sup]th[/sup], every unenclosed proposition is asserted.
6[sup]th[/sup], every oval with its contents is the precise denial of [del]its contents[/del] [ins]the proposition which it encloses[/ins]
