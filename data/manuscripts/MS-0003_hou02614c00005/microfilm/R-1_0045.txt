<u>Chapter I. The Fundamental Hypotheses of Pure Dyadics.</u>

It will be convenient to divide Dyadics into three parts, to be called the Alpha Part, the Beta Part, and the Gamma Part. The Alpha part is confined to what is absolutely requisite to making any dyadics. The Beta part adds a conception necessary for the fuller development of dyads, even as a pure mathematical theory. The Gamma part introduces certain conceptions which become necessary for the full development of the subject, when it is applied to logic. This Gamma part will, therefore, not appear in the present chapter.

<u>Hypothesis No I.</u> Let it be supposed that there is a object called a <u>sheet</u> and that there is an intelligent agent called a <u>graphist</u> who can cause determinations of a sheet, called [del]graphs[/del] [ins]“graph-replicas”[/ins], if, and only if, these accord with two sets of general principles, namely, a certain set of general hypotheses concerning what is supposed to be actually true of [del]the[/del] a sheet and its determinations, and [del]an[/del] another set of general hypotheses describing what [ins]general[/ins] sorts of [del]gene[/del] modifications [ins]of the “graph-replicas”[/ins] would be permissively possible under different general conditions.
