To the interpreter, within certain limits and in certain respects,
as to what its object or meaning shall be regarded as being,
it may be called [u]general[/u], or non-individual. Let us use
the Italic lower-case letters to represent general signs. It is
evident that no sign can be at once vague and general in
the same respect. For as long as the determination of the
meaning depends upon the choice of one party, the other
must follow it, or they will be at cross-purposes; and the
sign will fail to function as such.

Let [del]a[/del] [ins]$\alpha$[/ins] stand for any dyadic relation, and $\mathbb{D}$ for its con-
verse. Then if $A \ \alpha \ B$, also $B \ \mathbb{D} \ A$; if Sortes loves Plato,
Plato is loved by Sortes. Likewise, if $A \ \alpha \ \beta$, then $\beta \ \mathbb{D} \ A$; if
Sortes loves some man, some man is loved by Sortes. Further if $A \ \alpha \ \mathbb{b}$,
then $\mathbb{b} \ \mathbb{D} \ A$; if Sates loves every man, every man is loved by
Sortes. So also $\alpha \ \mathbb{b} = \beta \ \mathbb{D} \ \alpha$ and $\underline{a} \ \mathbb{b} = \underline{\mathbb{b}} \ \mathbb{D} \ \underline{a}$.
