If there is no horizontal stretch we imagine an infinitely short
one interposed at the lowest point. If this is not at either end, but
at $n = I$, we make the stretch horizontal by separating the
weight $w_I - w_{I-1}$ into two parts $w_I - w_H$ and $w_H - w_{I-1}$ such that
$$w_H = w_I - Q \tan \tau_I = w_{I-1} - Q \tan \tau_{I-1}$$
which (4) shows to be possible. If the lowest point of the cord is
at one tower, there is an upward pull on that tower. We balance it
by an equal weight at an infinitesimal distance on the cord, making
either $w_H = Q \tan \tau_0$
or $w_H = - Q \tan \tau_N$;
for $\tau_N$ will be negative if $N$ is the lowest point.

We may apply the principle of virtual velocities. This at once
shows the horizontal component of the stress is [del]the[/del] same at all points[del], for[/del][ins]. For[/ins]
consider the portion of the cord between any two points. Move one hori-
zontally by an infinitesimal distance. The centre of gravity will be
raised or lowered. Carry the other extremity horizontally in the same direc-
tion by the same amount. The two motions will merely shift the [del]whole[/del]
horizontally. Hence there are equal and opposite [ins]horizontal[/ins] forces on the two points
due to the intervening weight. We also show that the vertical stress equals
the weight between the point and the horizontal stretch. For consider the
portion of the cord between any point and the horizontal part. Raise
both ends vertically. The form of the intervening portion is unchanged, and its
centre of gravity is raised by the same amount. But there is evidently
no vertical stress on the horizontal part. Hence, this measures the vertical
stress at the other end.
