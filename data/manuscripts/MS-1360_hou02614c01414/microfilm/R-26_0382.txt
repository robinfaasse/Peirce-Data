We now come to the question of the live load. This is supposed
to be uniformly distributed over a portion of the cable. It therefore
hangs in a common catenary. But all common catenaries are
of the same shape and differ only in size (their limits not
being considered.) The load equals the weight of 25 metres of
the cable. We will therefore begin by supposing the part of the
cable loaded (whose limits we will designate as p and q) to
be increased in length 25 metres, [del]q[/del] [ins]Q[/ins]/[ins]k[/ins] and H remaining fixed.
After deducting the additions to x_N and y_N due to the 25 metres,
owing to the [del]t[/del] [ins]τ[/ins] having a greater absolute value in the part above
the loaded part than before the addition of the 25 metres, the
[ins]remaining[/ins] x_N will not be quite 1000 metres while the remaining -y_N will
a little exceed 50 metres. We shall therefore have to make corrections
of Q/k and H, by solving the differential equations.

We begin by calculating τ, s, x, y, etc for p and q before
