But this is certainly not strictly true to its whole extent and therefore it is best to use it as a mere suggestion, going back to the principle of virtual velocities on which it is founded. If we conceive the suspenders to be so strong that no sensible amount of work is done in stretching them (as I am obliged to assume since their dimensions have not been furnished to me), then the whole elastic work consists bending the truss and stretching the main cable and backstays. Then, we are to conceive that after the centre of gravity has found its level, there are various deformations which might occur, and which do work [ins](positive or negative)[/ins] in stretching the cable (positively or negatively) and also in bending the truss (positively or negatively.) But these two amounts of work would necessarily sum up to zero; otherwise there would be a tendency actually to undergo such deformation.
