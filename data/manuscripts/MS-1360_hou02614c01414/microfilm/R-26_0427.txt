In measuring the modulus of elasticity, E, of a cable of length, L, cross section B, we attach a weight W, which descends, by [del]pull[/del] stretching the cable, for a distance [math]\delta[/math]L, doing the work by gravity W[math]\delta[/math]L, [del]at the expense of the cab[/del] [ins]and increasing by that amount[/ins] the positional energy of the molecules of the cable. The stress is [math]\frac{W}{B}[/math] on each unit of cross section; the strain is [math]\frac{\delta L}{L}[/math]; the modulus of elasticity is the ratio of the former to the latter, or [math]E = \frac{W L}{B \delta L}[/math]. The pull on the cable is [math]E B \frac{\delta L}{L} = W[/math] and the work which this has done is [math]\frac{W^2}{E B} L[/math].

In the main-chains, the inclination of the chain to the horizon at any point being denoted by [math]\tau[/math], the pull is H sec [math]\tau[/math]. The length through an element, dx, of horizontal distance is sec [math]\tau \cdot dx[/math].
