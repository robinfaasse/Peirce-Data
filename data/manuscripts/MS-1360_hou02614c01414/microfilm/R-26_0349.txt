§ 4. Regular distribution of weight. Since, by (5) and (3),

$$\frac{ds}{dx} = \sqrt{1 + \frac{w^2}{Q^2}},$$

and also $s_N$ and $y_N$, if one knows $w$ as a function of either $s$ or $x$, we can find all the other quantities.

It is also plain that if the maxima stresses are given, that is, $q_0$ and $q_N$, we have only to know $w_0'$ or $w_N'$ to have $\tau_0$, $\tau_N$, $Q$, etc.

The differential calculus affords the formulae

$$\rho = \frac{\sec^3 \tau}{d^2y/dx^2} = \frac{ds}{d\tau}.$$

By (5) and (3)

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = Q^{-1} \frac{dw}{dx}.$$

Consequently

$$\frac{1}{\rho_H} = Q^{-1} \left(\frac{dw}{dx}\right)_H.$$

We have by (4)

$$s = \int \sec \tau . dx = \frac{1}{Q} \int q \, dx.$$
