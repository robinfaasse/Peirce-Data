§ The Common Catenary. Let
$$Fs = ks$$
where $k$ is constant. Then
$$\tan \tau = \frac{ks}{Q}$$
is the "intrinsic" equation of the curve. To transform to rectangular co-
ordinates,
$$\sec^2 \tau \cdot d\tau = \frac{k}{Q} ds$$
and remembering $k$ is constant the integrals expressing $x'$ and
$y'$ can be resolved. Namely, attending to the [del]limits[/del] lower limit,
$$\frac{kx'}{Q} = \frac{k}{Q} \int \cos \tau \cdot ds = \int \sec \tau \cdot d\tau = \log \tan (45^\circ + \frac{\tau}{2})$$
$$\frac{ky'}{Q} = \frac{k}{Q} \int \sin \tau \cdot ds = \int \sin \tau \cdot \sec^2 \tau \cdot d\tau = \sec \tau - 1.$$

Using the notation of Gudermannians,
$$\tan \tau = \sinh \frac{kx'}{Q} = \sqrt{(\frac{ky'}{Q}+1)^2 - 1} = \frac{ks'}{Q}$$
$$\sec \tau = \cosh \frac{kx'}{Q} = \frac{ky'}{Q} + 1 = \sqrt{1 + (\frac{ks'}{Q})^2} = \frac{k\rho}{Q}$$
$$\sec^2 \tau = \cosh^2 \frac{kx'}{Q} = (\frac{ky'}{Q} + 1)^2 = 1 + (\frac{ks'}{Q})^2 = \frac{k\rho}{Q}$$
$$\tan \frac{\tau}{2} = \tanh \frac{kx'}{2Q} = \frac{y'}{s'}$$

Finally,
$$\frac{k\xi'}{Q} = \frac{kx'}{Q} - \frac{y'}{s'}$$
$$\frac{k\eta'}{Q} = \frac{1}{2} (\frac{y'}{s'} + \frac{ky'}{Q} - 1).$$
