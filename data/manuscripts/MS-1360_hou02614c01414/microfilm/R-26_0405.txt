The dead load on the truss has the following parts:

[u]1[ins]st[/ins] Part[/u]. The weight, equal to $Kdx$ upon every element of its length from $-x_2$ to $+x_2$

[u]2[ins]nd[/ins] Part[/u]. The first part of the lift of the cable, until it begins to be uniform throughout the suspended portion. To see what this is imagine the suspenders to pass through holes in the truss, so that they can fall down through it, but are arrested when they are drawn up so that their lower ends reach the truss. The suspenders must be supposed to have such lengths that when they reach this position, the cable, in the part from which they are suspended takes the form of a parabola. The [del]cable being[/del] [ins]truss being[/ins] held up into its place by other supports, say scaffolding, and the cable being [ins]at first[/ins] so long that none of the suspenders reach their lower bearings, is gradually drawn up. The first suspenders to reach their bearings are those in the centre; for the outer parts of the cable being weighted by its own oblique position and by the longer suspenders hangs too vertically for the [ins]ends of the[/ins] suspenders to be caught in the truss. As the cable is drawn up, more and more suspenders take their places, until at the moment the last ones reach their bearings the downward stress on the cable varies uniformly with $x$. The [del]last[/del] [ins]outermost[/ins] elements do not lift the truss at all, and the load upon [del]them[/del] is that due to the weight of cable and the weight of suspenders, or
