To measure the modulus of elasticity E of a cable we hang
a weight W [del]to[/del] [ins]and[/ins] pull on the end and note the increase of length
$\delta$ l it produces. Then, B being the cross-section, the tension $\frac{\text{W}}{\text{B}}$
is brought on each unit of cross section and the strain $\frac{\delta\text{l}}{\text{l}}$ is
produced on each unit of length. Then the modulus is taken as the
ratio of the stress to the strain, or $\frac{\text{W}\text{l}}{\text{B}\delta\text{l}} = \text{E}$. Thus, the cable being
stretched to any [del]point[/del] degree, $\delta\text{l}/\text{l}$, the pull upon it being [del]$\frac{\text{W}\delta\text{l}}{\text{E}\text{B}}$[/del] [ins]$\text{E}\frac{\delta\text{l}}{\text{l}}\text{B}$[/ins],
the work done by any infinitesimal additional elongation will be
equal to this pull multiplied by the elongation. [del]At any point of
the main cable, where its inclination to the horizon is $\tau$, the
pull under the dead load is H sec $\tau$.[/del]
