It is important to consider what the effect [del]will[/del] [ins]is[/ins] upon
the common catenary [del]will be[/del] of varying $H$ and $Q$ while
$s_N$ remains constant.

All common catenaries are of the same shape (except of
course, as to their limits) and a change of $H$ does not change
the size; it only moves the whole catenary. Suppose $H$ be in-
creased. Then imagining the catenary to extend into the minus
side of $O$, the effect is to shove it down, so that a portion that
was on that minus side of $O$ comes to $O$. To keep $s_N$ con-
stant an equal piece has to be cut off at that end. Thus,
the motion of $N$, when $O$ is fixed is in a line
parallel to the line bisecting the angle between the
tangents at $O$ and $N$.

[fig]Catenary curve with points O and N and tangent lines[/fig]

$$\delta_H x_N = (\cos \tau_O - \cos \tau_N) \delta H$$
$$\delta_H y_N = (\sin \tau_O - \sin \tau_N) \delta H$$

[marginalia]Suspension[/marginalia]

Since $\tau_O$ is negative and its absolute value not far from $\tau_N$ for [del]the[/del]
bridges, $\delta x$ is very small, and $\delta y$ is negative.

A variation of $Q$ changes the size of the catenary. Supposing
$H$ to remain fixed and horizontal, for any given value of $\tau$, $\frac{y}{x}$ will
remain constant. But $s$ will be increased in the same ratio in
which $Q$ is increased. The consequence is that, $O$ remaining fixed,

$$\delta_{\frac{Q}{k}} x_N = \left( \frac{k x_N}{Q} - \sin \tau_N + \sin \tau_O \right) \delta \frac{Q}{k}$$
$$\delta_{\frac{Q}{k}} y_N = \left( \frac{k y_N}{Q} - \sin \tau_N \tan \tau_N + \sin \tau_O \tan \tau_O \right) \frac{\delta Q}{k}$$
$$= (\cos \tau_N - \cos \tau_O) \frac{\delta Q}{k}$$
