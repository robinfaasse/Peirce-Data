§ [u]The Common Catenary[/u]. Let
[del]T's = k's[/del]
where k is a constant. Then
[u]tan τ = [fraction]ks'[/fraction][/u],
is the equation of the catenary. To transform this to rectangular
coordinates, we have
sec² τ dτ = [fraction]k[/fraction] ds',

[fraction]kx'[/fraction] = [fraction]k[/fraction] ∫ cos τ . ds' = log tan (45° + [fraction]τ[/fraction]),
[fraction]ky'[/fraction] = [fraction]k[/fraction] ∫ sin τ . ds' = ∫ sin τ sec² τ . dτ = sec τ - 1.

Using the notation of Gudermannians
Sinh [fraction]kx'[/fraction] = tan τ = [fraction]ks'[/fraction] = √([fraction]ky'[/fraction] + 1)² - 1
Cosh [fraction]kx'[/fraction] = sec τ = √1 + ([fraction]ks'[/fraction])² = [fraction]ky'[/fraction] + 1 = [fraction]q[/fraction]
Cosh² [fraction]kx'[/fraction] = sec² τ = 1 + ([fraction]ks'[/fraction])² = ([fraction]ky'[/fraction] + 1)² = [fraction]kq[/fraction]
[del]Coth [fraction]kx'[/fraction] = cosec τ = [fraction]y'[/fraction][/del]
tan [fraction]τ[/fraction] = [fraction]y'[/fraction]

For a suspension bridge, except up near the towers, if the cord is a
catenary, the following equation is a good approximation
3 [fraction]x'[/fraction] = 2 + [fraction]1[/fraction]
Thus for τ = 20° 3 [fraction]kx'[/fraction] = 1.0691347 2 [fraction]ks'[/fraction] + [fraction]s'[/fraction] = 1.0699605
