The force upon the truss has the following parts:
$1^{\text{st}}$, its weight
$2^{\text{nd}}$, so much of the lift of the cable is brings the downward
force upon it everywhere up to what it is at $x = x_c$ and $-x_c$
To this must be added algebraically the weight of the suspenders.
$3^{\text{rd}}$, the lift due to additional horizontal pull of the cable.
$4^{\text{th}}$, the reactions of the four piers.

Part $1^{\text{st}}$. The weight of the truss is $k \int\limits_{-x_B}^{x_B} dx$

Part $2^{\text{nd}}$. The weight of the cable is $K \int\limits_{-x_c}^{+x_c} \sqrt{1 + 4 \frac{(y_0-y_c)^2}{x_c^4} x^2} \cdot dx$; that of the suspenders
is $K' \int\limits_{-x_c}^{+x_c} \frac{x^2}{x_c^2} \cdot dx$. Thus the negative downward force due to this
together with the weight of the suspenders is
$\int\limits_{-x_c}^{+x_c} \left\{ K \left( \sqrt{1 + 4 \frac{(y_0-y_c)^2}{x_c^4} x^2} - \sqrt{1 + 4 \frac{(y_0-y_c)^2}{x_c^2}} \right) + K' (y_0-y_c) (2 \frac{x^2}{x_c^2} - 1) \right\} dx$

the last effect, and bring the cable, carrying the suspenders, into
place, the vertical pull upon the cable at $\pm x_c$ is the weight
of the suspenders and cable between that point and the horizon-
tal point. The weight of the cable is
