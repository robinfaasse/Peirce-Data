[u]§4. The arc and curvature.[/u] The equations (5) and (3) give

$$\frac{ds}{dx} = \sqrt{1 + \frac{w^2}{Q^2}}$$

Suppose, then, the distribution of the weight is known; that is, suppose
$w$ is a known function either of [u]$x$[/u] or of [u]$s$[/u]. In either case
we are supposed to know the total span, $x_1$, and the total length
of cord, $s_1$. Therefore, by integrating the above with two values of
$Q$ and applying the rule of false, we can soon ascertain $Q$,
which is the weight from $\tau = 0$ to $\tau = 45^\circ$.

If we know the form of the cord, we have no difficulty. For
$dy/dx = \tan \tau$, and from this and the total weight we at once
get $Q$ from (5).

From $Q$ and the distribution of weight we find $\tau$, and thence
[u]$x$[/u] or [u]$s$[/u], and $y$.
