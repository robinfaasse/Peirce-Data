The load, or weight per unit of length, is by Rankine denoted by $w$, as if it were a weight. But its dimensions are really those of a mass divided by the square of a time. Melan uses a $q$, and this we will follow, so far as to denote the live load, [del]considered as varying[/del] [ins]meaning the live load[/ins] at the point considered, by $q$. But when the live load enters into the moment so that it does not vanish for the point considered, although the latter be outside the loaded part, it is necessary to have a different symbol, to avoid error, and that being a constant will be denoted by $Q$. The dead load may be distinguished by a vertical death-stroke [del]$\text{\foreignlanguage{hebrew}{ק}}$[/del] [ins]$\text{\foreignlanguage{hebrew}{ק}}$[/ins].
