[pn]Plate XI[/pn]

The lettering of this plate seems to have been done
somewhat at haphazard. Beginning at this left, [u]s[/u] is a
reservoir for the petroleum which feeds the lamp, and [u]t[/u] a glass tube
to show the height of the oil in [u]s[/u]; [u]r[/u] is the counterpoise of the telescope tube, and [u]x[/u] the
clamp for the movement of the telescope in altitude. The object glass at [u]a[/u], with its tube, is
removable, and may be replaced by either of two others, or by the apparatus for
reducing the image of the sun or moon to a stellar point; at [u]c[/u] is the eye-piece,
and at [u]d[/u] a reflecting prism (missing 1875-6) for zenith observations; at [u]z[/u] is the
pedestal of the instrument, movable
in azimuth. The letter [u]y[/u] is near the
probable location of the fixed Nicol prism,
for the adjustment of which the screws
near [u]y[/u] seems to be designed. The letter
[u]f[/u] stands at the socket in which the
tube carrying the other prisms turns;
at [u]g[/u], [u]h[/u], are the two graduated circles;
that nearest the telescope is the altitude
circle, the other gives the setting of the
prisms; the clamp above [u]h[/u] binds them
together. At [u]m[/u], [u]m[/u], are the handles
for getting the movable prisms, one
of which is probably about that part
of the tube marked [u]i[/u], [u]j[/u], the other just
to the left of the screen [u]v[/u]. The "color
circle" is at [u]k[/u], and at [u]l[/u] is the milled
head for revolving the quartz plate. The
clamp of the "color circle" does not appear.

The letter [u]q[/u] is at one of the standards
set upon [u]z[/u] the oil tube to support the
instrument; [u]v[/u] is a screen (black),
[u]n[/u] a tube carrying concave lens and
pinhole, [u]o[/u] a plate with pinholes of
different sizes, [u]p[/u] the chimney of the lamp,
[u]u[/u] one of the 3 small, and [u]w[/u] one of the
two large tubes to supply the flame
with air (the large tubes deliver the air as
the sides of the flat wick). The lamp and
oil reservoir are placed at opposite ends of the
instrument to enable it conveniently to be
attached to a
large telescope
(Carl's Repertorium
Vol. I, p. 198).

The double
milled head
above the
maker's
name
enables
the tube
of the
object glass
to be turned
round
in azimuth
(when held vertical) without
unscrewing: it is unscrewed
at the single milled head
above.
