To the Editor of the Journal of Speculative Philosophy
Dear Sir

I send you by mail a copy of a paper by me entitled "A Description of a Notation for the Logic of Relatives," and although it relates to the despised subject of formal Logic, you will not perhaps think that some account of the results I have reached will be out of place in your journal, especially as the subject has never been treated except in a mathematical form which repels most readers.

Terms are usually divided by Logicians into [del]absolute and relative[/del] [del]terms[/del]; but I find that it is necessary to divide them into three classes. A term of the first class represents a thing simply as a something, as a horse, a tree, or a man. These are absolute terms. A term of the second class represents a thing as one of a pair, thus introducing the conception of [del]relation[/del] [ins]discrimination[/ins] into the logical form of the term. These are [ins]simple[/ins] relative terms such as "lover of -", "father of -", "similar to -", "identical with -". A term of the third class represents a thing as one of a triplet or larger collection, introducing the conception of bringing things into relation, such as "giver of - to -", "buyer of - [del]from[/del] for -", etc. These I [del]can[/del] [ins]call[/ins] conjugative terms.
