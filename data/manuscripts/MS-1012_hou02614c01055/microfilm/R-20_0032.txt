In order to ascertain accurately the inclinations during flight, and thus to be able to deduce the correct amount for a giving out of ballast or of gas, [del]it was planned[/del] [ins]the plan was[/ins] to carry the following instruments:

1) a tubular water level, showing inclinations from 0 to 2½° at every 10', the [del]10[/del] ten-minute divisions being at distances of [del]one[/del] [ins]successive[/ins] centimetres along the tube, made by Paul Bunge, Freiburg in Saxony.

2) a circular water level, of about 32cm diameter, giving inclinations up to ± 15°. The glass was cut by Dr. R. Steinheil of Munich. The single degrees are visibly distinct by divisions of half centimetres.

3) a pendulum attached to the forward gondola, hanging through the table-top which had a slit cut through it for the purpose. The pendulum has a graduated arc of ± 10°.
