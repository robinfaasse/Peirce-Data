Ithaca, N. Y., Feb. 2, 1897

Professor C. S Peirce,
84 Broad St., N. Y.

My dear Mr. Peirce :-

Yours of the 27th isreceived, referring to the matter of rock cleavage.

The matter is one that has very greatly interested me, and for many years, especially since I havebeen in this part of the country where there are so many beautiful and extraordinary illustrations of it, but I have no facts such as you need.

I do not believe that itis due to crushing; it seems more likely to be the result of slow, long continued flexture, occurring under heavy pressure, and with extremely uniformly distributed load, but I have seen nothing like it in any of my experiments.

I am very glad to hear from you and to know of your present location. I wish that we might see you here again.

Very sincerely yours,
R. H. Thurston
