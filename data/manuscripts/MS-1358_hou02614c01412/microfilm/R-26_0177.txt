<u>A somewhat new departure</u>
inasmuch as I will place the suspenders where they
[del]have been but[/del] [ins]really are, and[/ins] hang the weights from them.

Now I will investigate the geometrical condition when [del]one weight[/del] [ins]suspender[/ins]
[del]only is lifted to [unclear]y[/unclear][/del]
the suspenders are lifted generally. I wish thus to show
[del][unclear]y[/unclear] = [unclear]S[/unclear][/del]
that the inclinations of the suspenders are of no account.

$\eta_0 = y_0$
$x_s = \sqrt{1 - y_s^2}$ [fig]triangle[/fig]
[fig]triangle[/fig]

The parabola is $y = \frac{1}{4R} x^2$

[fig]triangle[/fig] That is $l$, length suspender
$l = \frac{1}{4R} x^2$

[fig]triangle[/fig] The length of section of cable
between $n^{\text{th}}$ and $(n+1)^{\text{th}}$ suspenders
$= \sqrt{(dx)^2 + (dy)^2} = \sqrt{S^2 + \frac{1}{16R^2} [(n+S)^2 - n^2]^2}$
$= \sqrt{S^2 + \frac{1}{16R^2} (2nS + S^2)^2}$

[fig]triangle[/fig]

Then the two equations to determine $\xi_{n+1}$ and $\eta_{n+1}$ are
$(\xi_{n+1} - \xi_n)^2 + (\eta_{n+1} - \eta_n)^2 = [del]\frac{S^2}{16R^2}[/del] (S^2 + \frac{4n^2 S^2 + 4n S^3 + S^4}{16R^2})$
$(\xi_n - x_n)^2 + (\eta_n - y_n)^2 = \frac{1}{16R^2} [del]x_n^4[/del] n^4$
