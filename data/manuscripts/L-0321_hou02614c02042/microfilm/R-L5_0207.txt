Before giving proofs of my four truths, I must tell
you that the branch of mathematics to which these truths
belong is unparalleled in mathematics for being the field
in which not ignorant pretenders but the very greatest
of mathematicians, such as Fermat, Leibniz, Euler, Le Gendre,
Gauss, Cayley, Sylvester, — perhaps ever the impeccable
Dirichlet have fallen into misstatements. Such faults in
mathematics can have no other origin than looseness of
definition: so, as definition is in my line, I will preface my
proofs of the four truths by some strict definitions of arithmeti-
cal terms in the senses in which I shall use them quite regard-
less of any care for propriety of language.
