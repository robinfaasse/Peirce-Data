§ 9 The substitution of conclusion for a premise is as we have seen the substitution of one term for another.

Now, it is evident that the only such substitution which necessarily yields a true conclusion from true premises is the substitution for a [ins]subject or[/ins] predicate of [del]another[/del] [ins]a term[/ins] which has as [del]subject or predicate[/del] no function or value beyond [del]that[/del] [ins]and[/ins] that of the term for which it is substituted.

We thus get such an argument as this -
S has no force as subject beyond M
P has no force as predicate beyond M
$\therefore$ S is P

Or in other language S is denoted by M
M connotes P
