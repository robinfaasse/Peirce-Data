We may conceive then that a general relative is a sum of
terms like $(A:B)$ with the condition
$$(x+,y)(z+,w) = xz +,xw +,yz +,yw.$$
This operation which is represented by writing one relative before ano-
ther may be termed the [del]simple[/del] [ins]relative[/ins] application of relatives. Cor-
responding to it precisely as addition is related to conjunction
is the operation of multiplication which is defined by the
equation
$$(x+y).(z+w) = x.z + x.w + y.z + y.w,$$
the two operations being identical for singulars. The difference
between the two operations may be illustrated by the two
formulae
$$\{(A:X) +, (A:Y)\} \{(X:B) +, (Y:B)\} = (A:B)$$
$$\{(A:X) + (A:Y)\} \{(X:B) + (Y:B)\} = (A:B) + (A:B)$$

<u>Note</u>. In the treatment of multiple algebra hitherto multiplication has
merely been defined by the associative and distributive laws. It then
becomes a matter of demonstration that every letter is a sum
of terms like $(A:B)$.

Relative terms are capable of formal classification. Singular
relatives are of only two [del]kinds[/del] [ins]forms[/ins] $(A:A)$ and $(A:B)$. General relatives
are of many forms. Corresponding to every absolute term as
$a = (A+A'+A'')$ is a relative $A:A + A':A' + A'':A''$. [del]The many[/del] [ins]If this be denoted[/ins]
by $(a_{s})$ then we have to note specially the relative $(\infty_{s})$. We note
also the relative not which contains every singular relative of
the form $(A:B)$ and none of the form $(A:A)$.
