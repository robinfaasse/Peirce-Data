The resemblance of $\times$ and $+$ (of wh I had not
thought before) is certainly striking, & affords a
reason for using $\times$ for the multiplication most
like addition. The commutative character of
internal multiplication is certainly a great
resemblance. Yet one might say that the
external product is like addition in that the
result is a vector like the things multiplied,
whereas in internal multiplication we pass from
vectors to a scalar wh is unlike anything in
addition.
