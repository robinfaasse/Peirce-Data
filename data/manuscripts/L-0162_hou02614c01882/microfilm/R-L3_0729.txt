Later in my lectures on mechanics at
Baltimore I used $\times$ and $\vert$ for external
& internal product, from considerations
founded [del]of[/del] [ins]on[/ins] Grassmann's names (if
I understand them.) The external product
wh depends on the component of $\beta$ outside of
$\alpha$ (i.e. perpendicular to $\alpha$) I indicated by the
sign representing perpendicularity, $\times$. The
internal product wh depends on the component
of $\beta$ wh lies in the direction of $\alpha$ (i.e parallel
to $\alpha$) I indicated by a sign representing
identity of direction. [u](1)[/u] I should have pre-
ferred $\parallel$, but that wd take longer to write
& might be mistaken for "is parallel to".
So I used $\vert$, wh may be regarded as two
superposed lines.
