[del]Tyndall's New Fragments contains fifteen[/del]
[del]papers. Of these seven are biographical[/del]
[del]& biocritical; three are personal adventure[/del]
[del]experiences in the alps.[/del] Four are [ins]scientific, all but one[/ins] [del]popu-[/del]
[del]larizations[/del] [ins]chiefly for children,[/ins] of the tritest truths of physics.
One is theological, being an attack on the
Scotch ideas about Sunday. The one really
interesting scientific paper concerns the
origin, propagation, and prevention of
phthisis. The use of spittoons containing water
[ins]and preferably with covers[/ins] is specially insisted on to prevent infection,
according to the views of Cornet.

Of the biographical pieces, which con-
stitute the chief interest of the book, one
relates to Tyndall himself; the others to
Goethe, Rumford, Pasteur, Young, and Carlyle.
These are all valuable studies.
