COLUMBIA UNIVERSITY
NEW YORK
FACULTY OF PHILOSOPHY

Dec. 22, 1897.

Professor C. S. Peirce,
108 West 89th St., New York.

Dear Mr. Peirce:

I have yours of the 21st. Before writing the article, I wish very much that you would take an opportunity to look through McLellan and Dewey's Psychology of Number. This has given rise to much of the recent discussion, and your article would be more valuable to teachers if you have this book in mind.

I shall also send you, [del]under separate cover,[/del] a paper on the other side of the question, by Dr. E. E. White of Ohio. Will you kindly keep in mind that the readers of the Educational Review are not professional philosophers or mathematicians, and that I am anxious to have your article enlighten them on the fundamental thoughts in mathematics.

I am sorry that the Educational Review cannot pay the price that you are in the habit of receiving, but I shall see to it that you are compensated at the best rate that we are able to pay to any contributor.

Yours very truly,
