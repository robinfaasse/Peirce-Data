[u]Notes on Benjamin's Rise of Electricity[/u].

p. 16 note. [u]ἤλέκτος[/u] is a doubly impossible word. Proofreader
Bigelow, though he didn't know Greek, would never have let
this go.

p. 17. The reference to Pliny is wrong. It is cap 2. But it would be
better to refer to the section. Pliny does not say [u]they[/u] do call
amber harpaga; but that they are said to do so.
It was the business of the historian to endeavor to find what
word is referred to. I can find none to answer the description
in the Syriac grammars & dictionaries. But the Arabians
borrowed a Persian word meaning "picking up straws" namely
[u]gah rubâ[/u] or [u]rubâ gah[/u]. It may be that this by its sound
reminded the Romans of [u]harpaga[/u], while the meaning more
than slightly approximated. The strength of this hypothesis lies in the
fact that there is no other. The genuine Syriac words refer
to the color, as the word electrum does.

p. 22. Rossignol's book is interesting; but what is the connection of it
with electricity? I don't believe the Dactyli were a "roving band
of miners."

p. 25. The idea that the notions of German miners about gnomes
were [del]derived[/del] derived from the Greek strikes me as to the last degree
improbable.

p 26. Mounts Pelion and Ossa (especially the latter) do not stand on a
"strip of land." True, there is a long marsh & marshy lakes, so
that the road from Larissa to Letochoro passes over a causeway.
But it has not in the least the effect of a strip of land.

p. 28. It is absolutely certain iron was known under the old empire. For
not only have several pieces been found, but one of these is of the
form of a figure on the wall of the same tomb which is said to be made of
[del]it[/del] [ins]biz or ba?[/ins]
