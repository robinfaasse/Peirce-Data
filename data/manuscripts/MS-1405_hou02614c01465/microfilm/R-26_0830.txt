Dr Benjamin endeavors to show that [del]Bacon treated Gilbert[/del]
[del]with special respect. It seems to us that the generally accepted view[/del]
[del]that[/del] Bacon placed his finger emphatically upon the weak [del]parts[/del]

In regard to Sir Kenelm Digby, Dr. Benjamin seems to us
to have fallen into some error. He seems to hold that in his
[u]Two Treatises[/u], relating to the Body and Soul, Digby plagiarized
from the [u]Principia[/u] of Descartes. Now that can hardly be.
For although it happens that Digby's book did not appear for
some months after the publication of the [u]Principia[/u], yet
it had been read, and the licence to print it issued, long before;
and in fact it must have been mostly printed before the
great work of Descartes appeared. It was, no doubt, the appearance
of that work which induced Digby to allow a certain time to
elapse before giving his own to the public. Moreover, there is
nothing of the character of an Eclectic doctrine about
Digby's. It is cruel to question his originality; for if he had not
originality, what merit had he? Digby is not a sagacious
thinker; but he is a singularly consecutive and resolute thinker.
It may be said that he plagiarized the conversation of Descartes.
But Descartes was then in Holland, while Digby was in Paris.
