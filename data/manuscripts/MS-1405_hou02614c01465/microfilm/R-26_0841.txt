p. 32 "Of science properly so called the Egyptian had none". This is rather
strong; but even if true, Buckle is no authority. For Buckle
wrote before either the mathematical or medical papyri had
been read. The statement about the astronomical observations
is exaggerated. Most of the Egyptian astronomy was borrowed
from the highly scientific Babylonians.

p. 32. It is true Thales seems to have had Phoenician ancestors on
his father's side. The statement on p. 33 is contradicted by
Aristotle, our best authority about Thales. Aristotle says Thales
was occupied with the question of what things were made of.

p. 34 The eclipse of Thales was certainly, not probably, 585 B.C.
Whether Thales predicted it or not is very doubtful.

p. 44. The university of Alexandria was founded by Ptolemy not Alexander.
Archimedes lived & worked in Syracuse. Hipparchus never ob-
served in Alexandria but at Rhodes and elsewhere. As for
Hero, he is several persons rolled into one.

p. 46. By Boesius, he means Boetius, or Boethius, a name some-
times in the middle ages written Boecius.
