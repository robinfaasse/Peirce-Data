[u]The Intellectual Rise in Electricity: a history[/u]. By [del]Park[/del] Benjamin. Appletons: 1895.

[pn]315[/pn]

It [del]is[/del] [ins]will be[/ins] gratifying to Americans to find that much the best history of early electricity has been written by an American. We should have been much better pleased if the author could have found room for a courteous reference to the graceful, useful, and beautifully got up translation of Gilbert on the Magnet by our countryman Mr. Mottelay. We have seen so many instances in which Americans have been willing to expose themselves to public scorn rather than to speak generously of what other Americans have done, that we begin to fear this contemptible behaviour may become an American trait. This will be prevented[ins], however,[/ins] if the public will always punish such meanness. Buy Mr. Mottelay's [del]book[/del] [ins]translation[/ins] of Gilbert, published by Wiley, first; afterwards, purchase Dr. Benjamin's, if you can afford both.

Dr. Park Benjamin is probably known to our readers as an eminent electrical and mechanical engineer and inventor. [del]He[/del] [ins]That he[/ins] comes of a literary family, we certainly need not say. He is known among the bibliophiles as a great collector of electrical literature. To all these eminent qualifications for his task, it was not to be expected that he should add that of thorough scholarship, — nor was it indispensable. In this respect, he ranks by the side of Mr. Mottelay.
