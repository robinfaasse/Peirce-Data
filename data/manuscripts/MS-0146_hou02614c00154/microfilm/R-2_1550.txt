On Space-Logic. By C. S. Peirce.
[[date]Written 1895 Nov. 13[/date]]

Chapter I. Unidimensional.

[u]Art. 1.[/u] Two points sever a self-returning line into two moieties.
Let $l_{ABCD}$ [del]mean[/del] [ins]signify the assertion[/ins] that on the line, $l$, C and D are in the same
one of the moieties made by A and B, and that B and C are
in the same one of the moieties made by A and D.
Thus, by definition,
$l_{ABCD} = l_{BCDA} = l_{CDAB} = l_{DABC} = l_{DCBA} = l_{CBAD} = l_{BADC} = l_{ADCB}$.
The definition of $l_{ABCD}$ is to be so understood that two
coincident points are to be reckoned as on both sides of one
another, not on neither side of one another. That is,
$\infty \prec l_{AABC}$. Hence,
$l_{ABCD}$ implies that neither A nor C coincides with either B or D.
[ins]It precisely signifies that B and D are on the same side of A and C, and removed from both.[/ins]
On the other hand,
$l_{ABAC}$ implies that either B or C coincides with A.
Immediate corollaries are that
$l_{ABAB}$ means that A and B are coincident,
$\overline{l}_{ABAB}$ means that A and B are not coincident.
There are three possible cases, viz:
$\Pi_X (l_{ABCX} + l_{ABXC} + l_{AXBC})$.
