[u]Art 2[/u]. By a topical singularity of a line, I mean a point on the line [del]at[/del] [ins]from[/ins] which the line extends a number of ways either less or more than two. Namely, a topical singularity is either an extremity, or point from which the line only extends one way, or is a [del]fork[/del] [ins]furcation[/ins], or point from which the line extends three or more ways. Every fork may be considered to result from the coalescence of bifurcations, or points from which the line extends just three ways. Thus, the points on a line [del]may be[/del] [ins]may[/ins] be considered to be either extremities, or ordinary points, or bifurcations.
