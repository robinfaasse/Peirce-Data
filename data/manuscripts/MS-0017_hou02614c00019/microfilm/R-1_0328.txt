[u]Section 1. Of the General Nature of Quantity.[/u]

[u]Art. 1.[/u] In order to comprehend the nature of quantity, it is neces-
sary to consider what mathematics is and what its method of
reasoning necessarily is. It will be found to relate to hy-
potheses and to reason about them by means of diagrams.
The nature of these hypotheses and diagrams being examined,
it will be found that a system of objects is required which
are all alike, although distinguishable from one another; and
between these there is a regular system of relations. This system
serves as a middle term of comparison to determine the
relations of order between other objects and constitutes a scale
of quantity. The present inquiry must begin by making these
points clear.

[u]Art. 2.[/u] Mathematics may be defined as the study of the sub-
stance of exact hypotheses. It comprehends
(A), the framing of such hypotheses, and
(B), the deduction of their consequences.
