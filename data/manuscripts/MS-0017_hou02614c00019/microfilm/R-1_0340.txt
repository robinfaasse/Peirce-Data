The deliberate laying open of the mind to a [del]possible[/del] [ins]an expected[/ins] sensation is observation. Of a mental creation there cannot properly be said to be any experience. For we can by an act of will annihilate that creation. Nevertheless, this distinction is only one of degree. We can influence the outward world too by availing ourselves of the proper means. We can shut our eyes to the facts of vision. Nor can we help our mental creations [del]taking a[/del] following a line of growth which we can neither foresee nor entirely govern; and this is shown by the fact that isolated mathematicians will be led to [del]assume[/del] [ins]adopt[/ins] nearly the same series of assumptions. Still, everybody knows there is a great gulf between outward facts and dreams in respect to their forcefulness upon a sane mind. Intuition is the feeling of a system of relations as relations. Most relations cannot be felt; they are only known mediately, by comparison and facts of the mind. But those philosophers (like Kant) are mistaken who assert that no relation can be immediately present as such. Such relations are particularly prominent in mental creations, and they exert a forcefulness similar both in degree and in quality to sensations. Such are the relations of time and space.
