Part 3. — Encyclopaedic

The first year, I would propose to print my masterly Syllabus of Science of which I here give a table of contents.

[u]Contents of Syllabus of Science[/u].

Dividing all science into

I. [u]Mathematics[/u], the study of ideal constructions.

II [u]Phenomenology[/u], which observes phenomena and seeks to identify their forms with those mathematics has studied.

III. [u]Pragmatics[/u], which studies how one ought to [del]behave[/del] [ins]act[/ins] in the light of experience,

I consider Mathematics

1. [u]Geometry[/u] and the mathematics of continua, giving only a few brief generalities [pn]§1[/pn]

2. [u]Arithmetic[/u], or the mathematics of discrete infinite collections. The special mode of reasoning explained [pn]§2[/pn]

3. [u]The theory of finite groups[/u]. The peculiarity of the reasoning explained [pn]§3[/pn]

II Phenomenology being divided into

1. [u]Philosophy[/u], or the universal characters of Phenomena

2. [u]Nomology[/u], or the discovery of the characters of classes of phenomena, and the accounting for them by the general principles of philosophy,

3. [u]Descriptive and Explanatory Science[/u], or the description of individual things, and explanation of their characters by the laws discovered by Nomology,
