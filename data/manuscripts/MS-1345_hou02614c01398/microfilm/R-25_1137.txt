[u]Step 2[/u]. [u]Philosophy[/u]. This is the Science of the most general, elementary, and obtrusive phenomena, and of the inferences to be drawn from them. It has two branches, namely,
A. [u]Logic[/u], or the Science of truth and its ascertainment, and
B. [u]Metaphysics[/u], or the Science of the universal characters of real things.
Of these two branches logic must precede: it is also the more mathematical.

[u]Step 3[/u]. [u]Nomology[/u]. The science of those laws which rest on special observations, not on the general data of philosophy. It has two branches, developed respectively out of social and feeding instincts, namely,
A. [u]Psychics[/u], the science of the laws of mind, and
B. [u]Physics[/u], the science of the laws of matter.
