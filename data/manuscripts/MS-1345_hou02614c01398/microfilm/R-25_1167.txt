Another reason, already pointed out by Auguste Comte, one of the first to employ this type of classification of science, further recommends it. It is, that the less general sciences have to employ the results of the more general, which because of their being less embarrassed by minutiae, are earlier developed.

[del]Art.[/del] [ins]Art.[/ins] 3. From this point of view, the great divisions of science are, as follows:

[u]First[/u], [u]Mathematics[/u], which does not inquire into facts, but only develops hypotheses;

[u]Second[/u], [u]Philosophy[/u], which makes no business of observation, but using merely the facts familiar to all of us;

[u]Third[/u], [u]Positive Science[/u], which makes researches to ascertain facts.

Philosophy, again, is divided into,

A. [u]Logic[/u], the Philosophy of Thought, which attends to the facts closest to us; and

B. [u]Metaphysics[/u], the Philosophy of Being, which attends to the facts of the whole universe.
