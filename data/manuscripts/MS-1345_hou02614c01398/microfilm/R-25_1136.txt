[u]Part 3 Encyclopaedia[/u].

Here we must adopt a classification of the sciences, not necessarily to inflict upon the user of the encyclopaedia, but to guide the compiler.

I divide all science into [u]three[/u] parts, the first much the smallest, the last much the largest. They are

[u]I. Mathematics[/u], the study of [u]ideal constructions[/u] independently of [del]the question of[/del] [ins]or Phenomenology,[/ins] their real existence.

[u]II. Empirics[/u], the study of [u]phenomena[/u] with the purpose of identifying their forms with those which mathematics has studied.

[u]III. Pragmatics[/u], the study of [u]how we ought to act[/u] in the light of the truths of [del]empirics[/del] [ins]experience[/ins].

I divide mathematics into 1 [u]Geometry[/u], 2 [u]Arithmetic[/u], and 3 the [u]Theory of Finite Groups[/u].

I divide Empirics into

[u]1. Philosophy[/u], or the study of the universal characters of phenomena.

[u]2. Nomology[/u], or the study of those characters of phenomena which, though not universal, belong to whole classes of phenomena, and the attempt to account for them by connecting them with the universal laws which philosophy discovers.

[u]3. Episcopy[/u], or the description of individual things, with a view to explaining them by the laws nomology makes out.
