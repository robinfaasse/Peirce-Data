[u]On the Classification of the Sciences.[/u]

[del]†[/del] Every systematic philosopher has to provide himself with a classification of the sciences. Comte first proposed to arrange them in a series of steps, each after the first based on the one preceding it. This plan will be adapted here, though carried out differently.

[u]Primary divisions[/u].

I. [u]Mathematics[/u], the study of ideal constructions without reference to their real existence.
II. [u]Empirics[/u], the study of phenomena, in order to identify their forms with forms mathematics has studied.
III. [u]Pragmatics[/u], the study of how to behave in the light of the truths of empirics.

Mathematics freely plays with the ideas it finds itself in possession of. Empirics considers what ideas experience forces upon us. Pragmatics studies the processes by which the outer world is to be brought into accordance with our wishes.
