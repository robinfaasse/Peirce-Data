[u]Rule of Notation 1[/u]. The four angles of a decussate cross
(especially if the two lines composing it are concave
upwards) placed between two expressions descriptive
of quantities (or ordered sets of quantities) to the right
and left of it, [ins]and called its members,[/ins] refer, the upper one to those values that
are common to the two members, [del]those to the two to[/del]
the left and right to those values which belong to
the [del]more[/del] nearer but not to the further members, and
the lower one to those values of the universe of values
which belong to neither member.

If any angle has a bar drawn across it, it shall
be thereby asserted that the values to which that angle
refers have [u]at most[/u] that multitude the sign of which
is within the bar.
