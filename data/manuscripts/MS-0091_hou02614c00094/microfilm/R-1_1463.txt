[u]Chapter I. The Symbols of the Calculus.[/u]

The letters [u]n[/u] and [u]m[/u] are usually employed as ordinal numerals; but for a set of three [u]i[/u], [u]j[/u], [u]k[/u], are convenient. The use of [u]x[/u] and [u]y[/u] for this purpose tends to confuse ideas.

To distinguish the different successions, [del]we may[/del] the employment of [u]u[/u], [u]v[/u], [u]w[/u] is usual and unobjectionable when they are unknown. When they are known, a, b, A, B, M, N, P, Q, R, are appropriate.

E, the "enlargement", signifies that the next higher value of the ordinal number is to replace that which is subscript to the successional [del]number[/del] quantity. Thus
