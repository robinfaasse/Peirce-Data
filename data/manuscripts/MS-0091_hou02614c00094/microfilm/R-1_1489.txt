From the associative principle it also follows that
(7) [u][del]$\phi^l\cdot\phi^m$[/del][/u] $\phi^l\cdot\phi^{m+n} = \phi^{l+m}\cdot\phi^n$

It further follows that (for positive integral values of $m$ and $n$)
(8) $\phi^{m+n} = \phi^{\underline{m+n}}$

For this is identically true when $m=1$; and if it be
true so long as [del]$m$ and $n$ are$[/del] [ins]$\underline{m}$ and $\underline{n}$ are[/ins] not larger respectively than
$M$ and $N$, then it follows that it is true when $\underline{m}$ is not
larger than $M$ and $\underline{n}$ is not larger than $1+N$. For
$\phi^M\cdot\phi^{1+N} = \phi^M\cdot(\phi\cdot\phi^N) = (\phi^M\cdot\phi)\cdot\phi^N = (\phi\cdot\phi^M)\cdot\phi^N$
$= \phi\cdot(\phi^M\cdot\phi^N) = (\phi\cdot\phi^M)\cdot\phi^N = \phi^{1+N}\cdot\phi^M$.

It further follows that (for positive integer indices of $m$ and $n$)
(9) $(\phi^{\underline{m}})^{\underline{n}} = \phi^{\underline{mn}}$

For when $n=1$, we have by (5)
$(\phi^{\underline{m}})^1 = \phi^{\underline{m}} = \phi^{\underline{m\cdot 1}}$

and if (9) holds when $\underline{n}$ is not greater than $N$ then it
follows [del]if[/del] [ins]it[/ins] holds when $\underline{n}$ is not greater than $1+N$. For
$(\phi^{\underline{m}})^{1+N} = ((\phi^{\underline{m}})^1)\cdot((\phi^{\underline{m}})^N) = \phi^{\underline{m}}\cdot\phi^{\underline{mN}} = \phi^{\underline{m+mN}} = \phi^{\underline{m(1+N)}}$
