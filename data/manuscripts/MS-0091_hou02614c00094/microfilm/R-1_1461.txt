[del]decussation[/del] asserts that the values so described,
instead of being the only ones, form the class of which
there are at most [u]m[/u]+[u]n[/u] that are not values of [u]w[/u].

It will be remarked that by the effect of the
last rule of notation [del]“[/del] [ins][fig]small circular diagram with letters and numbers[/fig][/ins] [del]”[/del] [del]not only[/del] [ins]makes[/ins]
an assertion, but is also a name for a certain
general description of quantity. Namely, it describes
a quantity as being either any one of certain un-
specified common values of [u]u[/u] and [u]v[/u] from [del]the[/del]
[ins]specified[/ins] (without saying how many such values there maybe in all)
in number, or one of from [u]f[/u] to [u]b[/u] values of [u]v[/u] that
are not values of [u]u[/u], or one of from [u]g[/u] to [u]c[/u] values
that belong neither to [u]u[/u] nor to [u]v[/u], or one of from
[u]h[/u] to [u]d[/u] values which belong to [u]u[/u] but not to [u]v[/u]. [del][fig]crossed out symbol[/fig][/del]
This is the meaning of the decussation when it is
a member of a decussation. But when it is
written alone, it is the assertion that that descrip-
tion comprises all the values of the value-universe.
