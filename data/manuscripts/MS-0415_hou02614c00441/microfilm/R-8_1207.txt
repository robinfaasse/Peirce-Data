The above neat definitions of affirmative and negative
propositions are suited to De Morgan's system alone, because it
alone is restricted to possible and contingent terms.

The affirmative propositions are at alternate
[del]edges[/del] [ins]vertices[/ins] of the cube; the negative propositions at the
other alternate vertices.

A proposition to verify which in detail would require
every possible state of things to be examined is called [u]universal[/u];
a proposition not requiring this is called [u]particular[/u]. All
the universal propositions are at the top face of the cube.
All the particular ones at the bottom.
