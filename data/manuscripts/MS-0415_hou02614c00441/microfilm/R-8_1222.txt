There is a class of propositions which may be called
[u]antispurious[/u]. Such is the proposition suggested by Aris-
totle as affording an argument against the quanti-
fication of the [del]premise[/del] predicate,
[u]Every S is every P.[/u]
This is not to be understood as asserting the exis-
tence of an S. But it asserts that the number of
S's and P's taken together [ins]does[/ins] [del]do[/del] not exceed 1.

Of course, a universal proposition is the assertion
that the number of objects of a certain description = 0.
