[u]§ 154. Spurious Propositions.[/u]

Dr. B. I. Gilman has made a very brief, but remark-
ably illuminative, investigation of these propositions, which
would, I am confident, richly repay further devel-
opment.[ins]*[/ins] He remarks that the spurious proposition,
[u]Some S is not some P[/u],
means that there is at least one S, at least one P, and
at least two things [del]al[/del] among the S's and P's taken
together. For instance suppose we have the two premises,
[del]Some M is P[/del]
[del]Some M is not S[/del]
Some M is P,
Some S is not M;
these might be written
[MP] [greater than or equal to] 1
[S[u]M[/u]] [greater than or equal to] 1
Adding and dropping the M
[S] + [P] [greater than or equal to] 2.
