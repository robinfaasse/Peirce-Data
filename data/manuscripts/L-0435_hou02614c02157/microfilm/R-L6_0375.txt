Stowe Vermont,
[date]September 3, 1931[/date].

To Mr. Paul Weiss,
Widener Library, Harvard University,
Cambridge Massachusetts.

My dear Mr. Weiss,

Your letter of [date]August 26th[/date] forwarded from Worcester, reached me a few days ago.

I am very pleased to know that a complete collected edition of Charles Peirce's works is to be issued. I hope nothing of his will be left out, which might easily happen, his writings being so scattered; and the omission of any of his earlier writings, certainly, would be most regrettable.

I had the privilege of studying two years at Johns Hopkins under Peirce, the last two years of his connection with the University. During that period Joseph Jastrow and I, were, I think, Peirce's only two graduate students, and I saw a great deal of Peirce during the second year, as I then contemplated making logic the major subject for my doctor's degree — which plan I abandoned, owing to Peirce's connection with the University then terminating, and his being succeeded by Professor [del]Em[/del] [ins]G[/ins]riffiot (I think this was his name), who was quite ignorant of formal logic except the very rudaments.
