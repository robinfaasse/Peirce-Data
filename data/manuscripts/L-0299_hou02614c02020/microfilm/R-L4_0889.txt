CHICAGO [date]August 1, 1902[/date]

My dear Dr. Peirce,

Your letter of July 27 is at hand.
I very much wish you would find the oppor-
tunity and inclination to write for the Transac-
tions the papers of which you speak
on the logic of Mathematics, in particular,
on the doctrine of Multitude and on Topical Geometry.
The suggestions that each paper be complete in
itself and that the series be not promised
are reasonable and desirable. I only
regret that the Transactions can give no
honorarium. — I note that you possess an analy-
sis of Cantor's statement that two collections are
always comparable as to Cardinal numbers (>, =, <).
— Let me express the hope that you will surely
get into shape for (printing) publishing your
work along these lines of mathematics and
logic. Why should not you personally
and our country have the recognition attach-
ing to such work? — As to the Carnegie
matter, in speaking of putting work into "defin-
itive shape" I was of course not meaning
