8. I cannot see why it should be deemed
logically indispensible in mathematics
to have express postulates supporting
possibilities. For in pure mathematics
the only kind of impossibility is logical
contradiction; and therefore whatever does
not contradict the initial hypotheses is
possible. However, I will grant that it
would be convenient to describe fully
every mode of possibility. In the doc-
trine of multitudes as it stands, however,
much that is presumably possible is neither
expressly included [del]for[/del] [ins]nor[/ins] expressly exclu-
ded from the range of possibility.

I think, for one thing, that whatever
collection be supposed, we should be allowed
to suppose a collection entirely outside
of it that is equal to it.

If that be the case, it should be stated
or else it should be recognized that any
thing is possible that the hypotheses do
not preclude.
