✓ V₂ Milford Pa [date]1902 March 20[/date]

My dear Sir:

I am very much obliged to you for your kind remittance of all these papers & as I am laid up today & unfit for work, my time is at my disposal to write something to you about the subject of one of them the value of which I fully appreciate although my own line of thought is quite different. I speak of your paper on The Axioms of Projective Geometry. I am not well enough & it would be too long to go into the real course of my thoughts. I can only sketch them. The subject is not a mathematical one; and although the aid of mathematics is necessary to setting it right & cannot be dispensed with, it is not very deep mathematics as mathematics. It requires aid from Logic of a much more difficult kind. It taxes the power of the logician to the very utmost; so that it is more a subject for a logician than for a mathematician.

Allow me to remonstrate on your [del]call[/del] calling these hypotheses Axioms. According to my notions of terminology when a term has been in use as a term of science since ancient times, it is proper to use it in the ancient sense, illuminated if necessary by modern understanding of the matter.
