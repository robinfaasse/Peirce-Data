[u]Examples and Exceptions[/u].

[u]F[/u] is regularly doubled in [u]distaff[/u], [u]tariff[/u], [u]scoff[/u], [u]stuff[/u]; and there are no exceptions. [u]Coif[/u], as written, is a French word, though Anglicized in speech. [u]If[/u] and [u]of[/u] are Graelistic, hereof, whereof are compounds.

[u]S[/u] is regularly doubled in [u]compass[/u], [u]guess[/u] (where the [u]e[/u] is a single vowel, the [u]gu[/u] a digraph), [u]kiss[/u], [u]loss[/u], [u]puss[/u]. It is an inflectional termination in [u]ashes[/u], [u]George's[/u] [u]oyes![/u], [u]reaches[/u]. [u]Riches[/u] is so spelled because it appears to be a plural, though it is merely the French [u]richesse[/u] Anglicized. There are a few exceptions. Two are names of substances, a class of words unapt to take a double final consonant, [u]copperas[/u] and [u]molasses[/u]. The others are [u]arras[/u], [u]bias[/u], [ins]caddis,[/ins] [u]grampus[/u], [ins]has,[/ins] [u]mopus[/u], [u]orris[/u], [u]tennis[/u], [ins]walrus[/ins].
