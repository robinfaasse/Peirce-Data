The following terminations may be applied either to a
verb or to a noun:

[u]-ous[/u], which applied to a verb signifies given to performing
the action of the verb, as rebellious, libellous, [del]but[/del] [ins]cavillous, [del]but[/del][/ins] applied to
a noun [del]signifies[/del] [ins]means[/ins] full of that which the noun signifies, as
perilous, [del]hazardous;[/del] but marvellous is spelled with two l's, probably
as derived from [ins]French[/ins] merveilleux; it were preferably marvelous.

[u]-ary[/u], but the significations when affixed to a verb and
to a noun are hardly distinguishable, as codicillary from
a verb not used, and pupillary from the noun;

[u]-ate[/u], which from the verb makes an adjective nearly
equivalent to a past participle, as [del]distillate[/del] [ins]cancellate, (the verb is cancel)[/ins], but from
a noun [del]either[/del] makes a verb as postulate, fossilate, or a noun
signifying state or office, as [del]marquisate;[/del] [ins]postulate[/ins]

The only words in -ion formed in the way in question
seem to be rascallion, medallion, rebellion, [ins]pommclion[/ins] the first two
[del]-age, from verb distillage, from noun pupilage, chaplaincy, vicarage[/del]
