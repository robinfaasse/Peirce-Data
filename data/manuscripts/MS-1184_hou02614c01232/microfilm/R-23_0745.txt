6. When a word ending in a final consonant immediately following a single vowel receives a termination or suffix beginning with a vowel, the consonant is doubled

i) if the vowel before it is accented after the addition of the termination or suffix, but not otherwise unless the final consonant in question is [u]s[/u], [u]c[/u], or [u]l[/u];

ii) if that consonant is [u]s[/u], irrespective of the accent, and if the consonant is [u]c[/u] it is virtually doubled by taking [u]k[/u] after it;

iii) if that consonant is [u]l[/u] and the word without the termination is a verb, even if it be not in use without the termination; but if the word is a noun or adjective the [u]l[/u] is not doubled. The effect of this rule is that the [u]l[/u] is doubled before the terminations [u]-est[/u] (2[ins]nd[/ins] person sing), [u]-eth[/u], [u]-ing[/u], [u]-ed[/u], [u]-er[/u], [u]-ee[/u], [u]-able[/u], [u]-ard[/u], while it remains single before [u]-ish[/u], [u]-ize[/u], [u]-ist[/u], [u]-ism[/u], [u]-ity[/u], [u]-ic[/u], [u]-ian[/u], [u]-iferous[/u].
