[u]K[/u] is used as a substitute for [u]c[/u] final and for the latter [del]plement[/del] of a double [u]c[/u], under the following limitations:

Final [u]c[/u] is used in polysyllables ending in unaccented [u]-ac[/u] and [u]-ic[/u] and in [del]some of[/del] the less English names of solid substances; as [u]almanac[/u], [u]bivouac[/u], [u]traffic[/u], [u]music[/u], [u]talc[/u], [u]zinc[/u], [u]ploc[/u], [ins]lac,[/ins] [u]marc[/u], [u]manioc[/u], etc.

But [u]chalk[/u], [u]cork[/u], [u]musk[/u], [u]monk[/u], [u]muck[/u], [u]slack[/u], etc are more English. But compound words follow the spelling of their components.

A [u]k[/u] is appended to [u]c[/u] [ins]in other cases[/ins] where the latter letter immediately follows a single vowel in a final syllable without a silent [u]e[/u], as in [u]attack[/u], [u]rebeck[/u], [u]kick[/u], [u]bailiwick[/u], [u]bullock[/u], [u]tussock[/u].

In [del]all[/del] other cases [u]k[/u] alone is used at the end of a word; as in [u]hook[/u], [u]speak[/u], [u]break[/u], [u]oak[/u], [u]bark[/u], [u]week[/u]
