No brachistic word contains a doubled consonant.

On the other hand, every word not brachistic must
have three letters at least. Consequently, if it would other-
wise be spelled with a single vowel followed by a single con-
sonant, the latter is doubled, if it is a consonant that
can be doubled, and otherwise a mute [u]e[/u] is added: as
[u]abb[/u], [u]ebb[/u], [u]add[/u], [u]odd[/u], [del]egg[/del] [ins]inn, ann,[/ins] [u]err[/u], [u]axe[/u], [u]aye[/u] (ever), [u]eye[/u], [u]awe[/u], [del]owe[/del] [ins]owe[/ins]. When [u]ay[/u] means an affirmative vote, it ceases to be
brachistic, and must be spelled [u]aye[/u]. [u]Ox[/u] and the names of letters
of the alphabet are exceptions.
