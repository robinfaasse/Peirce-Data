-able. This is usually an English termination freely used. It might be Latin, as -ible is, but I do not know that, as a matter of fact, it ever is so. It is found in [u]controllable[/u], [u]annullable[/u], [u]compellable[/u], [u]expellable[/u], [u]counsellable[/u] (better than counselable), etc.

-ate. As the termination of a participial adjective or substantive, -ate can only be affixed to a verb; and most verbs in -ate are derived from participial adjectives. Examples are [u]distillate[/u] (noun), [u]cancellate[/u] (adjective and verb), [u]postillate[/u] (verb). In recent scientific dialect a few verbs, as [u]fossilate[/u], are formed directly from nouns. The termination is also used to signify a state or office, and as such is affixed to a noun
