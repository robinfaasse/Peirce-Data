In the following words the doubled final consonant is
probably attributable to the same principle, though they cannot
be reduced to any precise rule:

[u]bigg[/u], a sort of barley, distinguished from [u]big[/u], the adjective.

[u]bitt[/u], a [del]piece[/del] [ins]part[/ins] distinguished from [u]bit[/u], a thing that bites, or goes between the
jaws, a piece, a bite.

[u]burr[/u], roughness left by a graver, rough rock, rough sound,
rough silk, padding to make a [del]rich[/del] [ins]rich-head[/ins], are more usually spelled with
two [u]r[/u]'s, distinguished from [u]bur[/u], the flower of a dock, a misty disk round
a light, a washer. Meaning sweetbread, probably [u]bur[/u] is preferred.

[u]butt[/u], a fish, a cask, the big end, a target, a ridge, a bearing and [del]a[/del] [ins]a remaining[/ins]
part of a hide, distinguished from [u]but[/u] the conjunction and preposition.

[u]jagg[/u], a pedlar's load, distinguished from [u]jag[/u], a projection, tooth.

[u]mandrill[/u], a monkey, distinguished from [u]mandril[/u], a spindle or other tool.

[u]mumm[/u], to mask, distinguished from [u]mum[/u], silent, beer.

[u]purr[/u], like a cat, distinguished from [u]pur[/u], the old word meaning
completely.

Working in opposition to this principle, is another, that
words not easily distinguished are spelled alike.
