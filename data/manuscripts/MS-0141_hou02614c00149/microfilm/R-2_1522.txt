serves its own determinations of motion, in spite of being swerved
from its path by other things. That the very existence of a thing
consists in its reactions with neighboring things is a conception
at once primitive and scientific. A thing is nearly what the
peripatetics called a [u]first substance[/u]. Disregarding distinctions
made for theological purposes, their definition comes to this: a
substance is anything having a real being of the nature of a
subject and existing [u]per se[/u], independently of anything else.
To say that it is of the nature of a subject is to define it
in terms of grammar, which introduces a foreign conception.
Leibnitz changed the definition in a way which those of his
followers who did not accept his monadology further changed
so as to make it that which is modifiable but not itself a
result of modification and consequently is perdurable.
The inadequacy of this [del]conception[/del] [ins]definition[/ins] is shown by Tait's using
it to show that energy is a thing. It would in fact make any
term of a general dynamical equation denote a thing, pro-
vided it does not involve time. The centre of gravity of the
universe, its velocity, its rotation-areas, etc would be things.
Tait's idea, at once, suggests to us that the distinct identity
of parts is an essential character of a thing.
