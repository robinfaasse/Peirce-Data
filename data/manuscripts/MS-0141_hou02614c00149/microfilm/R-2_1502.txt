We can imagine an animal to have no feeling but sight,
and to be blind to every color except a certain yellow, and
to have no experience of any variation or limitation of
this yellow, and without any consciousness of the flow of time.
I suppose such an animal would promptly go to sleep, but there
is no logical necessity for that. We can imagine its whole life to
consist in that yellow feeling; and yet that quality would
[del]retain[/del] be just what it is, in itself, for us. The quality of yellow
has no parts or elements. True, it has a certain luminosity,
a certain chroma, and a certain hue, but those words
express the results of comparing it with other colors.
Now in the yellow quality itself there is no comparison with
others, and therefore in itself it has no hue, no luminosity,
and no chroma. It is itself and nothing more. It thus [del]agrees[/del] [ins]satisfies[/ins]
[del]with[/del] the definition of a monadic experience. The same is
true of every smell, of every emotion, of every tone of mood,
of every flavor, if I may use the expression, of an idea, in
short of every quality of feeling, or of immediate consciousness
apart from all oppositions and connections. Quality of feeling,
then, is the first Kainopythagorean category.
