The word [u]fact[/u] could never have been extended from meaning
an act (as in such phrases as "he was caught in the very [u]fact[/u]"
and "accessory after the fact") [ins]to mean an event or element of state of things,[/ins] unless men had discerned,
subconsciously perhaps, a reactive [del]elemen[/del] constituent in the
latter idea. But the word is loosely applied by those who say that
it is a fact that roses are red. Properly, this is not a fact but a
generalization from a multitude of facts. As I propose to use
the term, a fact is the attachment of a quality to [ins]an[/ins] [del]a[/del] thing or to a
number of things. That one rose is red is a fact, [del]or[/del] [ins]and[/ins] that two roses
grow on one stem is a fact; but only on the condition that these
are not abstract statements but the entire quality in the one
case of the rose, in the other of the two roses and the stem. Of course,
in reality, they are not facts, but elements of the facts. In the real
universe, the entire facts are unattainable. But in a hypothetical
universe of the mathematician they are fully stated when the hypo-
thesis is fully stated.
