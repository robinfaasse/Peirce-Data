An occurrence from the nature of things can happen but once.
For if it is repeated, that makes another occurrence. Hence,
an occurrence, or reaction, is anti-general; that is to say, it can-
not be generalized without losing its essential character as an
occurrence. A generalized occurrence is a law. Now a law is
nothing but an impotent formula unless on every occasion
of its application there is a force then and there exerted, for
that occasion only, to put it into practice. It is like a judge
without a sheriff. Conversely, wherever there is individuality
there [del]the[/del] dyadic reaction will be found to lurk. Existing
things are individual, because the existence of a thing
consists in its actually reacting against other things. The
idea of a universe in which but one atom should exist is
absurd, since unless it acts, there is no meaning in saying
it exists. No matter how regular and reasonable an act may
be, that is, no matter if it takes place when a general rule
calls for it, yet in itself, being individual and anti-general,
there is no reasonableness [del]in it[/del] nor regularity in it. [del]This[/del]
Accordingly, a quality of brute arbitrariness attaches to
the second category. This quality is not the occurrence itself:
it is a quality of feeling attached to the idea of an occurrence
in general. Even the idea of an occurrence in general is,
because of its generality, quite contrary to the nature of an
occurrence in itself. It may seem strange that the dyadic cate-
gory should be characterized by individuality, which is a mode of
unity.
