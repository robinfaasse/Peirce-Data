It is not possible to find an instance of two objects occurring in
[del]our[/del] [ins]one[/ins] direct consciousness without any mediatizing third (such
as the idea of a pair) but standing in their naked opposition, ex-
cept it be the experience of a reaction. For there is no way in which
two ideas can be so brought into a dual consciousness except by
passing from one to the other, and that transition is the experience
of a reaction. Examine, for example, all those pairs that are
forever emphasized in consciousness, such as right and wrong,
truth and falsehood, dreaming and doing, life and death, success
and failure, and it will be found that the idea of effort is prominent
in each. The experience of Reaction, then, is the Second Kain-
pythagorean category.

A reaction is something that happens only once. It is essen-
tially singular; and cannot be generalized without losing its
character of action. A generalized reaction is a law. Now a
law, without [del]an[/del] [ins]a living[/ins] act to carry it out on each occasion is an
idle formula, as impotent as a judge without a sheriff. To
express this, we may say that a reaction is anti-general. It
may be ever so reasonable or conformed to law. That is to say,
it may occur under circumstances in which reason or law
would call for it; but in itself, as a living act, it is simply
blind, brute force, arbitrarily exerted.
