[pn]76[/pn] [u]Convention No. 3[/u]. A single dot, not too minute, or single congeries of contiguous large dots, whether in [del]the[/del] form of a line, spot, or other figure, when placed [del]in either of the three places[/del] on Metal, Colour, or Fur, on the sheet, will denote a single member of the Universe to which the tincture refers, but will not thereby be made to refer to any one [ins]member[/ins] determinately. Separate dots or aggregates of dots will not necessarily [del]refer to[/del] [ins]denote[/ins] different objects [ins]of which no analysis is intended to be represented[/ins]. [ins]An graph-instance of this sort is termed a spot.[/ins]

Ordinary predicates will generally be written in abbreviated form. Every predicate, however scribed, must have a definite point on its periphery appropriated to each of its Subjects, — that is to the things denoted by its subject, nominative, direct object, indirect object, and each of the other correlates [del]which[/del] [ins]with[/ins] it may connote* [del][Footnote * That is, to which it may denotatively refer.][/del] [ins]* To be resumed[/ins] And [del]each[/del] [ins]each[/ins] of these points is termed a [u]peg[/u] of the Graph.
