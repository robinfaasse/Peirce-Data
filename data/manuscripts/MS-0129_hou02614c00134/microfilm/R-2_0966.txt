[u]Definition [del]35[/del] [ins]35[/ins][/u]. A [u]spherical triangle[/u] is one of four regions into which any three planes called the [u]produced bases[/u] divide the [del]space[/del] [ins]region[/ins] round their common point, called the [u]centre[/u]. The rays of intersection of the bases are called the [u]vertices[/u] or [u]vertical rays[/u].

[del]36[/del] The [u]enclosure[/u] of a [del]plane[/del] [ins]spherical[/ins] triangle is the multitude of rays through the centre in the triangle, bounded by stretches of the bases each limited by two vertical rays. The [u]exclosure[/u] of the spherical triangle is the multitude of planes [del]none of which[/del] [ins]through the[/ins] centre none of which penetrates the enclosure.

[del]37[/del] The [u]bounding sides[/u] of a spherical triangle are those [ins](spherical stretches[/ins] sweeps of the bases from vertex to vertex which do not cut any plane of the exclosure.

[del]38[/del] The [u]periphery[/u] of the triangle is the aggregate of bounding sides.

[del]39[/del] The [u]cutting sides[/u] of a spherical triangle are those spherical stretches of the bases from vertex to vertex each of which cuts every plane of the exclosure.

[del]40[/del] The [u]external angles[/u] of a spherical triangle are those [del]angles[/del] [ins]sweeps[/ins] round the vertical rays as axes of rotation from base to base which do not pass [del]over[/del] [ins]through[/ins] any ray of the enclosure. The peritrophy is the aggregate of internal angles.

[del]41[/del] The [u]internal angles[/u] of a spherical triangle are [del]those[/del] [ins]the[/ins] spherical stretches [del]round the bases from vertex to vertex each of which passes through every[/del] [ins]vertical rays as axes through the enclosure from vertex to vertex.[/ins]
