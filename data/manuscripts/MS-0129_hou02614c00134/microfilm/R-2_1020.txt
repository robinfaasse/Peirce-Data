This is the principal and leading method. There are others, [del]other ways[/del] which some modern mathematicians regard as being, in theory, more important. But the most essential thing is that, however we proceed, we have to assume that general experience assures us of some fact, such as the unchangeableness of rigid bodies, which forms the basis of the principle of comparison. For the sake of simplicity, we shall assume that the moving about of rigid bodies is the only fundamental method of comparison, and that [del]every[/del] [ins]any[/ins] other method has to be justified by being shown to give the same results as that would do.

In reality, there are, of course, no absolutely rigid bodies, since [u]pressure[/u] and [u]heat[/u] change the sizes and shapes of all bodies. But minute corrections overcome that difficulty.
