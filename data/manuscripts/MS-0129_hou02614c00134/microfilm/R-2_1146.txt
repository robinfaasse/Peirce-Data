[u]Definition[/u]. Two points are said to be [u]strictly[/u] equidistant from the same point of the firmament when they are on one sphere centred at that point.

[u]Scholium[/u]. The reasoning of Theorem , that if two sides of a triangle are quadrants the angles opposite are right angles, proceeds upon the assumption that when the triangle is turned [ins]round[/ins] over there will be but one point upon [del]which[/del] [ins]each side[/ins] which is just a quadrant from the vertex. This is true, if we speak of [del]quadrantal equidistance[/del] [ins]strict equality to a quadrant[/ins], but not otherwise.

It is to be remarked that there is no particular plane which more than another is at the distance of a quadrant from the firmament. All are sensibly at that distance; but all are not strictly equidistant from a given point of the firmament.

Since reasoning upon such premises is difficult, it will in this book be limited to proving the famous "fifth postulate" of Euclid.
