[u]Corollary 16[/u]. If all the planes through each of two intersecting rays are perpendicular to one plane, then the latter is the metrical conjugate of the point of intersection of those rays.

Art. 14. [u]Theorem 14[/u]. [u]In every rotation, flat films in planes perpendicular to the axis of rotation remain in those planes[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let [u]{r}[/u] be an axis of rotation and [u](\pi)[/u] be a plane such that every plane through [u]{r}[/u] is perpendicular to [u](\pi)[/u], and let [u][A][/u] be any point in [u](\pi)[/u], and let [u][B][/u] be the point to which it is carried by any rotatory displacement round [u]{r}[/u]. Then, I say that [u][B][/u] lies in the plane [u](\pi)[/u].

[fig]Diagram showing axis r, plane pi, and points A, B, P[/fig]

For let [u][P][/u] be the point [del]in which[/del] [ins][u][\pi r][/u].[/ins] Let the flat film in [u](Ar)[/u] be carried to [u](Br)[/u]. [del]Call[/del] [ins]Denote[/ins] [u]{AP}[/u] by [u]{a}[/u]. Then, by Theorem , [u]\angle ar = \frac{1}{2} D[/u]. Hence, when the flat film from [u](Ar)[/u] arrives in [u](Br)[/u], so the the particle from [u][A][/u] reaches [u][B][/u], since the particle at [u][P][/u] is, by Definition , fixed at its point, the straight filament from [u]{a}[/u] is brought to [u]{PB}[/u], which we may call [u]{b}[/u]. And [u]\angle br = \frac{1}{2} D[/u]. Then the [u]\wedge rab[/u] is biquadrantal, in [u])ra([/u] and [u])rb([/u], and so, by Theorem , is birectangular at [u]{a}[/u] and [u]{b}[/u]. That is, [u](APB)[/u] is perpendicular to [u](rb)[/u] and to [u](ra)[/u]. But there is but one plane through [u]{a}[/u] perpendicular to [u](ra)[/u]; and that is [u](\pi)[/u]. Hence [u](APB) = (\pi)[/u]. Q.E.D.
