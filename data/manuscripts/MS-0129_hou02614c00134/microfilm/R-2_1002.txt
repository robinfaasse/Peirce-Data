[u]Section 2[/u]. Of the Three Types of Measurement.

[u]Art. . Division[/u] . When [ins]the part of the motion used[/ins] is laid down time after time in a row, one or other of three things may happen, viz.:

A. After a while the [del]parts[/del] [ins]part[/ins] of the motion used may come round again to where it was at first;

B. The row, though continued endlessly both ways, may always be limited to a portion of the extension which graphical geometry supposes;

C. As the limiting case between A and B, the limit of the [del]extent[/del] measurement may just close the ring, so that all of the [del]rational[/del] [ins]real[/ins] numbers, including [unclear]$\infty$[/unclear], may represent on the limits of spaces supposed in graphical geometry.

[u]Definitions[/u] . [u]Elliptical measurement[/u] is measurement round in a [del]way[/del] ring, so that the whole circuit is represented by a finite measure. This is case A, above.

. [u]Hyperbolic measurement[/u] is measurement such that the whole extension from the $-\infty$ to the $+\infty$ of measure fails to complete the whole cycle of extension supposed [del]by[/del] in graphical geometry. This is case B, above.

. [u]Parabolic measurement[/u] is measurement in which the $-\infty$ and the $+\infty$ fall at the same position, and close the cycle of extension of graphical geometry.
