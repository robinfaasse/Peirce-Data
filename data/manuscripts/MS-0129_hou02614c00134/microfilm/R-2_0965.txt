[u]Definition 27[/u]. A [u]plane triangle[/u] is one of four regions in which any [u]three points[/u], called the [u]vertices[/u] (singular [u]vertex[/u]) of the triangle, together with the three rays through pairs of them, called its [u]produced bases[/u], divide the plane of these vertices.

[del]28[/del] The [u]enclosure[/u] of the plane triangle is the multitude of points in [del]one of these regions[/del] [ins]the triangle[/ins], bounded by [del]three[/del] [ins]three[/ins] stretches of the [ins]each[/ins] bases [del]limited by two of the vertices[/del]. The [u]exclosure[/u] of the plane triangle is the multitude of rays in the plane none of which penetrates the enclosure.

29 The [u]bounding sides[/u] of a plane triangle are those stretches of the bases from vertex to vertex which do not cut any ray of the exclosure.

[del]28[/del] [ins]30[/ins] The [u]periphery[/u] of the triangle is the selfreturning broken [ins]actual[/ins] line formed by the three bounding sides.

[del]29[/del] [ins]31[/ins] The [u]cutting sides[/u] of a plane triangle are those stretches of the bases from vertex to vertex each of which cuts all the rays of the exclosure.

32 The [u]external angles[/u] of a plane triangle are those [ins]plane[/ins] sweeps round the vertices from base to base which do not pass any point of the enclosure.

[del]33[/del] [ins]33[/ins] The [u]peritrophy[/u] of the triangle is the [del]sum[/del] [ins]aggregate[/ins] of the external angles.

[del]32[/del] [ins]34[/ins] The [u]internal angles[/u] of a plane triangle are [del]three[/del] [ins]the[/ins] plane sweeps round the vertices [del]from base to base[/del] [ins]over the enclosure[/ins] [del]which pass over all the enclosure[/del] [ins]each of[/ins] [del]which[/del] [ins]which[/ins] pass [del]over[/del] [ins]es[/ins] [del]all the enclosure[/del].

[u]Notation 6[/u]. A plane triangle is denoted by $\Delta$ followed by the [del]letters for the vertices[/del]. Usually the smallest of the four $\Delta$s is intended.
