[del]Fourth[/del] [u]property of the Metron[/u]. All displacements of the metron
are optical, that is, every straight filament remains straight.

[u]Third property of the Metron[/u]. The fixation of a particle of
the metron at a point, or of a flat film to a plane, reduces
by one the freedom of the metron.

[u]Scholium 1[/u]. This gives the metron the requisite freedom
for purposes of measurement.

[u]Fourth property of the metron[/u]. If a particle of the metron can
move from one point to another, then the particles in the
two points can change places, while any one flat film containing
both particles is brought back to its plane. If a flat film of the metron
can move from one plane to another, then the flat films in the two
planes can change places, while any one [del]point[/del] [ins]particle[/ins] in both films
is brought back to its point.

[u]Fifth property of the metron[/u]. If a part of the metron can
be moved from one place to another, then any part of the metron
that may be in the first place can be moved to the second place.
[ins]Corollary 1. Hence, measures can continue endlessly in either direction.*[/ins]

[u]Sixth property of the metron[/u]. The metron fills all space.

[u]Scholium 2[/u]. These are not the only [del]proper[/del] [ins]independent[/ins] properties
of the metron; but it will be convenient to trace out the
consequences of these, [del]first[/del] before considering any other.

* N.B. The paradox of Achilles and the Tortoise shows that the space over which
the [del]endless[/del] measuring-steps are taken may, nevertheless, be limited.
