We write $\angle \alpha\beta = \angle \beta\alpha = \partial$. A half-circuit of inclination is called a [u]right-angle[/u]. A half-circuit of distance, which in ordinary measurement carries a point to an infinite distance, may be called a [u]quadrant[/u]. Two circuits bring everything round into its original place, not only the [del]planes[/del] [ins]flat films[/ins] through the fixed filament but all others. Two circuits are said to make a [u]revolution[/u].

Round any ray, we can measure off any inclination in either of the two ways of going round it. [del]So that[/del] [ins]Thus,[/ins] it is requisite [ins]in order[/ins] that the motion may serve as comparator, that there should be round each ray just two planes into which a given flat film of the motion may be brought, when another flat film is brought into a given plane through that ray.

With reference to any ray, there are two kinds of simple metrical motion. [del]Either[/del] [ins]Firstly[/ins], all particles on the ray may move along [del]it[/del] [ins]that ray[/ins], while all [del]planes[/del] [ins]flat films[/ins] containing that ray remain in their planes, [del]when[/del] Such a motion (all straight filaments remaining straight) is called a [u]translation[/u] along that ray [ins]as axis of translation[/ins]. Secondly, all flat films containing the ray may move round that ray, while all particles in that ray remain at their points. Such a motion (all straight filaments remaining straight) is called a [u]rotation[/u] [del]of[/del] around that ray, as [u]axis[/u] of rotation.

Every rotation is a translation, and vice versa, the axes of translation and rotation being every point of one distant a quadrant from every point of the other and every plane through one inclined by a right angle to every plane [del]of[/del] [ins]through[/ins] the other.
