[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Theorem[/u] . [u]With a rigid motion a half circuit of[/u]
[u]length along any axis of translation is the distance from[/u]
[u]any point to the intersection of that ray with the firmament[/u]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let {$AB$} be any ray cutting the firmament
at [$B$]. Then I say that |$AB$| is a half
circuit.

[fig]Line segment with points A and B[/fig]

For |$AB$| is infinite and if we measure
an infinite distance the other way from [$A$], we come to [$B$],
since there is, by [del]pro[/del] Corollary , but one point on {$AB$} at an
infinite distance from [$A$]. Hence [$A$] and [$B$] cut the whole
circuit into two equal segments, and |$AB$| is half the circuit. Q.E.D.

N.B. With hyperbolic measurement, the distance of any point from the firmament is only a quarter of a circuit.

[u]Theorem[/u] [u]A translation along any axis cutting the firma-[/u]
[u]ment[/u] [ins]with a rigid motion[/ins] [u]is a rotation round an axis contained in the firmament,[/u]
[u]and a rotation about any axis cutting the firmament is a trans-[/u]
[u]lation along an axis in the firmament.[/u]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. By Theorem , a translation along any
axis is a rotation about an axis which cuts [ins]each[/ins] [del]any[/del] plane through
the axis of translation at a distance of half a circuit along any
perpendicular to the axis of translation from the foot of the
perpendicular. But by the last theorem such a point is on the
firmament. Hence the whole axis of rotation lies in the firma-
ment. Hence a rotation about any axis cutting the firmament is
a translation [del]about[/del] [ins]along[/ins] none other than an axis in the firmament.
Since, then, it is a translation [del]about[/del] [ins]along[/ins] some axis, it must be along such an axis.
