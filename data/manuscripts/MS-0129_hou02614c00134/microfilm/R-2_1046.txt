[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Scholium[/u] . Let flat films turning about different axes from plane to plane be cut by one plane. Then, in this plane section we see straight filaments turning about points from ray to ray; and this gives the conception of plane angles.

Let [del]point[/del] particles moving along different axes from point to point be projected from one point. Then, in the projection we see straight filaments passing along planes from ray to ray; and this gives the conception of spherical lengths.

[u]Definitions[/u] . [u]Plane angles[/u] are measures of the intervals from ray to ray in one plane, to which a flat film of the motion is restricted in such measures.
[ins]A plane angle may be a right angle, or half circuit. Rays cutting one another at right angles are said to be perpendicular to each other. The point of intersection is called the foot of the perpendiculars.[/ins]

[u]Spherical lengths[/u] are measures from ray to ray through one point, to which a particle of the motion is restricted in such measures.
