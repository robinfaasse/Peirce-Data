[u]Art. 13. Definition 50.[/u] Two things are said to be
[u]conjugate[/u] when each stands to the other in the same
relation, and that a relation in which it stands to
nothing else.

[u]Definitions 51.[/u] Two rays are [u]metrically conjugate[/u], for
a given metric, when every point on either is distant a
quadrant from every point on the other, and every plane
through either is perpendicular to every plane through the
other.

A point is [u]metrically conjugate[/u] to a plane when
every ray through it is metrically conjugate to every
ray of the plane.

[u]Notation.[/u] We shall denote a metrical conjugate by a "[ins]u[/ins]" over the sign of its
conjugate. Thus [del](A)[/del] [ins]([u]A[/u])[/ins] will be the plane metrically conjugate to [A].
