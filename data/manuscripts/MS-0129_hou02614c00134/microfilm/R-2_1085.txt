[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Theorem[/u] . [u]In every rotation of the metron, there is, through every point, a flat film which never leaves its plane[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let [A] be any point of the axis, and ($\alpha$) any plane through that point. Let a small rotation take place. Then, the [del]plane[/del] flat film which was in the plane ($\alpha$) comes into a plane ($\beta$). The two planes have the ray $\{\alpha\beta\}$ in common, which contains [A]. Considering any plane through the axis, but not through [del]the[/del] $\{\alpha\beta\}$, it is cut by ($\alpha$) and by ($\beta$) in two different rays. We may call the axis, $\{x\}$ and those two rays $\{a\}$ and $\{b\}$.

Hence, at the limit there must be a [del]plane[/del] [ins]flat[/ins] film through $\{\alpha\beta\}$ which does not leave its plane, in the rotation considered. Then it will not leave its plane in [del]the[/del] [ins]any repe-[/ins] tition of that rotation; and since the [ins]displacement of[/ins] rotation may be taken as small as we please, there must be a flat film through $\{\alpha\beta\}$ which never leaves its plane in any rotation about the same axis.
