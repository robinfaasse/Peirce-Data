[u]Art. 17[/u]. [u]Theorem 20[/u]. [u]Every translation of the metron along[/u] [u]any ray, as axis of translation, is a rotation around the metri-[/u] [ins]cally[/ins] [u]conjugate ray, as axis of rotation[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let the metron receive a motion of translation along $\{AB\}$ as an axis, so that a particle is carried from $[A]$ to $[B]$; and let $\{RS\}$ be [ins]the[/ins] metrical conjugate of $\{AB\}$. Then, I say that the metron is [u]ipso facto[/u] rotated round $\{RS\}$ as axis, so that the flat film in $(RSA)$ is carried to $(RSB)$.

[fig]Geometric diagram with points A, B, R, S and intersecting lines[/fig] For since $(ABR)$ contains the axis of translation $\{AB\}$, the flat film in this plane remains in the plane. By Definition, $(ARS)$ is perpendicular to every plane through $\{AB\}$. Hence, by Theorem 1, $\{AR\}$ is perpendicular to $\{AB\}$. And in like manner $\{BR\}$ is perpendicular to $\{AB\}$. Hence, when the particle at $[A]$ is carried to $[B]$, the filament in $\{AB\}$ remaining in that ray, and the film in $(ABR)$ remaining in that plane, the filament initially in $\{AR\}$ is carried to $\{BR\}$. By Definition, $|AR| = |BR| = \frac{1}{2} D$. Hence, the particle at $[R]$ remains at that point. In like manner, the particle at $[S]$ remains at that point. Hence, since the motion is by Definition, optical, it follows that the straight fibre in $\{RS\}$ remains in that ray. Hence, by Definition, the motion is a rotation round $\{RS\}$ as axis of rotation. Q.E.D.

[u]Corollary 22[/u]. Every rotation of the metron round any ray, as axis of rotation, is a translation along the conjugate ray, as axis of translation.
