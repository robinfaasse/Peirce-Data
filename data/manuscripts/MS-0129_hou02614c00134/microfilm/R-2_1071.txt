<u>Theorem</u>. [del]In[/del] [ins]The[/ins] translation [del]with[/del] [ins]of[/ins] a rigid motion about an axis cutting the firmament, [del]every particle describes[/del] [ins]the[/ins] rays.

<u>Demonstration</u>. In the first place, no particle in the firmament is moved at all. For nothing can move out of the firmament; and a rotation about a ray in a flat film does not move the parts of that film relatively to one another. That is, in a rotation there is no motion within a plane through the axis, the whole motion consisting in carrying flat films from one such plane to another. Hence since every [del]flat[/del] straight filament remains straight, every straight filament is moved about its point of intersection with the [del]axis[/del] firmament.

It follows easily, by the method of geometrical nets, that all particles move along the rays between them and the intersection of the axis with the firmament. Thus, let A and B be two points on the axis of translation and C any third point. Let the particle which at one instant is at A and at [del]another at[/del] B, be at an intermediate instant at $\frac{\text{A}+\text{B}}{2}$. The filament through A and C at the first instant cuts the firmament in A-C. The filament that at the intermediate instant passes through $\frac{\text{A}+\text{B}}{2}$ and C cuts the firmament at $\text{C}-\frac{\text{A}+\text{B}}{2}$. At the second instant the particle [del]at C in the first instant[/del] is coradial with C-A and $\frac{\text{A}+\text{B}}{2}$. It is also coradial with B and $\text{C}-\frac{\text{A}+\text{B}}{2}$. Hence it is $\text{C}+\frac{\text{B}-\text{A}}{2}$.
