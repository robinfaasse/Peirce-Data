[u]Division[/u] . A derived inclination is either,

A. The inclination of a plane to a ray, that is, to the nearest plane containing that ray;

B. The inclination of a plane to a point, that is, to the nearest plane through that point. [In the ordinary mode of measurement, this is zero, [del]and[/del] unless the point is in the Firmament.]

C. The inclination between two rays [del]not[/del] having no common point, that is, through the furthest plane [del]of either[/del] [ins]through each[/ins] ray and the [del]nearest through[/del] [ins]most inclined to[/ins] the other ray.

D. There are also, in parabolical measurement, inclinations between points, etc. of the firmament.

[u]Definitions[/u] . A [u]length[/u] is the measure of the displacement of a particle along a limited line, [ins]one[/ins] from point to another.

An [u]angle[/u] is the measure of the displacement of a [ins]flat film[/ins] [del]plane[/del] round a ray from one plane to another, or of the displacement of a [del]ray[/del] [ins]straight filament[/ins] round a point in a plane from one ray to another. The latter is called a plane angle.
