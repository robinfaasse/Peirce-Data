Section I. Of the Nature of Spatial Measurement.

[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Scholium[/u] . We now come to the measurement
of spatial extensions. It will greatly aid us in acquiring a
real mastery of the elements of this subject to stop first
and analyze the facts, so as to discover just what the
measurement of spaces is.

A measure is, in the first place, a [del]number[/del] [ins]member[/ins] attached to
a part of space. The importance of it consists in the fact
that it enables us to apply arithmetic and algebra to
geometry, and to calculate exact results, even when the
[del]figures do[/del] configurations do not, in themselves, present
exact intersections.

All sorts of spatial extension being continuous, the kind
of numerical system with which we have to deal is the
system of real numbers. We reach the said numbers, not by
direct measurement, by as limits of series of unsatisfactory
measures.
