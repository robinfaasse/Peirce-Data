[u]Divisions[/u] . Spatial magnitudes are either,
A. unidimensional, as lengths, and ordinary angles; or
B. multidimensional, as areas, volumes, and conical angles.

. Unidimensional spatial magnitudes are either,
A. [del]Lengths[/del] Distances, as between two points; or
B. Inclinations, as between two planes.

. Distances and Inclinations are either,
A. Primitive, or
B. Derived.

[u]Definitions[/u] . A primitive distance is the measure of
[ins]the smallest displacement requisite to carry[/ins] [del]the remoteness of two points.[/del] a particle from one point to another.

[u]Division[/u] . A derived distance is either,
A. The distance of a point from a [del]line[/del] [ins]ray[/ins], that is, from the nearest point of the ray;
B. The distance of a point from a plane, that is, from the nearest point of the [del]plane[/del] [ins]ray[/ins];
C. The distance [del]of one[/del] [ins]between[/ins] two rays [ins]not in one plane[/ins], that is, between the nearest points of the rays;
D. [del]The distance[/del] There are also, in parabolical measurement, some other kinds of distance, requiring special examination.
