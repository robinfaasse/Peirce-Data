[u]Art.[/u] · Definitions · Ordinary points are points measurably distant [del]from us and[/del] from one another, and from one point called [u]our point[/u].

· Ordinary planes are planes measurably inclined to [del]none which are are to[/del] [ins]one[/ins] another, and to one plane through our point, called [u]our plane[/u].

[u]Theorem[/u] · A point infinitely distant from one ordinary point is in finitely distant from every other, unless every plane through the first and second points is infinitely inclined to every plane through the first and third.

Demonstration. Let [A] be an ordinary point; and let [B] be a point infinitely distant from it.
