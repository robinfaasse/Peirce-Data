[u]METRICAL GEOMETRY.[/u]

Chapter . Of the Principles of Spatial Measurement.
Section 1. Of the [del]Nature[/del] [ins]Nature[/ins] of Space Measurement.

[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Scholium[/u] . We now come to the consideration of measures of space-extensions; and it is proper that we should first reflect a little upon what that measurement is.

It is desirable that neither too great, nor too little, importance should be attached to such preliminary reflections, Mathematical students are protected from the former error, by their own mode of reasoning, which requires definite images to be brought before the mind; and they readily see how little generalities, not accompanied by such images, will serves the purposes of mathematical thought. But they are particularly liable to the latter error, and all the more because they may have high mathematical power without much of the power of making those analyses of facts which ought properly to go before the application of mathematics.
