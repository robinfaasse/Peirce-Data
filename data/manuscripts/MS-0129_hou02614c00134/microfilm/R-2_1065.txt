[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Definition[/u] . The firmament [ins]of a given metron[/ins] is a surface whose points are at the limit of measurable distance by that metron.

[u]Corollary[/u] . The film of the metron which is in the firmament can never move away from the firmament.

[u]Corollary[/u] . No particle of the metron can move to or from the firmament.

[u]Corollary[/u] . No flat film can ever move into or away from contact with the firmament.

[u]Scholium[/u] . It does not necessarily follow from anything which we have assumed above, that there is any firmament. We can even more easily [del]imagine[/del] [ins]conceive of[/ins] metrons with no firmament. But the fact that our measures are infinite does not prove that the measurable part of space is unlimited, any more than the fact that Achilles has to pass over an infinite series of spaces proves that [del]he will never[/del] [ins]in passing over them his[/ins] all time will be exhausted.

The firmament, if there is one, is a sphere. It may be an ordinary sphere, so that there is another world beyond it of which we can have no physical cognizance, or it may be that it is [del]everywhere distant[/del] a sphere of a quadrant radius, in which case it is a plane, and does not separate space into two regions.
