[fig]Isosceles triangle ABC[/fig] [u]Demonstration[/u]. Let [A], [B], [C] be three points such that |AB| = |AC|. Then, I say, that $\angle$ABC = $\angle$ACB.

Let the motion be rotated about {AB} as an axis through one circuit. Let it then be rotated round an axis through [A] so that the [del]point[/del] particles originally at [A], [B], [C] remain in their plane, and so far as to bring the straight filament originally in {AB} into {AC}, and the shortest length from [A] to [B] into the direction of the shortest length from [A] to [C]. Then, because the particle at [A] [del]always[/del] [ins]has[/ins] remained at that point, and |AB| = |AC|, the particle originally at [B] will now fall into [C]. And the straight filament originally in [del]S[/del] {AC} but then carried over to the other side of {AB} will [ins]now[/ins] fall into {AB}, and the particle originally at [C] will now fall upon [B]. And since two points of the straight filament originally in {BC} are now in {BC}, the whole filament will lie in that way. Consequently, the part of the motion originally [del]subten[/del] [ins]filling and[/ins] measuring the angle ACB now fills and measures the angle ABC, and $\angle$ACB = $\angle$ABC. Q.E.D.
