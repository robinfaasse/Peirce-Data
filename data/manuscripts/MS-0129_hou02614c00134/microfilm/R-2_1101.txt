[u]Art. . Definitions[/u] . A quadrilateral is a figure composed of four rays. Its sides are the portions of each [del]ray[/del] [ins]ray[/ins] cut off by two others which are assumed as adjacent to that ray. Its diagonals are rays which concur with two pairs of [del]of[/del] adjacent sides.

A parallelogram is a [del]q[/del] [del]pb[/del] quadrilateral [del]whose opp[/del] [ins]of which each pair of[/ins] non-adjacent (called opposite) sides are parallel.

[fig]Parallelogram ABCD with diagonal BD[/fig] [u]Theorem[/u] . [u]The opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal[/u] .

Demonstration. Let ABCD be a parallelogram, {AB} being parallel to {CD} and {BC} to {DA}. Then I say |AB| = |DC| and |BC| = |AD|.

For draw the diagonal {BD}. Then, in the two triangles ABD and CDB, <ABD = <CDB, because two parallel rays are equally inclined on the same side to a ray that cuts them both. And, for the same reason, <ADB = <CBD. Thence, the side BD being common to the two triangles, this side and its two adjacent angles in one are equal to the corresponding parts of the other. Hence the other sides are equal. That is |AB| = |DC| and |BC| = |DA|. Q.E.D.
