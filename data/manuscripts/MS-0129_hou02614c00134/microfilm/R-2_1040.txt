[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Definition[/u] . Places are said to be [u]constructively equal[/u] when the part of the metron which just fills the one would necessarily, no matter what the special characters of the motion of the particular metron employed may be, just fill the other, if the metron could be so moved.

[u]N.B.[/u] . Places constructively equal cannot be unequal by measure. One may, however, be infinite while the other is finite. That is, the former may be divisible into an infinite multitude of equal parts [ins]of[/ins] sensible magnitude, and the latter not. But in that case no part of the metron occupying [del]any[/del] part of the one can be transported to the other.

[u]Definition[/u] . Places are said to be [u]equivalent[/u] when it can be shown that the measure of the one is either equal to or the negative of the other.
