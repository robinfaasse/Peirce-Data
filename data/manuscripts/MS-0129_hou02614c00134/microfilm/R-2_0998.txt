METRIGAL GEOMETRY

Chapter [del]1[/del] [ins]I[/ins]. Of the Fundamentals of Space Measurement.
Section 1. Of the Nature of Spatial Measurement
Art. . [u]Definition[/u]. A metron is an imaginary body
filling all space and capable of displacements of the follow-
ing description:
1[sup]st[/sup], Every straight filament of the metron remains always
straight
2[sup]d[/sup], Its motion is always continuous;
3[sup]d[/sup], [del]Any[/del] [ins]Every[/ins] straight filament [del]can be brought into[/del] [ins]is capable of being brought to lie in [u]onto[/u][/ins]
any ray and that in each of two opposite ways; but this is not
to be understood as implying that there is nothing to prevent the
filament from being moved up to the ray;
4[sup]th[/sup], any particle can be moved along any ray through it,
and is capable of occupying any point of the ray, while all
the flat films through that ray remain in their planes; such
motion being called a [u]translation[/u] of the metron, that ray being
the [u]axis of translation[/u];
5[sup]th[/sup], any flat film can be moved so as to continue to contain
one ray in it, and is capable of occupying any plane through the
ray, while all the particles in the ray remain at their points;
such motion being called a [u]rotation[/u] [del]of the metron, that ray being[/del]
the [u]axis of rotation[/u];
