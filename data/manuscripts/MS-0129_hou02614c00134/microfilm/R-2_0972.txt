[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let $(ABC)$, $(ACD)$, $(ABD)$ be three planes all perpendicular to the plane $(BCD)$, and [ins]all containing the point $[A]$.[/ins]

[fig]Tetrahedron with vertices A, B, C, D and lines extending to E, F[/fig] Let $(AEF)$ be any other plane through $[A]$. Then I say that $(AEF)$ is perpendicular to $(BCD)$.

For let [del]$[EF]$ and[/del] $\{EF\}$ be the intersection of $(AEF)$ and $(BCD)$; and let $[E]$ be any point of $\{EF\}$.

Then, since the planes $(ABC)$ and $(ABD)$ intersecting in $\{AB\}$ are perpendicular to $(BCD)$, so likewise, by Theorem , is $(ABE)$ through $\{AB\}$ and $[E]$. And for a similar reason $(ADE)$ through $\{AD\}$ and $[E]$ is perpendicular to $(BCD)$. But the planes $(ABE)$ and $(ADE)$ intersect in $\{AE\}$. Hence, by the same theorem, $(AEF)$ which passes through $\{AE\}$, is perpendicular to $(BCD)$. Q.E.D.
