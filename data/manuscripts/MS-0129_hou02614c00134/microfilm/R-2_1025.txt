[u]Art. Definitions[/u]. An [u]axal motion[/u] is a motion relative to an axis.

. A [u]simple axal motion[/u] is either a translation [ins]along[/ins] or a rotation round the axis (in contradistinction to a screw motion.)

. An [u]element[/u] of an axis is a point in an axis of translation or a plane containing an axis of rotation.

A [u]circuit[/u] is the quantity of a simple axal motion which carries parts of the motion through all elements of the axis and leaves them at their initial positions.

N.B. It is not stated that particles not in the axis or flat films cutting the axis are restored to their positions. Thus, if a flat film be turned over, it comes back to the same plane through the axis; and this makes a circuit.

[u]Definition[/u]. A [u]revolution[/u] is a motion which leaves every filament in its initial position

[u]Notation[/u]. A circuit is denoted by $\pi$ (read "pi".) But Benjamin Peirce and his scholars denote a circuit by the sign $\mathcal{O}$, which is read "circ" or "circuit".
