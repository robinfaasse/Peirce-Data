[u]Theorem[/u]. [u]The sum of the internal angles of a plane triangle is one circuit.[/u]

[u]Demonstration[/u]. In any triangle, $\Delta ABC$, I say that taking the internal angles

[fig]Triangle ABC with line P parallel to BC through A[/fig] $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = \partial$.

For let $\{BC\}$ cut the firmament in $[P]$, and draw $\{AP\}$. Then, by the last theorem, $\angle B = \angle BAP$ taken externally to $\angle A$, and $\angle C = \angle CAP$ taken externally to $A$. But $\angle BAP + \angle BAC + \angle CAP = \partial$, since they have common limits and do not overlap, and the straight fibre sweeping through them successively is brought back to its initial ray $\{AP\}$. Hence also $\angle B + \angle A + \angle C = \partial$. Q.E.D.
