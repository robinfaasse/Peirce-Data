[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Division[/u] . The following additional kinds of distance and inclination have to be investigated

1. If two rays have a third ray [del]make[/del] at a plane angle and spherical distance of half a circuit from both, then the distance along this ray of the points of its intersection with the two rays and the inclination round this ray of the planes through it and the two rays, are [del]to be[/del] called the distance and inclination of the two rays.

2. If two planes, $(\alpha)$ and $(\beta)$, are cut by a third plane, $(\gamma)$, so that $\angle \alpha\gamma = \angle \beta\gamma = \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}$, then the distance[del]s[/del] of the two points [del]one[/del] a quadrant from $[\alpha\beta\gamma]$ along [del]$\{\alpha\gamma\}$[/del] $\{\alpha\gamma\}$ and $\{\beta\gamma\}$ [ins]is[/ins] [del]are[/del] [del]called[/del] called the [u]distance of the two planes[/u], $(\alpha)$ and $(\beta)$.

3. If two points, $[A]$ and $[B]$, have a third point, $[C]$, such that $|AC| = |BC| = \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}$, then the inclination of the two planes at right angles to $(ABC)$ and passing through $\{AC\}$ and $\{BC\}$ respectively is called the [u]inclination of the two points $[A]$ and $[B]$[/u].
