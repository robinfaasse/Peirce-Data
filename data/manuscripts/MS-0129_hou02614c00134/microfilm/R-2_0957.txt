[u]Art. 2[/u]. [u]Definition 3[/u]. Two places are said to be [u]optically[/u] [u]equal[/u] if the two first properties of the metron would [del]permit[/del] [ins]allow[/ins] the same part of the metron to occupy, at different times, those two places, but would not allow either to occupy a part only of the other.

[u]Definition 4[/u]. Two places are said to be [u]equal by superposition[/u] if the same part of the metron can, at different times, [del]occupy[/del] just fill those two places.

[u]Definition 5[/u]. Two places are said to be [u]equal by perversion[/u], if sufficient parts of them are equal to determine all the rest to be equal, [del]although[/del] [ins]by superposition, were it not that[/ins] owing to [del]one being[/del] [ins]having right and left transposed across a certain plane, [del]the[/del] [ins]no part of the[/ins] metron can be moved from one to the other.[/ins]

[u]Definition 6[/u]. Two places are said to be [u]equal by parts[/u] if they are composed of corresponding [del]parts[/del] integrant parts equal [del]of[/del] each to each.

[u]Definition 7[/u]. Two places are said to be [u]equal by further precision[/u] if owing to their measures by the metron being infinite a further condition has to be imposed to determine equality.

[u]Definition 8[/u]. Two places are said to be [u]modularly equal[/u], if having the same dimensionality they would necessarily be equal if the metron could be so moved as to allow of their being compared.

[u]Corollary 2[/u]. If two places are equal by superposition the metron can be [ins]moved from every part of the one to a corresponding part of the other.[/ins]

[u]Corollary 3[/u]. The halves of equal places are equal.
