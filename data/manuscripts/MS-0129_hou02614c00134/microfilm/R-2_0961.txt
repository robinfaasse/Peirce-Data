[u]Art. 7[/u]. — [u]Theorem 2[/u]. [u]All circuits of the same kind are optically[/u] [u]equal[/u].

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let {$a$} and {$b$} be two rays. Then, I say, that the whole [del]length of[/del] [ins]stretch through[/ins] {$a$} optically equals that [ins]through[/ins] of {$b$} and the whole sweep round {$a$} optically equals that round {$b$}.

Let [$C$] be any point and ($\gamma$) any plane containing it. Let [$D$] be any point and ($\delta$) any plane containing it. Then, I say, that the whole plane sweep round [$C$] in ($\gamma$) equals the whole plane sweep round [$D$] in ($\delta$), and that the whole spherical stretch through ($\gamma$) about [$C$] equals the whole spherical stretch through ($\delta$) round [$D$].

For let the straight filament through {$a$} be carried by an optical displacement into {$b$}, or a part of {$b$}, or into {$b$} and something else. The last supposition is impossible, since a straight filament by the Second Property of the Metron always remains straight. The second supposition is impossible since a part of {$b$} would have limits and [ins]this[/ins] would imply that the metron had been broken, contrary to the First Property of the Metron. Hence, the whole filament which initially occupied [ins]part of the metron occupying initially[/ins] just fills {$b$} after the displacement. Hence |$a$| = |$b$|. The [del]whole[/del] sweep round {$a$} must after the displacement occupy the whole sweep round {$b$} by the First Property of the Metron. Hence $\angle a = \angle b$.

In like manner, if the [ins]particle[/ins] [del]part[/del] of the metron in [$C$] is carried to [$D$] and the flat film in ($\gamma$) is carried to ($\delta$), the [del]one[/del] [ins]latter film[/ins] [del]occupies the former[/del] [ins]be precisely occupied by the part of the metron that initially occupied the former[/ins]. Hence, the two plane sweeps, or what is [del]the[/del] same thing, the two spherical stretches are equal. Q.E.D.
