former remark of mine which seems to
me worth communicating. Namely on
p. 23, § 28, you say the Greeks sometimes
failed to make use of the distinction between
the present & aorist subjunctive, and you
give three examples to illustrate this. But
my note is to the effect that all three
obviously conform to your rule. The first
of the three is from what Cyrus said to Cyaxares,
when the latter went into hysterics about the
former having so large a guard about him, in the
5th Book of the Cyropædia "ἵνα γάρ σε ἐγὼ φαίνω
κακόν ποιήσας" (Aorist by your rule, for it would
be an event, and so regarded)... "ἢν μήποτε μηδὲν
φαίνωμαι κακὸν πεποιηκώς" (Right again by your
rule since this would not be a single event but a
whole course of conduct.) So there is no exception
there. The second example is this from Demosthenes:
Εἰ μὲν γὰρ προσδέξαιτο Φίλιππος συμμάχους...
εἰ δὲ μὴ προσδέξαιτο, κ.τ.λ. I have not a Demost-
henes to gather from the context in what sense of
προσδέχομαι this, and I don't remember the
circumstances; but in any case, I take it that
to receive or accept the allies would be an event,
while not to do so would be a whole line of conduct. If
so, it follows your rule, just as the other example does.
