9. The chorisis of a point-figure must be a positive whole number; but it is subject to no general equational condition whatever.

10. Point-figures may be divided into the [u]artiad[/u], whose chorisis is even, and the [u]perissid[/u], whose chorisis is odd.

[u]Chapter II. Of Line-Figures.[/u]

11. A [u]line[/u] is a place every part of which can be occupied by a particle in some instant of a lapse of time.

12. A [u]filament[/u] is an object which at each instant occupies a line.

13. A [u]line-figure[/u] is a place in which a particle can move continuously but in which no filament can move utterly away from its line.

14. A line[ins]-figure[/ins] can have [del]topically[/del] singular points. A topically singular point of a line[ins]-figure[/ins] having singularity -1 may be called an [u]end[/u]. A point of a line-figure of singularity -2 may be called a topical [u]acenode[/u], A point of a line-figure of positive singularity may be called a topical [u]node[/u].
