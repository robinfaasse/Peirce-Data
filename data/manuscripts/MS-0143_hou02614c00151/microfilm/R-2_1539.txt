[u]Chapter I. Of Point-Figures.[/u]

1. Topic is the theory of continuous connections.
2. An instant is an indivisible time.
3. A point is an indivisible place.
4. A particle is an object which, at each instant, occupies a point.
5. A point-figure is a place in which no particle can move continuously.
6. A portion of anything is a part of it of like dimensionality with the whole.
7. A topically singular place, A, of another place, B, is a place contained in B from which an object filling it can, [del]by a suitable kind of deformation[/del] [ins]while remaining in B,[/ins] [del]move itself away (i.e. so as to leave every portion of it) in a different number of completely different paths (none of them having any part in common with any other) from the number of ways in which a body could by similar deformation leave an ordinary place of B of like dimensionality with A. By a "suitable" kind of deformation, I mean such a mode of deformation is to make the number of ways finite.[/del] [ins]quit [ins]to leave a place is instantaneously to leave every portion of the place vacated[/ins] [del]quitting[/del] leave[/ins] [ins]generate a portion of B different in number from the portions so generable from ordinary places in B having the dimensionality of A.[/ins]

The singularity of a singular place is the excess of [ins]the[/ins] number of [del]the above ways[/del] [ins]portions[/ins] over those for ordinary places. [ins]But since the singularity of a place may be different in different parts, Maximum Singularity.[/ins]
The total singularity of a place is the sum of the singularities of all its singular places.
The only singularity that a point can have is non-existence, if that can be considered as a singularity.
