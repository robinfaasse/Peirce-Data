My dear Sir:

Yours with enclosure, returned herewith, reaches me today. As to the first paragraph of page 2 of this MS., I think you cannot properly say that everything can be defined in terms of [del]&[/del] alone. Nor do I think it can properly be said that negation can be defined in terms of aggregation & composition alone. For example, I usually define the denial of [del]x[/del] [ins]x[/ins] by saying it amounts [del]to saying[/del] [ins]to saying[/ins] that from x anything you please necessarily follows (that is, is true.) But however advantageous that is from a formal point of view, I don't think it really defines denial in terms of consequence. How could that be? Still less do I see how you could define denial or consequence in terms of two [u]affirmative[/u] & [u]commutative[/u] operations. That seems manifestly impossible.

(And here let me say in passing that my [u]\(\Sigma_{i}\)[/u] and [u]\(\Pi_{i}\)[/u] are only [ins]analogous to signs of[/ins] [del]aggregation[/del] [ins]aggregation[/ins] & [del]composition[/del] [ins]composition[/ins]. For strictly to aggregate or compound [u]whatever there is[/u] is quite a different thing from aggregating & compounding [del]a[/del] [u]determinate objects[/u] just as a consequence with [u]whatever there is[/u] as consequent is only [u]analogous[/u] to and is not in strictness a consequence.)
