My dear Mr. Huntington:

Here is the "tedious" proof of the distributive principle to which I referred Am: J. Math. III 33.

By (3) $a \prec c$
" " $b \prec c$

[1] $\therefore$ by (2) $a \cdot c \prec b \cdot c \prec c$

By (3) $a \prec a$ and by (4) $a \prec a+b$ $\therefore$ $a \cdot c \prec a+b$
" " $b \prec b$ and " " $b \prec a+b$ $\therefore$ $b \cdot c \prec a+b$

[2] $\therefore$ by (2) $a \cdot c + b \cdot c \prec a+b$

from [1] and [2] by (2) $a \cdot c + b \cdot c \prec (a+b) \cdot c$

Now if [del]$(a+b) \cdot c \prec a+b \cdot c$[/del] [ins]$(a+b) \cdot c \prec a \cdot c + b \cdot c$[/ins] (That is if $a+b \cdot c \bar{\prec} a \cdot c + b \cdot c$)

there must be something, $m$, such that
$m \prec (a+b) \cdot c$
while $m \bar{\prec} a \cdot c + b \cdot c$

[3] from [3] by (3) $m \prec a+b$
[4] " " " $m \prec c$
[5] from [4] by p 28 (19) $a+b \cdot c \prec \bar{m}$
[6] whence by p 33 (3) $a \prec \bar{m}$
and by p 28 (11) $m \prec \bar{a}$
