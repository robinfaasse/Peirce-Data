According to my view of the algebra of logic all expressions
which can be joined by the signs of operation or the copula
[u]signify something when written alone.[/u] It is only the "umbral," as
Sylvester calls them, that cannot be written alone with a meaning.
And further, every such complete expression has one or other of
two values, your Z and I, according as it is false or true. In
my opinion, nobody who thoroughly understands the subject will take
any other view. But be this as it may, this view lends a special
mathematical significance to the algebra, and should therefore
be preferred in a pure mathematical development. Certainly, it
should not be excluded. The different kinds of mathematics, as
arithmetic of integers, the calculus, etc are distinguished by their
different systems of values and especially by the different mul-
titudes of these systems. Here we have the lowest possible multitude.
