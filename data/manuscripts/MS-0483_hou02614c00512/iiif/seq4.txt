Existential Graphs 4
Basic Conventions of the System of Existential Graphs.
1. Each unanalyzed verb shall be signified by an ideograph*,
a suitable point of which shall denote each blank, or wholly inde-
terminate subject, of the verb. †
2. A blank space, as an ideograph, shall be a verb void of meaning.
3. An ideograph having a line drawn across it shall have
its signification reversed.
4. A point or black dot shall denote an individual object
actually existing in the universe of discourse.
5. The writing down of a proposition without quotation mark
or disavowal shall be construed as asserting it, anything vague
in [del]the[/del] statement being [del]better[/del] understood [del]and[/del] [ins]or[/ins] designated so as to
be favorable to the [del]assertion[/del] veraciousness of the proposition.
6. A lightly drawn endless line encircling a proposition
or verb shall be a quotation mark; and whatever is vague in
the encircled proposition shall be understood [del]and[/del] [ins]in the parts of the graph[/ins] [del]designated[/del]
(after the similar determination of everything vague [ins]outside the[/ins]
quotation mark has been declared) in such a way as to be favora-
ble to the veracity of its author. And [del]if[/del] [ins]than undesignated[/ins] the author of a quoted pro-
position [del]is not given[/del] shall be [ins]understood to be the[/ins] advocatus
diaboli, or hypothetically indefinitely clever opponent of the assertion of
the graph.
* A direct ideograph is one directly suggestive of the idea; an indirect one
is an ideograph suggestive of the word that expresses that idea. Such, for
example, is an initial letter.
