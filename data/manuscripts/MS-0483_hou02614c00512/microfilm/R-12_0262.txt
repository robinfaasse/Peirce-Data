[u]Basic Conventions of the System of Existential Graphs[/u].

1. Each unanalyzed verb is to be represented by an ideograph,
which may either be direct (i.e. a picture or other directly suggestive
image) or indirect (i.e. directly suggestive of the word expressing
the idea, as an initial letter); and to each blank of the verb
is to be appropriated a suitably suggestive side of the ideograph.
In particular, if a circle [ins](or other oval)[/ins] [del]about[/del] [ins]be made a part of[/ins] the ideograph [del]be made a part of[/del]
it, and the circumference of this circle beginning at the left
hand horizontal part is divided into [ins]as many[/ins] equal parts as [del]many[/del] the
blanks of the verb, then they may be taken in order going
round clockwise, beginning at the left hand horizontal
point.

2. A black dot [del]may[/del] is to denote an indesignate individual
object existing in the universe of discourse.

3. The writing down of a proposition [ins]unenclosed[/ins] is to be construed as
asserting its truth when all that is left indesignate [ins]or vague[/ins] is chosen
[ins][del]vague is so determined[/del] so[/ins] as to be favorable to the truth of the proposition.

4. A lightly drawn endless line [del]drawn[/del] enclosing a proposition
or part verb is to have the effect of a parenthesis in algebra, while
at the same time it is a line of demarcation between regions in
which contrary [del]rules of determining[/del] [ins]what is left vague prevails.[/ins]
[ins]5. A blank space considered as a verb is a verb void of all meaning.[/ins]
