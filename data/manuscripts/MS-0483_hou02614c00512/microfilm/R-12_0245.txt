[u]Existential Graphs.[/u]
as a point.
A [u]valental system of graphs[/u] is one in which all spots of any given quality have a determinate number of sides at which bonds are [ins]or maybe[/ins] attached.
A [u]blank[/u] of a spot is a side at which a bonds are attached.
The [u]adicity[/u] (from -αδα the accusative termination of μόναδα, δύαδα, etc) of a graph is the number of blanks of spots in it to which no bonds [del]that are parts of this graph[/del] are attached. It is [del]to be also[/del] explained that though an entire graph may have its adicity zero, yet [ins]certain[/ins] parts of it may have high adicities, because the attached bonds do not belong to these parts.
According [del]to[/del] as the adicity of a graph is 0, 1, 2, 3, etc. it is to be termed a medad, monad, dyad, triad, etc.
