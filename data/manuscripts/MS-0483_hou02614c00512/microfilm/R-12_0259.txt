[u]Basic Conventions of the System of Existential Graphs.[/u]

1. Each unanalyzed verb shall be signified by an ideograph*, a suitable point of which is appropriated to [del]each to[/del] denoting each blank, or wholly indeterminate subject, of the verb. †

2. A black dot [ins]or point shall[/ins] [del]is to[/del] denote an indesignate individual object existing in the universe of discourse.

3. The writing down of a proposition without any quotation-mark [ins]shall[/ins] [del]is to[/del] be construed as asserting its truth, on the understanding that anything vague in the statement is to be so understood as to be favorable to the assertor's veracity.

4. A lightly drawn endless line encircling a proposition or verb shall be a quotation mark, and whatever is vague in the [del]so[/del] encircled statement shall be so understood as is most favorable to the [del]deliverer[/del] [ins]veracity of the[/ins] author of the proposition, after the precise determination of every reference designation outside the enclosure has been declared. And if the author of a quoted proposition is not given, [del]he[/del] [ins]he[/ins] shall be understood to be the [u]advocatus diaboli[/u], or hypothetical indefinitely clever opponent of the assertion of the whole graph.

5. A blank space shall be understood as void of meaning.
