[u]No A is non-A[/u]

this is [del]Some some-A is A. That is[/del] some [del]part of every[/del]
[del]part of a class belongs to that class.[/del] This is useful in
giving the following form

All A is B
Some some-A is A
<span style="padding-left: 20px;">∴ Some some-A is B</span>

You will ask what is the use of making syllogisms
of these immediate inferences. They are certainly made no
more obvious by that way. The object, however, is this.
We have been led to adopt the syllogism as a means
of classifying arguments; it is, therefore, proper that we
should reduce everything we can to this form in order
to have a homogeneous classification of arguments.
We see that to infer from All A is B to No A is

not-B and to infer from All A is B to [del]Some[/del] some-A is B
are like inferences in the 1st figure. To infer from No A
is B to No B is A is like an inference in the 2nd figure.
And to infer from Some A is B to Some B is A is like an
inference in the third figure. [del][Here skip.][/del]
