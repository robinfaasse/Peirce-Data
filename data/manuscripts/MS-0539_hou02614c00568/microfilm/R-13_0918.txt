The following terms were proposed by the author in [date]1870[/date], and
since they have been generally accepted by writers on the subject,
he is more bound to adhere to them than anybody else, although
he does not now think they were very judiciously chosen. Name-
ly, a juxtasulation is termed a [u]self-relation[/u], a contrasulation an
[u]aliorelation[/u]; a contrambilation is called a [u]concurrency[/u], a juxtam-
bilation an [u]opponency[/u].

There is but one ambilative sulation. It is the [u]juxtalation[/u], or
coexistence. There is but one contrambilative sulation: it is the rela-
tion of [u]individual identity[/u], called [u]numerical identity[/u] by the logi-
cians. But the adjective seems needless. There is but one ambilative
suilation: it is the relation of [u]individual otherness[/u], or [u]negation[/u]. There
is properly no contrambilative contrasuilation: it would be the
absurd relation of [u]incompossibility[/u]. These four relations are to be
termed the [del]four[/del] [ins][u]Cardinal Dyadic Relations of Second Intention[/u][/ins].
It will be enough to call them the [u]cardinations[/u], or [u]cardinal relations[/u].

Any peneperlation or penereperlation is a [u]juxtambilation[/u];
any perlation or reperlation is, in addition, a [u]juxtasulation[/u]. Any
penecontraperlation or penecontrareperlation is an [u]extrambi-[/u]
[u]lation[/u]; any contraperlation or contrareperlation is, in addition,
an [u]extrasuilation[/u]. Every ambilation is a [u]penereperlative pene-[/u]
[u]reperlation[/u]: every contrambilation is a [u]penecontrareperlative[/u]
[u]penecontraperlation[/u]. Every suilation is a [u]juxtareperlative[/u]
[u]juxtaperlation[/u]: every contrasuilation is an [u]extrareperlative[/u]
[u]extraperlation[/u].
