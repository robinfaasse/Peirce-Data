A relation having [ins]seeds[/ins] may be termed a [del]granulation[/del] [ins]granulation[/ins]. The term at present in use is a [u]repeating relation[/u], an [del]extra[/del] granulation being called a [u]non-repeating relation[/u]. Every juxtasublation is a granulation, and every [del]extra[/del] [ins]juxta[/ins] granulation is a sublation.

Extragranulations are of subsidiary importance ([del]not destitute of importance[/del] [ins]of very little importance[/ins]), since an essential extragranulation closely approximates to the nature of a reference.

A granulation may contain a spike, or it may not. In the former case, it may be termed a [del]spicalation[/del] [ins]spicalation[/ins], in the latter an [u]extraspicalation[/u]. [del]The[/del] Extressentispicalations, or granulations not necessarily containing spikes, comprise [del]some of[/del] the most important class of [ins]existential[/ins] relations in logic. This is the class of [u]transitive relations[/u] (the term was introduced by DeMorgan). A relation is [u]transitive[/u] if, and only if, any individual object in that relation to a second which is in the same relation to a third is itself in that relation to this third.
