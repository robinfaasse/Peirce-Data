Since the first, fourth, and fifth squares of the dexter diagonal (or say, dexter squares) have blank ranks, they need no consideration; and the same is true of the second and sixth dexter squares, which have blank columns. It is thus only necessary to consider the third and seventh dexter squares. The third will require dyads for the squares marked 3, [u]after which[/u] the 7[ins]th[/ins] will require dyads in the squares marked 7; and, as it happens, these do not [del]require[/del] make any new dyads requisite, as they might have done. It will [del]be necessary[/del] [ins]generally[/ins] to reexamine all the dexter squares until it is found that no new dyads are required.

In this example, there is no need of filling any of the dexter squares; but any of them can be filled without affecting the transitivity of the relation; and in general no [del]extragradation[/del] [ins]transitive extraspecialation[/ins] is necessarily juxtasublative; but it may be so in any way. That is, the modification of it by making it include [del]any[/del] identical pairs will never destroy its transitivity.
