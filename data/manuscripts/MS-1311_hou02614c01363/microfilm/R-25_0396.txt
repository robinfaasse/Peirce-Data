In the next place, by not attributing this work to any particular writer or writers, the Survey makes itself fully responsible for all the ignorance displayed in the treatise; and though this ignorance would be very pardonable in an individual magneticist, it is no less than deeply disgraceful to a bureau of a government which pretends to stand upon a par with those of Germany, France, Great Britain, Austria, and Italy, and Switzerland & Belgium & Holland.

On the third line, we are told, [ins]or seem to be told,[/ins] that the earliest, or one of its earliest names [ins]of Magnetite[/ins] was the "Hercules stone (heracleian stone)" I don't know what language the word "heracleian" is supposed to belong to. It probably ought to be Heracline or Herculean; and the latter word, in precisely the sense in which Ἡρακλεία was applied to the mineral in Greek, is as good an English word as there is, being used by Nashe, Milton, [ins]Cudworth,[/ins] Boyle, Berkeley, Boswell, Wesley, Byron, and writers of today. But the assertion that this designation is particularly early is [u]not true[/u].
