If Socrates dies, [ins]ergo,[/ins] Socrates is mortal.

This is a [del]case[/del] formal consequence. [del]The meaning of mortal is[/del] [ins]The consequent[/ins] is involved in the meaning of the antecedent.

Socrates dies bravely; ergo Socrates is mortal.

Socrates dies before Plato; ergo Socrates is mortal.

Socrates dies & Plato lives; ergo Socrates is mortal.

Socrates dies & Plato is a man; ergo Socrates is mortal.

Some man dies & another lives; ergo, some man [del]is mortal[/del] [ins]dies[/ins].

Some man dies & so does every other; ergo, some man dies.

Every man dies; ergo, some man dies.

These are all formal consequences.

The following is a material consequence.

[u]Socrates is a man; therefore, Socrates is mortal[/u]

for if a man were to discover [ins]the[/ins] Elixir of Life he would not thereby cease to be a man.

This distinction of formal and material consequences is one of the most practically important in the whole range of Logic.
