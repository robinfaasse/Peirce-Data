The third kind of reasoning may fairly be called Induction, although it does not agree precisely with any one of the meanings attributed to this word. I apply it to any method of inquiry which is justified as a provisional result of a method which in the long run must lead to the truth. But such justification has several grades. In the first place it may be that the method never will give a result in which there can be no error (even supposing no blunders are committed); but it may give successive approximations so that whatever amount of error be [del]some[/del] indicated in advance, the method will necessarily give a result whose error is less than that. Such for example are all known methods of calculating the ratio of the circumference of a circle to its diameter. It [del]is[/del] is known to over 500 places of decimals, but it cannot be exactly expressed by decimals or by any other known exact numerical description.
