Now it is not a very extraordinary theory that
the human mind has such a genius, — such an
imperfect inspiration — of the truth. For there is
some sort of unity in the facts, — some similar
character. At least, we must hope that there is,
since if there is not, no theory of them (not even
a false one) would be possible. How perfect this
unity of character may be we do not know. Well
the mind is itself one of the facts of nature and
therefore if there be a thread of connection run-
ning through these facts, we may hope that
it runs through the human mind too. If it does
not, then all reasoning is absolutely hopeless, and
as all our activity depends on reasoning, we may
as well make common cause with the swine! But
if there be a hope, it is our only hope, and if we are
to use our reason at all, its first dictate is to act
on the only hope there is in cases where there
is but one. So that is the justification for
