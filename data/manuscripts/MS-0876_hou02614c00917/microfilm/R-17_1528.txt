[u]Exhibition I.[/u]

This is intended to illustrate two things; first, that mathematics is not exclusively a science of quantity (though it is so largely); and secondly, that all reasoning is performed by the aid of diagrams, in a wide sense. But this last proposition cannot be made clear in one lecture.

I will take up the following question: Calling an unlimited straight line a [u]ray[/u], and meaning by a plane an unlimited plane, then in the space of projective geometry, which is merely the space of ordinary linear perspective, How many rays can each cut all of four given rays of which no two lie in one plane.
