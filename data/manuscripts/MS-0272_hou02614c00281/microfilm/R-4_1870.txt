[u]Remarkable points of a triangle[/u], certain points having remarkable relations to the triangle. These points are (1) the centre of gravity, (2) the intersection of perpendiculars [ins]from the angles[/ins] on the sides, (3) the centre of the circumscribed circle, [del](4) the centres of the inscribed and escribed circles, (5) the intersection of the lines from the angles to the point of contact of the opposite sides with the inscribed (or an escribed) circle[/del] [ins](4) the centre of the circle through the middles of the sides, (5) the centre of the inscribed circle, (6) the radical centre of the escribed circles, (7)[/ins] [del]the intersection of the three straight lines from the centres of gravity[/del] the point the sum of whose distances from the three sides is a minimum, (8) the [ins]or Lemoine[/ins] Grebe point, the sum of the squares of whose distances from the three sides is a minimum, (9) the Spieker point which bisects the distance between the centre of the [del]inscribed[/del] [ins]inscribed[/ins] circle and the intersection of the [ins]straight[/ins] transversals from the angles [del]to the points of tangency[/del] of the escribed circles
