represent the spurious syllogisms. These figures seem to me useful in teaching. They show that in all cases one extreme (or the part of it spoken of) is outside, the other inside the middle term; and therein lies the force of the reasoning. The objection to Euler's diagrams that they are almost impracticable in complicated problems seems to me trifling; first, because their purpose is to show the nature of the syllogism, not to solve problems; and secondly, because any complicated problem is very readily broken up into a succession of problems with four terms each, or fewer.
