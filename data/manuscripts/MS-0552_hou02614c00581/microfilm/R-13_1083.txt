A relative [ins]or non-relative[/ins] term is closely analogous to a
chemical atom or radicle with unsaturated
links. A non-relative term is a univalent
[del]atom[/del] radicle; it has but one unsaturated
link. A relative term is a multivalent
atom. These unsaturated links can only be
saturated by joining two of them, generally, though
not necessarily, belonging to different radicles;
and the result is always a new radicle unless
two univalent radicles are joined or unless two
links of a bivalent radicle are joined, in which
cases, a saturated compound results. In like manner,
the blanks in a relative term, together with the
place of the copula, can only be filled by identi-
fying two such blanks, either in two terms or the
same term. Thus, we may "lover of —" and "servant
of —" so as to make "lover of the servant of," or
"loved by the servant of," [del]which is the same as[/del] [ins]or[/ins] "served by a lover of"
