If this conjecture is the truth, it entirely clears the phenomenon (i.e. the statement by Iamblichus) of any element of surprisingness, since, granting that Pythagoras was in Egypt at that time, it would not be easy even for us with our superior knowledge of Medo-Persian history to think of any other way in which he could be likely to have been captured by Medes or by Persians. That is proved by the fact that no modern historian has ever suggested any such other way. Is there any such other? Why, yes — come to think of it — twelve years previous Cyrus himself took Lydia and Croesus, and Pythagoras might [del]have be[/del] be supposed to have been captured then and there if only there were any likelihood at all that he would be in Lydia at that time.
