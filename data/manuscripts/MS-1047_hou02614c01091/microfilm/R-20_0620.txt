A gas has a moderate resistance to compression and is elastic to [del]in[/del] indefinite extent. It affords no resistance at all to change of shape.

The physicist, then, puts gases & liquids in [ins]the[/ins] one class of fluids because the internal forces do not influence their shape but only their size.

In a liquid, these internal forces come to equilibrium of themselves, the Cohesion balancing the Expansive ness.

In a gas, the expansive force alone seems to act.

In a solid, as in a liquid, the internal forces come to equilibrium but these forces are of such a nature that they hold the particles at a particular state of condensation but also in a particular direction from each other.
