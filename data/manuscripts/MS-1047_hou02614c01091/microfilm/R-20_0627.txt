Experiment 3. Slowly heat a piece of brimstone in a
Hessian crucible. At 110° of the Centigrade thermom-
eter, it melts and becomes a clear, yellow, mobile, liquid.
At 140° it is dark yellow. At 190° it is of an orange
color and of a gummy consistency. At 260° it is
brown and so thick that the crucible may be
turned upside down without spilling the sul-
phur. Heated still more it becomes liquid again, and
if it is now suddenly cooled it becomes pasty,
transparent, brown, and as elastic as india-rubber.
In all these various states the sulphur weighs the
same. It is, therefore, the same [ins]substance or[/ins] kind of matter.

Experiment 4. Heat some mercury till it begins to boil
and keep it just at that temperature for some time.
Part of it will be converted into a powder, called red
precipitate.
In this case 100 parts of mercury are converted
into 108 parts of red precipitate. These 108 parts
of red precipitate can be converted into 100 parts
of mercury and 8 parts of air. Red precipitate
is, therefore, what is called a combination, and
is a different substance from either air or
mercury.
