The words Solid, Liquid, and gas, in scientific
nomenclature have the same meaning as in common
speech — only the man of science knows what he means.
An uneducated man, for example, might de-
fine them thus
A solid is a body which has a size & shape of its own.
A Liquid has a particular size but takes the shape of the
vessel which contains it.
A Gas takes both the size & the shape of the containing
vessel.
See how a physicist by means of accurate experience
& accurate thought gives meaning to the others meaning.
He observes that a solid resists alteration both of shape
& size. When slightly altered in either respect it forthwith
regains its first condition on removing the strain. A
greater alteration is permanent.
