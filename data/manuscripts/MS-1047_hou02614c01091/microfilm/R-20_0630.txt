CHEMISTRY.

I will begin with common salt.

[u]Experiment 1.[/u] Heat a piece of salt in a porcelain
crucible till it melts. Heat it still more; it boils
and begins to turn to vapor.
[u]Precaution.[/u] The salt must be well dried before hand,
or it might injure the crucible.
This is the effect of heat upon almost all bodies.
[u]Explanation.[/u] Every weight may be divided into
smaller weights and these again into still smaller
and so on as far as you please. However for you
continue this process I call the smallest weight
you reach, a 'jot'. Now each jot of every body attracts
all the other jots of that body, the nearest the most.
The effect of this is of course to bring the jots to-
gether and compress the body. But all bodies also
tend to expand. The consequence is that if the jots
are [del](very)[/del] [ins]very[/ins] near together at first, their attract-
