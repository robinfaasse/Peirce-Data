Ex. 2. To make dilute sulphuric acid:- "If sulphuric acid is poured into water considerable heat is evolved; but this heat is much stronger when the water is poured into the sulphuric acid. The mixture is best made in the following manner. Pour two & a half ounces of water into a sufficiently large stone jar, which is placed in a bowl filled with water; now weigh in a flask half an ounce of common sulphuric acid, pour this in a small stream into the water, stirring the rod continuously, with a glass or porcelain rod, and let the jar remain in the bowl until it is entirely cold." Put [del]the[/del] some clean iron nails to the amount of 2 to 2 1/2 ounces into a bulb glass or tumbler (capable of holding twice the amount of sulphuric acid you intend to put in) and pour on the acid. Ebullition immediately ensues. [del]While[/del] While this goes on put the glass into a chimney, or outside the window. D = 1.

Ex. 3. Flowers of sulphur may be substituted for brimstone. Hessian crucibles are of fire-proof earthenware, and may be got for a few cents. The crucible in this experiment should be covered with a piece of glass; [del]as[/del] second piece should be kept in reserve in case the first should get obscured by sublimed sulphur. A pair of tongs should be at hand. The sulphur cannot be heated too slowly. A gas heater is the best, but if that is not available an argand alcohol lamp will do. D = 3
