[u]Experiment 2.[/u] Put a piece of salt in water. It
dissolves and becomes liquid.

Here the attraction of the salt for the water over-
comes the homogeneous attraction of the salt.
Most salts dissolve in greater quantity in hot
water & all dissolve more readily.

[u]Experiment 3.[/u] Fused salt breaks more easily in
some directions than in others. It tends to
break in cubical pieces.

[u]Experiment 4.[/u] Slowly evaporate the solution of
Ex 2. The salt is deposited in cubical crystals.

The cause of this is not fully understood. We
know, however, that every chemical substance
has a definite crystalline form and that simi-
lar substances have similar crystalline forms.
Sometimes the crystals of common salt have
pyramids on their faces; these are piles of little
cubes.
