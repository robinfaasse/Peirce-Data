[u]On the Foundations of Mathematics[/u]

§ 1. All that we know or think we know by signs and
our knowledge is itself a sign. We use this word sign fami-
liarly, but the idea, or sign, that it excites in our minds is
hazy. Let us endeavor to make out its signification.

For that purpose, let us consider, to begin with, the sim-
plest kind of sign; such as a geometrical figure as the
sign of a triangle, or a determinant as the sign of a
quantity, [del]formed[/del] [ins]connected[/ins] in a certain way of other quantities. The
figure of a triangle is the [u]icon[/u] of a geometrical triangle;
that is, it represents it by virtue of being like it, or having
the same quality. So anything [del]red[/del] is an [u]icon[/u] of anything
scarlet. A quality is whatever it is in itself. It has such
