[del]his ecclesiastical superiors.[/del] What can a man expect, who loving and adoring that unknown
god, the Truth—[del]whatever it may be,[/del] nevertheless takes service under persons who believe themselves
to be in possession of infallible truth? On the one hand, he cannot foresee what proposition his
divine master, the unknown truth, may not command him to maintain. On the other hand, con-
sidering what meticulous minds they must be who believe themselves to be in any respect, infallibly
right, [ins]and considering that, in such small minds, deficiency of intellect is counterbalanced by pretty accurate[/ins]
instincts, [del]why did he not expect that they would be suspicious that such a person as he is would not be of the right stuff for brotherhood[/del] [ins]they will not feel the incongruity of their having in their brotherhood a man who trusts his own reason, while knowing it to be fallible,[/ins]
with them, and that they would be perpetually on the watch for some utterance of his that could be tortured into conflict with their
infallible belief? I feel the sort of pity for him that I should for a man who had got into the toils of gamblers.
