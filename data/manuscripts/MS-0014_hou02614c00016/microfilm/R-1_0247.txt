Another system of quantity is suggested by the multitudes of finite collections, that is, of collections to which De Morgan's "syllogisms of transposed quantity" apply. A syllogism of transposed quantity is [del]an[/del] inference which is similar in principle to the following:
Every Hottentot kills a Hottentot,
But no Hottentot is killed by more than one Hottentot;
$\therefore$ Every Hottentot is killed by a Hottentot.

For if two [del]groups[/del] collections, say the As and the Bs, are such that for every A [del]there is a B, while[/del] [ins]there is a B,[/ins] for every B there is not an A, but for every B but one there is an A, [del]then[/del] the Bs are [del]one[/del] [ins]one[/ins] more than the As. Compounding this relation of one more than in every possible way, we have an endless succession of [ins]finite[/ins] grades, which is the scheme of [del]multitude[/del] [ins]multitude[/ins]. But as long as this signification is retained, this system of quantity is one of applied mathematics. In order to get the scheme of pure mathematics we must evacuate it [del]of[/del] of the relation of this special character. We then have a mere [ins]endless[/ins] succession of grades, which must be systematically designated, as for example by
P, Q, QP, QQ, QPP, QPQ, QQP, QQQ, QPPP, etc
or by
[del]A[/del] A, AB, B, BA, BAB, BABC, BAC, BC, ABC, AC, C, CA, CAB, CB, CBA, etc.

But this is not the [del]complete[/del] [ins]whole of this[/ins] system of [del]quantity[/del] [ins]quantity[/ins] in pure mathematics, since it is not perfectly homogeneous; It presents a singularity, in that the first member has not the same relationships as the others; and this singularity is not inherent, but can be removed by extending the succession endlessly backward. We thus get the system of datary or ordinal number
