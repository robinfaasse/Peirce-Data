[u]Art. 4.[/u] The simplest of all possible [ins]apodictic[/ins] systems of quantity consists of
two grades. This system occurs in logic, where every proposition is either
true or false. Negation is the operation by which we pass from asserting
the truth of a proposition to asserting its falsity, or the reverse. But when
this meaning is given to the two grades, the system belongs to applied mathe-
matics. In pure mathematics there are simply two grades, distinguished
by two meaningless letters devoid of general signification, as [u]v[/u] and [u]f[/u],
and there is the operation by which [u]v[/u] is changed to [u]f[/u] and [u]f[/u] to [u]v[/u]. Denoting
this function by [u]N[/u], we have

[u]Nf[/u] = [u]v[/u] and [u]Nv[/u] = [u]f[/u].

There will also be functions of two letters, as for instance, a function [u]Φ[/u] such
that
[u]Φ[/u]([u]f[/u],[u]f[/u]) = [u]Φ[/u]([u]f[/u],[u]v[/u]) = [u]Φ[/u]([u]v[/u],[u]v[/u]) = [u]v[/u] [u]Φ[/u]([u]v[/u],[u]f[/u]) = [u]f[/u].
