[u]Division.[/u]

Of course, long division is perfectly simple.

Besides that operation, there is another leading [del]of[/del] processes by which any multiplication or division may be performed (when I say, "may be", I mean supposing human life to last long enough, but then, of course, this whole business of secundals flutters upon the butterfly wings of the "theoretically".) The immediate object of this rule is to divide one number [del]without[/del] expressible as a finite sum of different powers of two by another such number.

1[u]st[/u], Multiply the dividend by such a two-power, 2[sup]d[/sup], that the product shall be an odd [ins]whole[/ins] number; and multiply the divisor by such a 2-power, 2[sup]t[/sup], that the product shall be an odd [ins]whole[/ins] number.

2[u]d[/u], Strike off the last 1 from the divisor and add it to what remains, treated as a whole number. [In other words, you add one to the divisor and divide by 2] Call the result the applicand.

3[u]d[/u], Call the [del]result[/del] [ins]dividend[/ins] the operand. Strike off its last character and to what remains, treated as a whole number you add the product of that last figure that has been struck off into the applicand. [In other words, you [del]shall[/del] subtract from the operand whatever power of 2 not having a negative exponent is necessary will leave it divisible by 2; and then divide [del]it[/del] [ins]the remainder[/ins] by the highest power of 2 that will divide it, [del]and[/del] and to the quotient add the applicand.] The result is to be treated as a new applicand in the same way.

4[u]th[/u], This process is to be repeated until you reach the first applicand that is equal to a previous applicand. [ins]You strike nothing off this.[/ins] You now bring all the characters that have been struck off from the applicands vertically down into a horizontal row.
