Notes on Chemical Suffixes & Prefixes

The modern system of nomenclature is said
to be due to Guyton de Morveau. First published in
"Méthode de Nomenclature chimique, proposé par
MM. de Morveau, Lavoisier, Berthollet, et de
Fourcroy. Paris, [date]1787[/date]."

Has been much changed from time to time es-
pecially by Kekulé, Hoffmann, & others.

[u]=ium[/u] or [u]um[/u], affixed to names of metals, as
Lithium, Magnesium, etc. etc. - also to Selenium
[ins]though this is not a metal.[/ins]

[u]=ine[/u], applied to a group of [del]one[/del] univalent
non-metals, fluorine, chlorine, bromine
iodine.

[u]=on[/u], applied to three elements, carbon, boron, silicon

[u]=ide[/u], compounds [del]combining by[/del] [ins]in[/ins] an exterior
bond, the element or residue, the name of
which is modified by this suffix.
Hydride, chloride, sulphide, cyanide, etc.

[u]=uret[/u], same as [u]ide[/u], but restricted chiefly to sulphides
[ins]called sulphurets. Obsolete.[/ins]
