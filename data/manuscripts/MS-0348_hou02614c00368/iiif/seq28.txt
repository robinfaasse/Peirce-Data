But [del]by[/del] [ins]direct[/ins] experience we cannot know that two terms are not
in relation of subordination without knowing whether or
not they are coextensive; for to know that neither is wholly
within the other we must either have found that one is

other or that each is wholly within the other. The first two cases
exclude coextension, the last determines it. It follows that
if a proposition is true of A x C or A x D, it is true of B.

[del]Further;[/del] if we know by direct experience that two terms do not

found either that they nowhere coincide or that one is in the
other throughout. The former case determines exclusion; the
latter excludes it. It follows that if a proposition is true
of D x B or A x D it is true also of C.
