If we multiply $x + \frac{B}{2A}$ into itself we get

$$\begin{array}{r}
x + \frac{B}{2A} \\
x + \frac{B}{2A} \\
\hline
2x^2 + \frac{B}{2A}x \\
+ \frac{B}{2A}x + \frac{B^2}{4A^2} \\
\hline
x^2 + \frac{B}{A}x + \frac{B^2}{4A^2}
\end{array}$$

Hence, dividing the equation by $A$,

$$x^2 + \frac{B}{A}x + \frac{C}{A}$$
$$= \left(x + \frac{B}{2A}\right)^2 + \frac{C}{A} - \frac{B^2}{4A^2} = 0$$

or $\left(x + \frac{B}{2A}\right)^2 = \frac{B^2}{4A^2} \left(1 - \frac{4AC}{B^2}\right)$

The quantity $1 - \frac{4AC}{B^2}$ [ins](or rather [del]4AC - B^2[/del])[/ins] is called the discriminant of the equation. Taking the square root of both sides, and observing that minus any quantity when squared gives the square of the quantity, we get

$$x + \frac{B}{2A} = \pm \frac{B}{2A} \sqrt{1 - \frac{4AC}{B^2}}$$
$$x = -\frac{B}{2A} \pm \frac{B}{2A} \sqrt{1 - \frac{4AC}{B^2}}$$
