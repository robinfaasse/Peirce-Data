17[/pn] When De Morgan opened this road to logical inquiry, he opened a
way for the sempiternal honor of British logic; but unfortunately,
British logicians were small men. The new forms of inference reached
far beyond their cadre, they could not accept them; they could not reject
them, since they were evidently valid (at least within limits that had
not yet been precised;) The only thing they could do, since they
were not equal to discussing them, was to ignore them; and
this they did with the greatest unanimity and alacrity. Shortly
after, this [del]same[/del] accursed De Morgan, (as they must have
considered him, and as their conduct fully proves that they did,)
started the logic of relations, by [del]developing[/del] [ins]ploughing and harrowing,[/ins] in a modern spirit,
a field distinctly indicated by the old logicians, as one [del]they[/del] [ins]which[/ins]
had not had leisure to treat as it deserved.
