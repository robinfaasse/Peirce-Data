[u]Continuity of ideas[/u].

But what can it mean to say that we think of an idea that is utterly gone? How can we make any reference at all to it?

I hold it for evident that no idea can affect another unless there be between them an unbroken series of ideas so closely connected that the presence of one [del]is ipso facto[/del] [ins]involves[/ins] the diminished presence of neighboring ones. Without this, no [del]ideas distinct idea[/del] [ins]meaning[/ins] can be attached to the phrase that one idea affects another.

Consider, for example, past and present. If the [ins]all[/ins] past is quite past, it means nothing to say anything of the past. It is absolutely unknowable.
