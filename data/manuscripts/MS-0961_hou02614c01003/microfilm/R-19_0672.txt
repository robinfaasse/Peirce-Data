[u]The Law of Mind[/u]

Events seem to flow in time. Time is a certain system of relations. A process is somewhat indeterminate in its time relations; that is to say, for example, a process which occupies the whole of Monday, though subsequent to Sunday noon and previous to Tuesday noon, is partly a whole day subsequent to Sunday noon and partly not, etc. But the time occupied by any process is separated from other time by two instants whose time-relations [ins]of each[/ins] [del]to all[/del] [ins]with[/ins] every other instant are [del]absolutely[/del] completely determinate. The properties of the time-relations of instants are as follows:—

1. Of any two different instants one is [del]subsequent[/del] [ins]previous[/ins] to the other, the latter [del]or[/del] subsequent to the former. No instant is either subsequent or previous to itself.

2. Any instant, A, previous to a second instant, B, itself previous to a third instant C, is likewise previous to that instant C.

3. If any instant, A, is previous to any other instant [del]B[/del] [ins]C[/ins], a third instant, B, exists subsequent to A and previous to C. Furthermore, there is a continuous series of instants between A and C. This is an idea not easily analyzed.
