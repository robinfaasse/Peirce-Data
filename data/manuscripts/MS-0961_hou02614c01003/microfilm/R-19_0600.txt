But a general idea may have a certain con-
creteness, a here-and-now-ness, and its insis-
tency may be very great. In fact, the [ins]immediate[/ins] insistency
of the present is almost always eclipsed by that
of some general idea; so that the past does not
appear as a mere tinge upon the present, but
the present appears as a modification of some
general idea of the past which constitutes the
[del]subject[/del] subject nominative of our thought.
