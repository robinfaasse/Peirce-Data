[u]The Law of Mind.[/u]

[del]Let us[/del] Grant that the stream of consciousness exists in continuous time. We will inquire later what reason we have for believing this true; at first, we have to see what the proposition involves. Our future experiences are not yet before the mind; the most startling lightning-flash is [del]totally[/del] utterly absent a hundredth of a second before it comes. In like manner, past feelings are gone. [del]We[/del] [ins]I[/ins] may have reason to think that the blue color I am now looking on [del]was[/del] [ins]excites[/ins] a very similar [del]color[/del] [ins]sensation[/ins] to that which it excited one second before; but that previous sensation is gone and there is no means of comparing the two. Indeed, to say that they are "similar" can mean nothing except that after they are gone, an occult power in the depths of the soul, associates ideas of them together. But the past feeling, as past, is utterly gone, and gone forever. All that is immediately present then is capable of being felt within an instant, and can be felt only in a simple instant. Such consciousness [ins]we may[/ins] term feeling-in-itself. Let a continuous [ins]musical[/ins] note be sounded. Then, as consciousness flows on, the continuum of feeling is associated, in the mental representation of it, in the mode of similarity. Suppose, however, that the pitch suddenly changes.
