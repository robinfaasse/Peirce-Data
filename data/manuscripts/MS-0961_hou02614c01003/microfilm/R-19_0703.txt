Unless there can be two different ideas so close together that what is present to one cannot be utterly absent from the other, ideas cannot influence one another at all. But if there is not a continuity of ideas, take a past and a present one. The past one is absolutely past and what it contained may be completely absent now and indeed [del]can[/del] [ins]must[/ins] be so.

[marginalia]
Note the absurdity of the notion that something not in the mind should immediately excite the mind.
[/marginalia]

So with the influence of one mind on another. The prevalent notion is that A influences matter & then something material with no mind in it is conveyed to B and influences his mind. But if as I think mind can only influence mind immediately & not [del]by[/del] [ins]through[/ins] the intervention of matter, then there must be a continuity between A and B.
