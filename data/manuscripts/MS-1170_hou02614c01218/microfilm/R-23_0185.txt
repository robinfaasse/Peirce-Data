lamana A mnemonic [del]stem or[/del] root proposed by C. S. Peirce as a [del]the[/del] foundation on which to build names for certain kinds of relations. It represents three individual objects (but any two or all three may be identical) L, M, and N such that L is in a given relation to M [del]while[/del] [ins]and[/ins] M is in the same relation to N. [del]Standing to[/del] Used as a word a lamana of a given relation is a grain of that relation, that is, [del]denotes[/del] [ins]is[/ins] an individual M to which an individual L stands in that relation while M stands in the same relation to an individual N. If the vowel o is substituted for the first a, giving lomana, the individual L is different from other than M. If the same substitution affects the second syllable, giving lamona, the individual M is other than N. If it is the third vowel that is so altered, the individuals L and N are not the same. Then a lamanalation will be a lation [del]for which there is[/del] [ins]such that there are[/ins] individuals, L, M, and N, [del]L and M[/del] standing in that lation to M, and M in that lation to N. A lomanalation will be a lamanalation in which L and M are different individuals, and so on. Thus, a lomonolation will be such a relation [del]that[/del] [ins]say r, such[/ins] there is an individual [del]L[/del] [ins]L[/ins] in this relation r to another individual, M, which again stands in this relation to a third and different individual, N. An extra lomonolation will be a relation such that there are not three different individuals so related. A juxtalomonolation will be a relation such that every individual of the universe, represented by M, has [del]a differen[/del] another individual, L, in this relation to it, and [del]a third individual[/del] stands itself in this relation to an individual N, other than L and than M.
