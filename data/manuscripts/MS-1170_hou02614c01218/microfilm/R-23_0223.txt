[u]innumerable[/u]. Of [del]such[/del] a multitude [ins]as the multitude of a collection, say the Ns,[/ins] must be in order that there should be a relation, r, for which it should be true that every N stands in the relation r to some N to which no other N stands in this relation r, while it should not be true that to every N some N stands in the relation r. For example, suppose a man to make it a rule, on the day following his making any loan, to loan an equal amount and [del]pay[/del] [ins]off[/ins] with it [del]back[/del] the former loan. [del]Then[/del] Let the Ns be the loans and [del]the[/del] [ins]of[/ins] relation r consist in being paid off by the use of. If, then, [del]the[/del] the Ns, or loans, were innumerable, every loan might be paid off by the use of a loan, and still it need not be true that every loan is used to pay off a loan, [del]though[/del] [ins]although[/ins] if the loans were enumerable this must needs be the case.
