[u]Dynamogeny[/u] and [u]dynamogenesis[/u] are ill-defined, but you say you do not want corrections.

[u]ecbasis[/u] is said to mean a digression. It never was in use in English, nor in philosophy at any time. Aristotle uses the word near the beginning of the De Caelo apparently (but I look very hastily) in the sense of geometrical generation. The word should be deleted at the first opportunity.

[u]ecbatic[/u] is a rare term of Greek syntax and is correctly defined.

[u]Eccentric perception[/u], or [u]projection[/u] the mental achievement of attributing to sensations objective existence as qualities of things within a field of space. [del]To be carefu[/del] Not to be confounded with [u]localization[/u], [ins]as Külpe confounds or composite[/ins] by which sensations are associated with parts of the body. The application of the adjective seems to be a blunder. For the term is employed quite otherwise by physiologists. See Ziehen's Leitfaden ([date]1893[/date]) p. 56.
