system I have endeavored to revise my conception of how this word
ought to be used in philosophy. In Greek [greek]σύστημα[/greek] properly should
signify the result of the operation of [greek]σύστασις[/greek] which is the noun
of action of [greek]συνίστημι[/greek] to set together, to compose. But in literary
classical Greek [greek]σύστημα[/greek] was not used, its meaning being
expressed by [greek]σύστασις[/greek]. The new word [greek]σύστημα[/greek] was introduced
by specialists, iatrics, astronomers, [ins]musicians,[/ins] and writers on government, to
express special scientific conceptions. I have not the works of Hippo-
crates but I believe the word is not used by him as medical men have
used [u]system[/u] in modern times. In music it means something or other
which I believe never affected philosophy. In Plato it means the
government, working or mode of working of government, and the
L. [u]constitutio[/u] corresponds in formation to the Greek [greek]σύστημα[/greek].
Aristotle uses it albeit in this sense in that passage of the Nic.
Ethics from which Louis XV was quoting in saying "L'état c'est moi"
"The ruling part of a city or other government is the city or government"
[greek]πόλις τὸ κυριώτατον μάλιστ' εἶναι δοκεῖ καὶ πᾶν ἄλλο σύστημα[/greek]. Eucken
says the stoics applied the word to the government of the universe;
but Eucken is far from infallible, and I think their usual word was
[greek]σύστασις[/greek] (Stobaeus Ecl i. 312). I haven't either Plutarch or Sextus
Empiricus at hand. Aristotle in his work on generation several
times uses the word, apparently rather vaguely, as one should speak
of the constitution or the system. But I am confident that
that is aside from the main current of the history. L & S. say that
