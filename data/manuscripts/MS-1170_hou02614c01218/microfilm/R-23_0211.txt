[u]Coëfficients of Reality[/u], two elements which go to making up the attribute of reality, according to Prof. [del]John Dewey[/del] [ins]H. W. Stuart[/ins], the one being derived from the experience of resistence, while the other is a sort of confidence, a trust in the real as "ein feste Burg". [del]Dewey[/del] [ins]Stuart[/ins] regards both elements as emotions, but one may [del]accept[/del] admit that reality combines two such elements without reducing it to a mere emotion. Resistance is, in some measure, constraint, and [del]can be[/del] [ins]must be[/ins] experienced; and the acknowledgment that an object has constrained us and given us all that we wanted to make this submission, involves of itself, without any other coëfficient, the acknowledgment of the reality of that object. [del]But[/del] [ins]Still[/ins] as an experience, it is limited to reality in the past, just as the resolve, whether inhibited or not, that is implied in trust, has to do solely with reality in the future. Reality is an intellectual concept founded on the conative factors of constraint and resolve.
