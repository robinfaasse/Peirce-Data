[u]circuition[/u] [ins]Add:[/ins]

3. Proposed by C. S. Peirce as a name for that part of a self-returning change which brings back a return of those characters which do not distinguish the two senses in which change can take place along certain homaloidal lines, but does not bring a return of those characters which are different for those two senses of change. Thus [del]if[/del] a color, R, [del]changes in such a way that it is at all times capable of being exactly matched by a mixture of three sectors of a color-disk of three fixed colors, and by such a continuous change that the original color is at length restored, there will be if the original color was matched[/del] [ins]which can be exactly matched by a mixture of [ins]supplementary[/ins] sectors of [del]two[/del] fixed color disks be changed continuously by the admixture of a third [del]for[/del] constant color disk so that it ultimately becomes exactly what it originally was, there [del]will be a second[/del][/ins] [del]which is exactly[/del] [ins]to be[/ins] [del]more[/del] matchable by the mixture on a color wheel of two colors M and N, [del]one of which[/del] M is matchable by a mixture of two colors A and B, and N by the mixture of B with a third color C. Now let this color R be continuously changed so as always to be exactly matchable by a mixture of A, B, and C, [ins]and so as[/ins] finally to return to its original state. Then there must [ins]obvious[del]ly[/del][/ins] have been a [del]sta[/del] stage of the change when it was exactly matchable by a mixture of M and N, [del]as[/del] [ins]always[/ins] in the beginning, [del]but when[/del] [ins]although[/ins] its color was not [ins]then[/ins] at all the original color. [del]When[/del] [ins]If[/ins] this point was reached, the change would be said to have gone through a [u]circuition[/u], and when the original state was restored to have gone through a [u]revolution[/u], or [u]bicircuition[/u]. [ins]Were there several such stages each would be said to complete a new circuition, and each successive pair a new bicircuition[/ins]
