Upon what property of the copula does this depend?
Some of the treatises say, upon the principle of identity "A is A". Let us see. Put "loves" instead of [del]"is"[/del] [ins]"is"[/ins].
<span style="padding-left: 40px;">S loves M; M loves P:</span>
<span style="padding-left: 60px;">∴ S loves P.</span>
This follows, if, and only if the lover loves all [del]that the loved[/del] his beloved loves. [del]It has nothing to do with everything loving itself or not doing.[/del]
A relation of which such a formula is true is called a [u]transitive[/u] relation. De Morgan, then, is quite right in saying that the validity of [u]Barbara[/u] depends on the transitiveness of the copula; and Aristotle says substantially the same thing.
