We define multiplication and addition as follows:
$yz = \log_x[(x^y)^z]$ [and if there [ins]is[/ins] [del]is[/del] indeterminacy such a value is to be taken as to make $yz$ independent of $x$.]
$y + u = \log_y \log_x [(x^y)^{y^u}]$
We can go no further since neither $\log_x (x^u + x^v)$ nor $x^{\log_x u \cdot \log_x v}$ is in-
dependent of $x$.
These however, are not mere definitions of multiplication and
addition. They are important properties of involution. Namely,
$\log_x [(x^y)^z]$ is independent of $x$ or $\log_x [(x^y)^z] = \log_\xi [(\xi^y)^z]$
$\log_y \log_x [(x^y)^{y^u}] = \log_\eta \log_\xi [(\xi^\eta)^{\eta^u}]$
We also have the facts that in $x^y = z$, $x$ and $y$ may be any
numbers whatever; [del]but z can only be certain or[/del] but after either
$x$ or $y$ is chosen $z$ can only be one of a [del]peculiar[/del] [ins]suitable[/ins] set of numbers.
