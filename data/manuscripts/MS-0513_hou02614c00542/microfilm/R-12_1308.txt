I also write a "short" mark "$\breve{}$" over a relative to show that
the [u]converse[/u] relation is intended. That is,
$(\breve{l})_{ij} = l_{ji}$.
& other words $\breve{l}$ means "— is loved by —."

Of course, this is confined to dyadic relatives. For triadic
relatives two signs would be necessary. For instance, if we
were to write $\acute{g}$ in the sense, that
$(\acute{g})_{ijk} = g_{jki}$
and $\grave{g}$ in the sense that
$(\grave{g})_{ijk} = g_{kji}$
we should have
$(\breve{\acute{g}})_{ijk} = g_{kij}$
$(\breve{\grave{g}})_{ijk} = g_{ikj}$
$(\breve{g})_{ijk} = g_{jik}$
and three accents would be unnecessary since
$\breve{\breve{\acute{g}}} = g$
$\breve{\breve{\grave{g}}} = g$
$\acute{\acute{\acute{g}}} = g$
$\grave{\grave{g}} = g$
$\breve{\acute{g}} = \grave{g}\breve{}$
$\breve{\grave{g}} = \acute{g}\breve{}$
