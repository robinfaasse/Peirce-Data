Although the "general algebra of logic", as I name the system
above described is pretty nearly perfect for its proper purpose,
that of analyzing reasoning in the most minutely accurate
manner, yet it must be acknowledged that it is a very cumbrous
machine, utterly wanting in elegance and compactness: In the
hope of remedying that defect, I have invented two other
methods of representing [del]the[/del] assertions and performing
necessary inferences.

One of these is a graphical method, the other is an algebraic
system which seems to have fascinated Prof. Schröder much
more than it has me. I will first explain the graphical me-
thod. This method has two varieties, which I call Entitative
Graphs and Existential Graphs. In the [u]Monist[/u] for January [date]1897[/date]
I have given a very slight and imperfect sketch of the system
of entitative graphs. I will here explain the existential graphs
which are less philosophical but are much easier to use.
