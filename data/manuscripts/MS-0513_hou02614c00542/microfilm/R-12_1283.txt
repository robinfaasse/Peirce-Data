I call $1_{ij}$ meaning $i$ is identical with $j$, and $T_{ij}$ semi-logical
predicates. In order to define $1_{ij}$ we [del]must[/del] [ins]had better[/ins] employ the notation
$(p)_1$ to mean $p$ is true in the state of things $1$. Then, the defi-
nition in question is
$$\Pi_i\ 1_{ii}$$
$$\Pi_i\Pi_j\ \Pi_1\ (T_{ij})_1 \ \text{\rotatebox[origin=c]{180}{$\Pi$}}\ \Pi_2\ \prod_u\ (\bar{u}_{ii} + u_{ij})_2$$
Without indices expressing states of things, this can be sufficiently
expressed by writing
$$\Pi_i\Pi_j\ \prod_u\ T_{ij} + \bar{u}_{ii} + u_{ij}$$
or
$$\Pi_i\Pi_j\ \Pi_\alpha\ T_{ij} + \bar{r}_{\alpha ii} + r_{\alpha ij}$$

$$1_{ij} \prec \Pi_\alpha\ \bar{r}_{\alpha ii} + r_{\alpha ij}$$
$$\Pi_\beta\ \bar{r}_{\beta ii} + r_{\beta ji} \prec 1_{ji}$$
In whatever relation $i$ is to itself it is to $j$
$$\Pi_\beta\ \bar{r}_{\beta ij} + r_{\beta ii}$$
$$\text{\rotatebox[origin=c]{180}{$\Pi$}}\ \gamma = \breve{\beta}$$
$$\Pi_\gamma\ \bar{r}_{\gamma ji} + r_{\gamma ii}$$
