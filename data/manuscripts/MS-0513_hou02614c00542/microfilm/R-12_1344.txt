I will now glance at the matter from a point of view we shall have
later to occupy more deliberately, that of Relative Probabilities, or Statistical

I remark as a preliminary that if $n$ means "—is a negro", or
any other class, then $n$ may be treated as a dyadic relative, and
that so treated $n = n \bar{U}$. For by the general formula $l \prec l \bar{U} \prec l \bar{o}$, so that
it only remains to show that $n \bar{o} \prec n$. This is not true of ordinary relatives.
For since $0 \bar{U} \prec 0$ contraposition gives $U \prec 0 \bar{j} 0$. Hence $l \bar{o} \prec l (0 \bar{j} 0) \prec l 0 \bar{j} 0$.
So that if $l \bar{o} \prec l$, we have $l \bar{o} \prec l \bar{j} 0$, or a lover of anything is a lover of
everything. But in the case of a class term, it is plain that none of the [del]aggregant[/del]
pairs that make up the relative $n$ can have anything but negroes as relates,
nor can any of those that make up $\bar{n}$ have anything but non-negroes as
relates. Hence $\bar{n} \bar{n} \prec 0$ or by contraposition $U \prec \bar{n} \bar{j} n$. Hence, we have
[del]$n \bar{o} \prec n$[/del] $n \bar{o} \prec n (\bar{n} \bar{j} n) \prec n \bar{n} \bar{j} n \prec \bar{j} n \prec n$ or $n \bar{o} \prec n$.
