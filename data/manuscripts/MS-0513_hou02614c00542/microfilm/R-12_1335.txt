The logician of the theological stamp is inclined to say, we have seen that there is absolutely nothing in demonstrative reasoning but syllogism; why then delay longer upon it? But the experimentalist must try how this is in practice; and when he tries, he finds that however true that may or must be, yet the whole gist of reasoning lies outside the syllogism. Nor is it merely that new premises are used. No: so far, we have utterly failed to bring out the essential nature of reasoning.

Come, then, to practical applications, — not to fabricated examples; they can teach nothing except by being carelessly constructed. Come to the living applications. MacColl was the first to offer a really practical application of Boolean algebra, when by means of it he showed how to transform the signs of [ins]multiple[/ins] [del]definite[/del] integration when the limits of one integration involve the variables of another.
