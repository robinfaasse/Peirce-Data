In order to express that every man loves every woman, we write
$m \prec l \bar{j} \bar{w}$
or every pair of which the first member is a man and the second
a woman is a pair of lover and loved. Multiplying by $\breve{w}$
$m \breve{w} \prec (l \bar{j} \bar{w}) \breve{w} \prec l \bar{j} \bar{w} \breve{w} \prec l \bar{j} \Upsilon \prec l$
so that $m \breve{w} \prec l$

From this we can recover the original form of statement, for
$m \prec m \text{ l } \prec m (\breve{w} \bar{j} \bar{w}) \prec m \breve{w} \bar{j} \bar{w} \prec l \bar{j} \bar{w}$.

In the general algebra this is written
$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_k m_{ik} w_{jk} \prec l_{ij}$.

On the same principle to express that [ins]in[/ins] every way in which every
horse can be praised to a man there is some woman who
so praises them, we ought to write something like this.
$m^{\breve{h}} \prec p_{\breve{w}}$ [del]$\prec (m^{\breve{h}})^{\breve{h}} m'$[/del]

That is, just as $m \breve{w} \prec l$ means
$\begin{matrix} m_1 \breve{w}_1 + m_2 \breve{w}_2 + m_3 \breve{w}_3 \\ m_1 \breve{w}_2 + m_2 \breve{w}_2 + m_3 \breve{w}_2 \\ \text{etc} \end{matrix} \prec \begin{matrix} l_{11} + l_{21} + l_{31} \\ l_{12} + l_{22} + l_{32} \\ \text{etc} \end{matrix}$

so (supposing there are 3 men and two horses)
$\begin{matrix} m_1 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_1 \breve{h}_2 + m_1 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_2 \breve{h}_2 + m_1 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_3 \breve{h}_2 \\ + m_2 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_1 \breve{h}_2 + m_2 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_2 \breve{h}_2 + m_2 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_3 \breve{h}_2 \\ + m_3 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_1 \breve{h}_2 + m_3 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_2 \breve{h}_2 + m_3 \breve{h}_1 \cdot m_3 \breve{h}_2 \end{matrix} \prec (p \breve{w})_{11} \cdot (p \breve{w})_{12} \text{ etc}$
