It remains to make out the rules of illative transformation of the graphs.

All such rules are reducible to [u]permits to draw[/u] and [u]permits to erase[/u].

The whole graph is supposed to be in one enclosure marking the limit of the universe. We have to distinguish between parts [u]oddly enclosed[/u], that is, within an odd number of encircling lines, including that of the whole graph, and parts [u]evenly enclosed[/u], that is, within an even number of enclosing lines, including that of the whole graph. The parts of the graph are either [u]spots[/u], that is, parts having a positive descriptive significance, [del]and[/del] [ins]or[/ins] [u]lines[/u], which merely mark the numerical identity of objects to which the descriptions apply. It will simplify matters to assume that only one end of a line is within enclosures that the other end is not within. By simply marking [u]nodes[/u] on the lines at their outermost or innermost points, these nodes being spots whose descriptive implication is merely existence, we can readily make the lines conform to that condition.
