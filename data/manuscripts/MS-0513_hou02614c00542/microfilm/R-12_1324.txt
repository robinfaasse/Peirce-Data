[del]S[/del]yllogistic inference takes place in one or other of two ways
which are essentially the same, as follows

$$\begin{array}{c c c}
\bar{v} \prec u \quad \bar{u} \prec v & & 1 \prec u \quad 1 \prec (\bar{u} \tilde{\succ} v) \text{ or } 1 \prec (v \tilde{\succ} \bar{u}) \\
\therefore \quad \bar{v} \prec u \cdot (\bar{u} \dagger v) & \therefore & 1 \prec u (\bar{u} \tilde{\succ} v) \text{ or } 1 \prec (v \tilde{\succ} \bar{u}) u \\
\therefore \quad [\text{By } x(y \dagger z) \prec x \cdot y \dagger z] & & [\text{By } x(y \tilde{\succ} z) \prec x y \tilde{\succ} z \text{ or } (x \tilde{\succ} y)z \prec x \tilde{\succ} yz] \\
\bar{v} \prec \bar{u} \bar{u} \dagger v & \therefore & 1 \prec u \bar{u} \tilde{\succ} v \quad \text{ or } \quad 1 \prec v \tilde{\succ} \bar{u} u \\
\therefore \quad [\text{By } [del]x \cdot \bar{x} \prec 0[/del] & & [\text{By } x \bar{x} \prec 0 \\
\text{and if } x \prec y \quad x \dagger z \prec y \dagger z] & & \text{and } [del]\text{if }[/del] x \prec y \quad x \tilde{\succ} z \prec y \tilde{\succ} z] \\
\bar{v} \prec 0 \dagger v & \therefore & 1 \prec 0 \tilde{\succ} v \quad \text{ or } \quad 1 \prec v \tilde{\succ} 0 \\
\therefore \quad \bar{v} \prec v & \therefore & 1 \prec v
\end{array}$$

Now, [ins]in the sense[/ins] that this process [del]excludes[/del] depends upon
every fundamental formula not a mere equation, it is true that
all demonstrative inference is syllogistic.

For instance, the inference [del]from[/del]

$$\begin{array}{c}
\Sigma_i \Pi_j l_{ij} \\
\therefore \quad \Pi_j \Sigma_i l_{ij}
\end{array}$$

is in the Algebra of Dyadic Relatives

$$\begin{array}{c}
v (l \tilde{\succ} 0) \\
\therefore \quad v l \tilde{\succ} 0
\end{array}$$

involving one of the fundamental formulae of syllogism.

The second and third figures of syllogism introduce mere
equational formulae
