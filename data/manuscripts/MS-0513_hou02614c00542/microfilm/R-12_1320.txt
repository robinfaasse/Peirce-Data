I return to dyadic relatives and the converse. There is a sort of analogy between the [u]negative[/u] and the converse of the negative. If we denote the latter by a wavy line so that
$$\tilde{\bar{l}} = \bar{\tilde{l}} = \tilde{l}$$
we have
$$\bar{\bar{l}} = \tilde{\tilde{l}} = l$$
Then just
$$(l \prec b) = (\bar{b} \prec \bar{l}) = (\tilde{b} \prec \tilde{l})$$
and
$$(l \prec b) = \bar{l} \dagger b = \tilde{l} \ \text{\textdaggerdbl} \ b$$
The sign $\text{\textdaggerdbl}$, a dagger with a scorpion-tail, is read "[del](of every-[/del]thing) except" [del]by[/del] [ins]if[/ins] $b$ [del]= loves[/del] [ins]is[/ins] [del]everything except benefac-[/del]tory. Just as
$$v \prec \bar{l} \dagger l$$
Whatever coexists with anything nonloves it [u]or else[/u] loves it
or if it loves it loves it so
$$1 \prec \tilde{l} \ \text{\textdaggerdbl} \ l$$
Whatever is identical with anything is unloved by anything [u]unless[/u] by what loves that thing or is loved (if at all) only by lovers of it.
Also just as
$$l \dagger b = \overline{\bar{b} \cdot \bar{l}} \quad \text{so} \quad l \ \text{\textdaggerdbl} \ b = \overline{\bar{b} \ \tilde{\cdot} \ \tilde{l}}$$
That is, what [del]is not[/del] [ins]if A does[/ins] [del]loves[/del] [ins]not love[/ins] [del]everything unless[/del] [ins]it benefits B then[/ins] B is non-benefitted by something that is unloved by A.
