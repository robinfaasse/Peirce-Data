We will also multiply in $x + \bar{x}$, substituting for $x$ $\Pi_{k} f_{k}$, so that $x + \bar{x}$ becomes $\Pi_{k} \bar{f}_{k} + \Sigma_{k} f_{k}$. The product is
$$\Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} (\Pi_{k} \bar{f}_{k} + \Sigma_{k} f_{k}) \cdot (\bar{f}_{i} + w_{i}) \cdot (\bar{f}_{j} + a_{j})$$
Now identifying $i$ and $j$ with $k$, we get
$$\Pi_{k} \bar{f}_{k} \cdot (\bar{f}_{k} + w_{k}) \cdot (\bar{f}_{k} + a_{k}) + \Sigma_{k} f_{k} \cdot (\bar{f}_{k} + w_{k}) \cdot (\bar{f}_{k} + a_{k})$$
$$= \Pi_{k} \bar{f}_{k} + \Sigma_{k} f_{k} \cdot w_{k} \cdot a_{k}$$
[del]$$\propto \Pi_{k} \bar{f}_{k} + \Sigma_{k} w_{k} \cdot a_{k}$$[/del]

That is, either there are no fairies or something alive can work miracles. This is more accurate that the conclusion of "Darapti", which unless we take account of the complicated and obscure doctrine of modals is "Some thing alive can work miracles."
