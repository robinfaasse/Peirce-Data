The above described two relative operations, "relative addition" and "relative multiplication" belong (like the non-relative operations) to a set, which may [ins]variously[/ins] be conceived as [del]6 or 8 or 12 or 16[/del] [ins]numbering from 6 to 64[/ins], as shown in the following table.

[u]The Simple Combinations of Logic.[/u]

[u]UNIVERSAL[/u] [u]Non Relative[/u] [u]Relative[/u]
[u]Inclusion[/u] $l \prec b$ "if loves then benefits." $l \propto b$ "loved (if at all) only by benefactors of"
[u]Involution[/u] $l \bar{\prec} b = \bar{l} \prec \bar{b}$ "loves if benefits." $l \infty b = \bar{l} \propto \bar{b}$ "loves whatever may be benefitted by"
[u]Aggregation[/u] $l \text{ } \bar{\psi} \text{ } b = \bar{l} \prec b$ "either loves or benefits." $l \text{ } \bar{\dagger} \text{ } b = \bar{l} \propto b$ "loves all but what benefits,"
[u]Exclusion[/u] $l \text{ } \bar{\vee} \text{ } b = l \prec \bar{b}$ "if loves or benefits, then." $l \text{ } \bar{\wedge} \text{ } b = l \propto \bar{b}$ "loved only by those not benefitted by."

[u]PARTICULAR[/u]
[u]Exclusion[/u] $l \bar{\prec} b$ may love without benefitting $l \bar{\propto} b$ "[del]loved by[/del] [ins]benefitted[/ins] by others than lovers of"
[u]Aggregation[/u] $l \bar{\bar{\psi}} b$ may not love though benefitting $l \bar{\infty} b$ "benefits something not loved by"
[u]Involution[/u] $l \bar{\bar{\vee}} b$ neither loves nor benefits $l \bar{\bar{\dagger}} b$ "unbenefitted by something not loved by"
[u]Inclusion[/u] $l \bar{\bar{\prec}} b$ both loves and benefits $l \bar{\bar{\wedge}} b$ "benefactor of a lover of."

The relative operations may be multiplied by [del]eight[/del] [ins]four[/ins] by taking the converses of the two constituents and the whole.

Of [del]these[/del] operations there are certain ones which merit particular attention. One is a variety of involution. One I write with an exponent, $l^b = l \infty b$ "loves every benefactor of"
Another operation is $^b l$ "loves only benefactors of"

The following formulae hold
$(l \cdot b)^s = l^s \cdot b^s$
$l^{s+t} = l^s \cdot l^t$
$l^{(st)} = (l^s)^t$

$^s(l \cdot b) = ^s l \cdot ^s b$
$^{s+t} l = ^s l \cdot ^t l$
$^{(st)} l = ^s(^t l)$.
