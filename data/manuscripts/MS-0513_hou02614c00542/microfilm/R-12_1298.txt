As a final general example of the use of this method, let us
employ it for a slight discussion of some elementary points
concerning multitude.

To belong [del]to[/del] as unit to a collection and to possess as a quality
are substantially the same relation. To abbreviate we will say [ins]write[/ins]

A — possesses — B

meaning A possesses the quality B or A belongs to the [del]class[/del] [ins]collection[/ins] designated
by B.

Everything about multitude turns upon that class of dyadic
relations [del]who[/del] each of which is such that many things may be
in that relation to one and the same thing but the same thing can
never be in that relation to two different things. We will call it a
[u]ramal relation[/u], or better, a [u]many-to-one relation[/u]. Fig. 119 shows
a way of expressing that A stands to B and C stands to D in [u]one[/u] many-
