The proper way to use this algebra is, in my opinion, with a
light touch. Considerable can easily be done with it. Much
can be done with it at the expense of [del]endless[/del] [ins]great[/ins] puzzling. If any
body is in love with puzzling, this algebra affords him a decent
scope. If he has another purpose let him combine this algebra
with indices and quantifiers and trust to it for the easy steps
and to them for those that are more difficult.

<u>XIII</u>.

Let us now see how our apparatus works practically. Some very fine
examples have been given by MacColl of the transformation of multiple
definite integrals & by Schröder. But I will merely mention those,
and gang my own gait.

The most rudimentary kind of mathematics is that whose system
of values comprises but two degrees. This is the very system of
demonstrative logic which we have been studying.

Next in the order of rudimentariness, fundamentalness,
and abstractness comes arithmetic. This [del]depends on[/del] [ins]springs out of[/ins] the doctrine
multitude. This depends on the consideration of one-to-many relations.

Let $Y_{\alpha}$ mean that $\alpha$ is a one-to-many relation, and let
$r_{\alpha i j}$ mean that $i$ stands to $j$ in the relation $\alpha$. Then,

$\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} \Pi_{k} \bar{Y}_{\alpha} \mathbin{\psi} \bar{r}_{\alpha i k} \mathbin{\psi} \bar{r}_{\alpha j k} \mathbin{\psi} \bar{1}_{i j}$

or substituting for $1$ a definition of it,

(1) $\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{i} \Pi_{j} \Pi_{k} \Pi_{\beta} \bar{Y}_{\alpha} \mathbin{\psi} \bar{r}_{\alpha i k} \mathbin{\psi} \bar{r}_{\alpha j k} \mathbin{\psi} \bar{r}_{\beta i j} \mathbin{\psi} r_{\beta j i}$.
