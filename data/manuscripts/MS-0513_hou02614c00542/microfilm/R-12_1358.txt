In fact the whole rule of possibility about the last quantifier is pretty flimsy; for it allows exceptions and the exceptions occur. Thus,
$$\Sigma_{i} \Pi_{j} \Sigma_{\alpha} \ q_{\alpha i} \cdot \overline{q}_{\alpha j} + \overline{q}_{\alpha i} \cdot q_{\alpha j}$$
is evidently not true; for if $j$ be identified with $i$ it reduces to
$$\Sigma_{i} \Sigma_{\alpha} \ q_{\alpha i} \cdot \overline{q}_{\alpha i}$$
Consequently its negative is true
$$\Pi_{i} \Sigma_{j} \Pi_{\alpha} \ q_{\alpha i} \cdot q_{\alpha j} + \overline{q}_{\alpha i} \cdot \overline{q}_{\alpha j} \ .$$
Still, the general rule holds. The proposition that is not true is so only because of a special case, and the other is true only by virtue of a special case. It is the $\Pi$ in the former which makes it false, and the $\Sigma$ in the latter which makes it true. The former is false because the $\Pi$ enables us to reduce it to [del]a[/del] contradiction. The latter is true because a suitable choice of the index of $\Sigma$ reduces it to the form $A + \overline{A}$. The same remarks apply to
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{\beta} \Pi_{i} \ q_{\alpha i} \cdot q_{\beta i} + \overline{q}_{\alpha i} \cdot \overline{q}_{\beta i} \ .$$
But let us take another example. The proposition
$$\Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{\beta} \Sigma_{i} \ q_{\alpha i} \cdot q_{\beta i} + \overline{q}_{\alpha i} \cdot \overline{q}_{\beta i}$$
is not [del]false[/del] [ins]true[/ins], because every character has a negative, — which is as much as to say that the proposition is [del]not[/del] false "because" its direct denial is true, [del]namely,[/del] [ins]that[/ins]
$$\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{\beta} \Pi_{i} \ (\overline{q}_{\alpha i} + \overline{q}_{\beta i}) \cdot (q_{\alpha i} + q_{\beta i}) \ .$$
But after all, it is only a very special [ins]and peculiar[/ins] [del]case which[/del] case which makes the latter true and the former false. Generally speaking, it is the other way.

Thus we find ourselves introduced at the very outset of the logic of mathematics to that peculiar doctrine which so pervades mathematics of the "general case," which may sometimes become an exceedingly exceptional case.
