[u]2[sup]nd[/sup] Step.[/u]

Into the product thus obtained, it may be necessary to multiply
propositions expressing general properties of the special universes
referred to. This will be the second step.

For example, if there are indices denoting characters, it may be
necessary to take account of the fact that any [ins]collection[/ins] [del]set[/del] of objects what-
ever have [del]all[/del] [ins]some common[/ins] character that is possessed by nothing else.

The expression of such a proposition may require a kind of
quantifiers not yet considered. These are of two varieties. In the
row of quantifiers a quantifier or series of quantifiers may be en-
closed in parentheses to mean that they can be repeated any number
of times, or that there may be an inenumerable collection of such
quantifiers, without producing falsity. Thus,

[del]$\Sigma$[/del] $(\Pi_i) \Sigma_\alpha \Pi_i q_{\alpha i} \cdot (\Pi_j \Sigma_i [del]l_{ij} +[/del] \bar{q}_{\alpha j})$

means to express the truth of any such proposition as

$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Pi_k \Sigma_\alpha q_{\alpha i} \cdot q_{\alpha j} \cdot q_{\alpha k} \cdot (\Pi_x \bar{l}_{ix} + \bar{l}_{jx} + \bar{l}_{kx} + \bar{q}_{\alpha x})$

where, of course, the $\Pi_x$ may be written directly after the $\Sigma_\alpha$.
Or we may imitate Schröder in writing a letter vertically under a
$\Pi$ or $\Sigma$ to mean, in the former case that any [del]logical[/del] predicate or
in the latter that some predicate, may be substituted for that letter
throughout. Thus

$\Pi_{\underline{u}} \Sigma_\alpha \Pi_x \Pi_y (\bar{u}_x + q_{\alpha x}) \cdot (u_y + \bar{q}_{\alpha y})$

will mean, for example, that substituting for $u$ $\Pi_i \Pi_j l_i + l_j$, it is true that

$\Pi_i \Pi_j \Sigma_\alpha \Pi_x \Pi_y (\bar{l}_{ix} \cdot \bar{l}_{jx} + q_{\alpha x}) \cdot (l_{iy} + l_{jy} + \bar{q}_{\alpha y})$.

It is easy to imagine other signs of the same general description.
