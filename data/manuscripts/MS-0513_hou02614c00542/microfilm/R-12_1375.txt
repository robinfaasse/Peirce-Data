Considering collections, the [u]sum[/u] of two quantities A and B, is that
which in the [ins]definitions of[/ins] [del]general form of[/del] A and B, allows their common part to stand
and [del]removes[/del] [ins]that[/ins] which distinguishes A from B, while retaining what
distinguishes A and B from other things. Thus, if A is the collection
of P and Q and B is the collection of U, V, and W. A+B is the
collection of P, Q, U, V, W. If the order of arrangement is essential
in the collections A and B, it will likewise be so of course in A+B.
For [u]non-relative logic[/u], or the logic of [u]monadic terms[/u], no other
algebraical operation but this is required.

The [u]product[/u] of two collections is the collection of pairs, [ins]each[/ins] formed
out of a unit of one factor joined to a unit of the other factor.
Thus, if A = P + Q and B = U + V + W
A x B = (PU) + (PV) + (PW) + (QU) + (QV) + (QW).

with reference to addition. Dyadic logic requires such an opera-
