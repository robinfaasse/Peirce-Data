$$I_1 D_t^2 \varphi_1 + \Big\{ \frac{I_1}{I_1+I_2} (\gamma_1-\gamma_2) - \frac{I_1}{(I_1+I_2)^3} \frac{(I_2\gamma_1+I_1\gamma_2)^2}{\mathcal{E}} \Big\} \varphi_1 = 0$$

Subtract this from the first equation

$$I_1 D_t^2 \varphi_1 = -\gamma_1 \varphi_1 - \mathcal{E}(\varphi_1-\varphi_2)$$

and we have

$$\frac{I_1}{I_1+I_2} \Big\{ (\gamma_1-\gamma_2) - \frac{(I_2\gamma_1+I_1\gamma_2)^2}{(I_1+I_2)^2 \mathcal{E}} \Big\} \varphi_1 = (\mathcal{E}+\gamma_1) \varphi_1 - \mathcal{E}\varphi_2$$

or

$$\varphi_2-\varphi_1 = \frac{1}{\mathcal{E}} \Big\{ \frac{I_2\gamma_1+I_1\gamma_2}{I_1+I_2} \mathcal{E} + \frac{I_1}{(I_1+I_2)^2} \frac{(I_2\gamma_1+I_1\gamma_2)^2}{\mathcal{E}} \Big\} \varphi_1$$

This shows that, except for a [del]local[/del] motion which may be set up, the upper point will always be a little more displaced than the lower in a fixed ratio.
