The Symposium refers to the partition of Mantinea, [date]385 B.C.[/date],
as recent. Consequently, [date]385[/date] B.C. is a probable date for
this dialogue.

The Theaetetus begins by [del]Socrates[/del] [ins]speaking of[/ins] Theaetetus as having
been wounded in the battle at Corinth. This would naturally
be understood to refer to the celebrated battle of Corinth,
[del]394[/del] B.C., where it is not unlikely that Plato himself
may have served, [ins]during[/ins] except for a year or two after [date]369 B.C.[/date],
when there was another fight near there. But in my opinion
the anachronism [ins]of representing Socrates as alive[/ins] would be too prominent and violent for
the latter date. It is far very different from a passing allusion,
where an anachronism merely provokes a smile; and
even for the great battle, it would only so long years after.
I do not, therefore, think that we have any indication of the
date of the Theaetetus further than that it must have been
written at least as late as [date]380 B.C.[/date], and before [date]368 B.C.[/date]
