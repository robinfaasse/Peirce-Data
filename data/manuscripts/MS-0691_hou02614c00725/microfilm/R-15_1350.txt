Kant, in particular, said that all deductive reasoning is
merely explicative, adding nothing to our knowledge and
but only showing what is implicitly involved in assertions al-
ready made. But there is a grand misunderstanding here.

Deductions are of two kinds, which we may call the corollarial
and the theorematic. Taking the thesis, or proposition to be
proved, and carefully analyzing precisely what it involves,
