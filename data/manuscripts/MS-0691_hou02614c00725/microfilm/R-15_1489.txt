The [u]Euthydemus[/u] is now [del]gener[/del] [ins]dated[/ins] by all critics [u]390 B.C.[/u], or not much later, for various reasons, of which the most definite seems to me to be its connection with the first oration of Isocrates upon the Sophists.

The [u]Cratylus[/u] contains a reference to the early closing of the gates of Aegina, a fact which would hardly be recalled by Plato long after his forced stay there in 387 B.C. We must put 386 or 385 B.C. as its almost certain date.

The [u]Symposium[/u] refers to the partition of Mantinea as a recent event. But that took place in [u]385 B.C.[/u]; so that the dialogue must have been written 385 or 384 B.C.

The [u]Theaetetus[/u] opens with a conversation between Socrates and others concerning a young man, one Theaetetus, who has been grievously wounded in the battle of Corinth. Now the Battle of Corinth took place [u]394 B.C.[/u], five years after the death of Socrates.
