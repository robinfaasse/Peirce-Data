The mere fact that abduction is our forlorn hope, that there is no other possible way in which positive truth can ever be ascertained would, in itself, suffice to justify the proceeding. But, in addition to that, experience shows that when it is carried on in an enlightened way, not very many hypotheses have to be tried before one is found which [del]other[/del] [ins]induction[/ins] proves is of high value. This shows that the human mind has a positive faculty for divining the truth, — not so great as to make it usually right but great enough to make it not very infrequently approximately right. In this respect, as well as in some others, abduction differs from deduction and induction [del]in that its validity rests, in part, upon experience.[/del]
