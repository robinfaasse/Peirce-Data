plainly is [del]to[/del] the discovery of Hippocrates of Chios
that certain [del]monilonees[/del] lines, or figures bounded by
two arcs of circles were capable of being squared; and Aris-
totle plainly means that this fact justifies a hope that the
circle can be squared. We know very well that that inference
was made; and it is a very natural one. Unfortunately, this
[del]inferen[/del] is the inference of a major premise, not of a minor
premise; namely, of the major premise of this syllogism:
Whatever is bounded by circular arcs can be squared;
The lunes of Hippocrates are bounded by circular arcs;
$\therefore$ The lunes of Hippocrates can be squared.
