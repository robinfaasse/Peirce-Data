there is no other possible way in which positive truth can
ever be ascertained would, in itself, suffice to justify the
proceeding. But, in addition to that, experience shows that
when it is carried on in an enlightened way, not very
many hypotheses have to be tried before one is found
which [del]in[/del] induction proves is of high value. This shows
that the human mind has a positive faculty for divining
the truth, - not so great as to make it usually right but
great enough to make it not very infrequently approximately
right. In this respect, as well as in some others, abduction [ins]and induction[/ins] differs [del]from deduction
and induction[/del] in that its validity rests, in part, upon experience.
