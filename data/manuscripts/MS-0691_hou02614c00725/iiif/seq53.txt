This deduction will be of one or other of two kinds,
namely, of that sort of reasoning by which we establish
corollaries, or by that sort of reasoning by which we
establish major theorems. It is true that no exact distinc-
tion exists between a corollary and a theorem. Confining
ourselves to the first book of the elements, [ins]for[/ins] which alone the
logic is very carefully elaborated, the theorems are those
steps which seemed to a mathematician of high powers
to require separate attention, while the corollaries are
deductions easy enough to be within the powers of
inferior commentators. [ins]Some of the theorems go no deeper than the corollaries.[/ins] Still, we all feel the difference
between one of the great steps and a corollary. The
corollary requires only careful analytic examination
[del]of the[/del] premisses in hand. The great steps involve invention.
