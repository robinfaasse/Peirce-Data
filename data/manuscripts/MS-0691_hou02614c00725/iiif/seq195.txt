The Euthydemus is now [del]genera[/del] [ins]dated[/ins] by all critics 390 B.C., or
not much later, for various reasons, of which the most de-
finite seems to me to be its connection with the first ora-
tion of Isocrates upon the Sophists.
The [u]Cratylus[/u] contains a reference to the early closing of
the gates of Aegina, a fact which would hardly be
recalled by Plato long after his forced stay there in
387 B.C. We must put 386 or 385 B.C. as its almost
certain date.
The [u]Symposium[/u] refers to the partition of Mantinea as a
recent event. But that took place in 385 B.C.; so that
the dialogue must have been written 385 or 384 B.C.
The [u]Theaetetus[/u] [del]begins[/del] opens with a conversation be-
tween Socrates and others concerning a young man, one
Theaetetus, who had been grievously wounded in the
battle of Corinth. Now the battle of Corinth took
place 394 B.C., five years after the death of Socrates.
