The Parmenides seems to me to be very obviously the reply 
of Plato to the criticisms of his new pupil Aristotle upon the
doctrine of ideas. This, of course, is not a new idea; but such is
the wonderful obtuseness of critics that it is not universally
accepted. It gives the very same objections that are urged in
Aristotle's Metaphysics; and elicits them from the mouth of 
whom? Of Aristotle! Only since Socrates has necessarily
to be introduced as the defender of the doctrine that had
been put forth in his name, it had to be another obscure
