The M’s are as few as the sum of the X’s and Y’s
[del]The sum of the Y and Z’s form a sum as small as the N’s[/del]
The [del]X’s and[/del] The N’s are as many as the sum of the Y’s and Z’s
The P’s are as many as the sum of the X’s and N’s.
That is to say there are eight relations, ε, ζ, η, θ, ι, κ, λ, μ such
that every I is [del]either[/del] [ins]ε[/ins] to an M to which no other I is ε ([ins]I[/ins]
thus abbreviate rather than to say ‘every I stands in the relation
ε to an M to which no other I stands in that relation’) or
is ζ to a Z to which no other I is ζ. Every M is either η to
an X to which no other M is η or is θ to a Y to which no
other M is θ. Every Y is [del]not[/del] ι to some N to which no other
Y is ι and to which no Z is κ, while every Z is κ to an
N to which no other Z is κ and to which no Y is ι. Every
X is λ to some P to which no other X is λ and to which
no N is μ, while every N is μ to some P to which no
other N is μ and to which no X is λ. Then it follows
that every I [del]is either as ε to and η to a λ[/del] [ins]stands to some P[/ins] or ε to a θ to a ν to μ etc.
