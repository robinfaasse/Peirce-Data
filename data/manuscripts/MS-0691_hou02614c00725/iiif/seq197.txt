The Theatetus begins by [ins]Socrates[/ins] speaking of Theaetetus as having
been wounded in the battle at Corinth. This would naturally
be understood to refer to the celebrated battle of Corinth,
394 B.C., where it is not unlikely that Plato himself
may have served, except [ins]during[/ins] for a year or two after 368 B.C.,
when there was another fight near there. But in my opinion
the anachronism [ins]of representing Socrates as alive[/ins] would be too prominent and violent for
the latter date. It is [del]far[/del] very different from a passing allusion,
where an anachronism merely provokes a smile; and
even for the great battle, it would only do long years after.
I do not, therefore, think that we have any indication of the
date of the Theatetus further than that it must have been
written, at least, as late at 380 B.C. and before 368 B.C.
