Given 4 endless straight lines, no two
complanar, how many endless straight
lines can cut each all of these four?

The phrase "endless straight line" being
unwieldy, let us replace it by a word
having no more letters than that phrase
has of words; namely, ray.

Let the four given rays be (a), (b), (c), (d).
Take any point [b] on (b). Then there will
be one plane, and only one, that will contain
[b] and the whole of (a). Call a sheet lying
in this plane {A}. Let {A} rotate about
(a) as an axis. Then in every position of {A}
it will be cut by (b) in one point and in
only one. But since every point of (b)
is [del]at by some[/del] lies in some plane through (a)
and in one only, it follows that when {A}
turns round (a), the point of intersection with
(b) will move along (b) without ever turning
back and will pass over the entire length
of (b). It will return to its original position
of (b). It will return to its original position
on (b) and in space when the sheet {A} has
turned through 180°, when it will be on the
other side of (a) on that sheet.
