You wake up in the morning and find the
wooden pail of water in your room has
a thin lamina of ice over it. You
break it, but still a piece of it sticks
to the side of the pail. In this condition
quite as perceptible, or more so, than the
four substances, wood, water, ice, and air,
are the six surfaces, of which one is
wood-water, as much one as the other,
another is [del]wood-ice[/del]; a third, water-ice,
the fourth, fifth, and sixth are wood-air,
water-air, ice-air. These [ins]six[/ins] [del]four[/del] surfaces
come together three by three in four
lines; of which one is wood-water-ice,
another is wood-water-air, another is
wood-ice-air, and the fourth water-ice-air.
These four lines all meet at both
ends in two points which are both
wood-water-ice-air. These points
are just as real as anything can be.
