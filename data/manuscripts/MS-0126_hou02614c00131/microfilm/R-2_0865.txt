A [u]topical singularity[/u] of a place (call
this place A) is a place (call it [u]a[/u]) [ins]of fewer dimensions than A has[/ins] forming
a part of the former, A, such that the number
of ways in which an object filling the par-
tial place, [u]a[/u], can entirely and instantane-
ously quit it, [u]a[/u], without quitting the whole
place, A, is different from the number of
ways in which the same object could
quit any of the other places which it
comes to fill after quitting the partial
place, [u]a[/u]. Thus, a point of branching of
a line can be totally and instantaneously
quit by a point occupying it, in [del]three[/del] as
many ways as there are branches of the
line along which it might start to move.
But as soon as it has left that point it will
be at a point that it can only quit by
moving on or by turning back. Therefore
