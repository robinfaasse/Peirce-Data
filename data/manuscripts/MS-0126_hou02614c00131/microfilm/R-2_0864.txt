A [u]filamentous object[/u], or [u]movable line-figure[/u], is a movable object which at any one instant has a part in every part of a fixed line-figure while every part of it is in some part of this fixed line-figure.

By the [u]chorisis[/u], [ins]or chorisy,[/ins] of a place, or the number of [u]separate parts[/u] of it, is meant the number of parts such that a particle could not, in a lapse of time at every instant of which it was in a single fixed point, be at one instant in one of these parts of the place and at another in another.

For if the particle were to make an instantaneous leap from one separate part to another, at that instant it must be either in both parts or in neither.

A line-figure whose chorisis is one is called a [u]line[/u]. A filamentous object of chorisis one is called a [u]filament[/u].
