And as the [del]rule[/del] [ins]result[/ins] agrees with the case in quantity, the denial of the result must agree with the denial of the case in quantity; that is the second proposition must be universal if the conclusion is universal. These principles will be plainer, if we take each mood of the first figure separately. First then, we have [ins]$\begin{array}{c} \text{A} \\ \text{A} \\ \text{A} \end{array}$[/ins] [del]corresponding[/del] to this is a mood of the second figure. The rule asserted is the first proposition in both first & second figures; the first A therefore will be repeated as the first proposition in the second figure. The second proposition in the first figure is the assertion of the case; while in the second figure the denial of the case is the third proposition. And the result which is the third proposition of the first figure, being denied becomes the second proposition of the second figure. So that to get the second figure from the first we transpose the second & third propositions & deny them both. I call that contraposing these propositions [ins]$\begin{array}{c} \text{A} - \text{A} \\ \text{A} \text{X} \end{array}$[/ins] Now if we deny A - if we deny All X is Y - we say Some X is not Y - which is O. The denial of A then is O. So we have $\begin{array}{c} \text{A} & \text{A} \\ \text{A} & \text{O} \\ \text{O} & \end{array}$. In the same way from $\begin{array}{c} \text{A} \\ \text{I} \\ \text{I} \end{array}$ we have $\begin{array}{c} \text{A} & \text{A} \\ \text{I} & \text{X} \\ & \end{array}$; the denial of I - or Some X is Y - is No X is Y - that is E. So we get $\begin{array}{c} \text{E} \\ \text{A} \\ \text{E} \end{array}$. From $\begin{array}{c} \text{E} & - & \text{E} \\ \text{A} & & \text{A} \\ \text{E} & & \text{O} \end{array}$ and from $\begin{array}{c} \text{E} & \text{E} \\ \text{I} & \text{A} \\ \text{O} & \text{O} \end{array}$. These therefore are the moods of the second figure.

Now we will take up another figure
Some rational beings are not free agents
All rational being are men
$\therefore$ Some men are not free agents.
This is the third & last figure. Here, we begin, by denying the result of the rule in the case in hand; but we assert that the case comes under the rule. If follows that the rule is not true. That is we reason from the
Denial of result
& Assertion of case
to Denial of rule

[pn]3/4[/pn] So that we have contraposed the conclusion with the other premise - not contraposed in the second figure. In this figure as before the rule is universal, & therefore the denial of the rule is particular. the case is affirmative. And the denial of the result agrees with the [ins]denial of the[/ins] rule in quality and disagrees with the case in quantity. So we get [ins]$\begin{array}{c} \text{O} & \text{A} & \text{O} \\ \text{A} & \text{X} & \text{A} \\ & \text{O} & \end{array}$[/ins]
