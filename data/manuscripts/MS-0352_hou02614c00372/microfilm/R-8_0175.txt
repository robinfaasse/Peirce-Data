I shall now take up another syllogism, quite
different from those we have considered:

[u]All men are free-agents[/u]
[u]Some rational creatures are not free-agents[/u]
∴ [u]Some rational creatures are not men.[/u]

This is, plainly, a good argument. It begins as the
first did with asserting a rule. But, then, instead of
going on to subsume a case under that rule, it denies
the operation of a rule in a particular case; the conse-
quence is that it must be denied - as it is in the conclu-
sion here - that that case comes under the rule.

This is called the second figure of syllogism. Here as
in the first figure the rule is universal; the case affirma-
tive and the result agrees with the rule in quality & with the
case in quantity. Only, as we proceed from the
Assertion of Rule
& Denial of Result
to Denial of Case

the first proposition is universal. As for the third, since
a case is affirmative, the denial of a case is negative,
therefore this conclusion must be negative, as it is
in the example on the board. And as the result agrees
with rule in quality, the denial of the result must dis-
agree with the rule in quality; that is the 2[ins]nd[/ins] proposition
must be negative if the first is affirmative & vice versa.
