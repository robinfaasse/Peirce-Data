He never was able to put aside a puzzle
until he had completely resolved it. Early in his studies
of Mars he obtained a theory which so accurately
represented its heliocentric longitudes, that he ever
after called it his "vicarious theory," inasmuch as
it obviated the necessity of reverting to the obser-
vations. It saved the appearances as far as helio-
centric longitudes went; and that would have
satisfied many an astronomer. But Keppler could
not be satisfied, since the theory did not agree with the
latitudes nor geocentric longitudes; and by far
the greater part of his labor came after he had
obtained this vicarious hypothesis.
