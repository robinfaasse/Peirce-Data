The following are my notions of ancient
Greek pronunciation.

[u]ζ[/u] must have had a sound much like
English [u]j[/u], but approximating to [u]dy[/u]

The rough breathing must have been a
strong Spanish aspiration, much like [u]yh[/u]
which I write as expressing something
related to [u]y[/u] as [u]wh[/u] is related to [u]w[/u].

The rough mutes, φ, θ, χ must have
been spirants with some explosive cha-
racter. That is, θ had not the smooth sound
of [del]the[/del] in [u]thin[/u], but approximated to a
true aspirate t'all, and so with the others.

The middle mutes, β, γ, δ must have
been slightly spirant as well as voiced. That is
they [ins]were often spirant and[/ins] never so abrupt as our b, g, d. More [del]going[/del]

Consonants coming together must have
been more separated than as we pronounce
them. Otherwise they would not decidedly
lengthen the syllable. Especially ρ, λ, μ, ν
with a mute must have been pronounced as
some English do, so as almost to add a syllable,
as comic writers write "teremble"

The τ was probably a dental, like Italian [u]t[/u].

As for the vowels [u]α[/u] was like French [u]a[/u], less
open than [u]ā[/u].
