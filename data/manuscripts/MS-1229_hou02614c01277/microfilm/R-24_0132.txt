5 [u]εἷς[/u] is not a true genitive, but is an adverbial form of [u]ἕς[/u]
resembling a genitive. It became a preposition in
historical times; so that we know how it grew to be such;
and the chief lesson of its history is that we cannot
often expect to guess the mode of such evolutions,
any more than of any other, if it was first a genitive
of which, it became and always remained an adverb
'just as'. It was then used before prepositions, nearly in the
sense of 'just as if', though without negative implication.
Now the Acc was almost sufficient of itself, as expressing
the object designed to be affective affected, to express the
person to whom a designed motion was intended to meet.

[u]Principal Uses of the Prepositions in Attic Prose.[/u]
1. [u]ξύν[/u], little used, its meaning essentially involves the D sense
'at', and only t. D. Meanings 'along with' ἐπαιδεύετο σὺν τῷ
ἀδελφῷ X; 'aided by' σὺν τινι μάχεσθαι X. 'with belongings' σὺν
παντὶ προσιόναι X 'with concurrent circumstances' σὺν τῷ χρόνῳ
προϊόντι 'with necessary accompaniment' σὺν τῷ σῷ ἀγαθῷ
'by means of' ἡ τῶν φίλων κτῆσις ἔστιν οὐδαμῶς σὺν τῇ βίᾳ 'friends
are not gained by force' X. Attic pr. gen. uses [del]ὅπλων[/del] or μετὰ w. G.

2. [u]πλήν[/u] being essentially of comparative meaning, only t. G.
Means 'more than' or 'save'.
