[u]To rationality[/u]. What is essential is that
[del]our[/del] all our cognitions should be
gathered into a unity and that our
actions should proceed from the
entirety of our knowledge. Because our
thought is only imperfectly brought to
unity, it requires effort to collect
it, and it requires a watchful
eye to be directed to the imperfec-
tions of this unity. But were we so
happily constituted that we should al-
ways without reflection completely
assimilate everything we learned, so
as to take due account of it in every
act, we might well be spared the
trouble of reflecting; and we should
be only the more rational if we could
thus behave with intelligence by the
first intention of the mind, without
reflection, — and knowing no more
of what was going on in our mind
than a healthy man does of what is
going on in his stomach —
