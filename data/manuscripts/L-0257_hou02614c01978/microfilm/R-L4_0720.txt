**LOWELL INSTITUTE,**
**BOSTON, MASS., U. S. A.**

**A. LAWRENCE LOWELL, [u]Trustee[/u].**
**WILLIAM T. SEDGWICK, [u]Curator[/u].**

[date]Dec. 1, 1903[/date].

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

As I told you yesterday over the telephone I am obliged to leave on Wednesday for Chicago and if there is anything I can do for you before leaving, pray call me up by telephone and let me know.

The young man who is doing your diagrams is Mr. W. C. Lounsbury, who can generally be reached by my telephone, Back Bay 1685. I think you will like his work and he will have both the blackboard work and the diagrams ready for you on Thursday evening. I am sorry that my cold prevented me from going to your lecture last night but I hope that everything went well.

Allow me to congratulate you on the marked success of your lectures. Considering the nature of the subject I think this has been quite remarkable and that you have every reason to feel satisfied with the size and quality of the audience. You certainly have a very pleasant style and a good voice, and knowing, as you do, how to express your ideas, your lectures are naturally clear and acceptable.

Sincerely yours,
W. T. Sedgwick
Curator.

Professor C. S. Peirce,
6 Prescott Hall,
Cambridge, Mass.
