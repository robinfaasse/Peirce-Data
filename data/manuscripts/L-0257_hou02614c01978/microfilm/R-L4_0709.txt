LOWELL INSTITUTE,
BOSTON, MASS., U. S. A.

A. LAWRENCE LOWELL, [u]Trustee[/u].
WILLIAM T. SEDGWICK, [u]Curator[/u].

Oct. 30, 1903.

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

I have just received your letter and at almost the same time the express parcel containing copy for you syllabus. I confess that I am somewhat appalled at the large amount of material which you sent and I am wondering if the word "syllabus" will accurately describe it. At the same time I shall take steps immediately to put it in the hands of an inexpensive printer in order to make the printing go as far as our fifty dollars will allow. I have had pleasure in paying the express charges and I am glad to know that you will make your own diagrams, this procedure being almost always the most satisfactory for lecturers.

I am leaving the city for the west again in a day or two but my address will always be simply Lowell Institute or better 491 Boylston Street. Please do [u]not[/u] send things to 20 Chestnut Street as I moved away from there some three years ago and am now living in Brookline.

I am hoping to have very soon your list of topics and all details in regard to the way in which you wish your own name to appear upon tickets, etc. I enclose herewith some specimen tickets for your guidance.

Cordially yours,
W. T. Sedgwick.
Curator.

How many copies of the syllabus will you want printed?

Professor C. S. Peirce,
Milford, Penn.
