[u]LOWELL INSTITUTE LECTURES.[/u]

It is expected that the lectures written, or prepared, for the Lowell Institute, will be first delivered there, and not be repeated elsewhere in Boston until the course has been completed at the Institute.

The lecture should occupy exactly an hour in delivery.

There is ample space (say, fifteen feet in height by forty feet in width) on the wall in rear of the platform for display of maps, diagrams, etc. These can be put up on the day or afternoon of the lecture, an assistant being at call. The platform itself is large enough for extra tables and all requisite apparatus.

When necessary, the Institute provides a stereopticon, but the lecturer should furnish the requisite "slides."

As a rule, when stereopticon views are given, it should be at the close of the lecture.
