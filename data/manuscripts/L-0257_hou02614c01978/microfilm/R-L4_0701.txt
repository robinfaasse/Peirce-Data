Boston 8th July 91

Professor Charles S. Peirce.

My dear Sir,

I am much concerned
at what you tell me of your pecuniary
difficulties. There is no reason however
why you should suffer from this cause,
as I should not hesitate to advance a
part of the money for the lectures, if
you would let me know how much you
would require to secure the proper
leisure for their preparation.

Such a course as you propose to
give would not only do credit to
yourself & to the Institute, but might after-
wards be published upon favorable terms,
and I cannot but think that it would
be a mistake & a misfortune if it were
to be given up.

I shall ask you to reply to this note
as soon as possible, as the time is getting
short, & if I must supply your place, I
must do so at once. I trust however that
this may not be necessary, but that
the way I have suggested may remove all
difficulties, so that we may yet have the
pleasure we had both anticipated from
this connection.

I remain

Yours very truly

Augustus Lowell.
