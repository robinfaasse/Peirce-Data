Chapter I. A Talk on Continuity.

The vintage was in progress at the ancient Ivy Manor Farm in Jarmen's Gap. From where the boys were seated in an grove of stately Black Walnuts, [ins]munching a couple of Albemarle pippins,[/ins] they could look down into the wine-press yard to and from which there were interrupted processions of all available hands bringing down baskets of grapes from the mountain-side. The boys were a white one of fourteen, bearing the illustrious name of Thomas J. Jefferson, and his full-black servant and friend, Euclid Easy, of nineteen. They had ridden all the way from Weyer Cave, and preferred to stop to eat their dinner in the quiet grove rather than to go on to the house that was nearly in sight.

"We are right on the boundary between Albemarle county and Augusta county," said Tom.

"There is the stone," said Euclid, "and you must be lying across the boundary line."
