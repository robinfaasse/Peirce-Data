Tremezzo, Lago di Como
[date]April 29 1902[/date]

Dear Sir:

Your letter of Feb. 13 was only recently forwarded to me but was I hope and judge answered by the Reference Librarian, as certain memoranda on the back seem to indicate. I trust there was no delay in sending you any books that you may have wanted.

I beg to thank you for the considerate lightness of touch with which you allude to the [del]obvious[/del] [ins]crying[/ins] shortcomings of my little book. The Nation's review was rather more severe. I hardly think the book are of a kind every likely to attain the second edition which you suggest but the subject is one which from necessity I must follow up in some form and I shall take great pains to improve any known shortcomings of preparation. I shall especially look for your book with the greatest of interest and make a careful study of it.

It is not quite clear to me whether you and the Nation refer to lack of grip on the sequence of argument, to neglect of logical form or to real or seeming unfamiliarity with the history of logical thought as connected with the problem but I shall hope to improve in all these respects before publishing again in any form. It was only after various refusals that I decided to give the lectures when I did and if I were [del]re[/del] publishing it would certainly be on a method which allowed reference to the Century Dict Classification and the essay of Agassiz. The reason for the omission, was that after a good deal of hesitation and in the impossibility of making clean cut and thorough work of my many memoranda on classification [del]of special[/del] [ins]of special[/ins] subjects I left out all such matters and carelessly included the one under caption of Science as limited to natural science. I should be very sorry indeed to fail to express a high measure of appreciation for the Century articles as all aspects of the subject for they have been a constant and prime source.

I note your suggestion as to method of Index and so far from considering it impertinent beg to thank you most cordially for it and for all suggestions.

Your letter came to me at Milan before I run up here for a ten day rest after a month of very grueling work on a troublesome palimpsest and I tried to find Burdins book at the Brera but it was not there. I will try again at Paris or London and will write of my final passage.

While I am anxious not to take myself or my little book too seriously — a couple of lectures for a Library School is all that it is — it represents the carefully matured (if rather hastily printed) result of considerable reading and thinking and I have rather hoped that men like yourself might find something just in some of the points of view.
