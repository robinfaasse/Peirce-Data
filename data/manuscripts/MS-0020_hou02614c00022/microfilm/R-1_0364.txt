LOGIC OF QUANTITY.

By C. S. Peirce.

[u]Art. 1. Definition 1.[/u] A [u]semi-correspondence[/u], $S_{imn}$, of one collection, [u]$m$[/u], to another, [u]$n$[/u], is the character of a relation, [u]$i$[/u], such that no object is in this relation to two different objects, but to every object of collection [u]$n$[/u] some object of collection [u]$m$[/u] is in relation [u]$i$[/u].
(1) $\Pi_i \Pi_m \Pi_n \Pi_e \Pi_f \Pi_g \Pi_s \Sigma_r \bar{S}_{imn} \dagger (\bar{r}_{ief} \dagger \bar{r}_{ieg} \dagger 1_{fg})(\bar{q}_{ns} \dagger q_{mr} r_{irs})$
(2) $\Pi_i \Sigma_m \Sigma_n \Sigma_e \Sigma_f \Sigma_g \Sigma_s \Pi_r r_{ief} \cdot r_{ieg} \cdot \bar{1}_{fg} \dagger q_{ns} \cdot (\bar{q}_{mr} \dagger \bar{r}_{irs}) \dagger S_{imn}$

[u]Corollary 1.[/u] The relation, $\{0\}$ of impossibility is a semi-correspondence of any collection to nothing considered as a collection.

[u]Corollary 2.[/u] The relation, $\{1\}$, of identity is a semicorrespondence of any collection to a collection included by it.

[u]Theorem 1.[/u] If [del]one[/del] [ins]a[/ins] collection, [u]$l$[/u], is in a semicorrespondence to a second, [u]$m$[/u], which is in a semicorrespondence to a third, [u]$n$[/u], then the first is in a semi-correspondence to the third.
