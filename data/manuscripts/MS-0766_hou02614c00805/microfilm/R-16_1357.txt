[u]Synopsis of the Discussion of the Ground of Induction.[/u]

[u]I.[/u]

The first and most obvious theory (when we approach the subject from the side of probability) is Laplace's [ins](virtually)[/ins] that the probability of an inductive conclusion can be calculated by inverse probabilities.

To that I make two objections:

1[sup]st[/sup], that [del]it[/del] is an idea founded on want of clear apprehension of the nature of probability, which signifies security in the long run. Since there is but one universe of which we have experience, it is absurd to speak of the probability that that universe is subject to a given law.

2[sup]nd[/sup], the calculation is founded upon [del]putting[/del] [ins]the assumption of[/ins] "equal possibilities". This is basing a conclusion on ignorance, which is absurd.
