are in English a number of vowel-sounds more or less similar to that with
which we all pronounce the word ‘drawl.’ Our ears are educated to distinguish
such sounds as the apprehension of a speaker’s meaning makes it necessary to
distinguish. We instinctively make excessively fine distinctions where the meaning
calls for them, while in other cases a great range of different vowels seem
[del]to us[/del] all the same. There is no [ins]other[/ins] region of the field of vowel-sounds where our
discriminations are so fine as in the neighborhood of this “long low back [del]round[/del]”
