Rules for spelling the long low back round vowel [u]au[/u].

I. Before [u]gh[/u], it is always [u]au[/u] or [u]ou[/u]. Namely, it is [u]ou[/u] in every Teutonic word in which it represents an original [u]o[/u] or [u]u[/u]; as in, [u]brought[/u], AS. bróht, [u]sought[/u] AS. sóht, [u]wrought[/u] AS. worht, [u]bought[/u] as. boht, [u]thought[/u] AS þohte or þúhte, [u]cough[/u] cognate with Dutch kuchen, Exception: [u]daughter[/u] AS. dóhtor.

It is [u]au[/u] in every other case before [u]gh[/u]; as [u]aught[/u] AS áwiht, [u]naught[/u] as náwiht, [u]taught[/u] AS tæhte, [u]naught[/u] AS ræhte, [u]caught[/u] cauzte, [u]fraught[/u] Swedish frakta, [u]haughty[/u] Fr hautain. Exception: [u]fought[/u] AS feaht

Cases in which the vowel is spelled [u]aw[/u].

(This is to be understood that [u]gh[/u] does not follow the letters so sounded.)

[del]2. The spelling is always [u]aw[/u] when an originally a [u]w[/u] followed the vowel; as,[/del]
[u]drawl[/u] from draw, [u]owl[/u] AS. ule, [u]jaw[/u] and [u]chaw[/u] AS. ceowan, [u]raw[/u] AS. hreaw, [u]thaw[/u] AS. þawan, [u]claw[/u] AS. clawu, [u]straw[/u] AS. streow, [u]paw[/u] Welsh pawen, [u]pawl[/u] Welsh pawl.

3. The spelling is always [u]aw[/u] when originally a Scandinavian [u]g[/u], [u]v[/u], [u]f[/u], or [u]b[/u], followed the vowel; as,
[u]bawl[/u] Icel. baula, [u]yawn[/u] (howl) Icel gaula, [u]craw[/u] Swedish kräfva, [u]crawl[/u] (creep) Icel krafla, [u]awkward[/u] Icel. öfg.
[del]On the contrary, if anger is spelled with an [u]a[/u] if followed by f, b, v, in Scandinavian, as in anger[/del]

4. The spelling is always [u]aw[/u] when originally a guttural or palatal followed the vowel; as,
[del]law[/del] [u]awe[/u] AS. ege, [u]haw[/u] (hedge) as. haga, [u]maw[/u] AS. maga, [u]saw[/u] (tool) AS. saga, [u]saw[/u] (say) AS. sagu, [u]gawky[/u] AS. geac, [u]dawn[/u] AS. dægian, [u]shaw[/u] AS. sceaga, [u]gnaw[/u] AS. gnagan, [u]daw[/u] cognate with OHG. taha, [u]hawk[/u] (peddle) ODutch hoeker, [u]awn[/u] Icel. ögn, [u]fawn[/u] (cringe) fagna, [u]flaw[/u] Swedish flaga, [u]hawk[/u] (clear the throat) W rwch.

The only exception I know of is [u]maudlin[/u] Fr. Magdeleine, and this is influenced by the name Maude. If the rule had said a non-Romanic guttural or gutteropalatal, this exception would be removed.

5. The spelling is always [u]aw[/u] when originally an [u]n[/u] followed the vowel, unless that [u]n[/u] is followed by a dental mute ([u]t[/u] or [u]d[/u]) whose sound is still preserved; as,
[u]brawl[/u] (dance) Fr. branle, [del]awning Fr. auvent, lawn (grass) Fr. lande[/del] [u]lawn[/u] (linen) Fr. linon, [u]pawn[/u] (pledge) Fr. pan [u]pawn[/u] (chess) Fr. paon, [u]tawny[/u] Fr. tanné, [u]fawn[/u] (deer) Fr. fan, faon, [u]prawn[/u] Prunmable Fr pren, Lat. perna, [u]awning[/u] Fr. auvent, [u]lawn[/u] (grass) Fr. lande, [u]spawn[/u] Fr. espandre Lat. expendere, [u]yawn[/u] AS. ginian
