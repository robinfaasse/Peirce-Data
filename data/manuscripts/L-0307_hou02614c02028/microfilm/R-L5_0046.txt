10 CHANNING PLACE,
CAMBRIDGE.

My dear Cousin Charles, -
Aunt Mary said that she had had a very nice affectionate letter from you, that she wished me to answer, - this is to say that her answer must be put off until she is stronger, for she has been ill again with Bronchitis with its concomitant of a severe cold, & now more weakness than usual, hardly anything else could be expected for two such sharp attacks in one winter to more than one person.
She still suffers from her [del]osfa[/del] [ins]asthma[/ins] to the head in touch with modern philosophy. -

Are you comfortable in the house, so far in the country? We seem to need all the conveniences of Suburban life to keep us going this severe weather, but the children enjoy the skating. We have moved to a little larger & much more convenient house since you were in Cambridge, where we can stay until late in the summer when it is necessary.

With kind remembrances to your wife, and affectionate greetings to you from us both

I am
Yours very sincerely
Helen L. Muirhead.

P.S. Aunt Mary is slowly but steadily improving.
