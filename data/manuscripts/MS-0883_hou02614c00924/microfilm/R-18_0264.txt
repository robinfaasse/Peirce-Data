Prof. Charles Sedgwick Minot sends an undated Second Report to the American Psychical Research Society on Experimental Psychology. It seems to refute almost completely the argument for thought transference drawn from the reproduction of diagrams of which so much [ins]has[/ins] been made in the reports of the English Society. Professor Minot distributed a large number of postcards, bearing this [del]ingenious[/del] ingenious request: "Please draw ten diagrams on this card, without receiving any suggestion from any other person, and add your name and address." [del]The number[/del] Answers to the number of 501 were received. The result was that 40 per cent of the persons drew circles, 34 per cent squares, 31 equilateral triangles, 25 crosses, 16 diamonds, etc. It follows that the chances of accidental coincidence turn out to be enormously greater than the English researchers had supposed; since the coincidences [ins]related[/ins] were for the most part precisely to such simple diagrams as are now found to be [del]made[/del] [ins]drawn[/ins] so often.
