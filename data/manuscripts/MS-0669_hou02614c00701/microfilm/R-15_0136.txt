[ins]Footnote to p1 of copy.[/ins] * Any dispute on this point must, I think, be a dispute about words. For all I mean is that if a diagram or model, or [del]a[/del] [ins]true[/ins] representation of any kind that [ins]should[/ins] any being could make, represent the [del]Copulate[/del] Premiss to be true, it would [u]ipso facto[/u] represent every Deductive Conclusion to be true, although, there being endlessly manifold, it could not [u]expressly[/u] represent this of every such conclusion, that is, it could not concentrate attention on them all; [del]although it would mean all that consistency would make it mean; and being an assertion, it would assent all that.[/del]

It is not words that are "asserted", but [ins]by way of[/ins] facts; [del]any dictionary[/del] [ins]to[/ins] ought [ins]to[/ins] say that. Consequently, "Cain killed Abel", "[del]Abel[/del] [ins]Abel was[/ins] killed [ins]by[/ins] Cain", and "The death of Abel was directly due to the intentional agency of Cain" are not three assertions, but only three forms [del]of[/del] [ins]by which[/ins] one and the same assertion may be clothed.
