The line figure may have isolated points, which are [del]lines[/del] [ins]points[/ins] of singularity $-2$. The destruction of one of these increases the singularity by $2$ while diminishing the chorisis by $1$. Hence, if the equation $S = 2(\kappa - \chi)$ did not hold before such destruction, neither would it do so afterwards. Let all such isolated points be destroyed.

Let every part of the line-figure consisting of a simple oval [fig]simple oval[/fig] be destroyed. Such a line has no singularity, its chorisis is $1$ and its cyclosis is $1$. Hence, if the equation $S = 2(\kappa - \chi)$ did not hold before such destruction, neither would it hold afterwards.

Let every part of the line figure consisting of a line with two extremities, but no other singularities, be destroyed. Such a line has $S = -2$ $\kappa = 0$ $\chi = 0$. Hence, if the equation $S = 2(\kappa - \chi)$ did not hold before such destruction, it would not hold afterwards.

If [del]there[/del] any of these collections are not finite, the equation is indeterminate. It is therefore not false even in that case.
