My dear Peirce,

I have nothing to do but
send this back to you; you will supply the
regrets and know them to be sincere. I should
have done so Tuesday, as I said on my postcard
but I wanted to take some advice in case etc.
I have tried the chapter on an expert whist-
& chess-player, given to solving puzzles, &
adding columns of four figures at one swoop
& so on; and he says it is hard for him
to follow. So I haven't tried the "young
person". I suppose in the upper air you in-
habit things seem pellucid which down here,
where unselfish interest in mental things is so
[del]the only[/del] rare, appear more or less opaque. It's
our misfortune, & perhaps fault, but anyhow
I think it would be difficult to see a substan-
tial commercial success in a thing requiring such

Sincerely yours, W. C. Brownell.
