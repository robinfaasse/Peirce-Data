The Meno is a supplement to the Protagoras, as was therefore [del]probably[/del] [ins]presumably[/ins] composed later: yet Plato here drags in by head and shoulders an allusion to the bribery of Ismenias, which took place 395 B.C. He had to introduce, as one of the characters of the dialogue, Anytus, the chief of the accusers of Socrates. Now Anytus had, ten years before the accusation of Socrates, been himself accused of corruption and only escaped by bribing the judges. According to Diogenes Laertius, he was afterwards banished for the part he took in the persecution of Socrates. [ins]He was of humble origin, being the son of a tanner.[/ins] In the Meno, Socrates says, "Here is Anytus, son of a rich and [del]clever[/del] [ins]astute[/ins] father, who became rich, not as a matter of course or by anybody's gift, as Ismenias the Theban has lately piled up the wealth of Polycrates, but by his own talent and industry."
