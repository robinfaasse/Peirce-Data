[u]ordinary[/u] interpretation reduces both to nonsense. The
first becomes,

Comprehension can be taught,
Virtue is comprehension;
<span style="padding-left: 20px;">∴ Virtue can be taught.</span>

In the first place, this is [del]a[/del] [ins]or very near done,[/ins] [u]petitio principii[/u], since there
is no way of proving that virtue is comprehension, except
by its being taught. In the next place, nobody in Aristotle's
time had ever used this absurd argument, nor had anybody
seriously doubted, what all experience, shows that virtue
can be taught. A very few ethical writers of modern times have
denied it; but it had never been denied then, except as a
temporary shift in debate.
