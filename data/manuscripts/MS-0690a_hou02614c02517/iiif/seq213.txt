The Protagoras appears to contain allusions to Xenophon’s
Memorabilia; but the date of that work is not known, further
than that it was some years after the death of Socrates, [del]say[/del] [ins]perhaps[/ins]
from 395 to 392 B.C. It was while it was fresh that it would be
likeliest to be noticed by Plato. Moreover the Protagoras introduces
Alcibiades as a disciple of Socrates; and in my opinion the Protagoras,
like the Apology, Crito, Euthyphro, and Charmides, [ins]if not some other dialogues,[/ins] is intended to be
[del]the[/del] history,—as strictly so as the Greeks understood history. Now Iso-
crates, in the Busiris says, rather pointedly, that nobody but Polycrates ever
asserted that Alcibiades had been a disciple of Socrates. This seems to me
to have been intended as a marked snub of the young sophist, Plato. Further
the Protagoras mentions the [del]the[/del] peltastic as a distinct corps of the army.
But [del]they[/del] [ins]it[/ins] only became so for the Athenian army somewhere between
393 and 391 B.C. Altogether, there seems to be an exceptional mass
of evidence to show that the Protagoras must have been written in
or about 392 B.C.
