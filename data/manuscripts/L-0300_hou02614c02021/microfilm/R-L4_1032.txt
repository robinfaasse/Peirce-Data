The problem to be solved is to determine; first, the moments in the stiffening truss, from which the chord strains can be deduced by dividing by depth of truss; and second, the shearing strains in the stiffening truss.

It will be assumed that the cable is in perfect equilibrium under dead load and that the stiffening truss is entirely unstrained by the dead load between points C and C'. The stiffening truss carries its own weight between B and C and between B' and C', supported only at A, B, A' and B', the portions AC and A' C' being treated as cantilevers. The strains to be calculated will be those produced by the moving load in the stiffening truss between the points C and C', to which must be added the effects on the extensions of this truss at each end. The moving load shall be assumed of any length or position necessary to produce maximum effect.
