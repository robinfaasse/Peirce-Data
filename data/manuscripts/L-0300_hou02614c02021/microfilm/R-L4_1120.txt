GEORGE S. MORISON
CIVIL ENGINEER
49 WALL STREET, NEW YORK

New York, August 12, 1901.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,

Milford, Penna.

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

I returned to the city late Saturday and have your letter of the 7th to-day. The matter of which I wrote to you has now taken a somewhat different shape. A diagram has been prepared by a young man named Woodard, who is in the employ of the Commission, which has completely solved the case. I have gone over this diagram myself and am entirely satisfied with it. He gets a differential equation which he cannot integrate, but he gets the curve by a series of tangents, the whole being very simple and satisfactory. I think it is probable that this is the same method that you would have followed.

I thank you for the interest you have taken in this, but I do not think it right to ask you to put any more time to it. While our Commission had a very large appropriation it is nearly all expended and this matter was really a matter of my own in which I was acting independently of the Commission because I felt a problem could be solved accurately which had only been solved in an imperfect way.

Very truly yours,
G. S. Morison
