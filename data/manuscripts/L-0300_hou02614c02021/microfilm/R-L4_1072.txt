Chicago, June 3, 1897.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,

108 West 89th Street, NEW YORK.

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

I have your letter of the 31st and am sorry to hear that you are in so much trouble; in one thing you certainly are to be congratulated and that is that Mrs. Peirce's life has apparently been saved. It certainly would be very unwise for you to take Mrs. Peirce to 84 Broad Street and there are places in New York where she could be cared for without real expense to you.

As regards the land, I understand your proposition to be that I purchase your 1145 acres at forty cents an acre, that is for four hundred sixty eight dollars ($468) and that of this I pay you in cash $368 and apply $100 on account of the $300 which you now owe me and for which I hold the Bierstadt picture as security. I assume, of course, that the land is entirely unincumbered.
