New York, July 9, 1895.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,
Milford, Pa.

My dear Sir:

I returned here today and have your letter of the 28th. I enclose memorandum showing that deposit was made on the 27th.

I do not feel certain that the plan which is reported as accepted for the New York & New Jersey bridge is likely to come to much; it certainly is all wrong both in appearance and design; it is one of those structures which is designed on the same basis that ordinary truss bridges are designed on, namely a geometrical diagram, failing to appreciate the main feature which must govern the design of any suspension bridge, that is its flexibility. I have had a perspective view of my design made which must at present be finished and I will send you a copy of it as soon as I have it reproduced.
