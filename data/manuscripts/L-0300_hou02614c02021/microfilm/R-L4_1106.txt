New York, April 11, 1899.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,
Milford, Penn.

My dear Mr. Peirce:-

I returned here last Tuesday and found your letter of March 2nd and your two letters of March 6th. The report was also here. I have examined the report but I must confess that I cannot follow the whole of it without reviewing and revising my own mathematical education. There are [del]two[/del] [ins]three[/ins] things which I think would render it more clear; [ins]one[/ins] is a diagram of the structure marked to correspond with the notations given in the report; the other would consist of two or three more pages to follow those already written and which would briefly summarize the method of applying the formulae.

Your note to page 44 is certainly very amusing, but it leads to the question as to what the word impracticability means; if a method is not impracticable which would require sixty million people to work for an immense number of centuries, how shall we determine the mathematical limits of practicability.?
