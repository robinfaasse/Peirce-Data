GEORGE S. MORISON
CIVIL ENGINEER
35 WALL STREET, NEW YORK

New York, [date]Jan. 17, 1899[/date]

MRS. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,
Milford, Penn.

My dear Mrs. Peirce:-

I have your letter of the 10th.

I have not yet succeeded in seeing my friend whom I wished to have buy the canary diamond; he seemed interested in it, but lives out of town, I expected him this week but he has not turned up. I will not however stop here but will press the matter a little further.

As regards water supply I have had some experience in this connection. A good windmill erected costs about $50, to which must be added the cost of the pipe you have to lay. Before deciding on putting in a windmill I should advise investigating the situation pretty carefully and see if you cannot get a gravity supply. The principal cost of a pipe line is the digging of the trench and laying the pipes and as you will not want to use this water in the winter you can lay your pipes on the surface of the ground, which reduces the cost enormously.

I am very glad to hear that Mr. Peirce is progressing with the suspension bridge work.

Very truly yours, [u]G. S. Morison[/u]
