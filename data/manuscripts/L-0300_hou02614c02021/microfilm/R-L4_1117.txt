GEORGE S. MORISON
CIVIL ENGINEER
49 WALL STREET, NEW YORK

New York, May 27, 1901.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,
Milford, Penn.

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

Your letter of the 15th reached me just as I was leaving town. I laid it aside to answer it on my return and overlooked it when I got back. I have no real excuse to offer for not replying earlier.

The stock of the St. Lawrence Power Company, both common and preferred, is still in the hands of Clark, Dodge & Company held on my account but as collateral for your account and you have an interest in it to the extent of any value beyond the indebtedness.

The Bierstadt picture is still in Mr. Stickney's office, though I have been thinking of removing it to this office. Its situation is entirely unchanged.

Just now I have comparatively little work of a scientific character and I do not see anything which I could throw into your hands. If I come across anything, either of my own or any other person's, I shall be very glad to remember you.

I am sorry to hear of Mrs. Peirce's illness; her former operation was so successful that a second one must be less appalling than to many people; we sometimes get this kind of consolation for our troubles. Will you please give her my regards.

Very truly yours,
G. S. Morison
