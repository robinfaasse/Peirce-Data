My dear Mr. Morison:

It seems odd about the box of insts. I had had it in Cambridge and left it there, the key being lost. I sent for it & my sister who sent it failed to say she had had a key made; so I assumed the same state of things.

Our water pipes froze under ground while the temperature was moderating & that gave me two days hard work. Reading your pamphlet took another. Besides, some grave doubts arose in my mind as to the most judicious way of treating the problem and that has lost me, I can't quite say how many days. But it wont be much longer.

Suppose the whole cost of the truss were put into cable, towers, & anchorages, would not that enable you to draw your cable so tight that no truss would be needed. Probably not. Then the real problem to be solved seems to be what is the [del]point[/del] [ins]degree[/ins] of stiffness where nothing would be gained or lost by an infinitesimal decrease of cost of the truss with the same money spent on cable, towers, and anchorages. I cannot solve that with such data as I have; but it seems I should endeavor to arrange my formulae so they should be useful to that end.

Yours very truly
C. S. Peirce
