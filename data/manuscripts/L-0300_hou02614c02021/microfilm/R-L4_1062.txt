Chicago, February 10, 1896.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,
74 West 53rd St., NEW YORK.

My dear Mr. Peirce:-

Your postal card has just reached me here. I am sorry to say that I am not likely to be in New York before the middle of April. I am making my plans for a trip to the City of Mexico, which I have intended to take for several years and now for the first time have a good opportunity. Please give my regards to Mrs. Peirce and tell her that I am very sorry that I am not to have the pleasure of calling on her.

As regards the completion of your report, I have been revising my report on the North River Bridge and putting it in the form of a paper on the general subject of suspension bridges of this magnitude. I shall have the paper in shape to be written out before I leave for Mexico and shall probably present it to the American Society of Civil Engineers immediately after my return. [del]In[/del] [ins]In thi[/ins] paper I have thought it best to reduce the moving load somewhat from the figures we last talked of and have fixed upon it as 12 000 lbs. per lineal foot for the whole bridge which is 6 000 lbs. for each truss and 3 000 lbs. for each cable; the two cables on each side in your calculations were treated as a single cable. The gross section of the chord of the stiffening truss remains as before, 6 000 square feet, and is estimated to have a modulus of elasticity of 30 000 000 pounds. The section of each cable remains as before, 750 square inches, or 1500 for the two cables and the modulus of elasticity should be taken at 28 000 000.
