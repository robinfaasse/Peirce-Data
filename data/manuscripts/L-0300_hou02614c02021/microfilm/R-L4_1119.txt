GEORGE S. MORISON
CIVIL ENGINEER
49 WALL STREET, NEW YORK

August 3, 1901.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,

Milford, Penna.

Dear Mr. Peirce:

On my return to this City I have your two letters of the 23rd and the 25th. The first shows that you were thinking the matter over in a way which is likely to lead to the desired results. The second, however, indicates something which is much more nearly what I should like. I think a diagram showing how the curves would run which would express the behavior of the lake, accompanied with an explanation of the method used in preparing it, would be the best possible form in which the result could be expressed.

Although I gave you the problem in a perfectly general form, it is an actual problem which has come up in the study of the control of the Chagres River at Panama. I have made an approximate actual solution myself, which has met my present preliminary needs. A theoretical study has been made by Professor Burr, but he has made certain assumptions which vitiate his conclusions, though it is impossible to say how great an error they introduce. While there is no great hurry about the matter, it would be a great satisfaction to me to have a general solution, the accuracy of which is unimpeachable and which could be used to measure the error in the other man's work.

Very truly yours,
G. S. Morison
