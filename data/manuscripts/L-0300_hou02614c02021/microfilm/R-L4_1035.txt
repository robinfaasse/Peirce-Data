GEORGE S. MORISON,
CIVIL ENGINEER,
35 WALL STREET, NEW YORK.
184 LA SALLE STREET, CHICAGO.

New York, March 26th, 1895.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,
MILFORD, PA.

Dear Sir:-

Your letter of the 22nd reached my office yesterday but I was at Philadelphia attending the launching of the St. Paul, which did not take place. I am very glad to hear that I may expect the specific answers about the end of next week, as I have reached a point in which I can make good use of them. Since I saw you I have been thinking of the possibility of using nickel steel in the chords of the stiffening truss, which would enable me to increase the strain on these chords quite materially. Nickel steel, however, is as yet a high priced article, the use of which should be limited as much as possible; I think it very likely that before this bridge is built this condition will have been essentially changed.

The deposit which you ask for was made on the 21st of March and I suppose you received my letter advising you that I would make it, immediately after receiving yours.

Very truly yours,
Geo. S. Morison
