GEORGE S. MORISON
CIVIL ENGINEER
35 WALL STREET, NEW YORK

New York, April 19, 1900.

MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,

Milford, Penn.

My dear Mr. Peirce:-

I arrived here yesterday from the Isthmus having been absent from the city since January 6th and in so inaccessible a region that I thought it best not to have any letters forwarded but those which contained a particular request to that effect; this explains why your letters of January 13th and February 11th have been so long unanswered. You certainly would be a very useful man either on McClure's Encyclopedia or as a examiner for degrees to be conferred by the Smithsonian.

So much time, however, has elapsed since your last letter has been written and I have been so far from obtaining any information that I do not know at all what may have happened. If I can do anything to help you in this way I shall be very glad.

As regards the mortgage on your place I trust that you have been able to make some arrangement. Such mortgages are so entirely out of my line of work that I hardly know in what direction to turn to help you, but I shall be glad to hear from you and hope that the last two months may have developed something in your favor and the return of warmer weather may be helping Mrs. Peirce.

Very truly yours,
Geo. S. Morison
