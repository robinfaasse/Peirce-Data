MR. CHARLES S. PEIRCE,

Dear Mr. Peirce:-

A problem has recently come to me which I should like to have you think of a little at your leisure. It may be stated briefly. The inflow into a lake is assumed to have been constant for a sufficient length of time for the inflow and the discharge over a fixed weir to have become equal. On this condition comes a flood which increases at a uniform rate for a given length of time. The inflow into the lake for any given length of time will of course be equal to the discharge over the weir in the same time plus the amount of water stored in the lake as it rises.

[u]A[/u] = area of lake or reservoir, a constant
[u]b[/u] = length of weir, a constant
[u]r[/u] = rate of increase of inflow into lake per second, a constant
[u]t[/u] = time from beginning of flood in seconds, a variable
[u]h[/u] = Head of water over weir, a variable
[u]Q[/u] = discharge over weir per second, a variable.

The problem is to find general expressions for the values of Q and h in terms of A, b, r and t.

The formula for the discharge over the weir may be taken as Q = 3.5 b h[ins]^[sup]3/2[/sup][/ins] all dimensions being in feet. If metric dimensions are used the formula becomes practically Q = 2 b h[ins]^[sup]3/2[/sup][/ins].

I think you will find this problem much more complicated than it looks at first. The only solution which I have seen of it makes certain assumptions which are inconsistent with each other, but how great an error they produce I cannot say. What do you think of it?

Very truly yours,
