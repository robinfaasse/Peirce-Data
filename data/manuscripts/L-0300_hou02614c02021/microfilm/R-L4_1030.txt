Dear Sir:

I have reduced the problem of the suspension bridge stiffening truss into the simple form shown by the accompanying diagram and the descriptive matter which follows: To simplify matters I have reduced the inclined back stays to equivalent horizontal back stays and made a corresponding change in section, the actual stays being inclined at an angle of two horizontal to one vertical and having the same section as the main cables. It has seemed to me that we could safely neglect any changes due to the shortening of the towers under pressure; these towers will have an effective cross section of about 1200 square inches.

You will observe that I have assumed the same modulus of elasticity for the cables as for the chords of the truss. Although this is not strictly correct, it is very nearly so in spite of the fact that the ultimate strength of the material in the cables is at least three times as great as that of the material in the chords of the truss.
