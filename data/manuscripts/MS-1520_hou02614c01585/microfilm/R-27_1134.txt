[u]Introduction[/u].

[u]A corricolo is a calessino[/u]. [del]But since there is no such[/del]
[del]thing as an exact synonym, the difference between the[/del]
[del]two had better be explained.[/del] [u]A corricolo, then, is a sort[/u]
[u]of sulky. In theory, it carries one person and is drawn by[/u]
[u]one horse: in practice, two horses are harnessed to it,[/u]
[del]and from twelve to fifteen people ride in it.[/del]

Now let it not be supposed that it advances at a walk,
like the ox-cart of a Merovingian king, nor at the correct
trot of a victoria at 30 francs a day. No, no; it goes at a
high gallop. The charriot of Hell that abducted Proserpine
from the [ins]grassy[/ins] bank of Simethus did not eat the ground faster
than a Neapolitan corricolo, as it goes striking fire from
the pavement of lava, and raises a cloud of ashes as it flies.
