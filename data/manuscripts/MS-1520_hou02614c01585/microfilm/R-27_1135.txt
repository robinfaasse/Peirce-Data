[del]It really is drawn by only one horse, the one between the
thills; for the other, called the bilancino, which is harnessed
to one side, merely frisks and curvets and excites his
mate. He probably wonders, with Tityrus, what god has
conferred upon him this immunity from toil. 'Twas luck;
'twas Providence; 'twas fate. Horses, like men, have their
stars.[/del]

It is stated above that this sulky, though meant for one
person, ordinarily carries twelve or fifteen. Perhaps the
passage demands [ins]a word of[/ins] [del]an[/del] annotation. "Where there is enough
for one, there is enough for two," says the old French proverb;
but never in any language have I hitherto met with the
proverb, "Where there is enough for one, there is enough for
fifteen." That, however, is the way with the corricolo. Ad-
vancing civilization finds some new use for everything.
