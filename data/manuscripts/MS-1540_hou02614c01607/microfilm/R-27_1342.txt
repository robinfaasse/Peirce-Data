[pn]4[/pn] Ridley Mark Ridley in the work to which Browne refers, and which
was published in [date]1613[/date], describes himself as "Doctor in Physicke and Philosophy
lately Physitian to the Emperour of Russia [not physician to the late emperor,
nor had there been any Tsar previous to Michael Romanoff, who came to
the throne the very year the book was printed] and one of the Eight Principalls
or Elects, of the Colledge of Physitions in London." He was a Copernican
and a follower of Gilbert, but not in all the latter's theories, nor in the
one here in question. Besides the book mentioned, he published in
defense of it, [del]an[/del] [ins]a very[/ins] offensive defence, against Barlowe, in [date]1617[/date],
another, Animadversions Magnetical. He had so much the best of the
dispute; but he is today only remembered as an inexact observer of
the dip of the needle.
