[del]4) Ridley. Mark Ridley was a Copernican and adherent of Gilbert
who in 1613 published a work on Magnetical Bodies
[del]having with Barlowe and others, followed it up by Animadversion[/del]
Magnetical 1617. He is chiefly remembered today as an inaccurate
measurer of the dip of the needle. He describes himself as "Doctor
in Physicke and Philosophie, lately Physitian to the Emperour of Russia
[Bobilly Vasili, the last of the Rouricki family] and one of the Eight Principals
or Elects of the Colledge of Physitians in London". Though he follows Gilbert, he
avoids some of his master's theories. Descartes, Cabeus, Ridley, and
Kircherham[/del] [ins]Perrone[/ins] [del]represents as agreeing so perfectly, really
entertain wholly inconsistent theories. But they all agree in the
truism that attractions are relative and mutual, i.e. that action and
reaction are equal, though this neat phrase was not yet invented.[/del]
