These editions of Euclid which carry the commentary
of Campanus have give a peculiar text of Euclid, in
some respects better than that which has been usual,
in other respects not so. It is undoubtedly a translation from
the Arabic. "It pleases some authors to say that there is a
dispute as to whether this translation is by Campanus or
not; although, as far as I can perceive, the dispute consists
in this, that all those who have examined the matter carefully,
as Tiraboschi (than whom no writer of the nineteenth
century is more reliable) and Charles Jourdain, assert
positively that it is the translation of Adelard of Bath,
