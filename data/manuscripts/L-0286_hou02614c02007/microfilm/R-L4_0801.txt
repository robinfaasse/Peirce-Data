Before the Col. went to Washington,
when he himself thought he had
no chance or wish to go, we very
foolishly confined to him my great
anxiety about going — and the
hope I entertained of being able
to write a better report than the
regular Commission on account of
my German and acquaintance
with Krafft. I am afraid this
is bearing fruit now, for I
believe that they want me
to go. Now I have thought
that you might perhaps be
able to help me. I want very
little — no extra pay, no mileage,
or other emoluments — simply
permission to be away for three
months — with every thing else
in "statu quo." Could you not get me [ins]a[/ins] temporary
detail on Coast Survey duty, and then give me
permission to go to Germany? The thing may be
impracticable — but I am so very much disappointed
that I purpose to leave no stone unturned in my
endeavour to succeed. Don't get bored with my
long epistles. By-the-way, I borrowed a couple of
keys from Mulveh — and tried some observations,
