The considerations which really actuate those exact logicians who have virtually given in their adhesion to the doctrine of the quantified predicate are, I apprehend, such as the following:

First, exact logic is to take mathematics for its guide. Now in ordinary algebra equations (which are but expressions of the special modification of identity which refers to quantity) are the leading propositions.

Second, the majority of the fundamental formulae of algebraical logic are equations; and those that are not most simply so expressed may be, nevertheless, replaced by equations.

Third, the expression of logical formulae as equations enables us to unite logical with numerical algebra as one consistent whole, with great advantage.

Fourth, definitions [del]which[/del] [ins]and other[/ins] are convertible propositions, wherever they can be established in science, are of peculiar value; and the mind is not satisfied until such discoveries are reached. Until they are reached, therefore, it [del]subserves[/del] the purpose of science, to put our propositions into the form of equations, involving as they must, in the imperfect stage of knowledge, an indication of their imperfection.

Fifth, what has just been said of propositions is equally true of inferences. A conclusion from which the colligated premise cannot be reinferred, is an imperfect conclusion; and its imperfection should be marked in the logical representation.
