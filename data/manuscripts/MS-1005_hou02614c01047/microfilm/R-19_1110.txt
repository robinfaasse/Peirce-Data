3 To His Excellency,
the royal minister of state
Baron von Zedlitz, [del]Victim of the[/del]

[del]I find the favor of the sciences on its own account is ready to work for your Excellency's own interest; for this is indissolubly bound up with them, not merely by the elevated post of a patron, but also by the much more intimate one of an amateur and an enlightened connoisseur. On this account I avail myself both of the only means which is in some measure in my power, of showing my gratitude for the gracious confidence wherewith your excellency honours me, as well as to contribute anything I can to this regard. I always with security and content to him whom a speculative life delights under moderate wishes the approbation of an enlightened, competent judge is a power-ful encouragement to exertions whose utility is great although remote and therefore wholly mistaken by common eyes.[/del]

4 To such a one and to this gracious object, I now dedicate this work, and, to his patronage, all my future [del]literary[/del] destiny and am with the deepest respect

Your Excellency's
most humble servant
Immanuel Kant

Königsberg. [date]29 March
1781[/date].
