[pn]5[/pn] [u]Preface[/u].

The human Reason has the strange fate, in one kind of its cognitions, to be teased with questions which it cannot thrust aside; for they are propounded to it through the Nature of the Reason itself, but yet which it cannot answer, for they surpass all the power of the human reason.

It falls into this dilemma without any fault of its own. It sets out from principles whose employment is inevitable in the course of experience, and at the same time is sufficiently warranted by this. With these it mounts (as also its nature carries it along with itself) higher, to more remote conditions. But it discovers [del]then[/del] [ins]then[/ins] that in this way its business must always remain unaccomplished, since the questions never cease; so it sees itself forced to have recourse to principles which transcend all possible [del]experience[/del] [ins]employment[/ins], and yet which seem so unsuspected that they stand in agreement with the common sense of mankind. But thereby it plunges into obscurity and contradictions, from which it can certainly infer that there are [ins]hidden[/ins] errors working somewhere, but which it cannot detect, since the principles which it employs, while they go beyond all bounds of experience, no longer acknowledge any touchstone of experience. The arena of these endless contests is now called Metaphysic.
