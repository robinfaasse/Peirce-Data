I understand however by this, not a criticism of the books and systems, but of the power of reason itself in general, in relation to all cognitions, for which it may strive [del]independently[/del] [ins]independently[/ins] of all experience, consequently, the decision of the possibility or impossibility of a metaphysics in general, and the determination as well of the sources as of the compass and boundaries thereof, but all from principles.

This road, the only one left, I have now taken and flatter myself that I have found on it the abolition of all errors which had hitherto set the reason at variance with itself in its non-empirical use. I have not anywhere evaded its questions in but by excusing myself with the weakness of the human reason; but I have fully specified them according to principles and after I had disclosed the point of misunderstanding of reason with herself, I have solved them to her full satisfaction. It is true the answer of these questions has not at all turned out as dogmatic-ravishing curiosity may have expected; for that could not have been satisfied except by magic, which I do not dabble in. But that was not certainly the design of the nature-determination of our reason and the duty of philosophy was to break up the delusion which sprang from misinterpretation whatever, no matter what prized and darling error would [del]be[/del] [ins]get[/ins] brought to naught.
