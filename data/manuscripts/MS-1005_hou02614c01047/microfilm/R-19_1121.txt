[pn](2[/pn] It can, as it seems to me, serve the reader as no small
inducement to join his exertion to that of the author, if he has
the prospect of executing wholly, and yet durably, a great and
important work according to the above scheme. Metaphysics is,
now according to the above conceptions, which are here given
of it, the only one of all the sciences of which such a completion
and certainly in a short time and with only slight but combined
effort can be promised, so that nothing remains for posterity
but to arrange the whole in a didactic manner for their purposes,
without being able to add thereto in the least. For it is nothing
but the Inventory of all our possessions in Pure Reason, systematic-
ally ordered. Nothing can here escape our notice, since, what
reason itself creates, cannot hide itself, but will be brought
to light by reason itself, as soon as only the common principle
thereof has been discovered. The thorough unity of this kind of
cognitions, and indeed is derived from pure conceptions, without
anything of experience or even a particular intuition, which
could lead only to the determined experience, being able to have
any influence upon them to enlarge or extend them, render this
absolute completeness not only practicable but also necessary.
[u]Tecum habita et noris, quam sit tibi curta supellex[/u]. [u]Persius[/u].
