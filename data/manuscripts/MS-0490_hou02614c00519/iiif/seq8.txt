[del]The morning of the 13th of June[/del]
The next morning, at the usual hour, I
called on Monsieur Dupin. He was
alone, and sitting in his library,
reading a book which I found, upon
enquiry, to be a treatise on the nature
of the human mind. After some conver-
sation on general subjects, he said,
"I have been thinking, since we met
yesterday, of the case of the Murders
in the Rue Morgue. It is certainly a
very extraordinary one; and yet, after
all, it does not seem to me so very
difficult of solution. If you have
nothing better to do, we will go and
take a look at the house." I readily
assented, and we set out together.

way through the throng, and, having
obtained permission from the officer
in charge, entered the building.
