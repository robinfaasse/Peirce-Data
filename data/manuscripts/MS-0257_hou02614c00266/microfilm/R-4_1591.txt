[u]Art.[/u] . [u]Postulate[/u] . Space is everywhere unbroken and returns into itself.

[u]Scholium[/u] . Space where we [del]seek[/del] [ins]can[/ins] examine it appears to be perfectly continuous. If it is joined together in the same way in the parts most remote from us, it would follow as a necessary consequence (we shall not here show how) that it returns into itself. It is not meant by this that any real object going off toward the north pole of the heavens could return from the south pole; because the distance might be infinite. But it means that if there were any kind of shadow [ins]or appearance[/ins] for which distance made no difference (which there is not, for a shadow [ins]or optical image[/ins] loses its intensity with distance) then such an object might go off toward the [del]north[/del] pole going faster and faster the more it was removed and might reappear returning from the south pole. Whether that is really so or not, no observation can show, unless it should be a finite distance. But, in the first place, it is fair and reasonable to assume that the parts of space we do not know are joined together like those we do know, and in the second place, the assumption cannot introduce any error into any proposition about a part of space [ins]of which[/ins] we can have any experience, [del]of[/del] while it [del]immense[/del] vastly simplifies [del]of[/del] our reasonings about space.
