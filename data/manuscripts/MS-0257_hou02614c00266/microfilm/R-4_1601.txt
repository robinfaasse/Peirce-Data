But if we admit infinitesimals in the sense of finite numbers divided by infinite numbers, that is, if we admit that it is possible for [del]a[/del] equidistant points on a line to be as many as the points of a line without being in contact, then the property in question is possessed by such a [del]row[/del] [ins]line[/ins] of points.

If the points on a line possess this third property, the system of real numbers is inadequate to discriminating them. If $A$ represents the distance of any point from the origin and $A + \underline{i}$ the distance of another point sufficiently near $A + \underline{i} = A$, that is, the same number will attach to both; and in that sense $\underline{i} = 0$. But a new system of numbers may be affixed to the points between the points $A$ and $A + \underline{i}$, and according to that way of measuring $\underline{i}$ and $0$ will cease to be equal.

If a series of points possesses this third property I shall say that it is continuous.
