3[/marginalia] The doctrine of pragmatism was publicly enounced about
thirty years ago, when it was needed. During those thirty years it
has become in large measure assimilated, in one form or another,
by all the best thinkers; so that there are few who are today in
marked opposition to it. Some carry it out more thoroughly and
consistently than others; but that is about all. The tradition of
its being a peculiar and disputable doctrine remains, however;
and the consequence is that today those who [del]do[/del] [ins]are[/ins] not
disposed to accept it, imagine it to [del]have[/del] consist in some-
thing they deny, which is for the most part, [del]entirely different[/del] [ins]quite as opposed[/ins] to true
pragmatism as it is to their own beliefs
