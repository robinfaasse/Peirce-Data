The system of algebraic notation which I shall here use is called Peirce's Universal Algebra of Logic, and is not my Algebra of Dyadic Relations which Schröder chiefly employs in Vol III of his Algebra der Logik. [del]Concept[/del] The expression of a proposition in this system consists of two parts, the Boolian and the Peircian. The Boolian expresses a proposition concerning individuals each denoted by a distinct letter called an index. These individuals are called the subjects both of the proposition expressed by the Boolian and of the other proposition expressed by Boolian and Peircian taken together.
