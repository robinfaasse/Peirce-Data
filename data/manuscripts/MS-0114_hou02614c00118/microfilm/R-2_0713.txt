One object is said to be independent of another when facts
are true of it which do not consist logically in anything
being true of the other; so that those facts might conceivably
be true although the other object did not exist. The characters
consisting in such facts may also be called independent.

A [u]collection[/u] is an individual object concerning which no
facts whatever are true except such the truth of which consists
in the truth of facts concerning other [ins]individual[/ins] [del]other[/del] objects independent of
the collection and of each other; and these objects are called the
members of the collection.

Although such seems to be the essential idea of a collection, yet
the conception admits of enlargement in several ways, to advantage.
In the first place, all the independent individuals of the universe that
are not members may be considered as contributing negatively to
the determination of the collection, almost as much as the members.
