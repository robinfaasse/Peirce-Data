The first form agrees in the two terms, the second
does not. The obvious explanation is that the first
form supposes the interior of the circle representing
the absolute to be the map; the [del]latter[/del] second, the
exterior.

It remains to inquire whether [del]this[/del] [ins]the[/ins] formula [del]just obtained[/del] [ins]just obtained[/ins]
agrees with the hyperbolic equation

[fig]Right triangle with sides a, h and angle B[/fig] $\tanh h = \frac{\tanh a}{\cos B}$

[del]If so, we ought to have[/del] [ins]This gives[/ins]

$\frac{[del]\times[/del] \tanh \frac{h}{2}}{1 + \tanh^2 \frac{h}{2}} = \frac{\tanh \frac{a}{2}}{1 + \tanh^2 \frac{a}{2}} \sec B$

or

$\tanh \frac{h}{2} + \coth \frac{h}{2} = \left( \tanh \frac{a}{2} + \coth \frac{a}{2} \right) \cos B$

we ought to have

$\frac{r}{L} = \left( \tanh \frac{h}{2} \right)^{\pm 1}$

and therefore

$\left( \frac{r}{L} + \frac{L}{r} \right) = \left( \frac{l}{L} + \frac{L}{l} \right) \cdot \theta$

Now the equation of the circle is

[fig]Circle with intersecting lines and variables r, L, l[/fig]
[del]$r^2 + L^2 = l^2$[/del]
$r^2 + L^2 = r^2 + 2lr + l^2$
$\therefore$
$l^2 + L^2 = 2l$
$q - l = \frac{L^2}{l}$
$q - 2lq + l^2$

[del]$r^2 + L^2 = q^2$[/del]
$q - \sqrt{q^2 - L^2} = l$

$\frac{l}{L} = \tanh^2 \frac{a}{2}$

$\frac{\cdot \sec \theta}{\frac{r}{L} + \frac{L}{r}} = 2 \frac{\cos \theta}{\tanh a}$
$= \cos \theta \frac{1 + \tanh^2 \frac{a}{2}}{\tanh \frac{a}{2}}$
$= \cos \theta \left( \tanh \frac{a}{2} + \coth \frac{a}{2} \right)$
$= \cos \theta \left( \frac{l}{L} + \frac{L}{l} \right)$

Therefore it does agree.
