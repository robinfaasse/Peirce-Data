Suppose, however, we were to subject the following
(which seems hardly distinguishable from it to a loose
thinker) to the same test.

$$\Sigma_{k} \Pi_{a} \Pi_{b} \bar{q}_{ka} (\bar{q}_{kb} + 1_{ab})$$

Squaring this, we have

$$\Sigma_{k} \Pi_{a} \Pi_{b} \Pi_{c} \Pi_{d} \bar{q}_{ka} \bar{q}_{kc} (\bar{q}_{kb} + 1_{ab}) (\bar{q}_{kd} + 1_{cd})$$

Now identifying $d$ with $a$ and $c$ with $b$, we get

$$\Pi_{a} \Pi_{b} 1_{ab}$$

which is as much as to say that everything is identical
with everything. So that in a universe of more than
one object this proposition is false.
