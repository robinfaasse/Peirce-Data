For example, it is required to prove that
$$\Pi_a \Sigma_k \Pi_b \ q_{ka} \cdot (\overline{q}_{kb} + 1_{ab})$$

That is, that every object, $A$, has [ins]whether individual or general,[/ins] a quality, $K$, which
is a quality of no object, $B$, unless $B$ is identical
with $A$. Now this can only lead to an absurd result
if $k$ be eliminated. But without "logical involution," or
the compounding of the premise with itself, $k$ can
only be eliminated in two ways, $1^{\text{st}}$, by eliminating
$q_{ka}$ and $q_{kb}$ independently, and $2^{\text{nd}}$, by identifying
$b$ with $a$. In the first way, we can only get
$$\Pi_a \Pi_b \ (\overline{1}_{ab} + 1_{ab})$$
which is true; and in the second way we can only
get
$$\Pi_a \ 1_{aa}$$
which is equally true. We next proceed, then to square
the proposition in question, and so get
$$\Pi_a \Sigma_k \Pi_b \Pi_c \ q_{ka} \cdot (\overline{q}_{kb} + 1_{ab}) \cdot (\overline{q}_{kc} + 1_{ac})$$
