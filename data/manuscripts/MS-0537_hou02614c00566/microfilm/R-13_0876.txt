The first of these is that the [del]ideas[/del] [ins]characters[/ins] included in a concept
are [del]coördinated[/del] [ins]conjoined[/ins] so as to form a whole. It is this which is
expressed by the copula. [del]But[/del] [ins]But[/ins] the copula does not express this
alone. It also [del]implies[/del] [ins]expresses[/ins] that the [del]idea[/del] [ins]concept[/ins] is applicable to
something, or in other words, that it is a concept. For
instance, the expression "[del]black[/del] horse" implies the conjunction of
the two ideas "black" and "horse" but it does not assert
this conjunction. It is not a proposition. Because it is
not asserted that the concept "black horse" is applicable
to anything. The copula, therefore, is a symbol which
expresses that the predicate is a concept applicable to the
subject. [del]Now[/del] It is, therefore, a symbol of application.
But [del]as[/del] [ins]since[/ins] the subject is itself a concept, the copula
implies that the predicate is applicable to whatever the
subject is applicable to. It is, therefore, a symbol of
the relation of [del]inclusion[/del] [ins]containment[/ins] of one concept in another.
Thus, the copula [del]is[/del] [ins]expresses[/ins] two things: 1st, the conjunction of
characters in a concept, and 2nd, the relation of containment
of one concept in another.

But there is a third thing which the copula expresses.
It is that the concept is applicable to [del]reality[/del] [ins]the universe of discourse[/ins].
This is what is called the existential function of the
copula. It is this which is expressed by the word "is"
when it is used as a predicate, as when we say "God is."

These three functions of the copula are not independent of
each other. They are, on the contrary, three aspects of
the same thing. For a concept is a whole of characters
which is applicable to a class of things. [del]And[/del] [ins]But[/ins] this class
of things is itself a part of the universe of discourse.
The copula, therefore, expresses the relation of the
concept to the universe of discourse.
