§ 5. [del]Pairs[/del] [ins]Dyads (dont say pairs)[/ins] are really of two types A:A and A:B. In Fig 1, The monads are all alike A=B=C. The dyads are [del]also alike in one sense[/del] [ins]alike in themselves but unlike in their relations.[/ins]

[fig]Three-by-three matrix of dyads[/fig]
A:A  A:B  A:C
B:A  B:B  B:C
C:A  C:B  C:C

The identical dyads are A:A  B:B  C:C which are entirely alike

Of a different form are A:B  B:C  C:A which by the arrows are brought under one class, thereby bringing B:A  C:B  A:C under the [del]other[/del] [ins]reverse[/ins].

Put A:A + B:B + C:C = I
A:B + B:C + C:A = l
Then B:A + C:B + A:C = Kl
lll = I  ll = Kl
