[u]Summation Formulæ Generalized.[/u]

Actuarial science and mathematics present us with a great variety of summation formulæ. In order to remember these formulæ and to reproduce them on short notice, and to select in practice the one best suited to the case in point, we believe a classification and generalization will be very useful. Without such classification it is difficult to comprehend the problem of summation as a whole.

It is the object of this paper to generalize and classify summation formulæ, and this will point to some obvious simplifications.

[u]SERIES.[/u]

The conception of a series lies at the foundation of summation formulæ.

A series is a class, in which the individuals are determined in order.

A class is determined, first, by a generic description which covers all the individuals belonging to the class, and second, by specific descriptions which discriminate the individuals from each other. In mathematics the generic description is the general term, expressed as a function, [u]Fx[/u]. The specific descriptions are the particular values given to the independent variable [u]x[/u]. The individuals of the class are quantities. The relations between these quantities create an order, thus converting the class into a series.

Thus every series is determined by three descriptive elements:—

First, the generic description or general term.
Second, the specific descriptions or individual values.
Third, the order or place of each individual in the series.
