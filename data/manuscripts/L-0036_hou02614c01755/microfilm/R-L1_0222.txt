definite sum under the same general term $u_x$ at unit intervals of $h$.

We need only to expand the operator in powers of $\Delta_h$.

This may be done by the infinitesimal calculus, or by ordinary algebra, the method of indeterminate coefficients being the simplest.

The coefficients of the expansion constitute the famous "Numbers of Bernoulli", who was the first to investigate them.

We will have by the binomial theorem, on putting for brevity $^n/_h$ equal to $t$ and $\Delta_h$ equal to $\Delta$,

$$\frac{1}{(1+\Delta)^t - 1} = \frac{1}{t\Delta + \frac{t(t-1)}{1.2}\Delta^2 + \frac{t(t-1)(t-2)}{1.2.3}\Delta^3, \&c.}$$

$$= \frac{1}{1 + \frac{(t-1)}{2}\Delta + \frac{(t-1)(t-2)}{6}\Delta^2} \cdot \frac{1}{t\Delta}$$

And if we put

$$\frac{1}{1 + \frac{(t-1)}{2}\Delta + \frac{(t-1)(t-2)}{6}\Delta^2 \&c.,} = a + b\Delta + c\Delta^2 + \&c.$$

And clear of fractions by multiplying, and equate coefficients of $\Delta$, we easily find by ordinary algebra the values of $a, b, c,$ etc.

Substituting these values we obtain after restoring the values $t = \frac{n}{h}$ and $\Delta = \Delta_h$, we have

$$\Sigma_n \left| \begin{matrix} b-n \\ a \end{matrix} \right| u_x = \frac{1}{n} \left( \frac{\Delta_h}{h} \right)^{-1} \left\{ u_b - u_a \right\}$$

$$- \frac{n-h}{2n} \left\{ u_b - u_a \right\}$$

[pn]6[/pn]

$$+ \frac{n^2 - h^2}{12n} \left( \frac{\Delta_h}{h} \right) \left\{ u_b - u_a \right\}$$

$$- \frac{(n^2 - h^2)h}{24n} \left( \frac{\Delta_h}{h} \right)^2 \left\{ u_b - u_a \right\}$$

$$- \frac{(n^2 - h^2)(n^2 - 19h^2)}{720n} \left( \frac{\Delta_h}{h} \right)^3 \left\{ u_b - u_a \right\} \&c.$$

If $h$ is finite we have $\left\{ \frac{\Delta_h}{h} \right\}^{-1} = h\Sigma_h$; if infinitesimal $\int dx$.

If in this general equation we make the unit interval of the required series $^1/_n$ and the unit interval of the known series ($h$) equal to unity, we will have Lubbock's formula,

$$\Sigma^1/_n \left| \begin{matrix} b-^1/_n \\ a \end{matrix} \right| u_x = \left\{ n\Sigma + \frac{n-1}{2} - \frac{n^2-1}{12n}\Delta + \frac{n^2-1}{24n}\Delta^2 \right.$$

$$\left. - \frac{(n^2-1)(19n^2-1)}{720n^3}\Delta^3 \&c. \right\} \left\{ u_b - u_a \right\}$$

If we make the unit interval of the required series $^1/_n$ and that of the known series ($h$) infinitesimal, then will also $\Delta_h$ be infinitesimal, and we will have Woolhouse's formula.

$$\Sigma^1/_n \left| \begin{matrix} b-^1/_n \\ a \end{matrix} \right| u_x = \left\{ n\int dx - \frac{n-1}{2} + \frac{1}{12n}\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right) - \frac{1}{720n^3} \right.$$

$$\left. \left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)^3 + \&c. \right\} \left( u_b - u_a \right)$$

It will be noted that we have here used nothing but very simple algebraic processes, in fact nothing but ordinary algebraic multiplication and division. There is no integration in this process.
