$1^{\underline{\text{st}}}$, they may be in coincidence.

$2^{\underline{\text{nd}}}$, their line may be a tangent line. In this case a displacement may bring them into coincidence. They are then called "parallel."

$3^{\underline{\text{rd}}}$, one of the pair may belong to the absolute.

$4^{\underline{\text{th}}}$, they may be harmonically situated each on the polar or pole of the other.

$5^{\underline{\text{th}}}$, one of them may be harmonically situated towards one of the absolute points or planes of their line with reference to the other and the other absolute point or plane of their line.

$6^{\underline{\text{th}}}$, they may be equianharmonically situated with reference to the absolute.

The mode of superposition is as follows

[fig]Conic with intersecting lines and points P, Q[/fig]

Given the point O. Draw two lines through it, and through the intersections two conics meeting at two points P, Q, harmonic to O. Then by projection from Q, OA is superposed upon OB, by projection from P, OB is superposed on OC, and by projection again OA or OC is superposed upon OD
