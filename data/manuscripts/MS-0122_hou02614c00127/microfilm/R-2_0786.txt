[u]Sketch of a Synthetic Treatment[/u]

Chapter I. Geometry of points on a line.

1. Taking any three points A, B, C, any [del]fourth[/del] point X is either 1[sup]st[/sup] on the way from A to B not passing C, 2[sup]nd[/sup] on the way from B to [del]C[/del] [ins]A[/ins] not passing [del]A[/del] [ins]C[/ins], or 3[sup]d[/sup] on the way from C to A not passing B.

2. In each of these divisions the multitude of points is such that they cannot in any way be placed in one to one correspondence with the integral numbers.

This does not say that there is a continuous succession; but I do not see that the idea of continuity is possible without a second dimension.
