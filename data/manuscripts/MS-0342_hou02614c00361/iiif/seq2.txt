The first point is that instead of writing words, we
write letters. Thus instead of horses we write [u]h[/u] instead
of black [u]b[/u] and so forth. [u]h[/u] stands not for one
horse but for all horses, – the whole class of horses col-
lectively. [u]b[/u] stands for all black things.

Then, if we wish to write [u]all black horses[/u], it will
be natural to put [u]bh[/u] – black horses. This is analo-
gous to multiplication in arithmetic for as three
times two means [u]three twos[/u] and six times seven, [u]six[/u]
[u]sevens[/u], so we may say that [u]black horses[/u] are black
into horses. Three times two implies a [u]three[/u] : each of
whose units is a [u]two[/u] : In the same way [u]black horses[/u]
implies all black things each of which is a horse. Perhaps
you will think this analogy fanciful; I do not say that
it is not. But it will serve our purpose very well as
you will see.
