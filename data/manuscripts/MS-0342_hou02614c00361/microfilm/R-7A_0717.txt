The first point is that instead of writing words, we write letters. Thus instead of [u]horses[/u] we write [u]h[/u] instead of [u]black[/u] [u]b[/u] and so forth. [u]h[/u] stands not for one horse but for all horses, — the whole class of horses collectively. [u]b[/u] stands for all black things.

Then, if we wish to write [u]all black horses[/u], it will be natural to put [u]bh[/u] — black horses. This is analogous to multiplication in arithmetic for as three times two means three twos and six times seven, six sevens, so we may say that [u]black horses[/u] are black into horses. Three times two implies a three [del]:[/del] [ins]⁝[/ins] each of whose units is a two [del]⁝[/del] [ins]⁝[/ins] In the same way [u]black horses[/u] implies all black things each of which is a horse. Perhaps you will think this analogy fanciful; I do not say that it is not. But it will serve our purpose very well as you will see.
