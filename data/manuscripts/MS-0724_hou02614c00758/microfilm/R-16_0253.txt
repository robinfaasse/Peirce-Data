[u]LOGIC.[/u]

[u]CHAPTER I. TERMS.[/u]

A representation is anything which is supposed to stand for another
thing. Representations are of three kinds; viz.

1. Such as stand for other things merely by resembling them. In this sense
[del]the[/del] [ins]and[/ins] James for money and a mountain stands for a human face.

2. Such as stand for others through a purely arbitrary convention or by
a mechanical association. Such are proper names, political representatives

3. Such as stand for other things owing to the likeness of the latter to such
things as the representation stands for in the second way. Thus, a savage
will call a particular tree by some [del]proper[/del] [ins]proper[/ins] name & this becomes soon
extended to everything like it, namely, to trees in general. So a particular
feeling is called up by [del]an act of[/del] [ins]a[/ins] murder by [del]mere[/del] association and so
is a representation of the [del]3rd kind of[/del] [ins]that[/ins] murder, but afterwards
the feeling arises upon all occasions similar at all to this murder
& so becomes general in its application & forms a conception of
the [del]injury[/del]. Representations of this third class will be called symbols.
When they are inward feelings they are conceptions.
