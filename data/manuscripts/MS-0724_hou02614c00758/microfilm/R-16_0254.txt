2. Every symbol may be regarded as a store-room of knowledge or
[del]feeling[/del] [ins]fancied[/ins] knowledge; and hence it stands for the mind where-
with it does. In fact, the mind itself is only such a symbol within us.

3. Every symbol may be regarded as belonging to its object in the same
way that the attribute on account of which it is applicable to that object
belongs to the object. Hence it stands also for this attribute as its tangible
embodiment.

Logic is the science of the relation of symbols in general to their objects.

A symbol may be used with either of the above references specially
in view, viz

1. If it is intended merely as the embodiment of an attribute it is called
a term.

2. If it is intended as a store of knowledge it is called a [u]proposition[/u].

3. If it is intended as a ground of acquiring knowledge it is called an
[u]argument[/u].

As no symbol can really be free from relation to an object, terms & proposi-
tions are subject to the laws of Logic as well as arguments, though
not so immediately.
