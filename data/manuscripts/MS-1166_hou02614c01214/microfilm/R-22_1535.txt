[del]PRECISE, a.[/del] [u]Precise definition[/u], one that contains nothing unessential, nothing superfluous. Thus, to render the usual definition of a circle precise, the word "within" should be struck out, so that it shall read, A circle is a line all the points of which are equally distant from one point, ; and it will be a theorem to prove that this point can only be within.

In applied math., "precise" refers to the result of very nice measurement, and "exact" to having neglected nothing in a mathematical result.

PREDICABLE, n. For the definition.
A logical term considered as capable of being universally predicated of another ; usually, one of the five words, or five kinds of predicates according to the Aristotelian logic, namely, genus, species, difference, property, and accident.

Predicable taken properly is the same as universal, only they differ in this, that predicable is defined by "is said of," while universal by "is in." For predicable is what is born apt to be said of many and universal is what is born apt to be in many. Petrus Hispanus. [ins]Book not found[/ins]

The predicables are five in number, viz. genus, species, difference, property, and accident. This number is gathered thus. Whatever is affirmed of another after the manner aforesaid, is either contained in the essence of its subject or not. That which is contained in the essence is either predicated in the enunciation in which it is said what the subject is, or in that in which it is said of what quality or sort it is. That which is predicated in the enunciation in which it is said what it is, either expresses the essence of it imperfectly, and is the genus, or fully and perfectly and is the species. That which is contained in the essence and expresses the quality is the difference. That which is not contained in the essence of the subject is either predicated of it necessarily and is the property or casually and is its accident. Burgersdicius, tr. by a Gentleman. Book I. Chap. x., Ax. 7. [ins]Book not found[/ins]
