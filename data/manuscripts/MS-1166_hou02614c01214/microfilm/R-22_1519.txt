[u]PARTITION, n.[/u] [[u]partitio[/u] was used by Cornificius in the logical sense]
9. In logic, the separation of an integrate whole into its integrant parts ; the separation
of any whole into its parts, except that the separation of a genus into its species or of a spe-
cies into genus and difference are not called partition.

Division divideth universal things into their particulars, and partition divideth particulars
into their parts, and most commonly followeth division...as for example, when division hath divi-
ded a sensible body into a man and beast, then followeth partition and divideth man into soul and
body, and the body into his integrall parts, as head, brest, belly, legges, and such like. Blund-
vile, Arte of Logicke, 1619. book II, chap. 3.

[u]PARVA LOGICALIA[/u]- The name given in the middle ages to the branches of logic which were treated in
the various supplements which were added from time to time to the Summulae of Petrus Hispanus.
These subjects were the doctrines of supposition, ampliation, restriction, distribution, appellatio
exponibles, syncategoreumata, obligations, insolubilia, consequences, etc.
