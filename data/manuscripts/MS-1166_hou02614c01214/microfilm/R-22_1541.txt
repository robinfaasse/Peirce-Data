What is propertie? It is a natural inclination or [u]property[/u], incident to one special kind; which is to be understood foure manner of waies. First, it is called proprium, which is proper to one onely kind, as to be a poet or musician is proper to man, but not to every man: secondly, it is aclled proper that belongeth to all the kind, but not to that kind alone; thirdly, it is said to be proper when it belongeth to one onely kind and to all that kind, but yet not alwayes, as to be bare-headed or bald is proper to man in old age, but yet not alwaies: fourthly, it is said to be proper, or rather most proper, which is incident to one kind alone, to all that kind, and alwaies, as to have a natural aptnesse to laugh or to speake to man onely, to every man, and alwaies, and therefore this kind of [u]property[/u] is said to be convertible with the kind whereunto it belongeth, as whatsoever hath naturally power to speake or laugh the same is man, and whatsoever is man the same hath power to speake or laugh. The fourth of these properties Porphyry calls properly proper: for the rest, says he, are only proper in some respect, and for some time. This definition of property is extant in the first book of the Topics, cap. 5. Properties, says he, do not declare what a thing is, that is, they are not contained in the essence of the subject, but follow it, reciprocated with that in which alone then are, that is, with the first and proximate subject, not those contained under that proximate subject; for of [u]properties[/u], some are of genus's some of species's: the properties with genus's not species's, and those of species's with species's not individuals, are to be conversed and reciprocated.
