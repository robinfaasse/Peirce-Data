[del]PERCEPTION, n.[/del] [del]For 1 and 2, substitute:[/del]

1. The mental act or process by which we gain knowledge of something new, including the first sensation and the various processes which immediately follow up to the point where they come under the control of the will. Also, the corresponding faculty and the resulting cognition. By perception we recognize our acquaintances, their distance from us, their motions. In contemplating a mathematical diagram in the imagination, we learn by perception certain relations between its parts.

Perceptio, which has been naturalized in all the principal languages of modern Europe, with the qualified exception of the German, in which the indigenous term [u]Wahrnehmung[/u] has again almost superseded it, [u]perception[/u], in its primary philosophical signification, as in the mouths of Cicero and Quintilian, is vaguely equivalent to Comprehension, notion, or cognition in general.

2. From this first meaning it was easily deflected to a second, in which it corresponds to an apprehension, a becoming aware of, in a word a consciousness. In this meaning, though long thus previously employed in the schools, it was brought more prominently and distinctly forward in the writings of Descartes. From him it passed, not only to his own disciples, but, like the term idea, to his adversary Gassendi, and, thereafter, adopted equally by Locke and Leibnitz, it remained a household word in every subsequent philosophy, until its extent was further limited, and thus a third signification given to it.
