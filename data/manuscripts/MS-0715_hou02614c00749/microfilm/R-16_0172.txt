[del]Objects[/del] [ins]altogether unseparated into[/ins] [del]unanalyzed or so[/del] parts he calls [u]units[/u]. English: [u]monads[/u].

Having different marks he calls [u]distinguished[/u] or [u]distinguishable[/u].

A plural of [del]parts[/del] [ins]monads[/ins] he calls a [u]collection[/u].

He calls an ordered pair an aspect of the collection of two.

A collection of two regarded as a unit he calls an [u]aggregation[/u]. ?

The partial collections of a collection may have different marks.

Collections are distinguished in form (1) by [del]their[/del] [ins]their[/ins] plurality, (2) by the modes of marks of its parts.

One particular kind of marks he neglects, the places of the units.

An aggregation is a distinct unit united to each of a number of units.

A partial collection he calls a [u]component[/u]. Also an aspect of a component is called a component.

Mutually exclusive collections are called [u]detached[/u].

A collection intersecting each of two collections is said to connect them.
