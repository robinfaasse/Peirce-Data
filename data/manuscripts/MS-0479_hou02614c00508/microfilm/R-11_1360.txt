[u]On Logical Graphs.[/u]

William Kingdon Clifford introduces the term
graphs to denote diagrams of spots and lines drawn
on a surface to represent singular states of [del]things[/del] [ins]a continuum[/ins], such as the invariantive forms of
an algebraic expression. The suggestion of such
diagrams came from those employed by chemists
to represent the constitutions of compounds.

By a logical graph, I mean any [ins]spatial[/ins] geometrical
diagram [del]intended[/del] [ins]contended[/ins] to represent logical relations.

In this paper I intend to describe three systems of
logical graphs and to show that all such systems
belong to one or other of two general systems very
simply related [del]to one[/del] each other.
