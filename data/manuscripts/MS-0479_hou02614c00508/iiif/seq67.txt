[ured]On Logical Graphs[/ured].

William Kingdom Clifford introduced the term
graphs to denote diagrams of spots and lines drawn
on a surface to represent singular states of [del]things[/del]
a continuum, such as the invariantive forms of
an algebraic expression. The suggestion of such
diagrams came from those employed by chemists
to represent the constitutions of compounds.
By a logical graph, I mean any [ins]superficial[/ins] geometrical
diagram [del]intended[/del] to represent logical relations.
In this paper I intend to describe three systems of
logical graphs and to show that all such systems
belong to one or other of two general systems very
simply related to [del]one an[/del] each other.
