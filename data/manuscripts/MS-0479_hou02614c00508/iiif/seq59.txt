Dichotomous mathematics does not amount to much,
but it does amount to something. For example, the subject
of higher particular propositions [ins]x[/ins] in consequence of not
being perfectly familiar will call for considerable re-
flection to understand it in its entirety and in its
connections. Complicated questions of non-relative deduc-
tive reasoning are rare, it is true; still, they do occur,
and if they are garbed in strange disguises, will now
and then make the quickest minds hesitate or blunder.
Euler’s diagrams are the best aids in such cases, being
natural, little subject to mistake, and everway satisfactory.

to problems involving many terms; but it is an easy art
