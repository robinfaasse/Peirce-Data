connotes B which it did not before; and B as now
understood denotes A which it did not definitely do be-
fore. At the same time we decrease the indefinite ex-
tension of A, since non-B is excluded from it & we
decrease the indefinite comprehension of B since we

The following are the rules for comparing the
extension & comprehension of two terms.

If it is known that All A is B & all B is A; the
[del]total extension & total comprehension of the two terms
are equal[/del] - the two term are equal both in total compre-
hension & total extension.

If it is known that All A is B & that some B is not A
the total extension of B is greater than that of A &
the total comprehension of A is greater than that of B.

In these cases, the extension & comprehension of
A & B are entirely definite relatively to each other;
so that the total comprehension & total extension of each
are equal to the definite comprehension & definite ex-
tension of each relatively to the other. [ins]And[/ins] These are the only
cases in which [del]the[/del] [ins]two terms can be compared in[/ins] total extension & comprehension.
