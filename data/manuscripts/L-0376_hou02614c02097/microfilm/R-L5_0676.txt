Trapezium of Orion. A [ins]famous[/ins] group of six stars in the
great nebula of Orion. Two of the stars are faint, and
the name is derived from the figure formed by the
other four. Huyghens gave a figure of these stars in
1656, but only three of them are shown. Mairan
in 1731 and Messier in 1771 gave the principal four
as we see them now. The fifth star was discovered
by Struve in 1826, and it has been seen [del]after[/del]
sunrise in the 15-inch refractor at Cambridge, Mass.,
although Struve sometimes missed it at midnight,
and Sir John Herschel, who examined the trapezium
with great care seven months before Struve's dis-
covery, saw no sign of it. The sixth star
was discovered by Sir John Herschel in 1830, and
independently by Lassell in 1841. It is a difficult
object, and is [del]probably[/del] [ins]perhaps[/ins] variable. Several other [del]minute[/del]
stars [ins]in and near the trapezium[/ins] have been reported, but their existence is [del]not[/del] [ins]hardly[/ins]
established.
