[date]Nov. 13 1895[/date]

Dear Professor:

Your postal card came duly, and I was pleased to hear from you once more. I shall be much interested to know what you have to say about my "maiden effort" in book-making. The review has not yet appeared. It may possibly be in tomorrow's issue. So far as the virial theorem is concerned, I may say that I omitted it because it hardly seemed to be in keeping with the very elementary character of the book. The question of elasticity, I suppose, is that referred to on page 168: I did not dwell upon it in any detail, because it appears (to me) to be a difficulty inherent simply in the old elastic-solid theory of light. I am not aware that there is any analogous difficulty in the electromagnetic theory; and you will doubtless have noticed that I have assumed this latter one to be correct.

Anyhow, the "Molecules" is not at all a pretentious book! But I am at work on another one — on "Thermodynamics" — which I hope may be more worthy of your attention.

Yours, respectfully
A. D. Risteen
