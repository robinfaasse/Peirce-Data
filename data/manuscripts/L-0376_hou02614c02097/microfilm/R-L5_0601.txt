[date]June 23[/date].

Dear Professor:

Enclosed please find "Torsion". I know it is rather late, but I have worked on the words faithfully, several times getting up in the middle of the night to catch the 3 o'clock A.M. train for Boston, so that I might have the whole day in the library there. I don't think I should make a success at dictionary work. In fact you will remember that I expressed myself to that effect both in a previous letter to you, and also orally at Milford. I don't know words enough, and do not think clearly enough, to be precise, and my words seem more like short essays, thus far, than like definitions.

"Toucan" is too modern a constellation for Ideler, and I do not find it in any of the astronomies I have looked in. That is, I find nothing more than "a modern southern constellation"; or something of that sort. Of course the star lists give $\kappa$ Tucanae, and other stars, but I have not run across anything satisfactory in regard to the constellation as a whole.

In Carr (I believe it was) I find the following passage: "If $T_0$ changes sign while passing through the values zero or infinity, there is a point of inflected torsion."
