[u]Hessian transformation[/u]. If the minors of the determinant which represents
the Hessian of a curve of the $n\text{th.}$ degree be denoted by
$A_{11}$, $A_{12}$, etc., and if for the variables $x_1, x_2, x_3$ of
any ternary quantic the symbols $A_1$, $A_2$, $A_3$ be substi-
tuted, the result is called the Hessian transformation of
the given quantic. Every conic is the Hessian of some
other conic. If the curve to be transformed is of odd
degree, it must be squared before making the substitutions.
(The symbols $A_1$, $A_2$, $A_3$ are merely umbral, but their
squares and products give interpretable results, that is,
$A_{11}$, $A_{12}$, etc., the minors of the determinant.)


[u]Homographic transformation[/u]. (1) Let there be any system of point
coördinates, $xyz$, in a plane, and a system of line
coördinates, $\alpha\beta\gamma$, in the same plane or a different one.
A point in the first plane corresponds homographically
to a line in the second, if the coördinates $x, y, z$, of
the point, are respectively proportional to $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$, the
coördinates of the line. In the same case to any line
$lx + my + nz$ in the first system corresponds a point
$l\alpha + m\beta + n\gamma$ in the second.
