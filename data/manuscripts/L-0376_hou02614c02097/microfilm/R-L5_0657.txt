Hartford, Conn., June 20 1899

Dear Professor:

Your letter came at a time when it was very inconvenient for me to answer it promptly. I had just returned from one trip, and had to "hustle" and start off on another one, down in Maine. I passed through Middletown, N. Y., the day you wrote your letter (I think), and could probably have arranged to run over and see you, if I had known about the matter at that time. I should like, very much, to come out with Mrs. Risteen (there are no children!), and work over the Suspension bridge with you, but I don't see how I can do it. When I first came with this Company I did not have so much to do, but that I could get away for a time, now and then. But in the last few years a good many more duties have been assigned to me, so that now I do not have the same opportunities in this respect. I have had several opportunities (and apparently good ones) to leave here, but the Steam Boiler Company has paid nice things each time, and made it an object to me to stay.
