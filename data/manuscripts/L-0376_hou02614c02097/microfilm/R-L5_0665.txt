Hartford, Conn. [date]February 6, 1903.[/date]

Dear Professor Peirce:-

Your letter was very welcome indeed, only I sincerely hope and pray that if the Carnegie Institution [u]did[/u] write to you, you did not give them any such strong recommendation as was suggested in your letter to me; for I am afraid they would think that your friendship for me had gotten the upper hand of your judgment! But I assure you it is a source of great pleasure to me, to know that you would be willing to give me such a strong certificate of character. I was very much interested in what you call your "pot boilers", which you enclosed, and should like to see any others that you may care to send. Neither Mrs. Risteen nor myself was aware of the illness of Mrs. Peirce, which I need hardly say we regret very much. When the weather gets a little better I assure you we shall certainly accept your invitation to visit Milford once more, though I cannot tell, at the present writing, when that will be possible. When I have completed the design of my porous plug apparatus I shall certainly send you a rough sketch of it, and would be pleased to have you criticise it if you care to do so. I have completed the design of its main features, but there are a number of details yet to be worked up. I have not heard anything directly from the Carnegie Institution, but I know that my fate has been decided one way or the other, for I see by a recent issue of [u]Science[/u] that the Committee has awarded all the money available, this year, for work of this kind.

Respectfully yours,
A. D. Risteen

[ins]Please give our best regards to Mrs. P.[/ins]
