[u]Torsion[/u]. The strain produced in a body by the action of a pair of equal and opposite couples. In most problems involving torsion the body acted upon is cylindrical or prismatic, [del]in form[/del] and the couples act at or near the ends, in planes normal to the axis of the cylinder or prism, and [del]with[/del] the axes of the couples coincide with the axis of the cylinder or prism. Under these conditions the strain on a normal section of a circular cylinder is a simple shear; [del]and[/del] an originally straight radial line remains straight; [del]and[/del] the intensity of the shearing stress at any point of a given section is proportional to the distance of that point from the axis of the cylinder, provided the material of which the cylinder is composed is not strained beyond the limit of torsional elasticity. The shearing stress produced [del]in a solid[/del] at the outer surface of a solid circular cylinder of radius [u]r[/u], by a pair of couples whose common moment is [u]M[/u], is $2\ M \div \pi\ r^3$.
