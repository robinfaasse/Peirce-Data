Hartford, [date]March 24. 1890[/date]

Dear Professor:

Mrs. Risteen wanted to see the pictures, so I held them till today; but they go forward by this mail, and others will follow if the sun ever shines again.

Now as to space. The only kind of non-Euclidean space that I have read up, is that described, by Newcomb, in which the assumptions are (1) "that space is triply extended, unbounded, without properties dependent upon either position or direction," (and of course like itself everywhere — I mean, in every place it is constituted as in every other place), and that in any given region of small extent it is indistinguishable from Euclidean space; (2) "that it is affected with such a curvature that a right line shall always return into itself at the end of a finite and real distance 2 D", without ceasing, "in any region of limited extent, to be what we conceive a straight line to be; (3) that if two right lines intersect at A and include a very small angle [greek]a[/greek], their distance apart, r units from A, will be s = [fraction]2 [greek]a[/greek] D / [greek]p[/greek][/fraction] sin [fraction]r [greek]p[/greek] / 2 D[/fraction]; (4) that two straight lines can intersect at only one point.
