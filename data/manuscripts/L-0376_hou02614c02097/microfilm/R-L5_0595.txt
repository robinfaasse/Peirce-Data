One moment more! In "three point problem" I say that it is occasionally used in geodesy. In the Massachusetts topographical survey I have known it to be used a number of times when the spherical excess of the reference triangle was sensible. They had a habit of observing this way:

[fig]Geometrical diagram showing a triangular pyramid with points A, B, C, D, E, F, G[/fig]

ABC is the datum triangle. D is next established by the 3 point problem. Then E, F and G in succession. The observations were none too good anyway, and no attention whatever was paid to the indeterminate case in which the point to be determined lies on the circumscribing circle. Often the tertiary points located in this manner could not be depended upon within 8 to 10 feet.

Notice the word "Bicycle" in the C. D. It does not cover the "safety" machine at all, so far as I can see.
