[u]Cremona transformation[/u]. (Often called simply birational transformation)
A birational transformation between two planes. The
example given under birational transformation is a
special case of the Cremona transformation. The general
theory of the Cremona transformation is as follows:
Let $X, Y, Z$ be rational integral functions, all of
the same order $n$, of the coordinates [del]of a point[/del] $x, y, z$,
of a point in the primitive plane, and let $x', y', z'$
be the coordinates of the corresponding point in the
second plane. Then $x' : y' : z' = X : Y : Z$, and to
a given point in the first plane there corresponds but
one point in the second. This is not necessarily the
case with the inverse transformation, for it is plain
that when $x', y', z'$ are given, the equation $x' : y' : z' = $
$X : Y : Z$ gives, in general, $n^2$ points $x, y, z$, instead
of one. If, however, the functions $X, Y, Z$ are
so constituted that the curves $X = 0, Y = 0, Z = 0$ have
$k$ common intersections, then among the $n^2$ points
that correspond in the first plane to $x', y', z'$ in the
second, these $k$ points are included. Disregarding these
