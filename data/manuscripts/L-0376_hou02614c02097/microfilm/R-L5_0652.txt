Hartford, Conn., [date]October 16 1891[/date]

Dear Professor:

I send your MS. on the Arithmetic by today's express, addressed to you at Milford. Just before I went away I destroyed a considerable part of my own work, foolishly perhaps, but the fact is that as I read it over it was really unbearable, and would never do at all. I am not logical enough to arrange the early part of the book, and I ought to have known better than to try it. If you would do the text of notation, numeration, addition, —, x, ÷, $\sqrt{}$ and $\sqrt[3]{}$, I will provide the examples for them by the time you get the text ready, and then, if you like, I will try and provide the text for the commercial part — interest, insurance, stocks, &c., and will supply the examples for this part too. I can do better that way, for I am convinced that I never could get the earlier part of the book into such shape that it would be logical, and intelligible to a youngster.
