these? There is a good telescope here in Hartford, with a
4-inch glass by Clark, and a clock for driving it. I could
easily get the use of it. There is also a diffraction grating
that is said to be good, but I don't know anything about
it. Should be inclined to doubt its worth. The Worcester Free
Institute has a beautiful and valuable grating ruled by Rowland
and originally intended for Sir Wm. Thomson; but I couldn't get
that, because Rowland wouldn't let it go without Prof.
Kimball's promise that he would keep it constantly under his
personal charge.

I should like very much to examine the spectra of some
of these stars, if it could be managed, and if there would
be the most remote likelihood of success. I have a personal
friend here who is very skillful in fine instrument making
and repairing, and I am sure he would be glad to contribute
his services, in fitting up the necessary appliances. I have
an induction coil giving a 3½ inch spark, which could be used
for [ins]giving[/ins] [del]giving[/del] comparison spectra if that were desirable. Do you think
that it would be worth while to make the effort?

Respectfully yours,
