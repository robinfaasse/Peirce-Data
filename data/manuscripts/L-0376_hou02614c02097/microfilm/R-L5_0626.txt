[u]Tschirnhausen's transformation[/u]. A transformation of algebraic
equations, the object of which is to form an equation whose
roots are any rational integral function of the roots of
a given equation. It is based on the following theorem:
The most general rational algebraic transformation
of a root of an equation of the $n$th. degree can be
reduced to an integral transformation of the degree
$n-1$ at most. In the cases of the quadratic and
cubic it follows that the most general rational function
of a root can be reduced to a linear function and
[del]an algebraic[/del] [ins]a[/ins] quadratic function of that root,
respectively. Let $f(x)$ be the given equation, whose
roots are $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3, \dots \alpha_n$. The general mode of
procedure in effecting the transformation is to form
the equation whose roots are $\varphi(\alpha_1), \varphi(\alpha_2), \dots \varphi(\alpha_n)$
where $\varphi(x)$ is a rational and integral function of $x$ of
the degree $n-1$, so that $\varphi(x) \equiv a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + \dots + a_{n-1} x^{n-1}$
Raise $\varphi(x)$ to the different powers $2, 3, 4, \dots n$ in
succession. By means of $f(x) = 0$ these may all be reduced
to the forms
$\varphi^2(x) = b_0 + b_1 x + b_2 x^2 + \dots + b_{n-1} x^{n-1}$
$\varphi^3(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2 + \dots + c_{n-1} x^{n-1}$
$\dots$
$\varphi^n(x) = l_0 + l_1 x + l_2 x^2 + \dots + l_{n-1} x^{n-1}$
