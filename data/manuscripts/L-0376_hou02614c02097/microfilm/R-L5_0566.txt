Dear Professor,

Enclosed is a list of 23 double (or triple) stars to which the spectroscopic method might perhaps be applied. The orbits given in the cases of triple stars are for the closest of them, and since we should care only for these, perhaps the effect of the distant one might be neglected. The symbols are as follow:

$T =$ passage of periastron; $\Omega =$ position of node (i.e. angle between the meridian and the line of intersection of the celestial sphere and the plane of the star's orbit); $\pi =$ longitude of periastron from node, in orbit; $i =$ inclination of plane of orbit (i.e. angle between it and the [ins]tangent[/ins] plane [del]to the[/del] [ins]to the[/ins] celestial sphere); $e =$ eccentricity of orbit; $a =$ semiaxis major of orbit, [del]viewed[/del] when looked at perpendicularly to plane of orbit and at a distance equal to that of the solar system; $P =$ period in years. The last column gives the computer's name.
