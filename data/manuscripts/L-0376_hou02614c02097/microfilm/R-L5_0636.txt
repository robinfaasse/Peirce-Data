The line for which the sum of the distances is zero
is the null line. Four such lines exist for each
triangle, the first of which is obtained by considering
the [del]perpendicular[/del] [ins]distances measured[/ins] toward the inside of the triangle as positive, while the
three others are obtained by considering the perpendicular
toward the exterior of one of the sides as positive. The
first of these null lines passes through the three points
in which the bisectors of the external angles meet
the opposite sides. Each of the other null lines passes
through one of these three points, and through the two
points in which the remaining sides are cut by
the internal bisectors of the opposite angles.

Oblique triangle. A plane or spherical triangle which
has no right angle.

Plane triangle. A triangle whose sides are straight
lines.

Polar triangle. A triangle, A'B'C', corresponding
with a given triangle, ABC, in such a manner
that each vertex of either triangle is a pole
of one of the sides of the other.
