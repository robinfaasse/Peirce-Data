But as he has never known anything of my possibilities as an experimenter, I thought it would be better to refer, for that, to someone who had known me longer and better. I have therefore taken the liberty of referring to you, in the following words:

"With regard to my competence to actually [u]execute[/u] the plans that I have formed, I desire to say that while Professor Gibbs is familiar with the apparatus as I have planned it, he cannot speak of my attainments as an experimenter with the same positiveness, for the reason that we have never been associated in any experimental work. I was graduated from the Worcester Polytechnic Institute in 1885, and while at that Institution I did a very considerable amount of laboratory work; but unfortunately Professor A. S. Kimball, under whose immediate direction I worked, is now dead. I may refer, however, to Professor Charles S. Peirce, with whom I was associated, at one time, on the United States Coast and Geodetic Survey, in connection with his pendulum work. I think he is still living at Milford, Pike county, Pa.; and at all events I am reasonable sure that a letter of inquiry would reach him, if addressed to that place."

In case the Carnegie Institution writes you on this point, I should be everlastingly obliged to you if you felt that you could give me a clean bill of health, so far as the probabilities of my being able to carry out the work is concerned. Of course I do not ask nor expect you to say anything in favor of the apparatus itself, which you have not seen. If you can say a good word for me, I shall strive to my utmost to see that you have nothing to regret for having done so. With the best of regards to you and yours, both from myself and from the "madam", I am,

Very respectfully yours,
A. D. Risteen.

I should very much like to see you again, and will try to do so before another New Year comes!
