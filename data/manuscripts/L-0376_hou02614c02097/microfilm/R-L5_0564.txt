Hartford, [date]Mar. 3, 1891[/date]

Dear Professor:

Your two notes came this morning. When I sent you the copy of "Science" I thought it was the only copy I had. Another one has "shown up" since, and I send it to you by this mail. I will also write to the publisher and find out if a few more copies may not be had. The edition was smaller than usual, I believe, — at any rate I remember that I had some difficulty in getting copies. Your application of [del]it[/del] [ins]the method[/ins] to the determination of the constant of Non-Euclidean space is very beautiful. I have thought of a number of stars that it would be interesting to try it on, and in a few days I will send you a list of them and see what you think of them.

Regarding the Arithmetic. I have been busy at it in my spare hours, though I have not written you much about it. I will send, shortly, a skeleton of it, for criticism and revision.

The photographic [del]method[/del] trouble has worried me considerably, and I am glad it is settled. I wrote Lundelius saying that I would pay him, but he wanted $12.00 and I couldn't spare that much at the time, on account of other expenses.
