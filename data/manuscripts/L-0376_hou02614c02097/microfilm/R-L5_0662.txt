Hartford, Conn. [date]January 5, 1903.[/date]

Dear Professor:-

For the past three years I have been taking a course in the Yale post-graduate department of mathematical physics, and next June I come up for a Ph. D. I am sure that will be good news to you. It will be good news to me also, if my thesis proves satisfactory! I am writing it on the subject of absolute temperature, and I am re-computing the corrections to the gas thermometer, to reduce its readings to the absolute scale, making use of the determinations of the coefficients of expansion made at the International Bureau by Chappuis, and of the studies of the departure of gases from Boyle's law made by Amagat. I have not yet completed the computation, but I have gone far enough to satisfy myself that the "porous plug" experiments of Joule and Kelvin are by no means as accurate as could be desired. In fact, they are far less accurate than the various secondary data that are required in the computation. So I have conceived the idea of repeating those experiments with apparatus strictly "up to date". I have devised an improved form of apparatus, in which every source of error that I can think of has been eliminated, and Professor Gibbs of Yale (under whom I am applying for my degree) says that he thinks it is a "very pretty piece of apparatus." The expense of making it, and of carrying out the work, will be considerable, however, and I have therefore applied to the Carnegie Institution for a grant of $750., to assist me.
