The 100 people have written again asking me to "please
send direct" def. of "order of a transformation". Now
the only thing I found in this line was the following
quotation from Cremona: "When the points of a space $S'$
have a (1,1) correspondence with those of another space $S$
in such a manner that the planes and the right lines of
$S$ correspond to surfaces $F$ of $m\text{th}$ order, and to curves
$C$ of the $n\text{th}$ order in the former space $S'$, I say that
the transformation of $S$ into $S'$ is of the $m\text{th}$ degree, and
that the inverse transformation is of the $n\text{th}$ degree."
In answer to their note I have sent this. True, I can't
make out precisely what it means, but Cremona
wrote it, apparently in English; as I found it as
a preliminary def. in an article by him on transforma-
tions. If he wrote it, presumably it is all right.
Now the question is, is there a difference between the
order and the degree of a transformation? I found
no definition whatever of the "order of a transformation".

Risteen.
