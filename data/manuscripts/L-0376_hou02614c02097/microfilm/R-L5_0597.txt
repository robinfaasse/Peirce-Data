Three point problem. (Also called Pothenot's problem.)
A problem often occurring in topographical work, and
occasionally in geodesy. Having given
three points, A, B, C, it is required
to determine the position of an observing
station, P, knowing only the angles
[fig]Triangle ABC with interior point P and angles theta, phi, alpha, beta[/fig] [ins]$\alpha$[/ins] and $\beta$ and the sides and angles
of the triangle ABC. The
geometric solution is very simple.
On the side b as a chord draw a circle
containing the angle $\beta$, and on the side a as a chord draw a
circle containing the angle $\alpha$. The intersection of these circles
will be the is the point sought. The trigonometric solution
is as follows:
$\theta + \varphi$ [del]$\theta + \theta$[/del] $= 360^\circ - (\alpha + \beta + C)$ (1)
$\overline{CP} \sin \alpha = a \sin \theta$ and $\overline{CP} \sin \beta = b \sin \varphi$
hence $\frac{\sin \varphi}{\sin \theta} = \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{\sin \beta}{\sin \alpha} = \tan \gamma$ (suppose)
and [del]$\frac{\sin \varphi + \sin \theta}{\sin \varphi - \sin \theta}$[/del] $\frac{\sin \theta + \sin \varphi}{\sin \theta - \sin \varphi} = \frac{1 + \tan \gamma}{1 - \tan \gamma} = \tan (\gamma + 45^\circ)$

Hence $\cot \frac{\theta - \varphi}{2} = \tan (\gamma + 45^\circ) \cdot \cot \frac{\theta + \varphi}{2}$ (2)
From (1) and (2) we can find $\theta$ and $\varphi$, and then
$\overline{CP} = \frac{a \sin \theta}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{b \sin \varphi}{\sin \beta}$ (3)
