(2) Another circle, sometimes called the six point circle, is found as follows: $S$ being the symmedian point, join $AS$, $BS$, $CS$, and take $A'$ on $AS$, $B'$ on $BS$, and $C'$ on $CS$ in such a manner that $A'B'$, [del]or[/del] $B'C'$, $C'A'$ are parallel respectively to $AB$, $BC$, $CA$. <u>Spherical triangle</u>. A triangle on the surface of a sphere, formed by arcs of great circles.

<u>Spherical excess</u> of a triangle. The sum of the internal angles of a spherical triangle is [del]always[/del] [ins]necessarily[/ins] greater than two and less than six right angles. The excess [del]of[/del] of this sum over two right angles is called the spherical excess of the triangle.
