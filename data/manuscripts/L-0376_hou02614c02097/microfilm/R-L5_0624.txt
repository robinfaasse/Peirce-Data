<u>Inverse transformation</u>. The transformation between the [ins]first[/ins] given plane $S$ and the plane $S'$ is called <u>direct</u>; the transformation between $S'$ and $S$ is called <u>inverse</u>.

<u>Transformation by reciprocal directions</u>. A transformation in which the spherical images of corresponding points, on the sphere of radius unity, placed anywhere in space, are inverse to one another with respect to a [ins]fixed[/ins] point $A$, [del]situated on a[/del]. The line joining two corresponding points is parallel to the line joining their spherical images, and the planes tangent at corresponding points intersect in a fixed plane, which is [del]perpendicular[/del] [ins]normal[/ins] to that diameter of the sphere on which the point $A$ lies. This transformation is rational, and in general to a given surface $\Sigma$, two surfaces $\Sigma'$, or at least two different nappes of a single surface, correspond.
