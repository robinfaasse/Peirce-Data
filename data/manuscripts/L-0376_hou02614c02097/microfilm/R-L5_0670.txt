I have read the "Electron"
book with much interest,
and will return it to you
very soon. There are some
points in it I wish to look
over again, first.

In a general way, the sub-
ject is fascinating, and
it looks to me like a sort
of first approximation to
the truth. But I wish some
able man would come along
and carry out your idea of
pushing an apparently
fruitful and promising
idea to its limit. For
example, the atomic (or molec-

ular) theory proved illuminating,
by attributing the constancy
of certain things to statistical
steadiness of certain highly
complicated motions. Now the
electron hypothesis pushes the
idea a step further. But I
note that it is assumed, in
this book, that the conservation
of energy governs the motions
even of the single electrons,
individually. I should like
to see someone disregard
the conservation of energy so
far as the individual electron
is concerned, and deduce it
as a statistical fact, true
for large groups of electrons
