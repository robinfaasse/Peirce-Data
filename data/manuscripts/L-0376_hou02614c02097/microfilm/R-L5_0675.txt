Dear Professor:

I found Duhamel's Theorem to-day, while looking for something else. It was quoted by someone in Liouville, in the course of an article on Series, to the convergence of which it relates. It applies [ins]in case[/ins] [del]when[/del] the ratio $\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_n}$ tends toward unity as $n$ increases. "Si l'on suppose ce rapport mis sous la forme $\frac{1}{1+\alpha}$, il y aura convergence ou divergence, suivant que la limite du produit $n\alpha$ (pour $n=\infty$) sera plus grande ou plus petite que l'unité" If $n\alpha$ becomes negative for $n=\infty$, it is less than unity, and therefore there is divergence. The theorem says nothing about the case $n\alpha_{n=\infty} = 1$, [del]and of course[/del] it does not apply unless $n\alpha_{n=\infty}$ approaches something definite as $n$ increases.

I suppose it is too late for you to make use of it, but I thought it would be best to send it along and make sure.
