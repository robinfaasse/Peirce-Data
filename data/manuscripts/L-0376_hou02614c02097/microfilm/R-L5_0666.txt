Hartford, Conn.,
[date]July 19, 1904[/date].

Dear Professor Peirce:-

I should very much like to come to Milford to see you for a day or two, if it would be convenient for you to have me. There is no particular errand in view, but Mrs. Risteen and I have wanted to pay you a short visit, for some years past, and now would be the most convenient time for me. I do not know whether you are aware or not that I am a Ph. D. now. I took the degree at Yale, last summer. New Haven is only 37 miles from here, and I used to go down to take lectures in the afternoon. I had lectures in thermodynamics from Professor Gibbs alone, in the evening; and I can assure you that that was a rare treat.

I can leave here about Saturday of this week (that is, July 23), if that would be convenient for you. If it would not be convenient for us to come, please be entirely frank to say so, and we will postpone it until some other time. I mention this particular date, because I expect, at that time, to have my vacation. I could come towards the end of it, if you preferred; but I suggested the commencement, in the fear that I might be called back to Hartford for some reason, in such a way as to have my vacation curtailed somewhat; and in that way I might get cheated out of the pleasure that we have both anticipated for so long a time.

Kindly give our regards to Mrs. Peirce.

Yours, respectfully,

A. D. Risteen

P. S. There are no little Risteens.
