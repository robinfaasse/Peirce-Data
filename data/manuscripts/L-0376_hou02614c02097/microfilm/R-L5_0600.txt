Eddy's theorem (published [date]1876[/date]. Henry T. Eddy, still living. Prof. of math. in University of Cincinnati.) relates to the elastic arch. It is as follows: "If in any arch that equilibrium polygon (due to the weights) be constructed which has the same horizontal thrust as the arch actually exerts; and if its closing line be drawn from considerations of the conditions imposed by the supports etc.; and if, furthermore, the curve of the arch itself be regarded as another equilibrium polygon due to some system of loading not given, and its closing line be also found from the same considerations respecting supports etc.; then when these two polygons are so placed that there closing lines coincide, and their areas partially cover each other, the ordinates intercepted between these two polygons are proportional to the real bending moments acting in the arch." Called by Eddy the "Theorem Respecting the Coincidence of Closing Lines." He says: "The possibility of obtaining a complete graphical solution of the elastic arch in all cases depends upon this theorem." See Am. Jour. Math. Vol I. No. 3. Also Van Nostrand's Magazine, Vol. XVIII. Also (probably) Proc. Am. Assn. Advanc[sup]t[/sup]. Science for [date]1876[/date].
