Complementary transformation. [del]A transformation of pseudo-
spherical surfaces; a special case of Bäcklund's
transformation. Let $S$ be a pseudospherical surface
of uniform negative curvature, $-\frac{1}{R^2}$, and let $G$
be a singly infinite series of geodetic lines drawn
upon it. The tangents to all these lines form the
normals of a series of parallel surfaces $\Sigma$. The
surface $S$ is an envelope of the evolutes of the
surface $\Sigma$. There is also another envelope, $S'$,
and this surface $S'$ is said to be complementary
to $S$ in reference to the geodetic lines $G$.[/del]

Central transformation. [del]A transformation in which there
exists between the radii vectores of $n+1$ curves a
relation of the form $F(\rho, \rho_1, \rho_2, \dots \rho_n) = 0$,
$\rho, \rho_1, \rho_2, \dots \rho_n$ being drawn from the same origin
and in the same direction. Special cases of
this kind of relation are $\rho^n = \rho_1 \rho_2 \rho_3 \dots \rho_n$[/del]
