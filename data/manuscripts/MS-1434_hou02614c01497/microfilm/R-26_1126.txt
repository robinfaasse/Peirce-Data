This list of [del]ethical[/del] classes of motives may, it is hoped, answer as a tolerable sample upon which to base reflections upon the relative acceptability of different motives as ultimate; and it makes no pretension to a more scientific value than that. The enumeration has been so ordered as to bring into prominence the various degrees of generality. It would be, well to compare the different ethical classes of motives in other respects. For instance, in express obedience we have dependence in its highest degree. In acting for approbation and in reverence we have a sort of generalized obedience. In imitative instinct, maintaining custom, and naturalism, an external exemplar is followed. In persuasion, love of home, patriotism, evolutionism, [del]there is a purpose directed to some[/del] [ins]the action is for the sake of[/ins] external object. In provision, benevolence, altruism, historicism, experimentalism, there is a bending of the motive toward something not present. In preference, self-preservation, utilitarianism, eutelism, dialecticism, pancratism, there is a balancing of one [ins]consideration[/ins] against another. In unrestrained desire, the pursuit of pleasure, individualism, sentimentalism, rationalism, educationalism, religionism, the [del]i[/del] element of otherness may be said to have disappeared. Again, in unrestrained desire immediacy is at its acme, In preference and pursuit of pleasure feeling is [ins]the chief thing but is[/ins] restrained.
