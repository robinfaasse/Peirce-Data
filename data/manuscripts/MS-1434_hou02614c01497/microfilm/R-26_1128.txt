Dr. Carl Pearson in an [u]Introductory[/u] chapter of ethical contents sounds the dominant note of his book. He opens with the declaration that our conduct ought to be regulated by the Darwinian theory. Since that theory is an attempt to show how natural causes tend to [del]give[/del] impart to breeds of animals and plants characters which, in the long run, promote reproduction and thus insure the continuance of the breeds, it would logically follow that making Darwinism the guide of conduct ought to mean that the continuance of the race is to be taken as the [del]Summum[/del] bonum and [del]multiplicamini[/del] as the epitome of the moral law.
