[u]Promptuarium of
Analytical Geometry[/u]

If $P_1$ and $P_2$ are any two points, any other point
$P_3$ in the same line may be represented by
$P_3 = \lambda P_1 + (1-\lambda) P_2$
where $\lambda$ is any number. Transposing $P_3$ and multi-
plying by any coefficients, we find that there are
three numbers $a_1, a_2, a_3$ such that
$a_1 + a_2 + a_3 = 0$
$a_1 P_1 + a_2 P_2 + a_3 P_3 = 0$
and conversely such formulae show that the three
points so connected lie in one line.

In like manner, if $L_1$ and $L_2$ are two intersecting
lines, any other line in the same plane pencil
may be represented by
$L_3 = \lambda L_1 + (1-\lambda) L_2$
and the equations
$a_1 + a_2 + a_3 = 0$
$a_1 L_1 + a_2 L_2 + a_3 L_3 = 0$
show that the three lines concerned pass through one
point and lie in one plane.

If $P_1, P_2, P_3$ are any three points, [del]any[/del] [ins]points in the[/ins]
same plane [del]must be in the line through $P_3$ and some
point in the line through $P_1$ and $P_2$, and is[/del] therefore
$P_4 = x_1 P_1 + x_2 P_2 + x_3 P_3$
where $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 1$
or
$x_1 P_1 + x_2 P_2 + x_3 P_3 + x_4 P_4 = 0$
where $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + x_4 = 0$
Or we may say that if the four points are coplanar
then and only then three numbers $a, b, c$ can be
found such that
$(a-b-c) P_1 + (a+b-c) P_2 + (a-b+c) P_3 + (a+b+c) P_4 = 0$

Similar formulae hold for four lines $L_1, L_2, L_3, L_4$ pro-
vided $L_1$ and $L_2$ intersect and $L_3$ and $L_4$ intersect and
in the plane of $L_1$ and $L_2$.
