If four points $P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4$ lie in a plane we can
find a fifth point $P_5$ in a line with $P_1$ and $P_2$ and
also with $P_3$ and $P_4$. For assuming

$$(a-b-c)P_1 + (-a+b-c)P_2 + (a+b-c)P_3 + (a+b+c)P_4 = 0$$

we have simply to take $P_1$ and $P_2$ [del]affected[/del] [ins]equated[/ins] with these
same coefficients and equate their sum to $P_5$ mul-
tiplied by the sum of these coefficients. Thus, the
equation

$$-2cP_5 = (a-b-c)P_1 + (-a+b-c)P_2$$

shows that $P_1, P_2, P_5$ are in a line, and subtracting
this equation from the first

$$2cP_5 = (a+b-c)P_3 + (a+b+c)P_4$$

which shows that $P_3, P_4, P_5$ are in a line.

In like manner we find a point $P_6$ in the line
through $P_1$ and $P_3$ and also in the line through $P_2$ and $P_4$.
Namely

$$-2bP_6 = (a-b-c)P_1 + (-a-b+c)P_3$$

$$2bP_6 = (-a+b-c)P_2 + (a+b+c)P_4$$

So we find a point $P_7$ in the line $P_1 P_4$ and in the
line $P_2 P_3$. Namely,

$$2aP_7 = (a-b-c)P_1 + (a+b+c)P_4$$

$$-2aP_7 = (-a+b-c)P_2 + (-a-b+c)P_3.$$

the second members. Then subtract this [del]first[/del] [ins]second[/ins] equation
in $P_6$ from the first in $P_7$ and we get

$$(a-b-c)P_1 + (a-b+c)P_2 + 2bP_6 - 2aP_7 = 0$$

$$-(a+b+c)P_1 + (a-b+c)P_3 - 2cP_5 + 2aP_7 = 0$$

Subtract the first in $P_5$ from the last in $P_6$ and we have

$$(-a+b-c)P_2 + (a+b-c)P_3 + 2cP_5 - 2bP_6 = 0.$$
