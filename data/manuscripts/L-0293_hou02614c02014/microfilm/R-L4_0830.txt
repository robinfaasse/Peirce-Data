The paper renders it quite clear that "argument" is there used
in a sense which covers every movement of thought proper.

The author did not claim that there was anything new in
maintaining that reasoning is capable of being represented cor-
rectly as consisting in substitution. But, perhaps it was there,
for the first time, shown [u]how[/u] this is true of all kinds of inference.
The theory there given was not a "substitution of similars," which
Jevons subsequently proposed.

It may be remarked that every logical substitution, whatever
may be resolved into two steps, [del]the first[/del] [ins]that which is logically the first (whether temporally so or not),[/ins] consisting in an in-
sertion, and the second in an omission. An inference is, more
correctly, regarded as an insertion of the conclusion into the
[del]thought[/del] concept of the universe. Only subsequently, may it
happen that the premisses are dropped out of mind.
