The author of that paper being pretty well read in logic, could, of course, not claim that the fact that reasoning [del]was[/del] is capable of being correctly represented as a substitution was a new idea, or had not been explicitly said many times. It may be remarked that every logical substitution whatever may be resolved into two steps, the first being an insertion and the second a removal; and an inference is much more correctly represented not as a substitution, but as an increment of the [del]thought[/del] [ins]judgment[/ins] of the premises by combining it with the [del]thought[/del] [ins]judgment[/ins] of the conclusion. It is only by a subsequent step that the premises pass out of mind.
