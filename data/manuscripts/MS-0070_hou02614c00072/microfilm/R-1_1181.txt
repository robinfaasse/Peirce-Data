By an [u]ordered collection[/u], I mean one of which each member
has a peculiar relation to the whole; as for example, if one is defi-
nitely the first, another definitely the second, a definite one the third, etc.
or if there is any other formal relation by which each is different from
all the others. There are also distinct collections which are formally
divided into subcollections and it may be in more than one way, whether
inadequately, adequately, or superfluously. By [u]adequately[/u], I mean just
sufficiently to make the collection an ordered one.

With this notation (7) can be expressed as follows, using
Hebrew letters to denote definite collections:

[u]$\Pi_x \Sigma_i \Sigma_j \Sigma_\alpha 2_{xi} \cdot 2_{xj} \cdot s_\alpha \cdot q_{\alpha i} \cdot \bar{q}_{\alpha j}$[/u]

As a further abbreviation, let us use the letter $\sigma$ so that

$\sigma_{ij} = \Pi_\alpha \bar{s}_\alpha \psi \bar{q}_{\alpha i} \psi q_{\alpha j}$     $\bar{\sigma}_{ij} = \Sigma_\alpha s_\alpha q_{\alpha i} \bar{q}_{\alpha j}$
