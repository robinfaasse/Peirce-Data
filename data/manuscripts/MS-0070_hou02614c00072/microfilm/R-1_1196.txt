[pn]N 1[/pn] Analysis of Demonstrations concerning definite Positive Integers.

[del]The[/del] [ins]X[/ins] Universe of l.c. italics is the aggregate of definite Integers not negative.
The Universe of Greek minuscules is the aggregate of definite characters which
might conceivably be predicated of integers not negative.

$q_{\alpha u}$ means that $u$ has the character $\alpha$.

(1) $\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{\beta} \Pi_{u} (q_{\alpha u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} q_{\beta u}) \cdot (\bar{q}_{\alpha u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\beta u})$ i.e. Every character has a negative.

(2) $\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{\beta} \Sigma_{\gamma} \Pi_{u} (q_{\alpha u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\beta u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} q_{\gamma u}) \cdot (q_{\alpha u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} q_{\beta u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\gamma u})$ [ins]Every pair of characters has a compound of them.[/ins]

Instead of directly introducing the relation of "as small as", I make a character of
certain characters, consisting in each of them belonging to every integer smaller
than an integer to which it belongs. $S_{\alpha}$ means that $\alpha$ is such a character.

(3) $\Pi_{u} \Sigma_{\alpha} q_{\alpha u} \cdot S_{\alpha}$ Every integer has a character common to all lower integers (formal)

(4) $\Pi_{\alpha} \Sigma_{u} \bar{S}_{\alpha} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\alpha u}$ i.e. There is no [del]greatest[/del] [ins]highest[/ins] integer

(5) $\Sigma_{u} \Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{v} q_{\alpha u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{S}_{\alpha} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\alpha v}$ i.e. There is a lowest integer (i.e. zero)

(6) $\Pi_{\alpha} \Pi_{\beta} \Pi_{u} \bar{S}_{\alpha} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{S}_{\beta} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\alpha u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} q_{\beta u} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\alpha v} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\beta v}$ [ins]i.e. if one integer has an S-character that another has not, it has all that the latter has.[/ins]

(7) $\Pi_{u} \Pi_{v} \Sigma_{\alpha} \Pi_{\beta} [del]\bar{N}[/del] \bar{S}_{\alpha} \cdot (q_{\alpha u} \cdot \bar{q}_{\alpha v} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\alpha u} \cdot q_{\alpha v}) \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} q_{\beta u} \cdot \bar{q}_{\beta v} \mathbin{\bar{\lor}} \bar{q}_{\beta u} \cdot q_{\beta v}$
[ins]Of any two integers, one has an S-character which the other has not, and either one has an S-character which the other has not, or else they are identical.[/ins]
