[pn]N 10[/pn] The utility of the symbols 1, 2, 3, etc is increased by employing
them as follows: [u]1[/u]i, [u]1[/u]ij, [u]1[/u]ijk, etc means that the indices denote
the same existing individual.

[u]2[/u]xi, [u]2[/u]xij, [u]2[/u]xijk etc means that the individuals denoted by the
indices belong to the doublet x

[u]2[/u]ijk, [u]2[/u]ijkl, etc means that all the individuals denoted by the indices
are members of one doublet.

([u]2[/u]·T)ij, ([u]2[/u]·T)ijk, etc, mean that the individuals denoted by the
indices belong to one doublet but are not all
one individual

[u]3[/u]xi, [u]3[/u]xij, [u]3[/u]xijk, [u]3[/u]xijkl mean that i, j, etc [ins]all[/ins] belong to the triplet x

[u]3[/u]ijkl, [u]3[/u]ijklm, etc mean that i, j, etc all belong to one triplet

([u]3[/u]·T)ijkl means that i, j, k, l all belong to one triplet but are not all [del]one[/del]

([u]3[/u]·[u]2[/u])ijkl means that i, j, k, l, are three different existing individuals

([u]32[/u])ijklmnpq [del]means (where note the absence of a dot - not 3·2, but 32) means that
the individuals indicated are all members of a triplet of doublets.[/del]
