[del]Of[/del] [ins]The[/ins] character, [u]1[/u], which I introduced in [date]1882[/date], when I was teaching
logic in the Johns Hopkins University, was in my mind one of
a class of notations which I left unmentioned in order that
some one of my pupils might have the pleasure of finding
it out for himself; but as nobody has, so far as I have noticed,
in the [del]qty of a century[/del] [ins]¾ of a generation[/ins] that has elapsed, I will give some illustrations
of the class.

$1_{ij}$ means $j$ is a member of the singlet, $i$.
$2_{ij}$ " " " " " " doublet, $i$, [ins]or unordered pair of objects[/ins]
$3_{ij}$ " " " " " " [del]triplet[/del] [ins]triplet, $i$, or unordered triad of objects[/ins]
$4_{ij}$ " " " " " " [del]quadruplet[/del] [ins]quadruplet, $i$, or unordered collection of 4[/ins]
$\vdots$
$9_{ij}$ " " " " " " nonaplet, $i$, " " " " " 9.
$\text{X}_{ij}$ " " " " " " decaplet, $i$, " " " " " 10.

Ordered collections I call, medads (0), monads, dyads, triads, etc.
Indeterminate as to being ordered are binion (or pair), trinion, quaternion, quinion,
senion, septene, octene, novene, dene (or denion), etc.
