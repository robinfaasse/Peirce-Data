[u]Distribution of the bright stars[/u]

The first magnitude stars are concentrated toward the milky way. Yet from Vega to Capella, a fourth of the galactic circle, there is not one near it. But on the opposite side within a shorter arc lie Sirius, Canopus, [del]x[/del] Crucis, Hadar and Almezan, a fourth of the whole number, with Procyon, Betelgeuse, Rigel, Achernar, Fomalhaut, Antares, and Spica, probably within [del]a[/del] [ins]the same[/ins] quarter of the sphere. On the other hand, the polygon [del]bounded by lines[/del] having at its vertices Altair, Fomalhaut, Achernar, Rigel, Antares, Capella, Pollux, Regulus, Arcturus, Antares, Vega, embracing on its north side nearly half the sphere, I think, contains no single star of the first magnitude. Distant more than 90° from Canopus are only Arcturus, Capella, Vega, Altair, Antares?, Spica? Pollux? or about 1/4 of all. No great circle can be drawn embracing within the half sphere nearest it more than 3/4 of the first magnitude stars; so that the concentration toward Canopus is about as marked as that toward the Milky way.
