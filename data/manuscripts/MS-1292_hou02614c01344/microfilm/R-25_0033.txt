[u]How Did Science Originate?[/u]

I must confess that if the Editor of [u]Science[/u] grants a lodging to this paper, which would not know where else to lay its head, it will be by favor rather than obligation; for it makes no pretension to adducing any new facts or to solving any scientific problem. It only undertakes to define a problem at present somewhat confused with others, and to show how far facts already made out go toward the solution of it. All I hope to establish is that our science has grown out of a Babylonian embryon, and that, notwithstanding all the ancient respect for the "learning of the Egyptians", there never was any living science in Egypt.

Rare phenomena are generally hard to explain, — above all rare psychical phenomena, as our generation has had occasion to learn. Now that science in a living condition is a rare phenomenon in this world must be admitted. It flourished in Alexandria for a few centuries, next in Western Europe in the seventeenth century, and now again since about the time of Lavoisier.
