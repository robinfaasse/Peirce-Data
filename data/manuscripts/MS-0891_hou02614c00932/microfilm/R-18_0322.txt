Observations may be wrong, but still it is not very
likely they are quite the contrary of the fact, and as
long as they are not, they are not essentially false;
they only need additions and modifications.

So likewise, logical fallacies produce propositions,
false indeed, as they were intended, but yet with a
modified meaning, true. This is obviously the case
with the Illicit Process. In the cases of the Un-
distributed Middle, Negative Premises, and Ambigu-
ous Middle, a modification of one of the pre-
misses will always make a conclusion pos-
sible.

given rise to the common saying that "geni-
