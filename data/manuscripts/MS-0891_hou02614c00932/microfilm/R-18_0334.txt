LXVI.
[date]1864 December 18.[/date]
Emotion is vague incomprehensible thought.
That explains
1[sup]st[/sup] why the highest truths can only be felt
2[sup]nd[/sup] why untried emotions are the most treache-
rous guides. It is antiquity [ins]or custom[/ins] which gives dignity to an emotion.

LXVII.
[date]1864 December 18[/date]
There are three essentials to every possible re-
ligion. Miracle. Myth. Mystery.
Miracle is the extraordinary circumstance [del]s[/del] evidencing
something mighty and specially connected with a
given religion.
Myth is the story of the god made imaginable.
Mystery is the great symbol awaking emotion.

LXVIII.
[date]1864 December 26[/date]
Government must rest upon a wide-
spread respect either for the voice of the
people or for the commands of the great or for
ancient customs.

LXIX.
[date]1865 May 2[/date]
It is a corollary from No. lxvi that there is
a truth of emotions which is essentially the
same as the truth of conceptions. The truths of
religion are inconceivable. How then is a religious
truth to be stated? Only in propositions; but in
propositions [ins]absolutely[/ins] inconceivable. But that which is
absolutely inconceivable is impossible. In pro-
positions therefore which though absurd present
an absurdity which is virtually solved by
an emotion which is true.
Anthropomorphology held as inadequate is
then the only true creed.

LXXI
[date]1867 March 2.[/date]
Such miracles as are peculiar to any one
religion are highly dubious. That sort which
are undeniable belongs to all religions in
common.
