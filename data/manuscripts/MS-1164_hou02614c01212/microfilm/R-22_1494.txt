12. A lace with tags at the end. Such laces, about 8 inches long, consisting of three differently colored strands of [del]strip[/del] yarn twisted together and [del]with[/del] having their ends wrapped with iron, were used in the middle ages to fasten the clothes together. They gave place to buttons in the seventeenth century. Holding up the hose, they corresponded to our braces or suspenders, and hence the ridiculous allusions to their breaking, or to being "untrussed." They were also much used as stakes in gaming, as forfeits, and as gratuities; circumstances explaining many allusions in old writers.
