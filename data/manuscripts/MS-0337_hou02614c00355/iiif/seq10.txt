[del]What makes up the identity of things?
A general problem of which the identity
of a person is only a specific case.
Not physical identity - for that is relative.
[ins]Identity of individuals[/ins]
(a) of material masses.
(b) of machines or artifacts.
(c) of organisms.
(d) of persons.
(e) of institutions, nations, etc.

I. Two factors:
(1) Continuity of the stuff - of the substance
(2) Identity of the form - or type -
But there is nothing like absolute identity of
stuff even in a lump of clay.
(a) The identity of a physical object is relative
to our needs. The desk is the same desk so
long as its parts are together & the substance has
continuity in space. If taken apart & put
together, we call it the same desk - though
it is [ins]not[/ins] the same continuous block.
The ship of Theseus.
(b) The identity of a machine.
The parts may be replaced - so long as the 
form is the same. Continuity of existence.
(c) Identity of an organism.
The metabolism of the animal involves a 
constantly changing material. It is the same 
individual so long as its organic type is 
maintained and the process of change is
gradual and continuous.
(d) Identity of a person.
What more than an organism?
Psychological continuity - i. e. Memory.
Locke's theory of memory.
Personal identity is not identity of substance
(which is unknown) but identity of consciousness.
So far as consciousness can be extended 
backwards to any past action or thought, so 
far reaches the identity of that person.
A person is a self-conscious individual.
Objections to this theory.
(1) It confuses identity with the knowledge of
identity. I am the same person who did
this or that even if I have forgotten it.
A person in a dreamless sleep is the same person.
(2) The memory is often deceptive. I may 
seem to remember what I never did.
(3) The memory is only of the personal 
experiences - not of the whole life.
Is a man the same person as the child he
once was? Yes. Why? because of the continuity
of his life - the organic and psychological 
continuity.
(e) Identity of an institution. 
A college - a nation. 
Continuity of purpose - of law - of tradition -
of personnel.

Personal identity.
The case of double personality.
The case of the amnesiac.
Is he the same person?
Legally - yes.
Morally -?
Psychologically - there is a break.
The self is a complex of memories, habits,
feelings, purposes.
It is a unity of consciousness.
But it is a growing, changing unity.
The identity of the self is a functional
identity - not a substantial identity.
It is the identity of a process.
The "I" of today is continuous with the 
"I" of yesterday.
This continuity is what we mean by 
personal identity.

Summary:
Identity is not a simple fact - but a 
complex relationship.
It involves continuity of existence and
identity of form or character.
In the case of persons, it involves 
psychological continuity - of which 
memory is the most important factor.[/del]
