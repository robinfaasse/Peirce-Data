Then the stress has to be considered. In pronouncing the
first syllable of "dearly," the [del]main[/del] [ins]greatest[/ins] force must not come at
the very beginning. Nor yet at the end. It must come nearer
the beginning than the end. On [del]both[/del] the accented syllable
of "beloved" the greatest stress will fall a little nearer the
end of the syllable than the beginning. On the accented syllable
of brethren there will be two maxima of stress, one toward
the beginning the other toward the end. The reason of this
is that the sentiment of this word is complex. It has two
elements. It will require industrious practice merely to get the
stresses right upon these three words, after you have succeeded
in producing the right quality of voice.

Now for the inflections, that is, the changes in the pitch of the
voice during the utterance of one syllable. There is a certain general
character which must belong to the [del]reading[/del] [ins]inflections[/ins] in reading the
service throughout. A pure tone without inflection is not speaking,
at all; it is singing. The great objection to chanting the service
is that you thus lose all the expressiveness of inflection.
But the solemnity of the church service requires that the inflections
should not extend over quite as much as one full tone of the
gamut. In places, they may with propriety be reduced to semitones.
