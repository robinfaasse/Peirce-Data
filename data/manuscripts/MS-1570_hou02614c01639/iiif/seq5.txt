How is that address to be read? Let me ask you to take
that for your first study. For that address gives the tone
to the whole service; and if it be badly read it may in
a measure defeat the purpose of the whole.
Now, there is this very important consideration to be
observed throughout. The greater part of your congregation
have been brought up in the church. They are perfectly
familiar with [ins]the[/ins] words and their meaning; and if you betray
your elocutionary technique, in the least degree, it will
have a chilling, and annoying, and distracting, and
irritating effect, the very reverse of what you desire.
So you must be careful to adhere in the main to the
conventional way of reading.
Still, that gives room for good and for bad reading.
The most difficult part of the whole service to read,
without comparison, are the first three words Dearly
beloved brethren. It is impossible they should be read right
unless the sentiments of them is welling up from your
heart. But [del]even[/del] [ins]that[/ins] is not enough. It must be feigned too.
The quality of the voice is all-important. It must be rich
and warm and vibrating. Every particle of air which
escapes from the larynx must be set in vibration. There
must not be the least bit of huskiness or whispering
