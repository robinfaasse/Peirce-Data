Let us rather resort to mathematics. My father defined mathematics as the science which draws necessary conclusions. Without stopping to dispute about definitions, — a kind of disputation which except between the most accomplished reasoners is apt to degenerate into logomachy, — it is certain a mathematician could not have deliberately said that, unless the acknowledged branches of mathematics were the chief theatre of necessary inference. Each branch of mathematics is a study of the relations involved in some ideal system. I might say an ideal infinite system; for finite systems, such as the icosihedron form only subordinate subjects of study. Of these infinite systems there are two kinds, the discrete, or number, and the continuous, or space. Of systems of number, the chief is the system of [del]the[/del] [ins]the[/ins] positive integers.
