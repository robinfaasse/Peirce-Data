This history of logic is not altogether without an in-
terest as a branch of history. For so far as [ins]the[/ins] logic of an
age adequately represents the methods of thought of that
age, its history is a history of the human mind in its
most essential relation, — that is to say with reference
to its power of investigating truth. But the chief value of
the study of historical philosophy is that it disciplines the
mind to regard philosophy with a cold and scientific
eye and not with passion as though philosophers were
contestants.
