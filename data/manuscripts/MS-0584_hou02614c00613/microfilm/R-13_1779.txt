Scotus Erigena was an Irishman who lived in the ninth
century, — when Ireland was very far beyond the rest
of Western Europe in intellectual culture, — when in fact
Ireland alone had any learning, — & was sending mission-
aries to France, England, & Germany who first roused these
countries from utter barbarism. He has excited great inter-
est in our own day and many books have been written
about him. Various editions of his different works have been
published of which the most important is his De Divisione
Naturae. Hauréau has in the 20[sup]th[/sup] volume of the Notices of manus-
cripts of the French academy published some extracts from a
gloss supposed to be by him upon Porphyry. Works upon his life
and writings of Scotus have been published by [ins]Hjort[/ins] Staudenmaier, Taillandier,
Möller, Christlieb, and Huber. Although he is not chiefly a
logician [del]his[/del] his writings are of great interest for this history of
logic and I should gladly devote several lectures to the consider-
ation of them. This pleasure I must deny myself and shall speak
of Scotus Erigena not to explain his position but only to throw
a light on those who followed after him and were influenced by
him. He is usually and rightly reckoned as an extreme realist and
yet the extremest [ins]such as[/ins] nominalists, Roscellin were regarded as
his followers. How could this be?
