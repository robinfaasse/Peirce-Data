The conception of the representation of something [del]to[/del] [ins]by[/ins]
somebody obviously involves [del]a[/del] perceptual judgment.
The mind proposes to tell the mind's future self what
the character of the present percept is. [ins]\*[/ins] The percept, on
the contrary, stands on its own legs and makes no
professions of any kind.

There are several other points of contrast between
the perceptual judgment and the percept that are
calculated to exhibit their disparateness. The
judgment, "This chair appears yellow," separates the
color from the chair, making [del]one[/del] the one predicate and
the other subject. The percept, on the other hand, pre-
sents the chair in its entirety and makes no analysis
whatever.

We have seen that the "singularity" of the
percept is a composite of two modes of definite-
ness. The first consists in this, that its faithful
and full interpreter has no freedom allowed to him.

---

\* There is no objection to saying that "The chair appears yellow"
means "The chair appears [u]to me[/u] yellow"; but the reference to
the future self [del]is[/del] is more pertinent.
