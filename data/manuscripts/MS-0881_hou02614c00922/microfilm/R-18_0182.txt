"This is the [del]peculiar[/del] kind of observation peculiar to
mathematics, the looking at things substantively that
have only been regarded transitively, or transitively, the
operation of abstraction. How do we know that is a
predicate? Precisely as we know that what is before
our eyes is yellow. We can, after the fact, invent a formula
to cover the case; but it will still [ins]have[/ins] something un-
defended. This really the compulsion similar to that of
the perceptual judgment with which we have to do.

Both the arguments happen to be stated in the
form of the reductio ad absurdum, which is very
appropriate to [del]the[/del] mathematics as bringing
out its ultimately irrational character. But it
is a mere form of statement. Every [u]reductio ad absurdum[/u]
can be stated as a direct argument. The reduction of
the figures of syllogism illustrates, if it does not
virtually demonstrate, this truth.
