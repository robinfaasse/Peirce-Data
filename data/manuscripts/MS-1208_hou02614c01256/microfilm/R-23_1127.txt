[u]pollard[/u], a tree with the top, or poll, cut off; but
perhaps this may have been called [u]polling[/u] the tree;

[u]poniard[/u], said to be from poing, the fist, but why
not from pugnare to fight, as something that fights
excessively;

[u]reynard[/u], an animal (or person) great in [del]regin[/del] [ins]repin[/ins],
counsel.

[u]haggard[/u] is said to come from haga, a hedge, and to
have first been the name of a sort of hawk.

[u]galliard[/u] and [u]tankard[/u] are of doubtful origin.

In [u]steward[/u], [u]awkward[/u], [u]forward[/u], [u]wayward[/u], the suffix
is ward. A steward is a sty-ward, & [u]Orchard[/u] [ins]is wort-yard[/ins].

[del]buzzard[/del], and perhaps [u]gizzard[/u], the d is ex-
