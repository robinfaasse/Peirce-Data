[u]Mature[/u] may possibly have this suffix in it. For [u]mater[/u] is she who brings to a proper size and [u]maturus[/u] is that which is brought to a proper size.

-ble sometimes comes from the Latin -bulum or -bula, or -brum signifying the instrument or place, as [u]stable[/u], [u]table[/u], [u]fable[/u], [u]cribble[/u] (where the doubling of the b is unetymological), [del]crucible probably belongs to this set of words[/del], [u]mandible[/u]. [u]Double[/u] and [u]treble[/u] are duplus, triplus. In many words ending in -ble, the b is either inserted between the stem and the suffix [ins]as in grumble[/ins], or belongs to the root [ins]as in Bible[/ins]; so that for these the suffix is [u]le[/u]. In the remaining words, -ble is the Latin -bilis, as in [u]able[/u] ([u]habilis[/u]), [u]feeble[/u] ([u]flebilis[/u]), [u]edible[/u] ([u]edibilis[/u]), [u]noble[/u] ([u]nobilis[/u]), [u]voluble[/u] ([u]volubilis[/u]).

-brious from the Latin -ber [ins]bris, or -briosus[/ins], occurs only in a few words, as [u]opprobrious[/u], [u]lugubrious[/u], [u]salubrious[/u] [u]tenebrious[/u], etc.
