It doubles the liquid consonants after single vowels
and such of the others as can be doubled when that single
vowel is accented both before and after the attachment of the
suffix, as in acquittal, [ins]begettal,[/ins] committal, transmittal. But, of course,
there is no doubling where a final [u]e[/u] is suppressed, so
that the consonant was not final, as in surprisal,
reprisal, proposal, disposal, refusal, perusal, recital,
requital, refutal. Among words having this suffix we may
reckon [u]rascal[/u], which seems to come from the Old French verb
[u]raseler[/u], to scrape; so that it means 'scrapings'. But [u]offal[/u] is
simply [del]off-fall[/del] [ins]off-fall[/ins]. [u]Marshal[/u] and [u]seneschal[/u] are compounds. [u]Petal[/u]
is from the Greek [greek]πέταλον[/greek] and several names of substances
in [u]-al[/u], have not this suffix, as [u]copal[/u], [u]opal[/u], [u]coral[/u], [u]crystal[/u],
and [u]metal[/u]. [u]Medal[/u], however, is from [ins]Fr.[/ins] [u]medaille[/u], so that it has
this suffix; and [u]medal[/u] and [u]metal[/u] have thus no connection.
