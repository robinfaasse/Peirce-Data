If space is topically non-singular, then the total quantity of "initial choice in ways of setting out from a point" — the total quantity of solid angle, in fact, — is the same at all points of space. If follows that "all perigons", as Prof. Halsted calls them, are equal, and hence — thirdly by 4 — that all right angles, as defined by Euclid, are equal.

3. I am responsible for putting the "dimension" postulate so far away from the other two topical ones. My reason was that the first four postulates named have a meaning for all n-way spreads, where n > 1. Even for n = 1, the first and second postulates have a meaning, but not the third and fourth. These latter, however, at once acquire a meaning for a spread of two or any higher number of dimensions. Then, I thought it most logical to mention last the postulate which specifies the number of dimensions possessed by Euclidean space — a specification which negatives, for example, the congruence of symmetric solids or the untying of a knot in an endless string.
