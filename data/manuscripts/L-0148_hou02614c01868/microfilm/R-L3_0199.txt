"Okataina", Foxton,
Manawatu, New Zealand,
[date]Sunday, 12 Feb! 05[/date]

My dear Mr. Peirce

How can I thank you sufficiently for your extreme kindness in sending me that delightful letter of 19th. October last (containing some of the profoundest thought that is being worked out on the planet at the present moment) — besides the various most interesting printed accompaniments? I assure you I appreciate your goodness: and, also, it is no small pleasure to me to find that my metaphysical lucubrations about Time do not meet with your entire disapproval. I have been haunted by the Time-problem since boyhood, and it is an immense relief to me to find that one of the three spirits of the world (for you and Bertrand Russell and Peano seem to me to have it all your own way on these ultimate questions) is not altogether shocked at the idea I have ventilated — be afraid, with fear and trembling — that succession is essentially logical cohesion.

I dare not yet attempt to deal with your criticisms, which merit a more earnest and intense consideration than I have so

[page break]

far had the energy to bestow on them. I am, temporary, a little exhausted by a more superficial controversy in which I have been banded with the Chancellor of the University here. He swears, in this year of grace 1905, by Dr. Alexander Bain and will not hear of anything outside the straitest sect of Empiricists! So you may imagine that I am anathema maranatha to him. I shall do myself the pleasure of sending you a copy of the correspondence soon.

It is now eighteen months since I returned to New Zealand, where I spent all the years of my early manhood. Failing health compelled me to abandon an active business life, and I have now gone in to mainly — by the doctor's orders — for outdoor country pursuits. I expect, however, to visit America next year or the year after, when I hope to have the great pleasure of renewing my personal acquaintance with you. I assure you I have always regarded those discussions with you in the flat at 79th. Street as constituting one of the highest privileges of my life.

Thanking you again, and hoping to write you more at length in a few weeks' time, I remain, dear Mr. Peirce,

Yours very sincerely

JWFrankland
