"Oroua Downs," Foxton,
Manawatu, New Zealand.
[date]25 October 05[/date]

My dear Mr. Peirce

[del]The outward mail leaves to-morrow[/del] and I must not any longer delay to answer your [del]valued[/del] letter of 20th. July last, in which you speak so sympathetically about my dear father's autobiography. I had [del]it[/del] already gathered [del]and[/del] [ins]I think[/ins] from some source or other that you were a chemist by profession, and that was the reason why I thought you might be interested in the autobiography, containing as it does, some account of his chemical researches.

Speaking for myself personally, nothing would give me greater pleasure than to comply with your request that you should have the book in the "Nation" as you suggest. But you will see from the Preface [del]of the[/del] [ins]to the[/ins] book that my father left it with instructions (by which the present, is "not published" but only printed for private circulation) to the discretion of his (the) children; so that you would have to get the permission of the other five, but especially of my two married sisters who edit the work. I am

[page break]

endeavouring that I have retired from the contest for the present, [del]what[/del] [ins]but[/ins] what you call our "confounded New Zealand system of counting votes" — by virtue of which a mere plurality can elect, without an absolute majority — certainly makes it incumbent on a loyal partisan to avoid "splitting votes".

Believe me to remain, with kindest regards and renewed thanks,

ever yours very sincerely
W. T. L. Travers
