My dear Mr. Peirce:

Please accept best thanks for your note received this morning. I think I must get the German translation of Veronese's book.

Since writing you last, I have, upon endeavoring to read various monographs received from England, stumbled across so many difficulties, due to what is from my standpoint the "elliptical" style of reasoning of the authors, that it has occurred to me to wonder whether it might perhaps be mutually convenient and agreeable to us if you could somewhat [del]enlarge[/del] [ins]extend[/ins], so to speak, your course of instruction to me in the subjects we take a common interest in. For this I ought of course to beg you to take a larger fee and I was wondering whether we could perhaps come to some arrangement on the matter.

Now for instance the enclosed paper by Cayley deals with a point I am interested in but do not feel myself sufficiently familiar with. Offhand it seems to me as if Cayley were wrong and Tait right in regard to [del]an[/del] [ins]the[/ins] [u]arbitrariness[/u] of the Coordinate method as compared with the Quaternion method. But a word or two from you would probably enable me to make up my mind. Then again, [ins]the[/ins] enclosed little monograph on Double Algebra brings me to your brother's memoir mentioned on page 186 of the same, and I thought that a brief synopsis from you of the point involved there would save me a world of time and trouble.

Please let me hear if you think we could do anything on these lines.

Yours very truly,

[fig]signature of G. W. Perkins[/fig]
