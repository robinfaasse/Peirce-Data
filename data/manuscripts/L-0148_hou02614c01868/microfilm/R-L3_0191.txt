New York, [date]March 12th, 1897[/date]

[u]Personal[/u]

Dr. Charles S. Pierce,

My dear Sir:-

Please accept my best thanks for your most kind letter. I should indeed be pleased if you could do me the favor of calling at my house (#293 West End Avenue, Corner 79th St.) on the afternoon of Sunday week, so that I could have a chance to discuss a few of these matters with you. They have been quite a passion with me since boyhood.

As regards combinations of numbers, let us confine ourselves to all of the possible combinations, not permutations, of two numbers. It seems to me that the "multitude" so produced is exactly identical with the multitude of all vulgar [ins](proper)[/ins] fractions. Now in regard to the latter, I am landed in just one of those self-contradictions which the idea of the infinite as a complete whole seems always to bring us to. For I understand, and can see for myself, that if all possible [ins]proper[/ins] vulgar fractions are arranged primarily in the order of their denominators, and all those with common denominator in the order of their numerators, we have a "Denumerable" multitude, according to your definition of the term, that is to say we can give to each unit in the multitude an ordinal value. But now if we arrange the vulgar fractions in the order of their magnitude, we have a multitude which is not Denumerable, and which I presume would be what you call "Primi-post-numeral", i.e. belonging to the next grade of multitudes above those which are Denumerable.

Yours truly

F. W. Franklin
