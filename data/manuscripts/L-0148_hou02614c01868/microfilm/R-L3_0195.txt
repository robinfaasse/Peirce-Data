New York, Dec. 28th, 1897.

Chas. S. Peirce, Esq.,
#108 W. 39th Street,
New York City.

My dear Mr. Peirce:-
It was a great pleasure to me to get your letter of the 13th inst., and I would have replied to it ere now only that I had not yet received decisive answers to some inquiries I had made with a view of trying to get for you such computing work in an Actuarial Office as you asked for. I have made inquiries among a few of the leading Actuaries in this City, but am sorry to say that so far the replies are entirely in the negative. I shall, however, bear the matter in mind, and if I should be so fortunate as to meet with better success later on, I will let you know.

As to the "Exposition," what I feel is that you have fully carried out the engagement. You are right, however, in your surmise that the small surplus of energy I have left, after discharging my duties to this Company, has been so much drawn upon by my own recent investigations, that I have been unable to master your Exposition. The way I feel is that, in my ignorance of the subject, and with the fatigued state of mind which I can alone bring to its study, some very elementary and even diffuse introduction to your system would be in order. By this I mean an explanation so full, and so destitute of "ellipses" in the reasoning, that a child would understand everything.

It would be a great benefit to me if I could have such a thoroughly elementary synopsis, one which would not skip any links in the chain of reasoning, however obvious. For this, however, I feel that I ought to draw another cheque.

I am, dear Mr. Peirce,

Yours very truly,

F. W. Frankland
