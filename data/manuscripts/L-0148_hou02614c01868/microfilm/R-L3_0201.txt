Looking, as I do, at the problems of life and the philosophy
of scientific research from the same point of view, to a
large extent, as yourself, and holding the opinion I do of your
almost unrivalled philosophical powers, I feel there are few
who could write so well of my father as yourself, and
I earnestly hope that my brother and my sisters may give their
consent.

And this reminds me to send you a thousand thanks for
the intensely interesting literature you have sent me. One
passage, in which you criticize Professor Karl Pearson,
was so irresistibly fascinating that I have taken the liberty,
as you will see from one of my enclosures, of duplicating
it. I expressed a very similar conclusion once, though in
far less happy phraseology, in a little paper (I don't know
if I ever sent you a copy?) entitled "Do any Topological
Theorems possess Apodeictic Certainty?" It was the slender
largess of 'pointers' you had kindly given me in the York
City about Listing's researches on cyclosis, periphraxis, & so on.
