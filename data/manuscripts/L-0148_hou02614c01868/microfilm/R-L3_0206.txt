My dear Mr. Franklin:

Though I am just getting up steam from unusually rushing work, I must take the time to say what particular pleasure it gives me to receive the announcement of the collected publication of your Essays etc. For you are one of the strong thinkers, and it will be well for mankind that this fact should be more widely & deeply felt & appreciated. I need not say, however, that I am as clearly opposed to your anti-infinitism as ever.

highest practical concern to us. But if you mean whether there be a universe from which we are so completely severed that it is inconceivable it ever should have any practical consequences for us, then of course the pragmatist would rank that with the statement that there is a pseudo-multitude in things, it being impossible to get the slightest inkling of what pseudo-multitude is like. In short, it is arrant nonsense. It is logically inaccurate to say that nonsense is false: it is never right to say that it is false; it is [del]perfectly[/del] [ins]nearer[/ins] true. For since it means nothing, it cannot involve any falsity, i.e. never could lead a man into unwise conduct.

You are clearsighted enough to deserve to become a pragmatist. But after you have thoroughly assimilated that doctrine, you will find your anti-infinitism to require revision.

Very faithfully
C. S. Peirce

P.S. To say that a class is called infinite when many may be taken from it without diminishing its number is to yield to Reid's vulgar conception which many have.
