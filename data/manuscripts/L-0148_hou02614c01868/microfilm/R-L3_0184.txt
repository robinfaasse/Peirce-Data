4[sup]th[/sup], Clifford interposes two metrical postulates between topical postulates.

5[sup]th[/sup], Clifford's 3[sup]d[/sup] postulate (which he borrows from Riemann) is in my
opinion no postulate about space at all. In one sense, no metrical postu-
late relates to space itself, [del]but[/del] [ins]such postulates stating[/ins] only the [del]properties of[/del] [ins]modes in which[/ins] rigid bodies can move
in space. This third postulate merely says rigid bodies can move alike every-
where in space.

6[sup]th[/sup], Clifford has no postulate of optical geometry. If it is a part of geometry
to say how rigid bodies can move, surely it ought to be a part to say
how rays of light can move.
