Dear Sir

Please accept my best thanks for your very interesting letter of 5th. inst.

1. I should like to know Cantor's definition of continuity. His researches on infinite multitudes are extremely interesting — as also are your own on the same subject: though, as at present advised, I disagree with both of you in denying the [ins]logical[/ins] possibility of any such multitude existing at any moment of time or throughout the whole of past time. I cannot get over the typical paradox that if
A = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + ... ,
then 2 A = 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10 + ...
= A - { 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + ... }
< A, which is self-contradictory.

Still, I admit that if a continuous manifold exist, then I should like also to know at what points in the Euclidian chain of reasoning, each one of his four topical postulates is tacitly assumed. Explicitly, none of them is mentioned in the Euclidian scheme.

(B.) In regard to Cyclosis. Take the surface of a wedding-ring, and consider a [del]filament[/del] [ins]line[/ins] constituted by tying a thread tightly round it. That filament cannot shrink to nothing while remaining in the surface, without breach of continuity. [del]Space[/del] Euclidian space is not a 3-way spread analogous to such a 2-way spread as the surface of a wedding-ring. Am I right? [ins]Later. On re-reading your letter I see I am all wrong about Cyclosis, Periphraxis, & Apeirasis. I will write fully about them in a day or too.[/ins]
