New York, [date]March 9th, 1897[/date].

[u]Private[/u]

My dear Dr. Skinner:-

The only continuity which has, in my opinion, been successfully established in the Number-System is the unlimited possibility, if endless future time is taken, of interpolating between integers, (a) vul[del]gar[/del] fractions, (b) irrational algebraic numbers, i.e. the roots of algebraic equations, and (c) transcendental numbers, i.e. numbers which are defined as the limits of series in which new terms are taken throughout endless future time. So you will see that I have steered clear of admitting [u]anything[/u] in the shape of infinity, except as attributable to future [del]t[/del]ime. [u]This kind of infinity, namely, endlessness of future time, you will bear me witness that I have always not only admitted, but asserted.[/u]

Many thanks for your most interesting note, which it was a great pleasure to me to receive.

One of the most interesting letters I have lately received has been from Dr. Charles S. Peirce, formerly of Harvard University, but now living in New York City, calling my attention to the work which has been done by Listing on this subject, and which is wholly new to me. I have sent the letter to the Doctor, and on its return will forward it to you.

With kindest regards, I remain, as ever,

Yours sincerely,

(Sd) F.W. Frankland

P.S. Would you kindly remember me to Mr. Walter C. Wright, if you happen to think of it when you see him.
