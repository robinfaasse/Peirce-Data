New York, [date]Jan. 12th, 1898[/date].

Personal

Mr. dear Mr. Peirce;

It was a great pleasure to me to receive your note of 10th inst. I think the plan you hit upon -- a narrative of the process of thought you went through yourself -- will just exactly fill the bill! It was a very happy thought, it seems to me, and I do not think it would have occurred to me.

On the 15th of this month, when I get my salary, I will draw a check for $15, which I am sorry to find is the maximum I can expend at the present time on this subject, and make it out and address it to the destination you have named.

I shall indeed be eager to read anything you may have to write to me about Discrete Manifolds. "Nature", in a recent brief allusion to my researches on this subject, makes the enigmatical remark that "almost concurrently with their publication Signor Veronese has contributed to the [del]Atti dei Lincei[/del] [ins][u]Atti dei Lincei[/u][/ins] a disquisition on the postulate of continuity in which he arrives at conclusions contrary to those of Schoenflies." Do you know anything of Veronese or Schoenflies?

Sincerely yours,

F. W. Frankland
