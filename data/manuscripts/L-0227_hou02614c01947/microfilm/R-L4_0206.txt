$1^{\text{o}}$ If $x \prec y$ and $y \prec z$ then $x \prec z$ whatever $x$, $y$, and $z$ may be.

$2^{\text{o}}$ $x \prec x$

$3^{\text{o}}$ If $x \prec y$ and $y \prec x$ then $x$ and $y$ may everywhere be substituted for one another.

Every signification of the copula of inclusion $\prec$ has an [del]converse[/del] signification $\prec'$ such that $x \prec y$ is the same as $y \prec' x$.

Two logical term-signs are suggested [del]and[/del] [ins]and[/ins] defined by the copula. They are zero $0$, and $\infty$ infinity. Their definitions are as follows:—

Zero is such a term that $0 \prec x$ whatever $x$ may be;

Infinity is such that $x \prec \infty$ whatever $x$ may be.

Of course the zero of one progression may not be the zero of another progression; and therefore I have greatly enlarged the usual meanings of [del]the words[/del] these definitions.

The copula of inclusion is subject to [del]a[/del] rather complicated rules of algebraic manipulation. To simplify this process we may use the copula of identity $=$. The definition of this is that $x = y$ is the same as $x \prec y$ and $y \prec x$.

The third class of algebraic signs [del]consists of[/del] signs of [del]inclusion[/del] operation. Signs of operation are of three orders according as they are applicable to all terms or only to [del]relative[/del] [ins]and conjugative[/ins] terms or [del]only[/del] to conjugative terms alone. The sign of conjunction $+$ is the first sign of the first order. It is defined by three conditions, as follows:—

$1^{\text{o}}$ $x \prec x + y$

$2^{\text{nd}}$ $y \prec x + y$

$3^{\text{o}}$ If $x \prec z$ and $y \prec z$ then $x + y \prec z$.
