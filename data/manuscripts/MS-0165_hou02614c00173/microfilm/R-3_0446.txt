<u>Corollary 59</u>. The two roots of a quadratic
$-\frac{1}{2}A + \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}A^2 - B}$
$-\frac{1}{2}A - \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}A^2 - B}$
being multiplied together give
$\frac{1}{4}A^2 - (\frac{1}{4}A^2 - B) = B$
while their sum is $-A$.

60. Hence a quadratic is the equation which expresses the
problem required two numbers whose sum and product
are given. Namely the two numbers (putting $\Sigma$ for sum
and $\Pi$ for product are
$\frac{1}{2}\Sigma (1 + \sqrt{1 - 4\frac{\Pi}{\Sigma^2}})$
$\frac{1}{2}\Sigma (1 - \sqrt{1 - 4\frac{\Pi}{\Sigma^2}})$.

61. We also see that the square of the sum of [ins]any[/ins] two whole numbers
[del]always exceeds[/del]
[del]diminished by[/del] 4 times their product and the excess is always
a square number, namely, it is the square of their difference.
Thus $(10+13)^2 = 529$ and $4 \times 10 \times 13 = 520$. The difference is
$(13-10)^2$. In fact the square of the sum of $p$ and $q$ is
$(p+q)^2 = p^2 + 2pq + q^2$
and the square of their difference is
$(p-q)^2 = p^2 - 2pq + q^2$
These differ by $4pq$. Hence if the difference and product
of two numbers are given, the numbers are
$\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\Delta^2 + 4\Pi} + \frac{1}{2}\Delta$
$\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\Delta^2 - 4\Pi} - \frac{1}{2}\Delta$
where there are [del]two sets of answers, one for[/del] [ins]the[/ins] positive root, [del]the other for the negative[/del].
