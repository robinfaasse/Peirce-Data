[u]Art. 76.[/u]

[u]Postulate 5.[/u] [[u]Principle of the Limit.[/u]] If by virtue of a general rule [del]all[/del] the members of one simple sparse sequence are attached in their order to the members of another such sequence, then, if this rule is carried out completely, and without exception, [ins](as it always is, for a surd the limit of a series of rational numbers)[/ins] the limit of the one sequence will [del]be[/del] likewise be attached to the limit of the other.

[u]Scholium 31.[/u] For example, when Achilles pursues the tortoise, and at each stretch goes to where the tortoise was when Achilles began to run over that stretch, there is a simple sparse sequence of places. These are connected with a similar sequence of instants of time. If Achilles runs twice as fast as the tortoise the interval between any two successive instants of this [del]series[/del] [ins]sequence[/ins] will be [del]twice[/del] as long as that between the latter of these instants and [del]and[/del] the next instant of the sequence. The attachment, or connection, between the sequence of places and the sequence of times will consist in this, that Achilles reaches these places at these times, the tortoise, at each instant, being in the next following place. If this rule is followed out completely and without exception, Achilles and the tortoise will both be at the limit of the sequence of places at the limit of the sequence of times. It is difficult to conceive that this should be otherwise. Nevertheless, it must be admitted that it might be otherwise in some cases.
