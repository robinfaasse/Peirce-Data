It will be seen that Huygens ventures to speak of the
square of a time, because it was usual to speak of the square
on a [u]line[/u], [ins]and he represents times by lines, as Galileo had done[/ins]. But he does not leave the expression to stand
alone, because he knows that it would be objected to.

Long after such awkward periphrases had been abandoned,
there remained mathematicians who objected to saying that a
velocity is a space divided by a time, [del]and[/del] [ins]or[/ins] wished, [del]and[/del] in
using the expression, to be understood as meaning that the
measure of a velocity is the measure of a space divided by
the measure of a time. To those who do not understand mul-
tiple algebra, the latter expression may well seem more exact.
