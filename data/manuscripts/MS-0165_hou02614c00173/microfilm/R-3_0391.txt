55. [u](From the Bamberg Rechenbuch, 1483)[/u]. Two couriers set
out together. One goes 6 miles a day. The [del]other goes[/del] [ins]one[/ins]
mile the first day, and every subsequent day 1 mile
more than on the day before. In how many days
have they gone the same number of miles?

Let [u]x[/u] be the number of days. Then 6[u]x[/u] miles
are travelled by the first. As to the second, write
under his miles, the same in reverse order
[fig]Two rows of numbers: 1 2 3 4 5 over 5 4 3 2 1[/fig] [fig]Two rows of numbers: 1 2 3 4 5 6 over 6 5 4 3 2 1[/fig]
The sum of two, one over the other, is always [u]x[/u]+1, and
there are [u]x[/u] such sums, making [u]x[/u]([u]x[/u]+1), But this
counts his miles twice over. Therefore, he has gone
[frac]1[/frac]/2 [u]x[/u]([u]x[/u]+1) miles. Thus the equation is
[frac]1[/frac]/2 [u]x[/u]([u]x[/u]+1) = 6[u]x[/u].
This is satisfied by [u]x[/u]=0. But we do not want that
solution. The [u]x[/u] we want is [u]not[/u] 0. We may therefore
divide by [u]x[/u], when the equation becomes
[frac]1[/frac]/2([u]x[/u]+1) = 6
giving [u]x[/u] = 11.
