[Gloss 3. With the Alexandrine Greeks the name mathematics (ἡ μαθηματική) came to be applied chiefly to astronomy. Thus, Ptolemy's text-book of that science, known as the Almagest, has for its genuine title Μαθηματική σύνταξις (Mathematical Compendium.) In the middle ages, by a "mathematician" was understood an astrologer, or even a sorcerer. So late as Queen Elizabeth's time, the learned Dr. Dee was practically driven out of England, because he was reputed a "mathematician."

In the Roman and Medieval schools, the mathematical sciences, — that is to say, the four they studied, arithmetic, geometry, music, and astronomy, — were collectively called the Quadrivium, a fanciful name signifying literally the four roads.]
