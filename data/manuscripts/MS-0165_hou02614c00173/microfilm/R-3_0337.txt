[u]Art. 38. Theorem 19[/u]. [u]If the product of two [del]whole[/del] [ins]cardinal[/ins] numbers is zero, one of those numbers is zero[/u].

(since by Definition 38, every [ins]cardinal[/ins] number not 0 is greater than 0) Suppose that when P has the particular value M, and Q the particular value N, M and N being both greater than 0, the product is greater than 0. Then, when either P or Q takes the value next greater, it remains true that the product is greater than 0. For, by Axiom 9, the product is greater than before, and being before greater than 0, by Axiom 5 and Definition 38, it is so still.

But, when, P and Q are both 1, by Corollary 13, the product is not zero. Hence, by Theorem 4 and Definition 38, the product is not zero as long as neither P nor Q is zero. Q.E.D.
