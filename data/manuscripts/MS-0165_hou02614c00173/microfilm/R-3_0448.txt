88. A speculator hearing [ins]in it[/ins] the price of certain stock quoted,
determines to invest $160; but, before he can secure [del]them[/del] [ins]the stocks,[/ins] the
price per share is $1 more; so that he [del]buys[/del] [ins]gets[/ins] 3 fewer shares
for his $160. How many shares did he buy?
Let n be the number. Then,
$\frac{\$160}{n} = \frac{\$160}{n+3} + \$1$;
$\$180 = \$1 \times n(n+3)$;
$n = -\frac{3}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{9}{4} + 180} = -\frac{3}{2} \pm \frac{27}{2}$.
That is, he either bought 12 or sold 15. If he bought 12, he gave
$5 a piece for them. If he sold 15, he gave the buyer
a bonus of $5 a piece to take them off his hands.

the sum of their [del]squares[/del] [ins]values[/ins]? Let the numbers be the two roots

$x^2 - 41x + p = 0$
