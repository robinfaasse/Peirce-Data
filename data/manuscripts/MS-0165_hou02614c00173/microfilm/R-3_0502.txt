[u]Section 6. The Topical Census.[/u]

Art. [del]23[/del] [ins]97[/ins] [u]Scholium 48[/u]. The most [del]fundamental[/del] [ins]important[/ins] problem of topical geometry, after the fundamental propositions of [del]Arts. 12-22[/del] [ins]85-95[/ins] have been obtained, is the [u]Census Problem[/u]. The question here is, to determine the connection between the numbers of marked points, lines, surfaces, and spaces in any configuration.

Definition [del]42[/del] [ins]123[/ins]. The [u]cyclosis[/u] of a place is the number of times it has to be cut across to make it impossible for a self-returning filament to exist in it which [del]cannot[/del] [ins]by no motion in it[/ins] [del]shrunk[/del] [ins]shrink[/ins] to a point [del]by a motion in[/del].

Definition [del]43[/del] [ins]124[/ins]. The [u]periphraxis[/u] of a place is the number of times it has to be pierced to make it impossible for a closed sac to exist in it which can by no motion in it shrink to a line.

Definition [del]44[/del] [ins]125[/ins]. The [u]immensity[/u] of a place is the number of interruptions that have to be place in it to make it impossible for [del]a self-returning[/del] [ins]an unlimited solid[/ins] to exist in it which can by no motion in it collapse to a surface.

Definition [del]45[/del] [ins]126[/ins]. The [u]Census Number[/u] of a place is the number of its [del]separate[/del] [ins]pieces[/ins] [del]parts[/del] [ins]limited[/ins] by singularities [ins]which is called its chorisis[/ins], less its cyclosis, plus its periphraxis, less its immensity, this number being diminished by that of its limiting singularities, [del]themselves[/del] [ins]itself[/ins] diminished by the number of [del]their[/del] [ins]its[/ins] singularities, etc. [ins]But if the dimensionality of a singularity is two less than that of the place, it is to be added, not subtracted.[/ins]

[u]Illustrations[/u]. The census number of a point is 1, of two points, 2, etc. The census number of an oval, that is, a non-singular line [ins]Fig 24[/ins], is 0. For it has 1 piece, and its cyclosis is 1, its periphraxis and immensity 0, and it has no singularities. [ins]59.[/ins] The census number of a limited line is -1; for it has one piece, and no cyclosis, periphraxis, nor immensity. But it has two extremities which count 2.
