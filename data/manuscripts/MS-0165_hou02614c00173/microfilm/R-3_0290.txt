[u]Art. 21[/u]. [u]Definition 20[/u]. Of objects in a sequence, we say that one comes [u]next after[/u] another, which comes after, but not after any third coming after that other.

[u]Illustration 10[/u]. Thus, in the alphabet, we say that N does not come next after L; for though it comes after L, it also comes after M, which likewise comes after L. But N comes next after M, and M next after L.

[u]Definition 21[/u]. A sequence may be said to be [u]forwardly graduated[/u] (or [del]to advance[/del] [ins]by steps[/ins] [del]graduation[/del]) if every object in it except the last has another next after it.

A sequence is said to be [u]backwardly graduated[/u] (or to recede [del]by steps[/del]) if every object in it except the first [del]has[/del] [ins]is next[/ins] after [del]another[/del] [ins]an[/ins]other. A sequence everywhere forwardly and backwardly graduated may be called [del]sparse[/del] [ins]maybe[/ins] [ins]forward,[/ins]

A sequence is said to be [del]forwardly[/del] [u]sloped[/u] [ins]forward[/ins] or [u]proclive[/u], at any object in it, not the last, if that [del]is[/del] object has none next after it.

A sequence may be said to be [del]backwardly[/del] [u]sloped backward[/u], or [u]reclive[/u], at any object in not the first, if that object comes next after no other.

[ins]A sequence both proclive and reclive may be called [u]dense[/u].[/ins]

[u]Illustrations[/u]. 11. The alphabet is arranged in a sequence graduated both ways.

12. The different instants of a minute are in a sequence sloped throughout both ways. For no instant comes next after any other, but between any two instants there are others later than the one but earlier than the other.
