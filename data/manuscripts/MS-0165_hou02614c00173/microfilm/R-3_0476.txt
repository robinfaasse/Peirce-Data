[u]Postulate 6[/u] ([del]repeated from Art. 7[/del]) Space is continuous.

[ins]N.B. (9)[/ins] [u]Explanation[/u]. It is a question of physics whether it be true of real space, or not, that it is continuous. Suffice it to say, that the space which the mathematician, for [ins]good[/ins] [del]sufficient[/del] reasons, sees fit to imagine, or suppose, — his hypothetical space, — is taken as continuous.

We might say that it is [del]simply[/del] continuous. But that is not necessary, for that it is so, is [del]virtually stated and[/del] [ins]implicitly implied by[/ins] the [del]second[/del] [ins]seventh[/ins] postulate, as will be seen in due time.

Every part of space, no matter how small, is composed of smaller spaces homogeneous with the whole; and points are only the limits of [del]parts of[/del] between spaces. [ins]* Every part of space, no matter how small, consists of [ins]extended[/ins] spaces quite innumerable.[/ins] The fact that the limit of a series of points is always [ins]exists[/ins] in space, renders sudden changes of motion (because of the [ins]principle[/ins] [del]doctrine[/del] of the limit) quite impossible. Of course, this may not be true for real space: we are only considering the geometer's hypothetical space. It is certainly often convenient to neglect the gradualness and to imagine a motion to begin impulsively.
