[u]Memorandum[/u] 14. The above is the celebrated Census
Theorem, which is generally regarded as the most important
contribution to elementary geometry of modern times.

Leonard Euler, (born in Basle, 1707, died in St. Petersburg,
1783) who during his 56 years of activity made more numerous
mathematical discoveries than any man who ever lived, his contri-
butions, numbering near 800 pieces, being many of them extremely
important, first partially made out the formula in 1752. His
statement was
Faces + Summits = Edges + 2,
and this was given by Legendre in his [u]Éléments de géométrie[/u],
published in 1794, gave this (Book VII. [del]Theorem[/del] [ins]Proposition[/ins] 25). From Legen-
dre it has been copied into most subsequent elementary textbooks.
It was omitted, however, by Benjamin Peirce, who no doubt
remarked that it was not rigidly proved, perhaps even,
that it was not true. That it is not true is shown by
