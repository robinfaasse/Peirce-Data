[u]Section 4. Singularities.[/u]

[u]Art 92[/u]. [u]Theorem 44[/u]. [u]A particle can ordinarily move along a line in two opposite ways[/u].

The [u]demonstration[/u] is omitted, as unnecessary.

[u]Definition 112[/u]. [u]An ordinary[/u] point of a line is a point through which the generating particle of the line can move as it can through neighboring points. [u]A singular[/u] point of a line is one which differs from neighboring points in respect to the ways in which a generating particle can move through it.

[u]Division 4[/u]. Singular points of lines are

[u]1[ins]st[/ins][/u], those at which a particle can move only one way along the line, which points are [u]free extremities[/u].

[u]2[ins]nd[/ins][/u], those at which a particle can move more than two ways, which are [u]points of furcation[/u].

[u]Scholium 37[/u]. At a furcation the generating particle may be conceived to burst into two or more. But sometimes it is simpler to imagine [del]the motion of description begins or ends at the furcation, or[/del] that the describing particle returns to the point.
