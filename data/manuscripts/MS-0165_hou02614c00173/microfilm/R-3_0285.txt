[u]Art. 18. Definition 15.[/u] A [u]correspondence[/u] is a connection established in the mind between two collections such that, considering either, every object of it is connected with the same number of objects of the other.

N.B. (1) It is not said that any object [ins]of either[/ins] is connected with the same number of [del]objects[/del] [ins]the other[/ins] collection, since the two numbers may be different.

[fig]Diagram showing a ring of circles and dots connected by lines[/fig]
[pn]Fig. 75. Two-to-three correspondence.[/pn]

[u]Illustration 4.[/u] In [u]Fig. 75[/u] there is a collection of rings and a collection of dots. [del]Each dot[/del] The lines suggest mental connections. Each dot is connected with three rings; each ring with two dots. Such a connection is called a [u]two-to-three correspondence[/u] between the dots and rings, or a [u]three-to-two correspondence[/u] between the rings and dots.

[u]Scholium 5.[/u] Seeking to enlarge the idea, we remark that the two collections need not be different. Thus, in Figure 76, the [del]dots[/del] lines suggest a [u]three-to-three correspondence[/u] among the dots.
