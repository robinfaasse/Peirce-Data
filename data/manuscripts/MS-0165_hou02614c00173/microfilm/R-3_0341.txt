[u]Chapter IV. Factors.[/u]

[u]Art. 42. Definitions.[/u] 39. A [u]factor[/u] of a whole number, N, is any whole number which multiplied by a whole number gives that number, N.

A [u]part[/u] of a number, [ins]N,[/ins] is a factor of it [del]not[/del] [ins]not[/ins] the number, [ins]N,[/ins] itself.

A [u]divisor[/u] of a number, N, is a factor of it which multiplied by a number [del]different from that number, N,[/del] gives [del]that number, N,[/del] [ins]that number, N,[/ins] as the product.

Thus, the factors of 6 are 1, 2, 3, 6; its parts are 1, 2, 3; its divisors are 2, 3, 6.

40. The [u]greatest[/u] common divisor of two [ins]whole[/ins] numbers is that divisor of both of which every other divisor is a part. [ins]This is important in algebra, where the greatest common divisor may be numerically small.[/ins] [[del]The word greatest does not refer to its being higher in the scale of numbers, but to all other divisors being parts of it.[/del]]

41. A [u]prime[/u] number is a number with just one part.

42. A [u]prime factor[/u] of a whole number, N, is a prime number which is a factor of that number, N.

43. A [u]homogeneous[/u] number is a whole number having a single prime factor. The [u]multiplicity[/u] of [del]the[/del] [ins]a[/ins] factor is the number of times it occurs as a factor in the number of which it is a factor. A [u]heterogeneous[/u] number is a number having more than one prime factor.

44. A [u]composite[/u] number is a whole number having a [del]factor[/del] [ins]part[/ins] that is a divisor of it. Or, it is a number that is a product of homogeneous parts.

45. Two numbers are said to be [u]prime to[/u] each other if they have no common divisor.
