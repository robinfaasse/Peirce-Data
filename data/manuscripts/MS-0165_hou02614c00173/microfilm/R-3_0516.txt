[u]Art. 122. Definition 162[/u]. A [u]homaloidal system of places[/u] is a multitude of places of all dimensionalities, such that

1[sup]st[/sup], every point of space is a point of the system;

2[sup]nd[/sup], every extended place of the system is unbounded;

3[sup]d[/sup], every two points are contained in a line of the system;

4[sup]th[/sup], no two different points are contained in two different lines of the system;

5[sup]th[/sup], every three points are contained in a surface of the system;

6[sup]th[/sup], no three points not in one line of the system are in two surfaces of the system;

7[sup]th[/sup], every two surfaces of the system contain a line of the system;

8[sup]th[/sup], no two different surfaces of the system contain anything but what is contained in one line of the system;

9[sup]th[/sup], every three surfaces of the system contain a point of the system;

10[sup]th[/sup], no three surfaces of the system not containing one common line of the system contain any[ins]thing[/ins] [del]thing[/del] [ins]place[/ins] but one point of the system.
