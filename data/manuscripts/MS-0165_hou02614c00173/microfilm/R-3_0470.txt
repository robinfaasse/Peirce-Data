[u]Chapter X. Topical Geometry.[/u]
[u]Section 1. Continuity.[/u]

[u]Art 87. Definition 93[/u]. [u]Topology[/u], or [u]topical geometry[/u], is the study of the manner in which places are intrinsically connected, irrespective of [del]any[/del] their optical and metrical relations.

[u]Scholium 35[/u]. In presenting this part of geometry, we suppress most of the formal proofs, as likely to draw the scholar's mind away from that exercise of mathematical imagination which makes the chief [del]intellect[/del] educational value of this part of geometry.

[u]Art. 88. Definition 94[/u]. [u]Like parts[/u] are parts which are all connected together in the same general way, and which (together with their connections) make up the whole.

[u]Illustration 38[/u]. Thus, Figure 111. shows a heart which, like too many human ones, is composed of a white and a black half. Notwithstanding their different colors, those two halves are like parts, [del]each being[/del] [ins]since each is[/ins] connected with the other in the same general way, and since, together with the line of connection, they make up the whole heart.
