[u]Gloss 17[/u]. The word [u]algebra[/u] is pronounced al'jebraa.
So it has been accented for 200 years, at least; and so it
is in Spanish, Italian, and German. But in 1623 (ac-
cording to Minsheu) it was accented on the second syllable in
Spanish, as it ought to be. It is simply the abridged title of
the Arabic treatise from which Western Europe acquired
its first knowledge of this art. The full title is "Al-jebr
wa'l-muqābala," or "Redintegration and Opposition,"
[ins]Arabic[/ins] names of algebraical operations. The author was Muhammed
the son of Mūsā, surnamed the [del]Kh[/del] Chorasmian, or Khiva-ite
He lived in Baghdad, under the great Khalif, Al-mamūn,
in the early part of the ninth century. When the Arabians
began to gain wealth, in the palmy days of Islam, they
were quite ignorant of mathematics, and unable even
to keep accounts. Accordingly, Al-mamūn sought out a
man learned in such matters. Chorasmia, south of the Aral
Sea, was already overrun by the Turks; but it had formed part
of the Bactrian Kingdom, and had preserved the extra-
ordinary knowledge of arithmetic which the Bactrians
had possessed from ancient times. The learned Muham-
