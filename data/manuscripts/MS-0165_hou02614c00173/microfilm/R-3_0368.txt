What question in arithmetic do the datary numbers fail
to answer? Just as the cardinal numbers fail to answer
a question in subtraction, when the subtrahend is greater than the
minuend, so the datary numbers fail to answer a question of
division, when the [del]quotient[/del] divisor is greater than the dividend
or even when it is less, sometimes.

How is the defect remedied? Just as it was in the other
case; namely, by supplementing the system of numbers by
the insertion of the answers to such questions, that is, by
inserting the rational fractions.

What is the system [del]to enter[/del] [ins]of numbers[/ins] so enlarged called? It is
called the system of rational numbers. Don't ask why; it
is too long a story.

Does the system afford answers, not only to the question
what is the quotient of one datary number by another, but,
also, [ins]in all cases,[/ins] to the question what is the quotient of one rational
number by another? It does.
