[u]Tridimensionality of Space[/u].
[del]Schol 91. Scholium 37.[/del] [ins]Analysis 2.[/ins]
[del]§ 1.[/del] Imagine a bucket of water. The whole place within the bucket is occupied by water. Let a chunk of ice be formed in the midst of the waters, and remain suspended there. Then, every part of the place within the bucket is occupied either with water or with ice; and nowhere do water and ice occupy the same space. Yet there is a kind of place called a [u]surface[/u], [ins]the place of[/ins] a film without thickness, which is the mutual boundary of the ice and water, no more nor less occupied by water than by ice. Let us see exactly what we mean, and avoid selfcontradiction. We remember [ins](Def 37)[/ins] that a place is a part of space.
[del]Definition 46.[/del] The [u]boundary[/u] between two places is the mutual limit (Def 16) between the two endless series of places similar to it in the two regions (or places) on the two sides of the boundary.
[del]Scholium 38 resumed. We might define a surface as follows:
Definition 46. A place extended like all space is called a geometry or solid space.[/del]
[del]Definition 47.[/del] A [u]surface[/u] is a place, [ins]that is[/ins] the boundary between two solid spaces which together form one unbroken solid space. [ins]*[/ins]
