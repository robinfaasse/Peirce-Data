[u]Notation 15[/u]. It will be very convenient to denote
a [u]Point[/u], by enclosing the signs of the three planes, the
ray and plane, or the two rays which determine it between
[u]Square Brackets[/u], writing $[\alpha\beta\gamma]$ for the point where the
three planes $(\alpha)$, $(\beta)$, $(\gamma)$ come together, $[\delta e]$ for the
point where the ray $\{e\}$ cuts the plane $(\delta)$, and
$[f\cdot g]$ for the point where the two rays $f$ and $g$ inter-
sect (when they do intersect);
to denote a [u]Plane[/u] by enclosing the signs of the three points,
the ray and point, or the two rays which determine it between
[u]Parentheses[/u], writing, for example $(ABC)$ for the plane of the
three points $[A]$, $[B]$, $[C]$, writing $(De)$ for the plane
of the point $[D]$ and ray $\{e\}$, and $(f\cdot g)$ for the plane
of the two rays $\{f\}$ and $\{g\}$ in case they lie in one plane;
and to denote a [u]Ray[/u] by enclosing the signs of the two
points, or two planes which determine it between [u]Braces[/u], writing,
for example, $\{AB\}$ for the ray through the points $[A]$ and
$[B]$, and $\{\gamma\delta\}$ for the ray common to the planes $(\gamma)$ and $(\delta)$.

[u]Corollary 104[/u]. If the two expressions $\{(bc)(de)\}$ and
$\{[be][cd]\}$ both denote any rays, as they do if $\{b\}$ and
$\{c\}$ lie in one plane and $\{d\}$ and $\{e\}$ lie in one plane, if $\{b\}$
and $\{e\}$ have a common point and $\{c\}$ and $\{d\}$ have a common
point, then $\{(bc)(de)\} = \{[be][cd]\}$. That is, the two rays
are the same. This is [ins]made[/ins] evident [del]from[/del] by inspecting Figure 199.
