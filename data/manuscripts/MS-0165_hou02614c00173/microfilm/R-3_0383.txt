[u]Art. 66. Problem 6. To solve an equation with one
unknown quantity, and only one function of that quantity.[/u]

[u]Solution[/u]. Bring the [del]simplest[/del] function of the unknown
to one side of the equation, with all the rest upon the
other side, and perform upon the latter the operation
which is the inverse of that performed on the unknown.

[u]Demonstration[/u]. Let [u]Fx = a[/u] be the equation, where
x is a symbol for an operation. Let [u]F[sup]-1[/sup][/u] be the inverse
operation. Then [u]x = F[sup]-1[/sup]a[/u]

N.B. (7). This rule is not by any means useless; but
in the majority of cases it fails, because there is no
known inverse function. In fact, equations for which
an algebraical form of solution can be found are excep-
tional and relatively simple.

[u]Art. 67. Definition 72. A linear equation[/u] is one in which
no operations are performed upon the unknown quantities except
those of addition & and multiplication by known quantities,
or which is reducible to such an equation by clearing [del]of[/del]
fractions.

[u]Illustration 34.[/u] Thus [u]x + 3[/u]/[u]5x - 7[/u] = 11 is a linear equation; because
by multiplying by the denominator, it reduces to x + 3 = 55x - 77.
