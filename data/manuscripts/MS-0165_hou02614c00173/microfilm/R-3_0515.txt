[u]Chapter XI. Graphics and Perspective.[/u]

[del]Art 120.[/del] [ins]327[/ins] [u]Definition 161[/u]. [u]Graphics[/u] is that branch of geometry which studies the intersections and coincidences of points, rays, and planes.

[[u]Gloss 29[/u]. The word [u]graphics[/u] is from the Greek [u]τὰ γραφικά[/u], things pertaining to drawing, from [u]γράφειν[/u] to draw, from the root SKAR, cut, whence scribe, engrave, scalp, groove, grove, sculpture, shears, etc. It was introduced as the name of the branch of Geometry by [del]the[/del] William Kingdon Clifford about [date]1870[/date]. It had been best called [u]projective geometry[/u], which indicates the principal method of treating it. It was sometimes called [u]intersectional geometry[/u], [del]which[/del] was a good designation; and it went by various highly objectionable titles, such as [u]Modern Geometry[/u], when in fact it was pursued to [ins]such[/ins] lengths by the [del]Greeks[/del] [ins]the passing[/ins] that the moderns did not until our generation know what they meant. It was also called [u]Synthetic Geometry[/u], which was very bad, since it need not be studied synthetically, and much metical geometry, including elementary metrics, is also synthetic. The truth is, modern mathematicians did not, until many books had been written about it, see precisely what graphics was about.]
