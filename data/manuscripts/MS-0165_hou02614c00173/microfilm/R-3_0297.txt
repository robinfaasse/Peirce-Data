The first number, 1, has a [del]certain[/del] [ins]given[/ins] property; but this property is such that if any number
possesses it, so does likewise the next greater number; hence, all numbers possess this
property. This way of reasoning invented by the great Pierre Fermat [ins]about 1640 —?[/ins] is called Fermatian
inference. The greatest of ancient mathematicians, Archimedes (arkimee·deez), who
was killed at an advance age 212 B.C., thought it necessary to write a tract to prove that
the number of sands [ins]on[/ins] the sea-shore could be arithmetically expressed. Had he been
acquainted with the Fermatian inference he need only have reasoned as follows:
Any number greater by 1 than an expressible number is expressible, and
1 is expressible; hence, all numbers are expressible.

This method is indispensible in reasoning about whole numbers.
