Yet precisely the same thing is true of the rows rela-
tively to the columns;

And the rows and columns correspond exactly to one
another in all their relations, as places (not as to the values
they contain)

Hence, it follows, that every general rule concerning the
rows in relation to the columns, equally holds [del]in reference[/del] [ins]concerning[/ins]
[del]to[/del] the columns in relation to the rows.

For instance, we know that in place of any equation
we may substitute that equation increased by any one
of the others. It follows, that we can substitute for any
column that column increased by one of the others. This
amounts to [del]substituting[/del] [ins]diminishing[/ins] for the unknown quantity of the [del]subtrahend[/del] [ins]increasing[/ins]
column, [del]its ex[/del] by the unknown quantity of the [del]minuend[/del] [ins]increaser[/ins] column.
For these two columns are say
$$\begin{array}{cc}
\text{A} & \text{B} \\
\text{A}' & \text{B}' \\
\text{A}'' & \text{B}'' \\
\text{etc} & \text{etc}
\end{array}$$
In the equations written out they would represent the terms
$$\begin{array}{c}
+ \text{A}\overset{*}{\text{x}} + \text{B}\text{y} \\
+ \text{A}'\text{x} + \text{B}'\text{y} \\
+ \text{A}''\text{x} + \text{B}''\text{y} \\
\text{etc.} \quad \text{etc.}
\end{array}$$
