The principle of the limit cannot be excluded from elementary
geometry. But some teachers regard it as so difficult, that they
prefer a detour to a direct appeal to this principle. Perhaps they
are right. Suppose, then, that the [del]figure[/del] [ins]figure[/ins] represents a plane configu-
ration and that you are looking at it through a pin-hole in a
card held up to your eye. That pin-hole is supposed to be a point,
which we may denote by $[P]$. The points $[Y_{1}]$ and $[P]$ lie, by
Corollary 48, in a ray $\{Y_{1}P\}$. Take any point $[Y'_{1}]$ (read: "Y
one prime") in that ray, but not in the plane of the figure.
