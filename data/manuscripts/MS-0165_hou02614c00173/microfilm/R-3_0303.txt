[u]Art. 24[/u]. [u]Definition 24[/u]. Two collections are said to be equal to one another, if they can be put into one-to-one correspondence.

A collection is said to [u]inclusible in[/u] another, if it can be put into one-to-one correspondence with a part of that other; and the latter is said to be [u]inclusible of[/u] the former.

A collection is said to be [u]less[/u], or [u]fewer[/u], than another if it is inclusible in, but not equal to, that other; and the latter is said to be [u]greater[/u], or [u]more[/u], than the former.

[u]Corollary 6[/u]. Every collection is equal to itself.

[u]Corollary 7[/u]. By corollary 1, if one collection is equal to another, and that other is equal to a third, then the first is equal to the third.

[del]Corollary[/del] [ins]Axiom 5.[/ins] A part of a part is a part of the whole.

[u]Corollary 8[/u]. By axiom 5, if a collection is inclusible in another inclusible in or equal to a third, [del]or[/del] if the first is equal to the second and the second inclusible in the third, then the first is inclusible in the third.
