[u]Notation 17[/u]. We will write $\Upsilon$ (the initial of the Greek
$\dot{\upsilon}\pi\alpha\rho\chi\epsilon\iota\nu$, to happen) before the description of a place to
denote the number places of that description that exist.
In many cases the letter $\Upsilon$ may be omitted without any pos-
sible confusion.

[u]Illustration 81[/u]. Thus, putting $\Theta$ and $\Lambda$ for general
descriptions of places referring to certain original places
supposed to exist

$\Upsilon\Theta > 0$

will mean that there is at least one such place, and

$\Upsilon\Theta = 1$

will mean that there is just one such place.

$(\Upsilon\Lambda) \times (\Upsilon\Theta) = 0$

will mean that both kinds of places cannot exist;

$\Upsilon\Lambda = \Upsilon\Theta$

that as many places of one kind exist as of the other, etc.

[u]Scholium 55[/u]. According to the above notational system

$[AB]$

must mean the point which is at once the point $[A]$ and the
point $[B]$. The same remark applies to $\{b\beta\}$ and $(B\beta)$,
except that we have here [del]two[/del] [ins]for[/ins] rays and planes.
