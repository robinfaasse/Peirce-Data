Every part of the boundary of the ice-water surface will
be on one or other of these lines, which will nowhere overlap.
Yet there will be a kind of place, which will be double,
consisting of two separate parts, each called a point, a place
not extended at all; and the pair of points form the boundary
of the two lines.

[del]Definition 38. A Point is a place without parts.[/del]

A point is [del]also[/del] a place the common boundary of two
lines; [del]is[/del] the fact that the common boundary of two lines,
which are each the common boundary of two surfaces,
which are each the common boundary of two spaces, which
are extended like all space, has no [del]s[/del] parts is the property
of tridimensionality. It is embodied in the statement:

The common boundary of two lines is a point.*

[u]Footnote[/u] * This is Plato's definition of a point: Aristotle uses it. Eu-
clid's third definition is, "The boundaries of a line are points." But
his first definition is, "A point is that which has no parts." Eu-
clid must have been aware that his definitions involved
positive doctrines.]

We have thus analyzed the idea of a body in space, and
proceeding from the more to the less familiar, have shown
that it involves the ideas of surface, of line, and of
point. But these ideas once acquired, their relations are
more conveniently conceived by introducing the idea of
time and with its aid proceeding from the point, or indivi-
sible [del]s[/del] place, to the solid.
