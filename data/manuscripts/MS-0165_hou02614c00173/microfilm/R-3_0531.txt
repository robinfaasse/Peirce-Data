But by Corollary 99, from the last two propositions it follows
that
$$\{[\underline{b}\underline{e}][\underline{c}\underline{d}]\} \prec (\underline{b}\underline{c}).$$
In a similar way it evidently follows that
$$\{[\underline{b}\underline{e}][\underline{c}\underline{d}]\} \prec (\underline{e}\underline{d}).$$
But from the last two propositions, by Corollary 97, it follows that
$$\{[\underline{b}\underline{e}][\underline{c}\underline{d}]\} \prec \{(\underline{b}\underline{c})(\underline{e}\underline{d})\}.$$
But if one ray is contained in another, it follows that two points
at least of the latter are identical with points of the former,
and are therefore contained in the former. Whence the whole
of the latter ray is contained in the former, or
$$\{(\underline{b}\underline{c})(\underline{e}\underline{d})\} \prec \{[\underline{b}\underline{e}][\underline{c}\underline{d}]\}.$$
These two last propositions amount to the following:
$$\{(\underline{b}\underline{c})(\underline{e}\underline{d})\} = \{[\underline{b}\underline{e}][\underline{c}\underline{d}]\}.$$
We thus get a more formal proof of Corollary 104.
