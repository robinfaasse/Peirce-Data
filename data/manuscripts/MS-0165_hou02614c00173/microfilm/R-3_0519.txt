[del]Corollary 96.[/del] If two planes have a point in common
that point lies in their common ray.

Corollary 97. Any two points whatever lie in one ray.

Corollary 98. If any two points are contained in a plane
the whole of the [del]line[/del] ray through them is contained in that plane.

Corollary [del]99.[/del] [ins]100.[/ins] Every three planes [ins]contain[/ins] [del]have[/del] a common point.

Corollary 101. Every three points are contained in one plane.

Corollary 102. Three planes which do not contain a common
ray contain only one point in common.

Corollary 103. Three points that are not contained in one
ray are [ins]all[/ins] contained in only one plane.

[del]Corollary 104.[/del] [ins]Theorem 49.[/ins] [u]Every point and ray are contained in[/u]
[u]one plane.[/u]

Demonstration. Let [A] be any point, and {b} any [ins]ray.[/ins] [del]ray.[/del]
Then, I say, there is a plane (Ab) which contains both [A]
and {b}. For let [C] and [D] be any two different points
contained in {b}. Then, by Corollary 100, there is [ins]a[/ins] plane (ACD)
which contains the three points [A], [C], and [D]. [del]Now take[/del]
[del]any point, [E] not contained in that plane. Then, there is a second[/del]
[del]plane (ECD) which contains the three points [E], [C], [D].[/del] And
by Corollary 98, since [C] and [D] are contained in that
plane the whole of the ray {b} in which [C] and [D] both
lie is contained in that plane. Thus both [A] and {b} are
contained in one plane. Q.E.D.
