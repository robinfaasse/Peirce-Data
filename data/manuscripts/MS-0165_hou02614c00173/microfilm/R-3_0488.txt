[del]Division 7[/del] [ins]topically[/ins]
[u]Definition 36[/u]. The [del]singular[/del] lines of surfaces are

1[u]st[/u], artiad nodal lines, from which [del]there are[/del] four or more sheets extends, and

2[u]nd[/u], perissid lines, from which an odd number of sheets extend. These include bounding edges:

[u]Scholium 40[/u]. Besides the above there are blunt edges, similar to the angles of lines, and cuspidal edges, similar to the cusps of lines.

Division [del]8[/del] [ins]8[/ins]. The topically singular points of surfaces are,

1[u]st[/u], singular points of singular lines; (Thus, if a sheet of paper be bent so that two opposite edges come together at one point, that point is an artiad node of a perissid line of the surface.)

2[u]nd[/u], isolated singular points, [del]or crinodes[/del] [ins]at which two, three, or more[/ins] sheets are tacked together;

3[u]d[/u], points partaking of both the above characters, two or more sheets being connected at an isolated point which [ins]lies[/ins] on a singular line or several singular lines.

[u]Scholium 41[/u]. Besides the above, there are [del]a[/del] cuspidal points of different kinds, and other singularities somewhat difficult to understand without models, and not very important.
