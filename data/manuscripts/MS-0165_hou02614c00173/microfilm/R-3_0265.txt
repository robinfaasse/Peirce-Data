[u]Scholium 4[/u]. There has been, since the days of Euclid, a regular form, universally approved, for the demonstration of a theorem or lemma. The proposition is stated, first. Next, is described, with letters, an icon of the conditions of the [del]problem[/del] proposition, which is called the [ins]beginning of the[/ins] construction. [del]Thirdly,[/del] the proposition is restated with reference to that construction. The construction is then completed by any additions or changes which may be necessary, [del]and its[/del] [ins]upon the construction[/ins] Fifthly, the consequences of certain experiments [del]are[/del] [ins]are[/ins] traced out, and the proposition is thus shown to be true of it. Sixthly, this is stated to be the case; and this statement is called the proof. To that, are appended the letters Q.E.D. for quod erat demonstrandum, [ins]a[/ins] which had to be shown.

[[u]Gloss 14[/u]. The word [u]construction[/u], used in English since the first English Euclid, in 1570, is taken from the Latin [u]constructio[/u], little used in the [del]geometrical[/del] sense, because the Romans were not geometers.
