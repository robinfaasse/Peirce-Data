Case 11. The second player occupies a corner square [fig]3x3 grid with O, X, and numbers 1 to 4[/fig] There are now 4 essentially different situations which the first player can occupy which are numbered in the figure. We distinguish them as cases, 111, 112, 113, 114.

Case 111. The first player occupies a compartment [del]next to[/del] [ins]adjacent[/ins] adjoining that of the second player. Then, to prevent an immediate tit-tat-toe, the second player must occupy the opposite side compartment. [fig]3x3 grid with O, X, and numbers 1 to 5[/fig] There are now five squares [del]either[/del] [ins]which[/ins] the first player can occupy, as numbered in the figure. They make cases 1111, 1112, 1113, 1114, 1115.

Case 1111. The first player occupies the [ins]other[/ins] side square [del]next to[/del] [ins]adjoining[/ins] the second player's corner square. Then the second player, to prevent instant tit-tat-toe must occupy the opposite compartment. Now the first player may either occupy one of the corner [fig]3x3 grid with O, X, and numbers 1 to 2[/fig] squares adjoining a side square occupied by him (Case 11111) or the corner opposite the second player's corner square (Case 11112.)

Case 11111. The second player is obliged to occupy the opposite corner square and the first player to occupy the last, and there is no tit-tat-toe. [fig]3x3 grid with O and X[/fig]

Case 11112. The second player occupies any square, and there is no possibility of tit-tat-toe. Thus there is no tit-tat-toe in Case 1111.
