[u]Scholium 25[/u]. Anharmonic ratios are of the utmost importance in the mathematical doctrine of measurements.

[del]Art 74[/del] [ins][u]Definition 83[/u][/ins]. A set of objects are said to be arranged in a ring, when all are connected together, but so that, taking any four of them, they make two pairs of opposites, so related that, if all connections are severed at any two that are not opposites, the other two are not separated; but if connections are severed at two opposites, the other two are separated.

[u]Illustrations[/u]. If we consider the letters of the alphabet in their order, and take any four of them, as A, E, I, O, we see that A and O will be separated if connections are broken at E and I, so that if the letters form a ring A and O are a pair of opposites. [del]But[/del] [ins]Yet[/ins] E and I the other two are not separated by breaks at A and O. Thus, there are not two such pairs of opposites. Again, suppose Z joined to G and and A to V, as in Figure 108. Then, take D, X, K, P. Suppose connections broken at D and X; still, K L M N O P remain connected; [del]and[/del] if the breaks are at D and K, P Q R S T U V W X remain connected, and if the breaks are at D and P, X Y Z G H I J K remain connected. Thus, there is again no pair of opposites.
