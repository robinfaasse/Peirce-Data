By the nature of the process of division, [ins]the[/ins] each remainder must be less
than the divisor. Thus, each remainder will be less than the preceding remainder.

Thus, the remainders belonging to a given remainder are part of the numbers less
than a given number, which is enumerable. The process goes on till a re-
mainder zero is reached.

Let R be the remainder before 0. Then X will be the greatest common
factor of B and 0. But R is a factor of 0 and no greater number is a factor
of R. Hence X = R.

The greatest common factor is also the greatest common divisor, in
case R > 1; for then the number which multiplied into it gives either A or B
is less than A or B, and is therefore a part of A or B. But when the greatest
common factor is 1, there is no common divisor.
