[u]Section 2. Homogeneity.[/u]

[del]Art. 18.[/del] [ins]89[/ins] [u]Definition[/u] [del]18.[/del] [ins]100.[/ins] To say that space is homogeneous means that every place, or region, is, in respect to the manner in which it is connected with [del]surrounding[/del] [ins]neighboring[/ins] places, exactly like every other place.

[u]Postulate 7[/u] ([del]copied from Art. 7.[/del]). Space is homogeneous in its connections.

[ins]N.B.(10)[/ins]
[del]Explanation.[/del] This is not supposed to be true in regard to distances and the motions of bodies, as will appear in due course.

That this property [u]really[/u] belongs to the all the connections of places, there is very little evidence. Certain phenomena, to be mentioned below, point that way; but the distant parts of space might be very different from those near the solar system without our discovering it. The property is assumed to be true, mainly because there is nothing against it, and because it is a very convenient assumption, greatly facilitating geometrical reasoning. It is also our natural belief.

[del]Art. 19.[/del] [ins]90[/ins] [u]Definition[/u] [del]19.[/del] [ins]101.[/ins] A place is any one of the like parts of space.

[ins]N.B.11.[/ins]
[del]Explanation.[/del] Since a place is not necessarily a homogeneous part of space, it may be itself indivisible.
