Returning to the five points
[fig]Arrow pointing up and right[/fig]
$x\quad y\quad z\quad u\quad v = x+y+z+u$
(or $v = -(x+y+z+u)$ but it is all the
same, the coefficient is neglected.)
these lie in pairs on ten lines
$x,y\quad x,z\quad x,u\quad x,v\quad y,z\quad y,u\quad y,v\quad z,u\quad z,v\quad u,v$
and by threes on ten planes
$z,u,v\quad y,u,v\quad y,z,v\quad y,z,u\quad x,u,v\quad x,z,v\quad x,z,u\quad x,y,v$ etc.
On what line do $x,y,z$ and $x,u,v$ cut?
The plane $x,y,z$ includes every point of
the form $px + qy + (1-p-q)z$
The plane $x,u,v$ or $x,u,\frac{1}{4}(x+y+z+u)$
includes every point of the form
$sx + tu + \frac{1}{4}(1-s-t)(x+y+z+u)$
$= \frac{1+3s-t}{4}x + \frac{1-s-t}{4}y + \frac{1-s-t}{4}z + \frac{1+3t-s}{4}u$
Then on the intersection [del]$\frac{1+3s-t}{4} = p$[/del]
$\frac{1+3t-s}{4} = 0$
$\frac{1+3s-t}{4} = p\quad \frac{1-s-t}{4} = q = 1-p-q\quad p = 1-2q$
$1-p = \frac{3}{4}(1-s)$

Answer: On the line $x\quad (y+z)\quad (u+v)$

Each plane cuts 6 of the others in lines
coinciding by threes so that there are but $\frac{60}{2\cdot 3} = 10$
and cuts the remaining 3 in $\frac{10\times 3}{2} = 15$ lines
Total 25 lines.

$\begin{aligned} 1-s+3t &= 0 \\ 1+3s-t &= 4p \\ 1-s-t &= 4q = 4(1-p-q) \end{aligned} \quad \begin{aligned} &= 4+8t \\ &= -4t \end{aligned}$
$t = -q\quad p = 1-2q$
$s = 1-3q$
And the point of intersection are $(1-2q)x + qy + qz$
or the line is $x, y+z$
