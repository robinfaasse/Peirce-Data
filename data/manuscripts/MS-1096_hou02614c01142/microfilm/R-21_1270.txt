[u]Cornell[/u] [del]U.[/del] [ins]Ithaca, N.Y.[/ins] Two Stations were simultaneously occupied here. They are distinguished by the names
of the pendulum supports placed in them, being distinguished as "Old Stand" and "New Stand."

The Old Stand was in a large dark inner closet ordinarily used for keeping acids and dangerous
chemicals, in the cellar of the Physical Laboratory of Cornell University, in Ithaca, New York.
The supports rested upon three stones which had been placed there some months [del]ago[/del] [ins]before[/ins], under the
obliging superintendence of my friend Professor E.A. Fuertes. The closet was never entered dur-

the closet was, was exclusively devoted to my work. The new stand was in a small wooden observatory
attached to the Engineering School of the University. This observatory has four rooms. First, an
office into which the outer door opens, next a transit-room, in which was the transit instrument
with which time was observed, then, [del]a[/del] [ins]a[/ins] round room with a dome-room for a small equatorial above it.

[u]Ann Arbor.[/u] The station was situated near the western extremity of the library of the University
of Michigan, in Ann Arbor, Michigan. The old stand was toward the south, and the outer wall; the
new stand was toward the north and an inner wall, and was better placed. The geographical position
according to Professor [del]Schæberle[/del] [ins]Schæberle[/ins] is latitude 42°16'26" N longitude 5[sup]h[/sup]34[sup]m[/sup]55[sup]s[/sup].7 from Greenwich.
I found the station to be 105 feet above the Michigan Central Railway at the station, which is
said to be 193 feet above the Detroit River in 1869, and 771 feet above sea-level. The pendulum[ins]s[/ins]
were protected by paper screens. I am indebted to President Angell, and to Professors [del]Schæberle[/del] [ins]Schæberle[/ins],
Langley, and others, for every possible aid in making my observations here.
