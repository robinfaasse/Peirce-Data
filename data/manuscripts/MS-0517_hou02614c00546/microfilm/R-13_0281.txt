that the copulative is general in its nature, and therefore
definite. For to assert

[u]A and B[/u]

is to assert a proposition which the interpreter is at liberty to
take as meaning A or as meaning B. Accordingly, we find that
the [del]expression of the[/del] principle of contradiction, which applies only to what is definite

[u]A or B[/u]

is to assert a proposition which gives the utterer the option [ins]between[/ins]
