Hypothetical proposition, in the old, and, as I conceive, the
only ethically justifiable sense of [del]the technical[/del] [ins]term as a[/ins] term, is a
proposition analyzed into two or more propositions not
connected by any identity. Hypothetical propositions are
of three kinds; the copulative, the disjunctive, and the equi-
valential. A copulative proposition is one which in the sim-
plest analysis consists primarily of two or more propositions,
[del]called the copulants[/del]
the truth or falsity of which each of which follows necessarily
from the truth of the copulate. A disjunctive proposition
is one which in the simplest analysis consists primarily of
two or more propositions, called the disjuncts, the truth or falsity
of each of which follows necessarily from the falsity of the
disjunct. An equivalential proposition is a proposition of
which there are two equally simple analyses, one of which
makes would (were it not for the other) render it disjunctive,
the other copulative. Simple copulative propositions
