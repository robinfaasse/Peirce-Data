It speaks of an antecedent, as that which being known
something else, the consequent, may be known. In
our vernacular the latter is inaccurately called the
[u]consequence[/u], which in the language of logic means
the proposition which expresses that when the antecedent
is known the consequent may be known, or it is the signi-
fication of this proposition.

A sign, B, which signifies a character [ins](or essential part)[/ins] which is
signified by a sign, A, is termed a [u]predicate[/u] of A.

A sign, C, which denotes a real object which is denoted
[ins](or substantial part)[/ins]
by the sign, A, is termed a [u]subject[/u] of A. The totality of the
predicates of A, as signifying [del]between them[/del] all the characters signi-
fied by A, is termed the [u]logical depth[/u] of A. This is
the oldest and much convenient term [del]word[/del]; but the
same thing is called by the Port Royalists the [u]compre-[/u]
[u]hension[/u] of A, [ins]by Germans the Inhalt, or content of A,[/ins] and by De Morgan [u]the force[/u]. The totality
of the subjects of A, as denoting between them all the
real objects of A, is termed the [u]logical breadth[/u] of A.
