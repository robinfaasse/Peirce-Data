"man was translated" is [del]indefinite[/del] in respect to men,
and allows the utterer to defend it by instancing
Enoch or such [del]man[/del] [ins]definite[/ins] [del]man[/del] as he can find; while "[del]Any man[/del]
[del]died"[/del] "Any phoenix rises from its ashes" allows
the interpreter to [del]falsify[/del] [ins]refute[/ins] it by producing any in-
dividual phoenix [del]he[/del] [ins]she[/ins] can find. If either party has
such liberty of choice the other is necessarily deprived
of it; so that a proposition cannot be at once vague
and general in the same respect. It can be both definite
and individual in the same respect, and may then be
termed singular in that respect.[ins]*[/ins] An individual propo-
sition asserts the existence, in reality, of an object cor-
responding to that part of it that is individual; a general
proposition asserts the non-existence of some kind
of object. A definite proposition attaches some [del]positive[/del] [ins]definite[/ins]
character to the nature signified by its definite part; a
vague proposition denies such character.
