[u]§ 2.2.3.2 Second Definition of Ordinals.[/u]

Clause I. There is a certain appurtenance, N, [ins](being next higher than)[/ins] such that [del]whatever[/del] [ins]and that[/ins] relation, r, may be, and whatever ordinals p and q may be, [del]either[/del] [ins]from[/ins] the facts that certain ordinals are N to certain ordinals [del]taken[/del] [ins]and that[/ins] certain ordinals are not N to certain ordinals [del]taken[/del] in conjunction with the clauses of this definition, logically necessitate p's being r to q, or else p is not r to q.

Clause II. Every ordinal is N'd by an ordinal.

Clause III. There is an ordinal that is not N to any ordinal (and any ordinal of that character is termed a [u]zero[/u], or a 0.)

Clause IV. Whatever ordinal is N to an ordinal is also in a certain relation, g, of comparative fulfillment to that ordinal.

Clause V. Whatever [ins]different[/ins] ordinals p and q may be, either p is g to q, or else q is g to p.

§ 2.2.4. I have a proof that every property of ordinals can be deduced from either of these definitions, and either definition can be [del]proved[/del] deduced from the other.

§ 2.2.5. Addition and multiplication may be defined either ordinally or multitudinally, and from either definition the other can be deduced.
