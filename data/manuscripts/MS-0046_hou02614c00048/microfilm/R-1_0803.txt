[pn]7/[/pn] [u]Second Ordinal Definition of Addition[/u]

Any ordinal [u]s[/u] is a sum of any ordinal, [u]m[/u], as addend, and of any ordinal, [u]n[/u], as augend, if and only if, [u]s[/u] stands in such a triadic relation to [u]m[/u] and [u]n[/u], that either [u]m[/u] is [del]N[/del] [ins]V[/ins] to an ordinal, [u]l[/u], and [u]s[/u] is N to an ordinal which is a sum of [u]l[/u] as addend, and [u]n[/u] as augend, or [u]m[/u] is not N to any ordinal and [u]s[/u] is [u]n[/u].

[u]Multitudinal Definition of Addition[/u].

A multitude, [u]s[/u], is the sum of a multitude, [u]m[/u], as addend, and of a multitude, [u]n[/u], as augend, if, and only if, the Ss, the Ms, and the Ns, being any three collections whose multitudes are [u]s[/u], [u]m[/u], and [u]n[/u], respectively, there are, by logical necessity, two relations, [u]μ[/u] and [u]ν[/u], such that every S is either [del]μ[/del] to some [del]M[/del] [ins]N[/ins] to which no other S is [del]μ[/del] [ins]N[/ins] or is [del]ν[/del] [ins]V[/ins] to some N to which no other S is [del]ν[/del] [ins]V[/ins]; while every M is [del]μ'd[/del] [ins]N'd[/ins] by some S by which no other M is [del]μ'd[/del] [ins]N'd[/ins] nor any N is [del]ν'd[/del] [ins]V'd[/ins], and every N is [del]ν'd[/del] [ins]V'd[/ins] by some S by which no [del]M[/del] [ins]N[/ins] is [del]μ'd[/del] [ins]N'd[/ins] nor any other N is [del]ν'd[/del] [ins]V'd[/ins].

[u]Multiplication[/u].

To define multiplication in the spirit of the first definition of ordinals, while that definition turns upon characters of single individuals, multiplication should be considered as referring to characters of pairs, that is to dyadic relations.
