House of Representatives U. S.,
Washington, D. C., January 17 1890

My Dear Charles:-

I have just received your letter of yesterday in regard to the defeat of the salary bill. I voted for the repayment of the salaries because I thought it was the only decent and straightforward thing to do. I agree with every word you say and the serious part of it is that such action brings discredit upon Congress. If Congress will not respect itself it is not likely to hold the respect of its constituency. There is only one point on which I differ with you. I have not received a single letter against the repayment of salaries nor do I believe any other Congressman has, and I doubt extremely whether there are any appreciable number of voters in the United States who think that the money ought not to have been paid.

Very Truly Yours
H. C. Lodge

Charles S. Peirce, Esq.
