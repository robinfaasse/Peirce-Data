Milford Pa [date]1899 Oct 30[/date].

My dear Cabot:

The information in yours of Oct 30 is astounding when taken in connection with facts I shall state. I beg you will do me the favor to read this letter.

You tell me that you wrote to Dr. Pritchett as soon as you heard of my candidacy and that he replied that the place of Inspector of Standards had been filled. A letter from Nannie to my wife shows that you had heard July 5. Dr. Pritchett does not to a powerful senator wait for an answer. Therefore, he must have written that about the middle of July.

But it was announced that this place would be filled from an eligible list to be made as the result of an examination for which the papers must go in on or before July 14. The date had been June 1, but it was

[pn]page 4[/pn]
[del]was said the appro[/del] [ins]selection was of such a character that it was deemed best to have a make-believe examination and to announce the salary as $3000.[/ins] I heard again in July that my papers had created some dismay, & that there was to be another examination to give the favored man a chance to try again. Though I took no stock in the story, I informed some men and said "Now we shall see". It appears to me we do see.

Both in ability and in experience no possible candidate could come near me. I am entitled to insist that promises and the law shall be observed. There seems to have been an attempt to trick you and me and the law. If I don't get the place to which I am entitled, I want redress. What business has Pritchett (whom I would have loyally supported had he appointed me, but who does not command the respect of the profession of engineers) what business has he to keep me expecting the place, since [del]April[/del] [ins]June or July[/ins] without giving me notice it was filled in June or July?

Your affectionate cousin C. S. Peirce
