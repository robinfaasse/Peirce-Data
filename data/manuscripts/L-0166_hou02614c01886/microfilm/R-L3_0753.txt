Dear Sir; Your favor 27[ins]th[/ins] Inst was gratefully
received. I do not know, but presume you are
older than I (58); consequently I can look to
you with corresponding respect. — I recognize
what may be of value in anything I have done,
has been performed under the direction of
God; I being but the willing (free) agent
to second & carry out His purposes: hence, if the
work ("Completion of Logic") should be pigeon-holed,
as you suggested in your first letter, it will not
disturb the equanimity of my personality to
any serious extent.

Having therefore the utmost confidence in the
integrity of your motives, as inferred from your
last letter; & seeing it may be some time before
you can visit New York, I make the following
proposal: —

I will send you by express for a leisurely ex-
amination, the first draft of Comprehen-
sive Philosophy. It is substantially the same
