#1208. Jackson Blvd
Chicago [date]Dec. 10th 1908[/date]

Dear Prof. Peirce,

Got your Dec 8th letter [ins]just[/ins] now and was mighty glad to hear from you. I sometimes want to say as I observe the goings-on, why Dr Carus "never opens his mouth but he puts his foot into it" but of course that would be only an extravagant out-to-manner of speaking for I must heartily like the Dr and moreover he is not without his points, as a philosopher. In his "Fundamental Problems" he makes "Form & Formal Thought" the core of his philosophy (I sometimes think it was I who "put him onto" even that) and however wide and vague and in part indeterminate that text is, if he would only stick to and study out and develop its possibilities and suggestions he would be pretty near on the right track to say the least. But the Dr. wants to figure as, is, or itches for [del]a[/del] leadership with an intense and persistent irritation. He has struck out a system and he feels himself committed to it and he is bitterly disappointed that it cuts so little figure in the philosophic world.
