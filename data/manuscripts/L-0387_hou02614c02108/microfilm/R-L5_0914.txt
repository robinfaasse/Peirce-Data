My dear Mr. Russell:

In the [u]Century Dictionary[/u], under [u]relation[/u], I have somewhat changed my nomenclature of classes of relatives. To this nomenclature I shall adhere. Dual relations may be divided in 9 classes, according as they contain under them [u]all[/u], [u]part[/u], or [u]none[/u] of the individual relatives of the form A:A and of the form A:B. Of these 9 classes, four are special logical relations. Namely,

[u]coexistence[/u] embraces all of form A:A and all of form A:B
[u]identity[/u] " " " " none " "
[u]otherness[/u] " none " " all " "
[u]incompossibility[/u] " " " " none " "

The other 5 classes are
1. [u]Differences[/u] or [u]alio-relations[/u], which contain [u]none[/u] of the form A:A
Thus, it is absurd to say A is greater than A, for A is not greater than A.
2. [u]Sibi-relations[/u] or [u]concurrencies[/u], which contain [u]none[/u] of the form A:B
Thus, it is absurd to say A is self-conscious of not A, for A is not self-conscious of not A.
3. [u]Agreements[/u] which contain [u]all[/u] of the form A:A. These are negatives of differences. "not greater" is an example.
