[pn]7[/pn] The only very important [del]differe[/del] divergence of the
Logic of Chrysippus from that of Aristotle is its utter
denial of the validity of Induction. It was natural that
this should be the logical doctrine of a philosophy expressive
of the sternest morality amid a corrupt state of society. Such
a morality maintains itself by a sharp demarcation between
the sheep and the goats. It can see no colors but black and
white. Nothing between necessity and impossibility.

in the direction of the "hypotheticals" which were regarded
as compounded of categoricals instead of being as they
