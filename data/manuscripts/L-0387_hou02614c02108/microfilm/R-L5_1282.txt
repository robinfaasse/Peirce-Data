My dear Russell [date]April 9, 1909[/date] [pn]V2[/pn]

On p. 294 of the [del]April[/del] Monist you take an
isosceles right [del]triangle[/del] and letting fall a perpendicular
from the right angle upon the hypothenuse, declare
that it [del]would[/del] be [del]almost[/del] an imputation on the
reader to prove that the two half triangles are
isosceles. Never mind the imputation! [u]I defy you to
prove it.[/u] If the sum of the angles of the original
triangle is $\pi - 2x$, then its 3 angles are $\frac{1}{2}\pi$, [del]$\frac{1}{4}\pi - x$[/del], and $\frac{1}{4}\pi - x$
and the 3 angles of one of the [del]half[/del] triangles are $\frac{1}{2}\pi$, $\frac{1}{4}\pi - x$, and $\frac{1}{4}\pi$
Therefore by the Pons Asinorum such half triangle is [u]not[/u] iso-
sceles. I hope you don't feel that to be an imputation!
very faithfully, C. S. Peirce
