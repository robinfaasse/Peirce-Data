RELIGION

I am encouraged by the title of this Review and by my knowledge of its author to believe that an article, or so, in defense of religion (in the sense in which I shall use that term) will find at least respectful tolerance in these pages. While I fully realize that personal history yields little if any edification, and that the same is in no proper way relevant to the subject to be discussed, still in order to forestall divers prejudices that may arise I will say here at the start that I stand for, have always stood for the very freest of free-thinking. If there is anyone who goes beyond me in reprobation of all attempts and any attempt to stifle or discourage free-thought or its proper expression all I can say is that I have never yet met such a person. I am also a thoroughgoing evolutionist and an enthusiastic believer in the superior excellence of the modern scientific customs of seeking the truth.

But I am also, (most thankfully do I say and dwell upon it) as I believe, a religious man. That is to say, I believe in God, in Prayer and in Free-Will. I believe moreover that we are each of us persons whose real existence is neither ended nor degraded by that event of our lives we call death. I believe that God loves Man with a love that is - Alas!- little understood and valued, and that man may and will sometime awake to an adequate perception of the love that God is. I believe that as men awake to an adequate perception of the love that God is, they will experience the saving and converting operation of that perception [del]and will with glad and conspiring souls requite that love, loving God with all their hearts, with all their minds and with all their strength. Doing this they will be an insuperable necessity, love their fellowmen after[/del] [ins]after[/ins] the same passion of love that God bears all men. How could they otherwise truly love God?
