If religion for the present and for a very long future is
certain to obtain, as it certainly is; if man cannot truly prosper
without a moral basis; if he cannot truly live without religion, it
is of course to the last degree desirable that these truths should
be generally recognized and accepted and that men should be led to
revive their interest in religion, all towards the end that a relig-
ion truer and more informing, more inspiring, more stimulating,
more sustaining and more consoling than any as yet extant may come
to prevail in the world.

Questions relating to what that better religion is likely
to prove to be are of secondary moment. The vital thing is to lead
men to know that religion itself is insuperable and this irrespec-
tive of whether we regard men collectively or individually.

There is much to be said concerning the causes that have
operated in the decay of religion and much more to be said concern-
ing the causes that operate as obstacles to the upgrowth of the bet-
ter religion and very much more to be said in delineation of what
may be prominent features of that better religion but all these
topics must await other opportunities.
