I myself began with defining logic as the
science of the general relations of symbols to their
objects. But on the whole, it certainly is concerned
with all signs and though the relation to their
objects is what logic has specially in view yet I
think it should begin with general semeiotic. A
sign may be defined as something which mediates
between an object and a mind, by being itself in
some way influenced or affected or determined by
that object and then in its turn producing an effect
upon the mind, which effect I call the [u]interpretant[/u]
of the sign, of such a nature that the mind is thereby
[ins]and therein[/ins] itself affected indirectly by the object. This wording
can be improved; but I think it a valuable definition
of a sign. Now we have at once to make certain
distinctions both with reference to the [u]Object[/u] and [del]with
reference to the[/del] [u]Interpretant[/u]. As to the [u]Object[/u], we have
to distinguish between the [del]D[/del] [u]Immediate Object[/u] i.e.
the object as it is represented in the sign, and the [u]Real
Object[/u] [[del]Don't forget my definition of real[/del], That is real
