My dear Sir:

[marginalia]
Consequences
of necessitarianism
[/marginalia]

I have been reflecting upon your suggestion that I should go to Chicago and become professor there. It seems to be the thing for me to do, provided they call me. During many years, I felt that for my peculiar powers the world had no use. Hence, I only threw off pieces here and there, and my deeper studies in logic remain today unpublished, and nobody dreams of the things I have found and studied, [del]just[/del] [ins]to[/ins] learn, with no ulterior design, or is pardonable if not commendable that I should wish to show what I can do in various kinds of literature. But I fear the telling of emotional stories is hardly compatible with the self-abnegation and exclusive devotion to the cause of learning and education [del]to which a man who proposes to become a professor must surrender himself.[/del] Therefore, if you think there is any prospect that I might receive such a call to Chicago, and if you have not gone too far in the matter of my reading to turn back, I think it will be best to give that up. If I am to be put into a position to do the work I was brought into the world to do, I desire to lay aside all other ambitions [del]at once[/del] and give myself up to that work exclusively.

Yours very truly,
C. S. Peirce
