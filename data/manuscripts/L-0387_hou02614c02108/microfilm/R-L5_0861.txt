Dear Mr. Peirce

Your last two letters of 22d & 25th inst
respectively reached me together yesterday
morning. I got your long letter of Sept 8th
all right and my lack of acknowledging
same must have been an oversight for it
is a letter that I highly prize on account
of its contents. In it you give a syllabus of
your book on Logic, you also enclosed a
power to [del]make[/del] [ins]use[/ins] in pledging your copy-
right &c. This power I now return.

When I said that your writings were
difficult, I meant only such as, say, your
paper of 1870 in Memoirs Acad of A & Sci,
& the more technical of those in the Proceed-
ing of same Society, and your papers in the
Am. Jour of Mathematics, your brochure on
the Logic of Relation, & the Appendix B. in
the Studies in Logic. As for your other writ-
ings, when you write discursively, you are
