P.O. Milford Pa [date]1908 July 10[/date]

My dear Russell:

I highly approve of your writing a logic & will give you all the counsel you desire; and I think I may promise that in some form my name shall go on the title-page if you desire it. But in every undertaking some one person must have the final say & responsibility; and that must be you; because I have by no means given up the hope of getting out a book of my own. The two won't in the least interfere with each other's sales: quite the contrary. My book will inevitably be harder to read than yours.

You must decide on the plan & everything else. But you may be sure that I will criticize without gloves. There is no time for formalities of manners in business of [del]vituperation[/del] [ins]deliberation[/ins]. I don't like the idea of making it historical. The reason for doing so must be that the development of the individual must go along the same lines as that of the race. That is true; but [del]there are two things I've said: 1[u]st[/u] that[/del] this is only true in a sketchy way and [del]2[u]nd[/u] that[/del] there is a reason for the truth of it & it is better to follow that reason direct. Mach had a most exceptional subject which was developed more rationally than any other owing to the great superiority of the leaders. Yet he filled a thick book with mere outlines easily stated in 50 pages, & better stated for most purposes. So with Muir's unfinished work on Determinants.
