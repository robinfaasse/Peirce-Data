On this, Aristotle's system was built, which he claims at the end of the Sophistical Elenchi was entirely original. But he was obliged to take some account of the inductive method of Socrates. He overlooked many things. First the simpler forms

If there be an S there is a P
There is an S and yet there is not a P

and these [del]supposed[/del] [ins]believed[/ins] to have been studied by his successor Theophrastus. At any rate, Boethius, about A.D. 500, knew something about them, without perceiving that their essential structure was simpler than Aristotle's forms [del]which[/del], added to them the assertion of identity; thus,

If there be an S, that same S is P
There is an S, and yet that same S is not P.

They also overlooked more complex forms such as

Any S is in the relation R to every P
Any S is in the relation R to some P
Some P is in the relation of be R'd by every S
Some P is in the relation of being R'd by some S

Also such still more complicated forms as

A abuses B to C
or A is in the triadic relation R to B for C.
