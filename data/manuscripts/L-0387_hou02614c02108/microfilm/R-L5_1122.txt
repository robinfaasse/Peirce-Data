Both classes are fated to be disappointed. Two issues only are com-
petent to supersede the need and the obtaining of religion. First,
the gaining of omniscience by man and second, the degradation of
man to that point when he shall lose all interest concerning his in-
tent and significance as one of the actors in the great drama of the
Immensities and Eternities. When man facing the Universe shall cease
to wonder, admire and aspire; when he shall cease to wish to compre-
hend after some sort the scene into which he has been cast; when he
shall cease to [ins]feel[/ins] [del]feel[/del] any interest in his own origin and destiny or
in the origin and destiny of those who accompany him; when he shall
cease to listen to the various suggestions and questions that occur
to him in the various fatalities and vicissitudes of his existence,
then religion may die out as it may die out [del]as it[/del] [u]will[/u] die out upon
the advent of omniscience.

But until the occurrence of one or the other of these two
issues the questioning, the aspiring, the reaching, groping soul of
man will as best it may never cease to seek to complement his whole
environment as an entire organic whole by the conjunction of such
beliefs as he has about what is as yet unknowable, with his knowledge
He will sift and assemble such various indications as may be avail-
able and draw conclusions of various warrants of assurance. Having
reached diverse conclusions of some sort as he certainly will they
will inevitably enter into and become part and parcel of his soul-
life. Indeed of all his holdings those very conclusions regarding
the as yet unknowable (until, as very possibly they may, they have
become of weak or vanishing tenure or have become corrected by more
knowledge or better counsel) will become and obtain as the very ones
to him the most fundamental and influential infusing all his other
holdings with their own proper [del]line[/del] [ins]hue[/ins] and tone.

Now any man who has any considerable body of such holdings
regarding the as yet unknowable, which body of holdings is not utter-
ly chaotic, has religion. If such holdings as any kind of system
enter into and modulate his life and conduct he is a religious man
no matter how he may be regarded by others or how he may regard or
call himself.
