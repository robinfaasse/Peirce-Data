But Cantor (and mathematicians generally) write the addend after the augend, which, in my view, is wrong. Of course it still remains true that every [del]ordinal[/del] Grade has a Grade next to it and therefore we run on to $\omega + \omega$, which Cantor writes $2\omega$. Then comes $2\omega + 1 \dots 2\omega + \omega$ $= 3\omega$ and so on, [del]to[/del] until he has an endless series of endless series of ordinals. To say that there is a next [del]order[/del] Grade to this seems to me a new extension of the idea of nextness, since this next, is neither next to any Grade nor next to any endless series of Grades, but is only next to an endless series of endless grades. Here therefore it seems to me clearly that we have a new kind of afterness, which I write after. But Cantor simply speaks of $\omega^2$ without regarding himself as introducing any new conception; and his reason is that the multitude is unchanged. That is to say there is an order of counting according to which $\omega^2 = \omega$. But it seems to me the better way of expressing this would be to say that the multitude of infinites, or transfinites, as he calls them, depends on the order of the count, and that the minimum count of $\omega^2$ equals that of $\omega$.
