[u]Dynamical Interpretant[/u], or the effect the sign actually

the sign as its reasonable effect, as the result of sufficient
inquiry. Now signs are to be divided in ten ways
1[sup]st[/sup], as to the Nature of the Sign in itself
2[sup]nd[/sup] as to the Nature of [del]its[/del] Immediate Object
3[sup]d[/sup], as to the Nature of the Real Object
4[sup]th[/sup], as to the Relation of the Sign to its Real Object
5[sup]th[/sup], as to the Nature of the Immediate Interpretant
6[sup]th[/sup], as to the Nature of the Dynamical Interpretant
7[sup]th[/sup], as to the Relation of the Sign to the Dynamical Interpretant
8[sup]th[/sup], as to the Nature of the Ultimate Interpretant
9[sup]th[/sup], as to the Relation of the Sign to the Ultimate Interpretant
10[sup]th[/sup], as to the manner in which the Sign represents its Object
to the Ultimate Interpretant.

All these ten divisions appear to be trichotomies, and
if they were independent would give 3[sup]10[/sup] different species of
signs; although there are still further distinctions to be considered.
