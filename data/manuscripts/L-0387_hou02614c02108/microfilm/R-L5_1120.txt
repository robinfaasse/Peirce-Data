that were we sufficiently enlightened, we should see the divine love
as above characterized paramount in every case.

Now of all the things needful in the world of to-day there
is needed, as I conceive, the prevalence of just such a love as that
love is that God is, a love that should obtain not as a mere aes-
thetic sentiment nor even merely as a generally commended excellence
but a love that should obtain and prevail as a real vital deep-seated
motive and passion ever moving men to conduct in true expression of
its aims and intent.

Such a love in man would I conceive regard freedom as its
cardinal beacon. However self-impelled it might be to resort
to self-serving compulsion in one or another of its almost infinite
phases, it would confess with humility and sincere lamentation that
all such resorts were at best mere make-shifts and lapses entailed
only by the imperfection of men's wisdom and discipline. Bearing in
mind and heart its prototype the love that God is and in contempla-
tion of [del]love[/del] [ins]the[/ins] love that love in consequence of itself left man free to
sin as well as to aspire, it would recognize a supreme voucher that
perfect love and perfect wisdom would have not merely no occasion
for self-serving compulsion but would on the contrary reprobate the
same in all its varieties and in all its grades.
