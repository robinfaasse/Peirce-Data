How far is this, or something
corresponding to this true of
all that is vague?

A law fails to confer existence on
its subject. That is, nothing perhaps
fulfils the conditions of the law. If it
does, it does so independently of the
law. A general then lacks existence.

Now take the principle, that in every
contest some contestant must [del]fail[/del] [ins]win[/ins]?
[del]This principle does confer[/del]
What is it, then, which it fails to con-
fer. What element of being is it that
the vague lacks. You cannot say
it lacks determinacy; for this is
equally lacking to the general. In
fact, determinacy is lacking to whatever
is not a complete reality. The vague
seems to lack what we may call [del]whol-[/del]
ness, self-identity, [del]that which makes
the principle of contradiction pertinent
to it.[/del] Is that so?
C. S. Peirce
