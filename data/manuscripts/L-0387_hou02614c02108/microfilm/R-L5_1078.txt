Chicago, Sept. 30, 1896.

Dear Mr. Peirce:

Yours of the 28th inst. has just come. As to politics, I am a Bryan man, not because that I have great faith that the free coinage of silver will bring about a millenium, but because I am a democrat, with socialistic proclivities, and it seems to me that whatever may be done to curb the plutocratic tendencies of the times, is well done. I am somewhat puzzled to solve the problem of how it is that a person with the principles I know from your writings you must possess has been led to favor the gold democracy, but "[u]chacun a son gout[/u]."

On May 1st I entered the office of Story, Westover & Story as a partner, thus resuming active practice which I had suspended since I [del]went[/del] out of office several years ago. My time is therefore very fully occupied, much more fully than I have a taste for, for I am naturally lazy. However, I am in for it and so have to do the best I can My suspending my letters to you is a part of the the things I have been obliged, by circumstances, to do.
