My dear Judge:

Yours of the 19th reached me yesterday afternoon at 5 PM. The previous letter from you which I answered briefly only reached me because a casual person happened to pick it up on the road so that is a specimen of the way my mail is served here. I wrote you a very long letter to which you make no allusion & I conclude you either did not receive it (it was written a day or two before I received your previous one) or else you have written me some letter I have not got.

I will say in a general way that in that long letter I told how I have the MS. of a Book on Logic ready for the press. It has been favorably reported to a firm of schoolbook publishers but they do not want it, because they

Considered as relatives, I take all the objects of the universe as correlatives as explained in Studies in Logic. [del]"Between" is an equiparant relation. "Between" etc. It is its own converse.[/del]

The relations of "among" and "in" differ but little. Among is simply the relation of the copula of inclusion; [del]all[/del] men are mortal, [ins]and[/ins] all men are among mortals are pretty much the same. "In" differs as [del]collective from[/del] [ins]distributive. This relation is the[/ins] subject of careful analysis in my book on Logic, being of great importance in mathematics. [ins]Between is a complication of a transitive relation not equiparant. To be in a relation of B between A & C. Then A<B B<C, B is in relation to that relation between A & C.[/ins] These things all receive full treatment in my book.
