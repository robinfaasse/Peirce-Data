To show the converse
$(a \bar{\jmath} 0) \prec (a \bar{\jmath} 0)^c (a \bar{\jmath} 0) \prec (a \bar{\jmath} 1)^c a$.

These two give his (13), and (14) and (15) are
similarly deducible. To get (16), on the one
hand, by $y \bar{\jmath} 0 \prec x$, we have
$a \infty \bar{\jmath} 0 \prec a \infty$
and on the other hand
$a \infty \prec a(b \bar{\dagger} \bar{b}^c)$ Put $b = \infty$ and this becomes
$a(\infty \bar{\dagger} 0) \prec a \infty \bar{\jmath} 0$
Equation (17) rests upon the principle that
$a b \bar{\jmath} c \prec a \infty \bar{\dagger} (0 \bar{\jmath} c)$
And this again upon
$(p \bar{\dagger} q) \bar{\jmath} c \prec p \bar{e} \bar{\dagger} (q \bar{\jmath} c)$
And this again upon
$p \bar{\jmath} c \prec p \bar{e}$ or else $0 \bar{\jmath} c$
[del]for[/del] $p \bar{\jmath} c \prec p \bar{e}$ or else $0 \bar{\jmath} c$
$(p \bar{\jmath} e)_{ik} = \Pi_j p_{ij} \bar{\jmath} e_{jk} = \Pi_j p_{ij} \bar{e}_{jk} + c_{jk}$
$\prec \Pi_j p_{ij} \bar{e}_{jk} + \Pi_j c_{jk}$
$\prec \Sigma_j p_{ij} + \Pi_j c_{jk}$

Having thus got
$a b \bar{\jmath} c \prec a \infty \bar{\dagger} (0 \bar{\jmath} c)$ Hence $a b \bar{\jmath} 1 \prec a \infty$
we put $c = 1$. Now $0 \bar{\jmath} 1 = 0$ and $a \infty \bar{\dagger} 1 \prec a \infty$

On the other hand
Hence $a \infty \prec a(\infty \bar{\jmath} 1) \prec a \infty \bar{\jmath} 1$.
All this is perfectly evident with the other
notation. For
$\Pi_k \Sigma_j a_{ij} b_{jk} \bar{\dagger} c_{kl} \prec \Sigma_i a_{ij} \bar{\dagger} \Pi_k c_{kl}$.
