Chicago [date]Aug 29th 1894[/date]

My dear Peirce,-

Since our meeting in Chicago now more than eighteen months ago, I have heard from you not a word directly, although considerable in an indirect way. - Had I believed that all went well with you I should probably await overtures on your part, But since I am fearing that your lines are not easy at present, and because in spite of whatever bones I have to pick with you, I care more for the prosperity of your mission in the world, I venture to make this first offer, And a venture, it is, I cannot help but feel, To be of any considerable service to you I must say or hint several things that I fear will be apt to arouse one or more of several very [del]stubborn[/del] [ins]untoward[/ins] excitabilities that I must impute to you. - My hope is that you will see in my lines and in spite of what I shall say my real, [ins]and hearty[/ins] good will towards you personally and my great solicitude that what you no doubt care most for.
