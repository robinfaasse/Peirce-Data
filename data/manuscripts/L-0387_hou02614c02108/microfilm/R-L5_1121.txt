It would draw us very much too far afield to dwell here in
any attempt to exhibit, even in the most general way, the contrast
that would be displayed between a civilization animated by this di-
vine love and the civilization that has obtained and now obtains.
Nearly two millenniums have elapsed since Jesus discoursed in Pales-
tine and since Paul wrote to the Corinthians telling them about the
divine love, and still even those who profess themselves disciples
of Jesus in the family form, and more emphatically con-
[gap] in the very heart and soul of their life to deny it. And so it
obtains that Christianity has thus far proved an almost entire fail-
ure. For it must never be forgotten that the progress, enlighten-
ment and amelioration of the last three centuries can by no means
be accredited to Christianity but much rather to the upgrowth of
free-thought and a staggering natural religion. And so we are brought
to the dawn of the twentieth century. And looking about us what
do we find and recognize? In the Material world copious
excellences and more in promise. In the Intellectual world much
but much more in germination and in promise. But in the Moral
and Religious worlds nothing but degeneration, confusion and anarchy.

How will it profit a man or will it profit man though he
gain the whole Material and Intellectual worlds if at the same time
he loses his life? Can mankind truly propser without a moral basis?
Can he truly live without religion? To me it seems as plain as day-
light that an emphatic No must be given to the last two questions.

A French author has written a book entitled "The Irrelig-
ion of the Future". It does not fall within the course I have laid
out to exhibit how the contents of the book belie its title. I only
mention the title in order to observe how the same expresses the
anticipations of many and the fears of many others.
