Chicago [date]Sept 10th 1892[/date]

Dear Mr. Peirce,

I write more as a notice that I am receiving your several letters and that I will address myself to the suggestions made therein with all the dispatch possible than [del]to[/del] in any wise any answers. The fact is the Courts here are now just starting up after vacation and a multitude of little preparatory things so press for attention that I have been more constantly occupied than I am wont to be, Still I shall get time at once to move in your affairs.

I however distrust my influence with Hegeler. He & I are on good terms enough but he is a pretty case hardened subject to influence, or rather he is a man of contradictory susceptibilities, you [del]seem[/del] to stir him with [del]a con[/del] [ins]con[/ins]siderations addressed to his Scylla side & bang you [del]run[/del] against the Charybdis rocks.
