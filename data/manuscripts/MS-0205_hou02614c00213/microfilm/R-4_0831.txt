Theorem V. Of any two different cardinal numbers, one is greater than the other.

Proof. For suppose N to be a cardinal number having this character, that it is either
greater or less than every other. Then the cardinal number that is G to N is, by the first two
conditions of the definition, greater than N and than every number less than N. And
by Cor. 1 from Theorem III, and the 2[ins]nd[/ins] general Corollary, the number G to N is less than every number
except itself that is greater than N. Hence the character belongs to GN if to N. But by Theorem II
it belongs to Zero. Hence by the Fermatian Principle, it belongs to every number.

Corollary. If the cardinal number, GL, that is G to L be greater than the cardinal number, GM,
that is G to M, by Th. IV. it cannot be less. Hence by Cor. 2 from Th. III, L cannot be less than M. But by the
1[ins]st[/ins] Cor. from Th. IV, GL is not GM, and therefore L is not M. Hence L is greater than M if GL is greater than GM.

[pn]23[/pn] Theorem VI. (Modified Fermatian Principle.) If a character, [char]alpha[/char], be such that,
taking any two cardinal numbers, A and Z, either [char]alpha[/char] does not belong to both A and Z,
or no cardinal number is greater than A and less than Z, or [del]there is[/del] [ins]some[/ins] cardinal number
greater than A and less than Z has the character, [char]alpha[/char], then, [char]alpha[/char] is also such that,
taking any two cardinal numbers, B and Y, either [char]alpha[/char] does not belong both to B and Y,
or no cardinal number is greater than B and less than Y, or every cardinal number
greater than B and less than Y has the character, [char]alpha[/char].

Proof. For were there an exception, there would be, at least, one cardinal number of
a class we may call the [u]n[/u]s fulfilling the conditions that every [u]n[/u] is greater than B and
less than Y, and no number at once greater than an [u]n[/u] and less than an [u]n[/u] possesses [char]alpha[/char].
Then, by Cor. 4 of Th. III, every [u]n[/u] [ins]and also every number greater than every [u]n[/u],[/ins] would be G to some cardinal number; and by Cor. 5 from
the General Precepts there would be some [u]n[/u] G to a cardinal number not an [u]n[/u], which
we may call M, and there would be some number greater than every [u]n[/u] which would be G to
some [u]n[/u] which we may call N. But then GN and M would possess [char]alpha[/char], and if any [u]n[/u]s existed
they would be greater than M and less than GN, and yet there would be no cardinal number
greater than M and less than GN having [char]alpha[/char]. Hence, it is absurd to suppose any exception.
