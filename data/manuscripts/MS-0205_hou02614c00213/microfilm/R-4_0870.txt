$N$ is by definition [ins]1[/ins] greater than $N'$ and therefore greater than zero, and
is not greater than $N$, by a former Corollary. Hence, by hypothesis $\frac{N'\cdot P}{N'\cdot Q}$ is
both as great and as small as $\frac{P}{Q}$. Thence, by the third condition $\frac{N'\cdot P}{N'\cdot Q}$ is both as
great and as small as $\frac{N'\cdot P}{N'\cdot Q}$. But $N = Q\cdot N'$! Hence $\frac{N'\cdot P}{N'\cdot Q} = \frac{GN'\cdot P}{GN'\cdot Q} = \frac{P + GN'\cdot P}{Q + GN'\cdot Q}$
Thence by the fourth condition $\frac{N'\cdot P}{N'\cdot Q}$ is both as great and as small as $\frac{P + N'\cdot P}{Q + N'\cdot Q}$
But this numerator and denominator are $\frac{GN\cdot P}{GN\cdot Q}$. Hence, $\frac{GN\cdot P}{GN\cdot Q}$ is both as
great and as small as $\frac{P}{Q}$
