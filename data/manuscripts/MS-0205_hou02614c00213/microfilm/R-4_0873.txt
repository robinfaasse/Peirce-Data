[u]Chapter I. Fractions arranged by merely counting them.[/u]

To the above theory that quantity is merely a mode of expressing serial
succession, the objection has been urged that it is not true of fractions
which suppose measurement by which a judgment of the equality of parts
can be attained.

This objection is worth attention as affording some entertainment.
In reply, I will first define fractions without reference to any higher
operation than addition and will then show how to arrange them in
the order of their values by mere numeration.

In treating of cardinal numbers, we have used the two relations
of [u]G[/u] and of being "greater than." We have never used the word "equal."
We may now regard rational fractions as having to one another the
relation of "being as great as," with its converse "being as small as,"
which we will define quite irrespective of the relations of [u]G[/u] and of
being greater than. [ins](Notwithstanding the cognate words, the relations signified are utterly unconnected in essence.)[/ins] Only cardinal numbers are for us "[u]G[/u]" or "greater than"
other cardinal numbers. Only rational fractions shall be in the relation
"as great as" to rational fractions. We will introduce "equality" as a relation
subsisting only between certain rational fractions and certain [ins]cardinal[/ins] numbers.

above, the denominator below, a horizontal line.][/ins] And rational fractions are
