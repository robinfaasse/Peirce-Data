[del]28[/del] The truly fundamental theorem of pure arithmetic is not the
proposition usually so called, but is the Fermatian principle, which
is as follows:

[u]Theorem I. The Fermatian Principle.[/u] [u]Whatever character belongs to
zero and also belongs to every number that is G of a number to which
it belongs, belongs to all numbers.[/u]

[u]Proof[/u]. For were there any numbers which did not possess that
character, their destruction could not conflict with the first precept,
since by hypotheses no number without that character is G of a
number with it. Nor would their destruction conflict with the second
precept directly, since by hypothesis zero is not one of the numbers which
would be destroyed. Hence, by the third precept, there are no numbers
without the character.

3[ins]rd[/ins], No number is greater than another unless the above two con-
