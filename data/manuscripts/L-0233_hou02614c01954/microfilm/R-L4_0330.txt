We are so placed in this perhaps both-ways endless series of fluids that we find our ordinary matter to consist, apparently, of a pair of vortex-fluids, the vortices of the two members of the pair being very near the same size, — that is, [del]having radii[/del] [ins]longer not[/ins] more [del]than[/del] [ins]perhaps[/ins] a hundred times greater than the smaller. The larger we call atoms the smaller electrons. If there are but about 1000 electrons to an atom of hydrogen, the linear dimensions of the electron are presumably about a tenth that of the H atom. Now the Uranium atom being only about 6 times as large as the Hydrogen atom, — [del]and more likely less than more, it is true,[/del] — one would expect an electron to differ no more from a hydrogen atom than a Uranium atom does, the other way, which I can hardly believe. Perhaps, for some reason, a vortex more that $\frac{1}{60}$ of the size of another will be much more independent in its motions than if it were smaller. An electron would be $\frac{1}{62}$ of an atom of U, but $\frac{1}{59}$ of an atom of Pb. The Pb then has thrown off all its inherent electrons while a U atom has not quite.
