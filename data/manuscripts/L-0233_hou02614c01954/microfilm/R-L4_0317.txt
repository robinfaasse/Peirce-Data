points of logic. I should at any rate,
give the true analysis of the nature of
probability, a science which is still in a
bad condition owing to the useless con-
ceptions of Laplace and other writers. The
common statement that chance consists
in our ignorance is ridiculous as applied
to the sciences on which life-assurance etc.
is based, or on which the kinetic theory of
gases is based. The true general fact that
underlies it is the multitudinous variety
in each of the three Universes. Now the
first question to be decided is whether we
shall suppose that this variety was all
introduced in one dose at the first creation
or whether we shall suppose that creation
is all the time going on, and variety is
continually increasing. If we solve any
problem by analytical mechanics, we find
that there is just the same number of inde-
pendent quantities in every state of the
system, therefore, so far as nature is go-
verned by mechanical law, there is no
increment of variety. But as long ago as
1891 I showed that there were reasons for
believing that variety is, nevertheless, on the
increase; and I could now marshal such
an array of argument as ought to be con-
clusive on this point. Others, and particularly

[pn]Page 7[/pn]
Renouvier, have taken the same general position. But there has been
no agreement as to manner in which the variety is introduced. However
introduced, it involves the denial of absolute necessitarianism. In order
to show that this world and the other have one constitution I must hold
that the existing sharp line of demarcation between mind and matter
is the result of development, or as people say now, of evolution. I insist
that logic requires that we should push unification [ins]of nature[/ins] and evolution as far
as the facts at all permit. I suppose therefore that in [del]some[/del] [ins]an[/ins] excessively
early stage of the three Universes, — a stage of which but very scanty relics
are left, — there was indefinitely little distinction between them.
