We have to consider complex consequences, that is, such as
contain more than one sign of consequence. The simplest of
these are of the forms
[u][u]A[/u]†B[/u]†C and [u]A[/u]†[u]B[/u]†C.
In the latter, A is the antecedent, B†C the consequent. The
rule of notation is, that the vinculum, or horizontal line, is to be
extended over the whole antecedent. According to this rule, the ante-
cedent is what is covered by the uppermost vinculum which begins
at the beginning of the formula. There is a good reason for this
notation, which will appear in the sequel. As an example of
a complex consequence, such as would be expressed in the form
[u][u]A[/u]†B[/u]†C,
take this: "if from any [del]being[/del] [ins]lying thing[/ins] being a man, it follows that
he will die, the Bible errs." As an example of a consequence
expressible in the form
[u]A[/u]†[u]B[/u]†C,
take this: "if any being is a man, then, if things take
their ordinary course, he will die." Far more complicated
expressions than these will present themselves.
