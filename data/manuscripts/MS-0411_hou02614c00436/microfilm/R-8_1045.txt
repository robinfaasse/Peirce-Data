shall mean [del]any[/del] [ins]the[/ins] state of things in which if A is true, B is true. [del]Thus[/del]

[u]A[/u] | [u]B[/u] true (i.e. substantially necessary)

I shall call the sign | the [u]sign of consequence[/u]. It is a sign of inclusion.* For

[u]A[/u] | [u]B[/u] necessary

means that every possible state of things in which A is true is included among the possible states of things in which B is true.

§ 83. An apology is due for [del]thrusting[/del] [ins]putting[/ins] forth a new sign of inclusion. For nothing could be neater than the sign which the [del]eminent[/del] [ins]admirable[/ins] logician Augustus De Morgan offered for this purpose in [date]1846[/date]. Namely, he wrote

A ) B

to signify that if anything belongs to the class A it follows that it belongs to the class B, or all the As are included among the Bs. Subsequently, De Morgan doubled his sign in practice. But that did not really alter its meaning at all. Sir William Hamilton, who
