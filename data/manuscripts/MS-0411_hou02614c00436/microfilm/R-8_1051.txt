Hispanus. It is impossible to read intelligently any
medieval doctor later than Aquinas without under-
standing [u]consequentiae[/u]. But the matter is stated
all wrong by W. W. R. Ball in his "History of Mathe-
matics in Cambridge," and his long account of
disputations is rendered absolute nonsense in con-
sequence. Such a mixture of utter ignorance with
brazen assurance is rare out of England.

Not entirely unknown, however, for Dr. Paul
Carus talks about Scotus apparently with no
knowledge whatever except of one spurious
treatise, and that most likely second-hand know-
ledge, and makes pretended citations & references
which are simply fictitious, there being no
such passages. He attributes to Scotus a doctrine
of Averroës, for which Scotus really expressed
the greatest horror, calling its author "iste dam-
natus Averroes."
