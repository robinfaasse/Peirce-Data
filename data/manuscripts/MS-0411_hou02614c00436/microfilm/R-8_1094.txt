[u]Rule 2'[/u]. The [u]non sequitur[/u] is invalid
$\Phi$ false,
$\therefore \Phi \overline{\Psi}$ false.

To say a non-sequitur is invalid from A false to B false,
is the same as to say that a consequence hold from
B true to A true. We may write $\Phi \overline{\Psi}$ true in the form
$\overline{\Phi \overline{\Psi}} \vert 0$ true.

Whence, since
$0 \vert \Phi$ true,
we have by Barbara
$\overline{\Phi \overline{\Psi}} \vert \Phi$ true;
and then, by Rule 4,
$\Phi$ true.

[u]Rule 3'[/u]. The [u]non sequitur[/u] is invalid:
$\Phi \overline{\Psi_1} \overline{\Psi_2} \overline{\Psi_3}$ etc. false
$\therefore \Phi \overline{\Psi_2} \overline{\Psi_3}$ etc. false.
provided
$\Psi_1 \overline{\Psi_2} \overline{\Psi_3}$ etc false.

This is obviously true; but the consequential proof
is rather intricate. It is left to the student. The Fermation
inference should be used.
