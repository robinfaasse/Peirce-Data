The name [u]principium exclusi medii[/u] was given by the Leibnizians in the 18[ins]th[/ins] century.

Many logicians have wanted the acumen to distinguish between this principle and that of contradiction; and others have seemed to think it a [del]merit[/del] [ins]good thing[/ins] to unite them together. Blunders about these axioms are very frequently met with, and can only be avoided by the [del]most[/del] strict watchfulness against the fallacy of begging the question. The expression ἀξίωμα ἀντιφάσεως, which I have attributed to Ammonius, is really far older; but it seems to be applied by early writers, as Alexander Aphrodisiensis, to the two principles confounded together.
