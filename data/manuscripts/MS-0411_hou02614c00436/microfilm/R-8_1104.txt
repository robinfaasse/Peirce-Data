§109. We require a sign for denial applicable to a single letter,
and such a sign suggests itself from the following considerations.
We have [del]seen[/del] [ins]recall[/ins] the rule for snipping. It is that two vincula
[del]begun[/del] of signs of consequence beginning at the same point
and uncovered or covered with an even number of vincula
can be cut off at any point, or under an odd number of
vincula elongated together. The rule for the vincula of signs
of inconsequence is just the reverse. Suppose we [del]carry[/del] [ins]apply[/ins]
this [del]rule[/del] [ins]formula[/ins] a little further than was intended, namely,
to such a formula as
[fig]logical formula with vincula over X, Y, Z[/fig] [u]true[/u].

Many of the advances of mathematics are due simply to
carrying rules further than was originally intended;
and indeed this is true of other sciences, too. What is it but
generalization? Cutting off the two vincula, we get,

[fig]logical formula with vincula over X, Y, Z[/fig] [u]true[/u].

What does this mean? The first expression meant that
either X is true while Y is false or else Z is true. The
second expression, according to analogy, removes X from
closer connection with Y than with Z. According to
the rule of transposition it is the same as
[fig]logical formula with vincula over Y, X, Z[/fig] [u]true[/u].

This must mean that if Y is true, then either X or Z
is true.
