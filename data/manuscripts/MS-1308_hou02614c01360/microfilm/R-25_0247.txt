My own impression had been, I confess, that [u]velocities[/u]
was a misnomer until Mach called my attention more
closely to the matter. I now see that [u]virtual velocities[/u] is
the correct phrase. First, as to the meaning of the word
[u]virtual[/u]. In the Century Dictionary, I attribute [u]virtualis[/u]
to Duns Scotus, as the inventor of the word; but Aquinas
has [u]virtualiter[/u], and [u]virtus[/u] had been in use to translate
[lang="grc"]δύναμις[/lang], and therefore as the synonym of [u]potentia[/u]
for several centuries. Many Scotistic terms passed into
literary English, because Scotus was the chief study
at the universities before the reformation, 1535. With
the rest came [u]virtual[/u]. What it means is well illustrated
by the citations in the Century Dictionary. The word
was brought into greater vogue by Leibniz. The sense
in which he uses it may be seen in the [u]Nouveaux Essais[/u],
Livre I, ch. i. § 5. It certainly does not mean [u]möglich[/u],
as Mach says. It means: existing in effect but not
actually.
