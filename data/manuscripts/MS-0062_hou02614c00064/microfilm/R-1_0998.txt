[marginalia]Ms. 62[/marginalia] I will celebrate the reception of this paper by the following
Notes on Secundal Numeration. [marginalia]c. 1905?[/marginalia]

[u]Part I. Notation[/u]

A number is denoted in this system by a horizontal row of figures
each of which is either a 0 or a 1.

The places of figures in the row are called [u]secundal places[/u].

Each secundal place has an ordinal number which is greater
by one than the place to the right of it, if there be any such.

The place whose ordinal is Zero is marked by [del]a[/del] [ins]a horizontal line
through the figure in it, [del]0[/del] or +.[/ins]

If $n_i$ is the figure in the $i$ th secundal place, the number denoted
by the row of figures is $\sum_{-\infty}^{+\infty} n_i 2^i$.

[del]Part II Addition.[/del] If the row is endless toward the right and
consists of [del]a left hand part with[/del] a right hand part composed of
endless immediate repetitions of a series of figures, this series
need only be written once if a dot is put over its two extreme
figures. Let $k$ and $l$ be the [ins]ordinals of the[/ins] left hand and right hand extreme
places, and let $x$ be any positive integer. Then the row will denote
$$\sum_{k+1-x}^{\infty} n_i 2^i + \frac{2^{k+1-l}}{2^{k+1-l}-1} \sum_{l-x}^{k-x} n_i 2^i$$
