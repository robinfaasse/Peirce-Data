a definite universe which contains, in some part of it or another, every [del]possible[/del] [ins]essentially[/ins] possible state of things [ins]that[/ins] which does not imply the [ins]absolute[/ins] [del]non-[/del]existence (as distinct from non-existence within a [del]supposed[/del] collection) of any description of thing, then I call that which exists in such a universe the [u]substantively-logically possible[/u]. It is this which I study.

I call an object considered apart from its characters, yet considered as having a sort [del]force of[/del] [ins]force of[/ins] self-identity which distinguishes it from all other such things, a [u]haecceity[/u]. All haecceities are just alike in themselves; so that whatever is possible of any one is possible of every one. The haecceities in the universe of substantive logical possibility must form a multitude so great that no property of the collection would be altered by supposing new haecceities to be added to it.

Let us express these propositions algebraically. Using the algebra of the quantified Boolian, let us use as indices Capital letters for haecceities, lower-case Italics for collections or non-relative characters, Greek minuscules for relations. Let us use your [u]$\Pi_u$[/u] [u]$\Sigma_u$[/u] not exactly as you do, but to mean, $\Pi_u$, whatever product can be obtained by varying $u$, etc. Let $q_{mA}$ signify that $A$ belongs to the collection $m$. Let $r_{\alpha AB}$ that $A$ is in the relation $\alpha$ to $B$, etc. Let us write $\Lambda_{0\alpha}$ to mean that $\alpha$ reduces to zero by logical rules, on substituting for it its definition.

Then to say that everything essentially possible and not implying the absolute non-existence of any description of thing exists somewhere, is the first thing to be expressed. The only part requiring any close attention is the question of when a proposition does express the absolute non-existence of something. $\Pi_A\ q_{mA}$ expresses the absolute non-existence of anything not $m$. $\Pi_i \Sigma_j\ l_{ij}$ expresses the [del]absolute[/del] non-existence of anything $i$ to everything, that is, to whatever there may be.
