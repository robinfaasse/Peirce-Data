Arrived at this point, he is able to eliminate $y$; but he cannot eliminate
$z$; and cannot solve the general problem, although he can do so in broad
special cases. It is to be remarked, however, that $a \prec x\bar{y}$ asserts that, taking
any two haecceities, $A$ and $C$, either $A$ is not $C$ or it is possible to find a
haecceity, $B$, which is $x$ of $A$ (that is, is $x$'d by $A$) but is not an $x$
of $C$. Now if we take eight haecceities, $H_{0}, H_{1}, H_{2}, H_{3}, H_{4}, H_{5}, H_{6}, H_{7}$,
