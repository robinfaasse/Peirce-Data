Boole had but one operation of multiplication. The logic of relatives
has two. The non-relative [ins]lines[/ins] product of [u]lover[/u] and [u]servant[/u] is "at once, a lover
and a servant." The relative [ins]or other[/ins] product is "a lover of a servant." The question
is how shall they be distinguished by signs. The rule requires that since I first
introduced relative multiplication [del]of[/del] into Boolean algebra, I should have the
right to dictate the sign for it. This I have done with strict regard to historical
propriety. The multiplication of non-relative logic may equally be regarded as
non-relative or as relative multiplication. Thus, when we speak of a [del]black man[/del] [ins]red cow[/ins],
we may say that we mean that which is at once a cow and a red thing, or
we may say that red is a relative term whose correlate is cow. One [del]mode of ana-[/del]
lysis is as correct as the other. Hence, in non-relative logic, all signs of multiplication
are equally applicable. But in relative logic, we must separate [del]them[/del] into two
classes, these signifying relative and those signifying [del]non-relative[/del] multiplication.
The two multiplications existed side by side in algebra long before they were both
used in Boolean algebra. All non-commutative multiplication, [del]such[/del] as that of
quaternions, is relative. All operational multiplication is relative. [del]The[/del] Relative
