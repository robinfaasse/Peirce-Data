Two semilogical binary relatives require special signs. These are "identical
with —" and "other than —". Throughout the calculus of operations, 1, has
always been taken as the sign of identity. [del]It[/del] [ins]This convention[/ins] could not be avoided. But in
order to show its peculiar character, as well as to avoid confusion with the
Boolean [u]1[/u], I would make the relative of identity an upright heavy stroke, [u]1[/u].
"Other than —" or "not —" is its negative; and may be formed by joining to
the vertical stroke an obelus, so as to make a T. Schröder uses 1° for "identi-
cal with —" and 0° for other than. These signs seem to me objectionable;
because they seem to imply, that identity is related to [ins]co-[/ins]existence as other-
ness is related to incompatibility. Now this is not true. If 1° is to be used
for "identical with —", then 0° should be used for "other than —" to show that the
relation to incompatibility is just the reverse of that of identity to coexis-
tence. But if we reject 1 for the symbol of the universe, we have no
reason to put any diacritical mark against it as the symbol of
identity. That once agreed upon, the symbol T seems almost required
for "other than —".
