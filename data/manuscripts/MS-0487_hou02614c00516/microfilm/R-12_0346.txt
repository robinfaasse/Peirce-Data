[u]Rule XI[/u]. A double enclosure, consisting of one oval immediately surrounding another, with nothing between except perhaps a line connecting [del]what is[/del] [ins]something[/ins] within the inmost to something [del]without[/del] [ins]inserted[/ins] without the outermost, has no effect, and may be [del]added[/del] [ins]inserted[/ins] or removed anywhere.

Proof. Anything may be illatively transformed into itself; for such transformation makes no difference, being a mere way of conceiving the doing of nothing.

Hence, [fig]circle with letter A inside[/fig] may be illatively transformed into [fig]circle with letter A inside[/fig], whatever graph A may be.

Hence, by Rule III, A may be illatively transformed into [fig]double circle with letter A inside[/fig] And by Rule IV, [fig]double circle with letter A inside[/fig] may be illatively transformed into A. That is to say, the double enclosure may be inserted or removed [ins]when standing alone[/ins].
