[u]Rule I[/u]. A blank sheet asserts nothing; but anything written down is asserted.

[u]Rule II[/u]. If a graph standing alone can be illatively transformed into another, it can be so transformed when it forms an unenclosed part of another graph.

[u]Rule III[/u]. If a graph can be illatively [del]transposed[/del] [ins]transformed[/ins] into an oval enclosing a second graph, then this second graph can be when unenclosed be transformed into an oval enclosing the first graph.

[u]Rule IV[/u]. If an oval enclosing a graph can be illatively transformed into a second graph, then an oval enclosing the second graph can be illatively transformed into the first graph.

[u]Rule V[/u]. Any graph already written may be illatively written again, the corresponding lines of connection being joined, provided the second writing is enclosed in at least all the ovals within which the first is enclosed.

[u]Rule VI[/u]. A connecting line [del]evenly[/del] [ins]oddly[/ins] enclosed may be protracted beyond [ins]any of its[/ins] [del]its utmost[/del] enclosures, and if evenly enclosed may be retracted within any of its enclosures.

[u]Rule VII[/u]. The inmost effective bond between two graphs is within the same enclosures as the graphs themselves.
