Rule XI. A double enclosure, consisting of one oval immediately
surrounding another, with nothing between except perhaps a
line connecting [del]what is[/del] [ins]something[/ins] within the inmost to [del]something[/del] [ins]inserted[/ins]
without the outermost, has no effect, and may be [del]added[/del] [ins]inserted[/ins] or
removed anywhere.
Proof. Anything may be illatively transformed into itself;
for such transformation makes no difference, being a mere way
of conceiving the doing of nothing.
Hence, [fig](A)[/fig] may be illatively transformed into [fig](A)[/fig], whatever
graph A may be.
Hence, by Rule III, A may be illatively transformed into [fig]((A))[/fig]
And by Rule IV, [fig]((A))[/fig] may be illatively transformed into A.
That is to say, the double enclosure may be inserted or removed.
