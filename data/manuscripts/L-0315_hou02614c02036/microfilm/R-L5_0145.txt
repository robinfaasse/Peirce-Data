Milford Pa [date]1894 May 15[/date]

My dear Newell:

I have a beautiful theory. All it needs is some facts to support it, of which at present it is almost entirely destitute. Perhaps you could supply them.

It is that in the ancient times and the ages long gone by, men preferred a jury of 12, or any council or committee of 12, and used [del]to[/del] [ins]a[/ins] count up to 13 to throw out extra candidates or to reduce the panel; and that they used archaic or strange numerals,

[page break]

It seems a natural guess that the panel for a jury would have been reduced to 12 in that way. The only thing wanted is some facts to show it was so, and the 13 superstition then will be sufficiently accounted for.

[del]Of course, such count-outs as children use differ from numerals solely in their limited application. Their want of meaning is not more complete.[/del]

[ins]Numerals differ from all other words in expressing no ideas, but only for experimentation, and in being used in a series. Though the small numerals are and must be numerals always.[/ins]

Very faithfully
C. S. Peirce
