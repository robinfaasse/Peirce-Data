There remains room for a word about artistic greatness.
The century has immortalized itself beyond peradventure in
music. Now though this art is passionate while mathematics
is cold, those two extremes approach one another in more respects
than the single one to which I wish here to call attention, namely,
that their beauty is of the same quality. For both seek to satisfy
a purely inward ideal, in contradistinction to the ideals formed
upon outward experience which the imitative arts, poetry, and
even architecture pursue. And if I do not deceive myself,
there is a sensible analogy between the characteristic tendency
of nineteenth century music and that of nineteenth century
mathematics. Just as the latter has disclaimed utility, so our
music has disclaimed the office of [del]merely diverting and amusing[/del]
[del]people[/del], and expects to be listened to for the sake of the pure
beauty it embodies. The point is that both adore an eternal
