It seems to be an axiom
with Professor Lawrence Smith
that a noun whose genitive
plural ends in [u]-ium[/u] must
have its accusative plural in [u]-is[/u]

Thus, I, i. 17 [u]ratis[/u] where we have
been used to [del]seeing[/del] [ins]reading[/ins] [u]rates[/u]

In I, ii. 3 dextera sacras jaculatus [u]arcis[/u]
where [del]we[/del] [ins]most[/ins] have been used to [u]arces[/u]

In I, ii. 8 Visere [u]montis[/u] instead
of [u]montes[/u].

In I, ii, 28 Seu rupit [u]teretis[/u] aper
instead of [u]teretes[/u],

In I, ii, 5 Terruit [u]gentis[/u] instead of [u]gentes[/u]
[bracket] we note also I, ii, 12 aequore [u]dammae[/u]
instead of [u]damae[/u].
