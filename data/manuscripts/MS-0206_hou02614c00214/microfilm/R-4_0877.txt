<u>Solution</u>.

First, what are the rational fractions? They are those fractions each of which multiplied by a suitable [del]whole[/del] [ins]cardinal[/ins] number greater than zero, will give unequivocally a [del]whole[/del] [ins]cardinal[/ins] number as the product. This definition is correct because the fractions arise as solutions of problems of dividing one cardinal number by another; and that is to say such problems are [del]reque[/del] of the form "Required a quantity which multiplied by one [del]whole[/del] [ins]cardinal[/ins] given number, gives another given [del]whole[/del] [ins]cardinal[/ins] number as the product." Now the true definition of an idea is that which makes the idea precisely cover its utility. Zero ought to be considered as a cardinal number. For the cardinal numbers are those which serve to answer the question, How many? Now, "zero" or "none" is often required to answer that question. Hence, [frac]0/1[/frac], [frac]0/2[/frac], [frac]0/3[/frac], etc. are rational fractions. But [frac]0/0[/frac], [frac]1/0[/frac], [frac]2/0[/frac], etc, are not rational fractions because they give no cardinal numbers as their unequivocal products with any rational numbers.
