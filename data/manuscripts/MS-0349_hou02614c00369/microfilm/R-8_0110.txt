Finally, modality has been objected to in toto as having nothing
to do with logic. This is an ill-considered objection. The premises
of a syllogism are assertoric, the conclusion is apodictic; of course
logic must consider the difference between them. The difference between
a possible, an actual, and a necessary fact has nothing whatever to
do with logic. But an apodictic judgment is one which is taken [del]as[/del] [ins]on some ground considered to determine it[/ins]
[del]a conclusion[/del]. An assertoric judgment is one which is taken for granted.
[del]An[/del] [ins]A[/ins] problematic judgment is one which is considered as a concept
& indeed is a concept rather than a judgment. No one can think of
denying that thus defined these are logical distinctions.
