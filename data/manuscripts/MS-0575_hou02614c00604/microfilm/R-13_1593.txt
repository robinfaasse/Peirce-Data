I define the logical products as follows:—

$1^{\text{st}}$, $A \mathbin{_{o}} B \prec A$;

$2^{\text{nd}}$, $A \mathbin{_{o}} B \prec B$;

$3^{\text{rd}}$, If $C \prec A$ and $C \prec B$, then $C \prec A \mathbin{_{o}} B$.

The exact [del]false[/del] correspondence between logical addition and multiplication appears from these definitions; and was, as Schröder acknowledges, recognized by me in my first paper on the subject.

We have, therefore,

$(x \mathbin{_{o}} y) \mathbin{_{o}} z = x \mathbin{_{o}} (y \mathbin{_{o}} z)$

$x \mathbin{_{o}} y = y \mathbin{_{o}} x$

$x \mathbin{_{o}} x = x$.

If $x \prec y$, then $x \mathbin{_{o}} z \prec y \mathbin{_{o}} z$.
