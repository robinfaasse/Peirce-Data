Whichever copula be adopted, it is impossible
to represent particular propositions [del]except[/del] by [del]means[/del]
[del]of relative terms[/del] ordinary class symbols and symbols
of their combinations without the introduction of
relative terms. The reason is that a particular pro-
position is not merely the subsumption of one
class under another, but asserts the [u]existence[/u] of
something.

On this account, it might be preferable, instead
of the copula of inclusion to adopt two, viz:—
$1^{\text{st}}$, $\smallsmile$, the copula of exclusion; writing No A is B,
A $\smallsmile$ B; and
$2^{\text{d}}$, $\smallfrown$, the copula of [del]inclusion[/del] [ins]intersection[/ins]; writing Some A is B,
A $\smallfrown$ B.

This system deserves following out. For the present,
I content myself with pointing it out.
