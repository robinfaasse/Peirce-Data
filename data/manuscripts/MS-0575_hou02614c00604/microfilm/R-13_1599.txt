[del]First part of[/del]
[ins]1st proof of[/ins] Theorem 2. $x_{y} + x_{z} \prec x_{,(y+z)}$ (Boole)
$x + yz \prec (x+y),(x+z)$ (Peirce)

<u>Proof</u>. By IV', III', III, II, I, IV,
$x_{y} \prec y \prec y+z \prec x_{,(y+z)}$
$x_{z} \prec z \prec y+z \prec x_{,(y+z)}$
$\therefore x_{y} + x_{z} \prec x_{,(y+z)}$

By corollary 1, $x+yz \prec (x+y),(x+z)$.

The converse of this theorem cannot be proved [del]without[/del] [ins]without[/ins] the aid of the third part of the definition of the copula.
