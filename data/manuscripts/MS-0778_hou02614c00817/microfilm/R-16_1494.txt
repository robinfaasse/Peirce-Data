[pn]12/[/pn] Now it is a perfect example of what weight ought to be
given to philosophical opinion, that simply because the
humanists found themselves in violent opposition to the
Scotists, the Dunces, they adopted the opinion of Ockham
([ins]William Ockham an Englishman of the same type of mind as
Hobbes, a little like J.S. Mill[/ins]) that universals, say the laws
of nature etc. were mere words, [del]and underscore the mere[/del]
because on all hands, it was perfectly admitted that
thoughts & words had the same structure & that laws were
of the nature of thought. Only the Scotists held that they were
real [del]and[/del] elements of nature, while Ockham and his
followers held that they are mere creations of the mind to
which nothing in reality corresponds. This is as much as
to hold that existence, — the reaction of things against
the other things of the universe, — is the only mode of real being
while the Scotists, like Aristotle & Plato held that besides the
existent realities [del]there[/del] [ins]are[/ins] higher kinds of reality, laws that
regulate the universe though they cannot be said to exist,
as well as potentialities or germs of being not yet developed
into actual existence, capacities. So because of this purely
exterior reason the humanists became Ockhamists or
Nominalists. They did [del]like[/del] Plato and Aristotle, [del]real worshipers[/del]
[del]of the Greek[/del] however, some of them took up the doctrine of the
stoics, materialist of the grossest kind but severe moralists,
while others became Epicureans, that is, they recognized feeling
as everything while at the same time they favored as no other
Greek school had done, inductive reasoning. Others copied
these first of modern philosophers; and thus it has come
about that all modern philosophy has been wedded
to this stupid and utterly anti-scientific doctrine that a
law of nature is nothing but a fabrication of the human mind.
