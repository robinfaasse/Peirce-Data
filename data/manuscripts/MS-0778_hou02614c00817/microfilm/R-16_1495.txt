[pn]13[/pn] ways of thinking. Logic has been almost totally neglected in modern times. To be sure there have been hundreds of writers on the subject. Bacon was no logician. So was Descartes. [ins]The celebrated Arnauld [del]Port Royal[/del] [del]logic[/del] [del]was[/del] came from his school.[/ins] Leibniz was quite an able logician & there was a whole school of Leibnizian logic among whom Lambert is quite celebrated. Ploucquet attempted a logical algebra. Euler the great mathematician did something slight but good. Tschirnhausen, another mathematician, wrote the Medicina Mentis & I could mention several others who are worth looking into. The Cockians too have their logics. Isaac Watts wrote a weak book. Condillac had considerable originality. Crousaz is not bad. But the great modern logic is the "doctrine of chances" or "calculus of probability" of which (begun by Pascal) Daniel Bernoulli may be said to be the founder & Laplace, Boole, & many others, including Karl Pearson, & F. Y. Edgeworth have done work of various degrees of merit & then there have been DeMorgan, Boole, [ins]C. S. Peirce,[/ins] Schröder, Maccoll, Mitchell, Mrs Franklin, etc. But on the whole the modern writers on logic are so flat and tepid as to turn one's stomach. Their wretched amateurishness even when they are best is disgusting. (I forgot by the way to mention J. S. Mill, Whewell, Jevons, Venn who all have a certain merit. Then there are a lot of modern German writers whose works are mostly bosh. Among the best are Herbart, Ueberweg, Sigwart, F A Lange, Husserl, Hillebrand, Marty, Meinong, Benno Erdmann (there is an old Erdmann too), Lotze, Miklosich, etcetera) Take them in the lot they are a wretched set of bunglers as ever were.
