Mathematics does not make itself responsible for any
categorical assertion. It only says: "If we suppose so and
so, then such and such will necessarily be the case."

Philosophy was by Jeremy Bentham distinguished
from the special sciences as [u]Cenoscopy[/u] against them
as [u]Idioscopy[/u]. Cenoscopy = κοινοῦ σκοπία the watch tower
of the common. Idioscopy is the watch tower of the peculiar.
This I think is right. The business of the special sciences
is mainly to find out peculiar phenomena. That of philo-
sophy is to think over our ordinary everyday experience
& make out what can be deduced from that before trying
to understand what is peculiar. It seems to me plain that
we have much knowledge that is fundamentally important,
that we all learn by the time we are fully grown, and which
is far more certain than anything that laboratory expe-
riments can ever prove, since these experiments take
this common sense knowledge for granted. In order to
explain why everybody does not think as I do on this
subject, why we still have the residue of the agnostics of forty years
