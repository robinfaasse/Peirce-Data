[u]Second Talk to the Phil. Club.[/u]

[u]Deduction. Define it.[/u] Several years'
reconsideration leads to this new definition.
[del]Not necessary but compulsive reasoning[/del]
Retroduction persuasive, seductive.
Induction appeals to you as a reasonable being
Deduction points to the premises & to their relation
& then shakes its fist in your face and tells you
"By the eternal powers, you have got to admit the
conclusion." [del]Effort require[/del] [ins]This distinguishes deduction[/ins]
That not necessary shown [ins]by definition of the
necessary[/ins] [del]as a[/del] argument about gold.
Compulsive means that you are [ins]logically[/ins] forced to
admit the conclusion.

Now, resume the division of reasoning.
You begin with perception which brings Surprise
To make that reasonable you resort to Retroduction.
[del]That finally finishes[/del]
This resembles perception in bringing the new,
and after this no new idea enters into reasoning
Now comes Deduction by which this new forces
you to join with it a transformation, [ins]— no new
matter but only a new form. And [del]this[/del] [ins]Deduction[/ins]
is defined as including all that.[/ins] Finally,
Induction, or the Experimental Method tests its truth.

Observe [u]Regular Gradation[/u] from the incoming
of the new, first in Perception
then in Retroduction
Then, formally only in Deduction.
while the reasonableness, quite absent in perception
begins to appear in Retroduction
still more forcibly in Deduction
and in its highest development in Induction.
