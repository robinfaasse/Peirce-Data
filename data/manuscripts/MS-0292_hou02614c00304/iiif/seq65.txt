[$\frac{\text{M}}{37}$] is indefinite in countless ways. That is, countless questions might be asked as to what they refer to, and would remain unanswered in the idea itself. But the thought is not directed to them all. On the other hand the concept of "something" not only leaves a [ins]possible[/ins] question as to what it refers to unanswered, but itself thinks of that question. That is what I call an [ins](this expressed by the loose end of a line of identity)[/ins] explicit indefinite. If we [fig]figure[/fig] convert any ordinary point of a line of identity on the recto into a point of teridentity by starting there a new branch of the line, [del]then[/del] if the end of the new branch be left loose, we shall [del]thus[/del] diagrammatize the operation of thought by which an implicit indefiniteness is made [del]ex[/del] explicit.
