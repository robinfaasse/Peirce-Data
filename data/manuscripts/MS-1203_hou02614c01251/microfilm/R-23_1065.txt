3. In a few words adopted from languages in which [u]qu[/u] is pronounced [u]k[/u], this spelling is retained in English, an [u]e[/u] mute being added if the sound is final. This spelling is convenient as suggesting an accent on the last syllable in polysyllables, and in monosyllables to distinguish one word from another pronounced like it. Even when the word is so far Anglicized as to modify the vowel-sounds, the spelling is retained by most proof-readers. If the consonants are modified, an objection to doing so is felt. Yet the [u]qu[/u] is retained in some words that are still further modified. The French termination [u]esque[/u] is freely used by English writers and is attached to English words. The following words are pronounced substantially as in the languages from which they are taken:
Mosquito, [del]Coquette[/del], Quay, (so in Old French), Pique,
Grotesque, Marque, Antique, Casque,
The word [u]barque[/u] meaning a small open vessel or a rowboat is usually spelled bark, but meaning a three-masted vessel having [ins]usually[/ins] only the mizzen fore-and-aft rigged is spelled barque.

In the following words, the pronunciation of the vowels is Anglicized:
Marquee, Burlesque, Opaque.
To these we may add [u]Turquoise[/u], although some of the best proof-readers strike off the [u]e[/u]. The word [u]oblique[/u] is pronounced differently by different persons.

In the word [u]piquet[/u] the final [u]t[/u] is pronounced in English, though the accent remains on the last syllable; and some excellent proof-readers spell it [u]picket[/u].
