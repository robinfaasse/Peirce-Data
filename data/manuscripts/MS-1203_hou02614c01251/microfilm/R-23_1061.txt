[u]English Spelling.[/u]

We will begin by stating how the different sounds are
regularly represented. And, first, of the consonant sounds.

The mute surd labial is always represented by p.
The mute surd dental is represented by t. In the word
[u]thyme[/u], which was borrowed during the Middle Ages from the
French, there was at first no h in English; but later it was inserted
in writing, to agree with the Greek, Latin, and French, while the
French pronunciation was retained. This is how it happens that
in this word the mute surd dental is represented by th.

What we may call the mute surd cerebral, though it is not
quite a simple sound in English pronunciation is generally
represented by ch. Only when it ends a monosyllable, or
[del]a[/del] monosyllabic part of a compound word, and is there pre-
ceded by a [ins]short[/ins] [del]simple[/del] vowel, [ins]or w,[/ins] it is represented by tch. Also, in
[u]despatch[/u] [del]and[/del] [ins]or[/ins] [u]dispatch[/u]. But [u]rich[/u] and its derivatives and
[del]which[/del] are exceptions. The principal words having tch are
as follows:
Batch, Catch, Hatch, Thatch, Latch,
Slatch, Match, Smatch, Snatch, Patch,
Ratchet, Cratch, Scratch, Watch, Swatch,
Etch, Fetch, Ketch, Sketch, Retch,
Sketch, Wretch, Vetch, Itch, Bitch,
Ditch, Fitch, Hitch, Flitch, Pitch,
Stitch, Witch, Switch, Twitch, Botch,
Scotch, Blotch, Notch, Potch, Crotch,
Hutch, Clutch, Smutch, Crutch, Grutch.
