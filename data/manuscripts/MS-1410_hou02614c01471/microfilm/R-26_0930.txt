A tolerably close examination has revealed no errors in the calculations, except such as necessarily come from the use of seven-place logarithms in calculating ratios of derived units; and these are perfectly insignificant in comparison with the uncertainties of the observational data. [ins]For instance,[/ins] Thus, the number of centimetre-cubes in a cubic inch is given as 16.387083, while the reciprocal of the cube of [del]39.37006[/del] the assumed [ins]part of an[/ins] [del]number of[/del] inch in a centimetre is 16.387087. [del]But there is very little reason to prefer 0.3937006 to Mr. Tittmann's calculation 0.3936982, and this last gives for the centimetre cubes in a cubic inch 16.387387, a discrepancy 75 times as large as the error due to the seven-place tables.[/del] But were we to use Mr. Tittmann's ratio of the yard to the metre, (and it is a mere question of opinion between competent persons whether it is not to be preferred) the centimetre cubes in a cubic inch would be changed by 75 times the amount of error due to using seven-place tables, (namely, to 16.387387.)
