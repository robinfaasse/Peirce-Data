[u]The Constellations[/u].

Down to the time of the invention of the tele-
scope, astronomers used to designate stars by
drawing certain conventional figures of men and
animals on their globes, and then describing
the parts of these figures upon which the different
stars fell. Thus, Sirius was the star in the mouth of
the dog, Canopus the star in the rudder of the
ship, Arcturus the star between the legs of the
herdsman, Capella the star on the left shoulder
of the charioteer, etc. In fact, the groups of stars
named after these figures are in use to this
day under the name of constellations and to
the number of 85.
