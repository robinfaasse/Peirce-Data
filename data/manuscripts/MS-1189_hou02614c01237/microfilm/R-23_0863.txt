[u]Anglo Saxon æ becomes a in most cases[/u]

[u]blade[/u] blæd, this
dissyllabic in Chaucer
[u]blade[/u]. Not explained.

[u]fret[/u] not from fretan but from
fr. freter. See Oxford Dicty.

[u]Nest[/u] not from hnesce but from hnesc.
[u]Ness[/u] not from næs but from nes
[u]Rest[/u] not from ræst but from rest

[u]steady[/u] seems to have the sense
of stæðig and the form
of stediġ.

[u]step[/u] from stepe, subverbis
[del]as[/del] steppan

[u]Exceptions[/u]

Key was till quite modern times kay
as it should be from cæg

clover. This form became common
late in the 17th century. The older
& commoner was claver. Neverthe-
less clover, though dialectic, did
exist. It comes from dialectic AS
clāfe while claver comes from the
ordinary AS clæfre.

[u]guest[/u] not from gæst but from
[u]gest[/u]. g before e followed by a
consonant tends to become y in
Late Anglo Saxon.

[u]grave[/u] from græf has taken the
form of the verb to grave from grafan
thought to be graf. Perhaps influ-
enced by Fr. adj. grave.

[u]raven[/u] may be suspected to be influenced
by Norse.

[u]whale[/u] hwal is modern hwal and the long
a may be due to Norse.
