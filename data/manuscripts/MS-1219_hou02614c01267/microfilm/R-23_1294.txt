The period and [del]comma[/del] [ins]colon[/ins] separate sentences; the comma and semicolon, parts of sentences; the dash and parenthesis marks, either, — but only in peculiar cases.

The main object of the points is to show the connections of the words. First, they must prevent the reader from [del]con-[/del] misunderstanding; Second, they must prevent him from puzzling over the meaning; and Third, they must prevent his beginning to try to put words together differently from the writer's intention. This general precept is supreme.

Certain general practices aid in its application. For the comma, the chief rule is that where the flow of meaning is interrupted, a comma is generally inserted, and not otherwise.

I take all my examples, or nearly all, from Goold Brown.
