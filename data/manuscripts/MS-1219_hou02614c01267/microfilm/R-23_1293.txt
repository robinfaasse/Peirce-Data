But why should the pauses of speech and the marks of
punctuation be so differently placed? Have they not
the same purpose, that of rendering our meaning plain?
Why, for this purpose, must language to be heard be dissected
in one way, and language to be seen in another? I think it
is sufficient to read over the above to see that it is mainly
because in speech inflection does an immense deal toward
the dissection, while in writing punctuation has to per-
form the whole task. So in the following:

Sir; the atrocious crime, of being a young man, which the
honorable gentleman, has; with such spirit and decency,
charged upon me, I shall neither attempt, to palliate, nor
deny; but content myself, with wishing; that I may be one
of those, whose follies, may cease with their youth; and not,
of that number, who are ignorant; in spite of experience.

Here the pauses are very effective in speech. But in print,
inflections would be understood to go with them which would
make the whole [del]meaningless[/del] unintelligible.
