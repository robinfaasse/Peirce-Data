The period and colon separate sentences; the comma
and semicolon, parts of sentences; the dash and marks
of parenthesis, either, but only in peculiar cases.

A comma or semicolon is inserted where the flow
of meaning is interrupted; and of these the comma is
chosen, unless it is requisite to divide the sentence into
parts, and these parts again into parts. This rule should
be the only one [ins]for the comma and semicolon[/ins], and should be subject to no exception but
this, that where there is danger of the words being wrongly
connected by the reader, a point, generally a comma —
sometimes a dash, or comma and dash, or even a paren-
thesis, — may be inserted.
