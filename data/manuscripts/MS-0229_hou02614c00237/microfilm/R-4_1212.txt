All that the doctrine of fractions consists of, or could conceivably consist of, is to establish their order of succession upon the ring of rational quantity.

Ring-order has this peculiarity, that it is a relation between four objects. That is, nothing can be asserted of the place of A upon a ring relatively to B and C. For if any [ins]two of the three[/ins] coincide, this is not a matter of ring-order. It belongs to the system of logical quantity to express whether two objects are or are not identical. Now if A, B, C are all on a ring and do not coincide, there is nothing about their order on the ring that is different from that of any three objects on a ring. But when [del]the[/del] four objects, A, B, C, D are considered, if no two coincide, each one has one of the others opposite to it and two adjacent; and thus they form two pairs.

When we compare the "values" of two fractions, that is, their places on the ring, then [del]instead[/del] [ins]supposing[/ins] of they are reduced to their lowest terms and therefore necessarily unequal, we must compare them with two others. If we compare them with [fraction]1/0[/fraction] and [fraction]0/1[/fraction] the question is, first, are they of [del]the[/del] opposite signs. If so, there is nothing more to be learned by comparing them with [fraction]1/0[/fraction] and [fraction]0/1[/fraction]; for the answer to that question determines the order of the four. But if they are of the same sign, that is adjacent to one another relatively to [fraction]0/1[/fraction] and [fraction]1/0[/fraction], then the question is which is the larger, that is, is opposite to [fraction]0/1[/fraction] and adjacent to [fraction]1/0[/fraction], and which is the smaller, that is, opposite to [fraction]1/0[/fraction] and adjacent to [fraction]0/1[/fraction].
