[u]Immortality in the Light of Synechism.[/u],
By C. S. Peirce

The word [u]synechism[/u] is the English form of the Greek [u]συνεχισμός[/u], from [u]συνεχής[/u], continuous. For two centuries we have been affixing [u]=ist[/u] and [u]=ism[/u] to words, in order to note sects which exalt the importance of [del]those[/del] [ins]those[/ins] elements which the stem-words signify. Thus, [u]materialism[/u] is the doctrine that matter is everything, [u]idealism[/u] the doctrine that ideas are everything, [u]dualism[/u] the philosophy which splits everything in two. In like manner, I have proposed to make [u]synechism[/u] mean the tendency to regard everything as continuous. [ins]*[/ins]

[del]I have[/del] [ins]I have[/ins] for many years [del]been endeavoring[/del] to develop this [del]idea[/del], and have, of late, [del]given some[/del] of my results in The Monist.
