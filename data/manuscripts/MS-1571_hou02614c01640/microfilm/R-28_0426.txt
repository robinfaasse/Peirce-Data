In a necessary proposition the predicate is said to be
contained in a certain word or [ins]general[/ins] class; in a contingent
proposition the predicate is said to be embodied in
certain observed things. But in the former case, since this
class [del]is[/del] [ins]must be[/ins] illustrated & embodied in some thing; the predi-
cate is [del]still[/del] indirectly embodied in that same thing. And
therefore the predicate of a proposition is determined, by that
proposition, directly or indirectly, to be embodied in some
thing.

These then are the parts which the subject and predicate
play in a proposition; the subject takes on attributes
and the predicate embodies itself.
