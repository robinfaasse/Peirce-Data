A multitude [u]p[/u] is the product of a multitude
[u]m[/u] as multiplier, and of [u]n[/u] as multiplicand,
if, and only if, [del]the[/del] the Ps Ms and Ns being
collections whose multitudes are P, M, and N
respectively, there is necessarily a triadic relation,
[u]T[/u], in which every P stands to an M for an N no
other P standing to the same M for the same
N, and such that to every M and for every
N one P and one only is in the relation [u]T[/u].

Finally, secundal fractions are to
be considered, pp 15-17.
