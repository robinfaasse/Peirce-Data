The old subject of this kind of reasoning is this:

All men are mortal

Socrates is a man.

Therefore, Socrates is mortal.

The rule "all men are mortal" is called the major premise; the case, Socrates is a man, is called
the minor premise. The general name mortal which is the character attributed in the conclusion
to the case, on account of the rule, is called the major term. The case subject, Socrates, is
called the minor term. And general name, "man" which links the subject of the rule and the attri-
bute, or as we say, in logical terminology, the predicate of the rule, and the subject of the
minor term, and links them both in rules and variations of the syllogism. But they are vanity,
we pass them by.

We next come to indirect syllogisms. And first what is called the minor, and indirect
syllogism. This is a complication of a direct syllogism. Suppose we start with the following
direct syllogism.

Rule. All men are mortal.

Case. All the patriarchs, including Enoch, and Elijah were [ins]men[/ins]

Result: Hence, all the patriarchs were mortal.

Now, if, instead of this syllogism, we accept as premises, first the rule of this syllogism, and
secondly, the proposition that from the result something stated would follow, as for instance,
that this rule is to be error, then we can infer that the same thing would follow with the
case. So the minor indirect syllogism would run:

Rule. All men are mortal.

Case. Enoch and Elijah were mortal, the Bible errs.

Hence, if Enoch and Elijah were [del]not[/del] [ins]not[/ins] mortal, the Bible errs.

This is a direct syllogism, the premises, are, first, the case of a direct syllogism; second
the major indirect syllogism, the premises, are, first, the case of a direct syllogism, second
the proposition that from the truth of the result of the same syllogism, something would follow,
and the conclusion is, that the same thing would follow from the truth of the of the rule of the
direct syllogism. The following is an example:

Case. All Patriarchs are men.

Rule. If all patriarchs are mortal the Bible errs.

Hence, if all men are mortal the Bible errs.

Examples of these syllogisms will be found among the exercises, We now come to consider
negations.

To say that any proposition is false, is virtually to say that if it were true anything and ev-
erything would be true. Thus we get the following forms of indirect syllogisms.

All men are mortal.

Some of the patriarchs were not mortal, i.e., that all the patriarchs were mortal is not
true.
