VB 36 # 3

Prof. C. S. Peirce
Dear Sir
You were very kind in taking as much trouble as you did in writing out for me the full Copernican Bibliography as it was brought out by the 400[ins]th[/ins] anniversary of his birth in [date]1873[/date].
The great work of Prowe in 3 vols, you have not, & of course you cannot lend it to me. It deals to a great extent with Copernicus' life & descent, in which I am not specially interested. Having been born a Polish subject, but raised in Germany, I can hold the balance even between those who claim him as a Pole and those who claim him as a German. What I want to know, is by what steps he worked out his "hypothesis." Montucla's Hist. de Math. which I have is almost silent: and if it [del]be[/del] [ins]is[/ins] anything it will be worthless as Montucla evidently knew no mathematics himself. The book De Revolutionibus itself must be hard reading to a man who like myself has studied mathematics only in the new methods of Descartes & Clairaut, and who is to busy to make a living in the legal profession, to study up a hard problem by the obsolete Euclidean methods which Copernicus must have followed.
