Moreover, these books, together with other sources of information of various kinds, enable put an accurate knowledge of the pronunciation of Shakespeare's day within our reach. The compiler of the present volume was the first to undertake a systematic study of all the evidences; and after pursuing it alone for a long time, he had the happiness of being joined in his investigations by his friend Mr. John Buttrick Noyes. Some four years after the publication of our results, appeared [del]the[/del] the first volume of Mr. Alexander J. Ellis's stupendous work on [u]Early English Pronunciation[/u]. Mr. Ellis's conclusions were mainly to the same effect. Thus, with the exception of some nice points, — such as the precise way in which some of the vowels were pronounced, — the pronunciation of the language at the time when the spelling became substantially fixed, is known beyond doubt. Comparing this pronunciation with the spelling, we find, that the latter followed Latin etymologies wherever they presented themselves, often in flat defiance of the pronunciation.
