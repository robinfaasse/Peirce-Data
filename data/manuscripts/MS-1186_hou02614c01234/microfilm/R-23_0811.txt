[u]Spelling[/u]

§1. [u]The Alphabet[/u]. English is written and printed in an alphabet composed of the 23 Roman letters, together with [u]w[/u], [u]v[/u], and [u]j[/u]. No accents or other diacritical marks are employed; and their suppression is one of the elegancies of our language. For since they are not needed to render words easily recognizable, their introduction would gratuitously increase the labor of reading, by adding to the number of forms to be noticed, and would so detract from the pleasure of the reader, and encroach upon his fund of mental energy available for the consideration of the substance of what is printed. It must be acknowledged that a pretty wide-spread usage mars this [del]elegance[/del] [ins]quality[/ins] by the employment of the diaeresis and of the characters æ and œ.
