[u]§2. History of English Letters.[/u] Some points in the history of the alphabet and of chirography and typography throw light on some spellings. The futhorc, or alphabet of runes, (run is a whisper in Anglo Saxon) is supposed to have been used by the Northmen before the Christian era. It was employed by the Angles, and was precisely adapted for Anglo Saxon; for it had characters for the sixteen consonants, p, t, c, b, d, g, f, th, s, h, m, n, ng, w, r, l, and for eight vowels, a, e, i, o, u, y, æ, eo. The Roman alphabet was brought into Kent, as an adjunct of Christianity, about A.D. 600; and appears in a very legible Gothic Gaulish cursive form.* For representing the vernacular two letters, q and z, were discarded, two runes, "wen", ƿ, for w, and "thorn", þ, for th, were retained, a second character for th, ð, was employed, and [ins]also[/ins] a vowel character, æ, to replace the rune "æsc".
