But however imperfect the phoneticality of Anglo Saxon
may have been, that of Middle English was far more so,
and in the course of its development became more so
yet. It naturally must have been so; for English practicality
could not fail to reflect that it was not necessary to
distinguish sixteen vowels in order to render words easily
recognizable, which is the only purpose of spelling, a
purpose rather hindered than helped by phonetic refinements.
