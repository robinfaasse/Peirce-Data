[u]Enumeration of[/u]
[u]Tenses[/u].

[u]Order 0. Without reference to times[/u]. Men always die.
[u]Order 1[/u]. [u]Aorist[/u]. The house fell.
[u]Present[/u]. The house falls.
[u]Future[/u]. The house will fall.

[u]Order 2, showing the relation of one event to another[/u].
{ [u]Pluperfect[/u]. The house had fallen.
{ [u]Imperfect[/u]. The house was falling.
[u]Past Future[/u] (Conditional). The house was about to fall,
(or would fall).
{ [u]Perfect[/u]. The house has fallen.
{ [u]Double present[/u]. The house is falling.
[u]Present future[/u] (nearly Subjunctive) The house is about to
fall, is doomed to fall, may fall.
{ [u]Future perfect[/u]. The house will have fallen.
{ [u]Future present[/u]. The house will be falling.
[u]Future future[/u]. The house will be about to fall.

These are the 13 tenses which complete the
scheme of those commonly in use. Others are readily
imaginable: I [del]not[/del] [ins]know[/ins] whether the house had fallen or was falling
[del][tense indeterminate between pluperfect and imperfect][/del] [ins]when he wrote,[/ins] but, if not, it
certainly was about to fall; for it had been about to fall sixty years
before, and, indeed, according to a prediction, it was to have [ins]already[/ins] fallen (had
been about to have fallen) long before he wrote.
