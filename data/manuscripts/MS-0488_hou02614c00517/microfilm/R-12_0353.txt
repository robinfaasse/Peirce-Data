[u]Conventions concerning Graphs.[/u]

1. Each unanalyzed verb [del]is to be[/del] [ins]shall be[/ins] represented by a special character, and to each blank of the verb is to be appropriated a special side of that character, so that anything denoting an individual being written close to the character on that side fills that blank of the verb. [ins]The circumference of the character being divided into as many equal parts as there are blanks to the verb, beginning at the left hand side, the blanks may be appropriated to successive divisions proceeding clockwise.[/ins] [del]shall denote[/del]

2. The end of a line [del]is to be cons[/del] [ins]shall denote[/ins] some existing individual thing existing in the universe of discourse.

3. The unbroken continuity of a heavy line is to be understood as imposing upon its ends the condition of being identical.

4. The encircling of a proposition by a lightly drawn endless line is to be understood as transmuting [del]it[/del] [ins]that proposition[/ins] into its precise denial.

5. The writing down of anything which can be construed as a proposition shall be understood as asserting it; and the writing down together of two or more propositions shall be understood as asserting them all.

6. A blank, [ins]when considered as a proposition,[/ins] is to be understood as [del]meaningless when considered as a proposition,[/del] [ins]corresponding to[/ins] [del]That is, as[/del] [ins]implied in every[/ins] [del]or[/del] something the analogue of which is implied in every proposition. Thus, "What I tell you is true" is a meaningless proposition. And every proposition which, in case a second proposition is true, is true by logical necessity, will be considered to be implied in that second proposition. For example, "every man is either green or not green" is implied in every proposition, because [del]it[/del] [ins]this proposition[/ins] is in any case true by logical necessity.
