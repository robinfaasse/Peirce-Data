By the Principles of Association are meant the
[ins]general[/ins] rules according to which one idea has a tendency
to suggest another. Most psychologists reduce these
to two, the principle of contiguity or coadjacency and the
principle of similarity. The principle of contiguity is that
an idea is apt to be accompanied and followed by those
ideas by which it has been accompanied and followed
on previous occasions and this tendency is the stronger
the oftener such previous association has occurred.
The principle of similarity is that an idea is apt to sug-
gest others similar or analogous to it. Thus, the
thought of Niagara may suggest a hero or anything
else that is grand, and so similar to the cataract;
or it may suggest a crowd of importunate hack-
drivers, much connected with the place in every
visitor's experience.

principles of association, such as Contrast, and
Causation; all, however, allow that Contiguity and
Similarity are the most important. That affinity,
